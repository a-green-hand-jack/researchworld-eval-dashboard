The primary estimand is error predicting unseen benchmark scores for held-out model rows, conditional on k observed benchmark scores. Each selection policy has the same budget and uses only training rows. Hyperparameters use validation rows. Test scores are revealed only at the chosen coordinates; all remaining observed coordinates are targets. Query count is not token/runtime cost.

Candidate method: shrink pairwise-observed covariance toward its diagonal after positive-semidefinite projection, and greedily choose measurements maximizing reduction in total posterior variance. Prediction uses conditional linear regression. This is an established optimal-design principle applied as a transparent baseline method; no novelty claim is made for covariance conditioning or greedy design. A mathematical Gaussian risk identity can be checked numerically; it is not an assertion that leaderboard scores are Gaussian or a guarantee under shift.

Planned controls separate prediction and selection: covariance with random, marginal variance, marginal correlation, D-optimal and A-optimal selection; mean, rank-one ability and direct ridge predictors at A-optimal queries. Random policy is averaged over independent query draws. Hold-out by model name prevents identical-name revision leakage; namespace group splits are a proxy for provider shift, not a verified lineage-family split. Dense target cohorts permit truthful query simulation, while natural/synthetic missingness may be retained in training. Primary reporting includes raw-score MAE/RMSE and task-standardized RMSE, paired effects and uncertainty. k-needed will be operationally defined by explicit error tolerances rather than asserted universally.
