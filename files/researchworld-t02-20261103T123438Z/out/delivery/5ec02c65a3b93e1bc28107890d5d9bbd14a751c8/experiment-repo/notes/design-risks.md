# Analysis design considerations for plan settlement

These are constraints/risks identified from the task and model, not empirical findings.

1. **Commutativity and the split.** An independently random split of ordered input pairs permits `(b,a)` to be in training while `(a,b)` is held out. For addition, label equality can then be used without recovering the full modular rule. A held-out accuracy near 95% still requires much more than this shortcut, but low/intermediate accuracy may partly reflect it. A useful robustness design assigns unordered orbits `{(a,b),(b,a)}` jointly to train or validation and reports its exact realized training fraction. Diagonal orbits have size one. This is an analysis/data control, not evidence of leakage in an implementation: the ordered pairs themselves remain disjoint.
2. **Initialization and width.** Default Linear initialization makes per-hidden-unit input scale independent of width and output weights scale as width^-1/2. Output variance is approximately constant but feature-learning rates are not guaranteed matched. 'Same optimizer/hyperparameters' therefore supports a protocol-specific width effect, not parameterization-invariant causality. A bounded initial-scale/lr robustness should be considered.
3. **Fourier template interpretation.** Full-grid class-centered logit energy in `(k,k,-k)` modes characterizes alignment of the computed function, not a unique internal circuit. Partial training fit alone may induce some alignment because training labels follow the arithmetic rule. Correlation of this score with generalization cannot establish mediation. Frozen-feature or other intervention controls would be required for a stronger mechanism claim, and even those have capacity/optimization caveats.
4. **Capacity versus delayed events.** Narrow runs failing to cross by the budget may be capacity-limited, slower, or both. The main data cannot distinguish these possibilities without longer follow-up/controls. Report censoring, final accuracy and losses, not just event-time means conditional on crossing.
5. **Task specificity.** p31 addition, full-batch AdamW and a two-affine-layer ReLU MLP do not establish a rule across Transformers, deeper networks, other arithmetic tasks, data fractions or primes. At least one bounded task/data robustness is needed to map scope; no finite grid establishes universality.
6. **Compute matching.** The gather MLP avoids multiply-by-zero work in the one-hot input layer. Dense-layer nominal FLOPs overcount the actual input affine operation. Parameter count times updates is a rough proxy, not measured compute. Report recorded wall/CPU times and optionally operation-aware estimates (dominant output-layer forward/backward scaling with n_train*p*width) with clear labels. Fixed-update and compute-matched comparisons answer different questions.
