# Suggested captions (self-contained; paper-repo may shorten, but should keep the definitions and the resampling unit)

Common definitions. *nMSE*: pooled squared error of the held-out model's scores on the fixed target benchmarks, each benchmark z-scored with training-model mean and sd; the column-mean predictor (k=0) scores about 1. *Protocol B*: a random 25% of benchmarks (34% when N<=7) are fixed targets that are never selected, the same for every k; selection ordering and all hyper-parameters are fitted on training models only; the new model reveals its first k benchmarks in the selector's order (among those it has scores for). *Bands/bars*: 95% percentile bootstrap over held-out models (500 resamples; paired for differences). *Full-information floor*: same rule, all non-target benchmarks revealed. Seeds: `--seed 0`, 3 repetitions of 10-fold CV (MTEB-56), 2 (MTEB-275), see experiment-repo `results/README.md`.

**Fig. 1 (`fig1_main_curves.pdf`).** Held-out nMSE against the number k of benchmarks run, for random selection and two greedy selectors fitted on training models (conditional-variance under the factor model, log-det/D-optimal), with three inference rules (ridge, kernel ridge, factor-analysis posterior mean). Rows: MTEB-56 (152 models x 56 tasks) and MTEB-275 (133 x 275). Dashed: column-mean predictor; dotted: full-information floor of the same rule. Bands: 95% bootstrap CI over held-out models.

**Fig. 2 (`fig2_scree.pdf`).** Spectrum of each standardised real score matrix (PCA on the completed matrix): (a) variance share per principal component, (b) cumulative share; dotted line = 90%. Matrix sizes (models x benchmarks) in the legend.

**Fig. 3 (`fig3_paired_vs_random.pdf`).** Paired difference in nMSE between an informed selector and random selection (same kernel-ridge inference, same held-out models); below 0 means informed selection is better. Panels: random folds on three matrices, then leave-family-out and temporal (train on the earliest 4/5 of dated models, test on the newest ~1/5, a single fold) on MTEB-56. Bands: paired 95% bootstrap CI over held-out models; dashed lines (random-fold panels only): the same CI from a bootstrap over strict families (`cluster_paired_B_*.csv`, cluster = family, 1000 resamples), which keeps all of a family's models together.

**Fig. 4 (`fig4_conditions.pdf`).** Sensitivity to data conditions on MTEB-56 at k=4 (top) and k=16 (bottom): extra missing-completely-at-random training cells, number of training models M, and fixed rank of the factor model used for selection and inference. Kernel ridge (left, middle) and factor-analysis posterior (right). Crosses: column-mean error under the same condition. Log y axis; bars: 95% bootstrap CI. At M=15 and k=16 the number of revealed benchmarks exceeds M and the regression-based rules are unstable. The "main" points are the main run (3 repetitions), not a separate ablation run.

**Fig. 4b (`fig4b_datasets.pdf`).** The Fig. 1 comparison on each real leaderboard matrix (protocol B, random folds); k axes differ because N ranges from 6 to 472.

**Fig. 5 (`fig5_shift.pdf`).** Held-out nMSE (kernel ridge, log axis) under random folds, leave-family-out and temporal splits; the temporal split has a single fold of about 30 test models, so its CIs reflect those models only.

**Fig. 6 (`fig6_theory.pdf`).** Theory against experiment. (a-c) SYNTHETIC: excess error over the noise floor for random k under Bayes-optimal inference in a known rank-r factor model (Monte Carlo) with the Jensen lower bound r c/(1+kc) and Wishart upper bound r/(k-r-1). (d) SYNTHETIC, N=16: fraction of the random-to-optimal gap closed by the two greedy rules against brute-force optimum, mean over 40 matrices and the worst matrix. (e, f) Model-implied (dashed, Bayes) and cross-validated (solid, factor-analysis inference) nMSE for a synthetic rank-3 matrix and for MTEB-56; for MTEB-56 the Bayes curve uses the covariance fitted in-sample, so it is optimistic.

**Fig. A1 (`figA1_all_selectors.pdf`).** All ten selectors (kernel ridge, protocol B). Leverage-score, pivoted-QR and highest-variance selection are worse than random at small k.

**Fig. A2 (`figA2_oracle_leak_cost.pdf`).** MTEB-56. (a) Oracle selection, which uses the held-out model's own target scores (an optimistic upper bound, not a method). (b) Leakage check: selector fitted on training models only (solid) vs also on the held-out rows (dashed). (c) Cost-aware budget in per-mille of the total evaluation time of the candidate tasks.

**Fig. A3 (`figA3_theory_vs_cv_all.pdf`).** Panel (e)/(f) of Fig. 6 for every matrix with a Bayes curve (synthetic matrices are marked "syn.").

**Table 1 (`table1_datasets.tex`).** Matrix sizes, observed fraction, leading-component shares, number of PCs for 80/90% of variance, participation-ratio rank, CV-selected factor rank, and the measurement-noise nMSE lower bound where repeat runs exist.

**Table 2 (`table2_kstar.tex`).** Smallest k whose 95% bootstrap lower bound of the gap-closed fraction G(k) = (E_mean - E_k)/(E_mean - E_floor) reaches 0.8 / 0.9 (pre-fixed criterion; floor = same rule, all non-target benchmarks). "$>$grid" = not reached on the k grid. Because the floor is the rule's own, a rule with a poor floor (ridge/kernel ridge on MTEB-275) reaches G quickly while its absolute error stays high; compare with the floor column and Table A1.

**Table 3 (`table3_crossing.tex`).** nMSE (± half-width of the 95% bootstrap CI) for every selector x inference rule on MTEB-56 at k=4 and k=16. Bold = lowest point estimate in the column; intervals overlap, see Fig. 3 for paired comparisons.

**Table A1 (`tableA1_kstar_best_and_absolute.tex`).** k* when the floor is the best rule's floor, and k* for absolute tolerances nMSE<=0.5, 0.25 (CI upper bound).

**Table 3 (updated).** As above but k in {2,4,8,16} and six selectors; the full ten-selector version is `tableA2_crossing_all.tex`.

**Table A3 (`tableA3_oracle.tex`).** Oracle selection (greedy under the factor-model predictor using the held-out model's own target scores, so an optimistic upper bound, not a method) against random and the two deployable greedy selectors; kernel ridge, same runs and repetitions; "--" = k outside that dataset's grid.

**Table A4 (`tableA4_leakage.tex`).** nMSE (kernel ridge) with the selection model fitted on training models only / also on the held-out rows ("leaky"). Random is identical by construction.

**Table A5 (`tableA5_synthetic.tex`).** SYNTHETIC matrices only (known rank-r factor structure; base r=3, noisy, clustered, heterogeneous, rank 8, M=30 training models); factor-analysis inference; column-mean error and nMSE of three selectors at k in {2,4,8}. Controlled data conditions, not evidence about any real leaderboard.
