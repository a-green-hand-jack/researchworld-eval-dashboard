\section{Discussion, Recommendations and Limitations}
\label{sec:disc}
\paragraph{What a reported score means.}
After automatic search, the winner's score on the search set is a maximum over noisy estimates. In our real audit it overstated the winner's true accuracy by 1--8 pts depending on $n$, budget and search procedure, and more than four fifths of the reported improvement over an average configuration at $n{=}100$ was optimism, because the 72 programs were (nearly) equally good. The effect is not a property of one tool: it appears for exhaustive random search, bandit-style successive halving, greedy coordinate ascent and a surrogate-guided search alike, and it is governed by a simple ratio of evaluation noise to true spread (eq.~\ref{eq:opt}). Whether a score can be taken at face value therefore depends on $(n,B,\tau)$, which a reader can check only if the paper states the search budget and benchmark size; when $\tau$ is large relative to $n^{-1/2}$ the naive number is nearly right (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}c), when it is small it is mostly noise.

\paragraph{Recommendations (supported by the experiments above).}
(i) State $n$, the number of configurations evaluated and the search procedure, and label the search-set score as such. (ii) Whenever a few hundred items can be spared, report the score of the selected configuration on items never used by the search; it is unbiased and has valid intervals, but plan for its cost: reserving 30\% of the benchmark gave an RMSE of \RmseHoldRSNHund\ pts at $n{=}100$ against \RmseBootRSNHund\ for the replay bootstrap. (iii) If all search-time evaluations are cached (or can be replayed cheaply), additionally run the \emph{replay bootstrap}: it removed \BootRemovedMin--\BootRemovedMax\% of the optimism (median \BootRemovedMed\%) and had the lowest error among generic methods, with near-nominal intervals; where it under-corrects (zero true spread, large budget) it errs on the conservative side of coverage but still leaves 1--2.5 pts. (iv) For exhaustive/random search over a pool with no outliers, empirical-Bayes shrinkage is more accurate still, but check the shape of the pool: it fails when a few configurations are genuinely much better. (v) For comparisons, apply the same protocol to both sides (tuned-vs-untuned and unequal budgets are the dangerous cases) and base the test on the corrected or held-out gap. (vi) Test any new reporting method as we did: known-truth bias and coverage over many benchmark draws, a no-signal control (true spread zero; here also a label-permutation control), robustness to the shape of the candidate distribution, and the rate of false ``wins''.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\emph{Scale and scope.} The real audit uses one small model (Qwen2.5-1.5B, 8-bit, CPU), one benchmark (MMLU-Pro), direct next-token scoring (deterministic; no generation, no chain-of-thought, no sampling noise, no LLM-judged metrics) and a small hand-built configuration space whose members turned out to be nearly equivalent (true spread $\approx\RealTau$ pt). Regimes with large true spread, multi-stage programs and larger models are covered only by simulation and by the theory; the simulated worlds are tuned to be neither adversarial nor exhaustive. The compute budget (4 CPU cores, no GPU) ruled out larger models and per-method LLM calls.
\emph{Search procedures are our reimplementations of algorithm families} (random search, successive halving, coordinate ascent, a MIPRO-like surrogate search), replayed on a cached score matrix; we did \emph{not} run DSPy/MIPRO end-to-end. A smoke test of DSPy 3.4.1 with a local 0.5B model produced unparsable outputs and ${\sim}$45 s per request on one core, so we abandoned it; the demonstration-leak measurement (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp}) is an isolated check, not an end-to-end replication.
\emph{Truth.} The population is the \RealN\ cached questions; benchmarks are drawn i.i.d.\ from this empirical distribution and truth is the exact accuracy on it. This avoids a finite-pool artefact (App.~\ref{app:wr}) but implies duplicate questions in benchmarks whose size approaches $|P|$ ($n{=}800$ draws contain about 55\% distinct questions) and means ``true'' refers to this distribution; confidence in its relation to MMLU-Pro as a whole is limited by $|P|$. Fresh questions are i.i.d.\ with the benchmark; we do not study distribution shift.
\emph{Replay bootstrap} needs per-item scores of everything the replayed search touches; for adaptive procedures that generate new candidates (LLM-proposed instructions) the replay must be restricted to candidates already evaluated, a variant we did not test. It under-corrects at small spread/large budget, and its cost is $b$ extra searches (free on a cache).
\emph{Baselines.} Ladder and Thresholdout were run with one or three untuned parameter settings and are known to need more data than we gave them; our results say they are not drop-in fixes at these sizes, not that they are useless.
\emph{Hyper-parameters} of our own methods (30\% hold-out, 100 bootstrap replicates, 5 folds, normal prior for EB) were fixed from synthetic pilots before the real data were analysed and were not tuned on the real pool.
