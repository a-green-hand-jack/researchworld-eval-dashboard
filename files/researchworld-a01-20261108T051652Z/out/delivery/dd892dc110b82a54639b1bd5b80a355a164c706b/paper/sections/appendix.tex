\section{Reproduction details}
\label{app:repro}

The complete instructions are in \texttt{analysis/REPRODUCE.md} of the supplementary material; this appendix gives the information the paper's claims rest on.

\paragraph{Environment.} Python 3.11.2, numpy 2.4.6, scipy 1.17.1 (HiGHS MILP, 120\,s limit per solve, 300\,s for Copeland), pandas 3.0.6, pyarrow 26.0.0, pytest 9.2.0; Linux, two CPUs and 4\,GB per container, no GPU. Figures use Matplotlib and the paper TeX Live (pdflatex, bibtex).

\paragraph{Data and versions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Open LLM Leaderboard \texttt{contents}: Hugging Face dataset \texttt{open-llm-leaderboard/contents}, commit \texttt{\seqsplit{9c09a7cae43334062a82cb164f2ef255013dafa2}} (2025-03-20), file \texttt{data/train-00000-of-00001.parquet}, SHA-256 \texttt{\seqsplit{f13a7061e63133bc8745d89f8c7a56148584940ede22d3b27e21cad1e078dca5}}, 4576 rows; 79 duplicate names removed (4497), one flagged model excluded (4496); no-merge variant 3789 models.
\item Open LLM Leaderboard \texttt{results}: commit \texttt{\seqsplit{aa81ecc38fdc5708254b833923368970efdf5ef5}} (2025-03-15); used only to read the number of items per task (noise model, normalisation floors).
\item HELM MMLU, release v1.13.0 (2025-01-10), 57 subjects $\times$ 79 models; per-file hashes in the data manifest (hash of the manifest \texttt{\seqsplit{830bdb10a5518c50689d60c86623cc3d79ff5317d7e6b96408ea7dc2058de12b}}). No other version was used.
\end{itemize}
Hashes of the processed matrices are in \texttt{analysis/data/processed/manifest.json}.

\paragraph{Licences.} Neither score table declares a licence (HELM: the code is Apache-2.0, the bucket and site state nothing for the scores; OLLB \texttt{contents}: no licence field, a per-model \texttt{Hub License} column; \texttt{results}: no dataset card). We use published evaluation scores, redistribute only derived cost tables and small processed score matrices needed to run the code, and do not commit raw downloads. Formal clearance would have to come from the data owners.

\paragraph{Seeds and determinism.} Seed 0 throughout (target sample, evaluation-noise, task-bootstrap and task-subset draws, Dirichlet weights, bootstrap intervals with 1000 resamples). Closed forms and MILPs are deterministic.

\paragraph{Commands and run time.} \texttt{./reproduce.sh data}, \texttt{test}, then one target per experiment, then \texttt{summaries} (see \texttt{REPRODUCE.md} for the list). Individual steps take from under a minute to about 40 minutes on two CPUs; the sum is several CPU-hours (roughly 3--5\,h sequentially, not measured as one run), \emph{more than the 4 CPU-hours estimated in the task}. Then \texttt{figures/build\_all.sh} (about 2 minutes) and \texttt{paper/build.sh}.

\paragraph{Disclosures.}
(i) The rank-rule $L_\infty$ and uniform-gain columns were first computed with a tie convention that gave a tie at a rival's score no credit; a consistency check showed $L_\infty=\infty$ with $L_1$ finite on some \texttt{front0} plurality rows. It was fixed, covered by a test against brute-force min-max, and every file containing rank-rule rows was regenerated; all reported numbers come from the regenerated files.
(ii) A mid-run edit of \texttt{reproduce.sh} made a few background jobs exit with an error; they were rerun.
(iii) The robustness-draw files were produced before the time-limit flag existed; they contain no missing values, and we independently verified the trimmed-mean feasibility labels, but not those of the draw files.

\paragraph{Withheld paper.}
The task forbids searching for or reading the published paper that studied this question, or any page citing it. In the work behind this paper no such page was knowingly searched for, opened or encountered: literature searches covered the starter literature (read through the arXiv metadata API) and component topics (the bribery-in-elections literature, through OpenAlex metadata), and data and code searches stayed on the dataset locations given with the task and package documentation. No encounter was reported to us by any role.

\section{Further checks}
\label{app:checks}
\textbf{Condorcet versus Copeland} (HELM, exact MILP, 7 caps $\times$ 78 targets). The Condorcet-winner bound is tight within $\varepsilon$: median paired $L_1$ ratio Copeland/Condorcet is 1 [1, 1] in every cap and rank group except \texttt{front0} (0.984--0.987, at most 1.3 points cheaper), the bound is never violated, and Copeland never reaches first place where the Condorcet requirement cannot. It is untested on OLLB, where the Copeland MILP over 4496 models was not run.
\textbf{Convex cost.} Under a quadratic cost the optimal plan touches all tasks ($K=6$ on OLLB, 57 on HELM for the mean and geometric mean), whereas the $L_1$ optimum touches 1--8 tasks for head targets under the ceiling: ``concentrate on one task'' is an artefact of a linear cost. Only the mean and the geometric mean were run with this cost.
\textbf{Weighted mean} (200 seeded Dirichlet weight vectors): the median per-target ratio of the weighted to the equal-weight $L_1$ is 0.475 [0.473, 0.479] (OLLB, ceiling) and 0.400 [0.375, 0.423] (HELM), with a wide 5--95\% range (0.21--0.85 on OLLB); the ranking of challengers is almost unchanged (Spearman 0.997 and 1.0). Weighting therefore changes the cost by a factor of about two, but the direction is confounded with a change of rivals.
\textbf{Tie margin.} With $\varepsilon=0.1$ instead of $10^{-3}$, median $L_1$ changes by 0 to $+3.6\%$ and infeasible shares by at most 2.6 percentage points; $K$ is less stable on HELM (the share of targets whose $K$ changes reaches 86\% for the geometric mean under $+10$), so we do not quote HELM $K$ to the unit.
