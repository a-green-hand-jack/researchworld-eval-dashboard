## fig_cost_by_rule_helm (main text; all six caps: fig_cost_by_rule_helm_allcaps) — claims C1, C4
Nine headline rules; median, min, plurality, Borda and Copeland/Condorcet are the rules whose cost does not follow the mean-type rules. Copeland and the Condorcet winner have nearly the same cost (Condorcet winner is an upper bound for Copeland-type rules, see *).
**Caption.** Minimum targeted investment to reach first place, by aggregation rule and cap on each task's score (HELM MMLU, 79 models as targets, 57 subjects).
Top: number of subjects touched $K$ (minimised); bottom: total score points $L_1$ (minimised separately). Each dot is one target model; black bar = interquartile range, white circle = median,
both over the targets for which first place is reachable under that rule and cap (feasible / 79 printed right of each row; infeasible targets are not drawn and are not counted as zero or as maximum).
Targets already first under a rule cost 0. $L_1$ axis: symmetric-log (linear below 1), ticks at 0, 10, 100, 1000. *Condorcet winner: cost of becoming the Condorcet winner, an upper bound for any Condorcet-consistent rule.
Caps: 100 = score ceiling; best existing +0 = highest score any model has on that subject; base +20 = target's own score plus 20 points (capped at 100).

## tab_headline_helm — claims C1, C4 (main text)
**Caption.** Cost of first place by aggregation rule and cap, HELM MMLU, 78 target models (the model already first under the mean rule is excluded). "feasible": targets for which first place is reachable under that cap. $K$ and $L_1$: median over the *feasible* targets with 95% percentile-bootstrap interval of the median over targets (`tables/main_by_rank.csv`, group `all`). Last columns: paired ratio of $L_1$ under the rule to $L_1$ under the mean rule, median and 95% interval, over the $n_{\text{pair}}$ targets feasible under both (`tables/vs_mean.csv`). *Condorcet winner: cost of becoming the Condorcet winner, an upper bound for Condorcet-consistent rules.
**Note for the text.** Under the caps base +10 and base +20 the number of feasible targets differs between rules, so the medians in the $K$ and $L_1$ columns are over different subsets of targets and a comparison across rows is conditional on feasibility; the ratio column compares rules on the same targets ($n_{\text{both}}$; $n_{\text{rule only}}$ = targets feasible for the rule but not for the mean, which the ratio cannot use). Infeasible targets are not treated as zero or maximal cost in any column.

## OLLB scope (read before the OLLB captions)
Score rules (mean, geometric, harmonic, median, min, power and weighted means) were run on all 4495 targets that are not first under the mean rule (`pop`). Plurality, Borda and Condorcet were run on 57 sampled targets only (the 20 best-ranked and 40 at log-uniform ranks of the mean rule). Copeland was not run on `pop`. The `top500` slice (the 116 sampled targets among the 500 best-ranked under the mean) has every rule except Copeland at all caps; Copeland ran at 100 on 30 targets and at base +10 on all 116, and nowhere else. Solver failures (Copeland, time limit): 2 of 30 at 100, 2 of 116 at base +10; they are neither feasible nor infeasible and are reported as "failed".

## fig_raw_vs_norm_ollb — claim C6
**Caption.** Effect of using raw accuracy instead of the Leaderboard's normalised scores on the cost of first place, by rule and cap. (a) Median over matched targets of $L_1$(raw)/$L_1$(normalised) with 95% bootstrap interval (log axis, 1 = no change); each cost is in points of its own scale (raw accuracy ×100; normalised score). (b) Share of matched targets for which the number of tasks touched $K$ is the same in both runs (95% Wilson interval). Matched = feasible and non-zero under both runs; at base +10 and base +20 the matched set is the set of targets feasible in both (20–333 targets at base +10, 507–1103 at base +20). Open markers: fewer than 50 matched targets. Source: `tables/raw_vs_norm.csv`; base +20 has score rules only.
**Note.** The rank rules were run on 57 targets and only some are matched. At cap 100 the ratio is 1.6 for min, 1.1 and 1.4 for the power means with $p=-2,-5$, and 0.85 for the mean, so normalisation changes the cost by a rule-dependent factor and the order of rules by cost is not invariant to it.

## tab_rule_transfer — claims C1, C5 (main text, compact)
**Caption.** Does the cost ordering of rules carry over from HELM to the Open LLM Leaderboard (OLLB)? Cap = 100 (every target feasible, so $K$ and $L_1$ medians are over all targets of the set). For each target set: median paired ratio $L_1/L_1^{\text{mean}}$ with 95% percentile-bootstrap interval over targets (`tables/vs_mean.csv`, group `all`) and median number of tasks touched $K$ (`tables/main_by_rank.csv`, group `all`). Target sets: HELM, 78 targets; OLLB top-500 slice, 116 targets (the same for every rule except Copeland); OLLB all targets, 4495 targets for score rules. $^\dagger$ plurality, Borda and Condorcet were run on 57 sampled OLLB targets only (their ratio is to the mean rule on the same 57). $^\ddagger$ Copeland on the top-500 slice: ratio over 27 targets with a finished solve at cap 100 ($K$ over 28 feasible of 30 run; 2 solver time-outs), not run on all targets. *Condorcet winner: cost of becoming the Condorcet winner, an upper bound for Condorcet-consistent rules; on HELM it equals Copeland's median ratio to the three digits shown (Copeland is never costlier). Last row: Kendall's $\tau$ between the orderings of rules by feasible-only median $L_1$ (first value) and $K$ (second) in HELM and in the OLLB set, at cap 100, over all rules run in both grids (18 and 17, including power, weighted and trimmed means not shown; `tables/rule_order_stability.csv`, `helm|ceil` against `ollb_top500|ceil` and `ollb|ceil`; $K$ is not computed for the trimmed mean).
**Reading.** Min is cheap relative to the mean in all three sets (ratio 0.15, 0.23, 0.25). The median rule's ratio rises from 0.16 (HELM) to 0.34 (top-500) and 0.60 (all OLLB targets), and plurality's from 0.07 to 0.38 and 0.50, so the size of the saving does not carry over for either; Borda falls from 0.78 to 0.39 and 0.37. The orderings agree only moderately ($\tau$ 0.46–0.63 for $L_1$, 0.46–0.49 for $K$). The geometric and harmonic means keep their place between the mean and the cheap rules. Table width: natural width at `\scriptsize` is about 4.7 in (`table*` or `\resizebox`).

## fig_example_plan_helm — claims C1, C5 (optional)
**Caption.** One target, five plans. Target: Claude 3 Opus (20240229) on HELM MMLU, rank 5 under the mean rule, cap "base +10" (each subject score of the target may rise by at most 10 points above its own current score). (a) Points added to each task by an $L_1$-optimal plan of each rule, tasks sorted by increase, both axes logarithmic; legend: tasks touched $K$ and total points $L_1$. Increases of 0.001 are the margin $\epsilon$ that separates a tie from a win: 3 of the 10 raises of the plurality plan, 10 of 27 (Condorcet) and 20 of 46 (Borda) are of this size, so $K$ counts them. (b) Rank of the target under each rule (columns) after applying the plan optimal for the rule in the row; 1 = first (green). Single target and one optimal plan per rule (plans are not unique), so no uncertainty is shown; it illustrates the aggregate results, it is not evidence for them. Source: `code/results/example_plan.csv`, `example_plan_summary.csv`.
**Reading.** The mean plan costs 154 points in 16 tasks and also makes the target first under median and plurality (rank 4 under Borda, 2 under Condorcet); the cheaper plans of median (6 points, 5 tasks) and plurality (5 points, 10 tasks) win only under their own rule, leaving the target 5th–7th elsewhere. The Borda plan also wins under plurality and Condorcet.
