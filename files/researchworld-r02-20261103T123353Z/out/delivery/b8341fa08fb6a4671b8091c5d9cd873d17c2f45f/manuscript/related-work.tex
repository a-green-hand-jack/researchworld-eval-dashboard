\section{Related work and positioning}
\paragraph{Calibration and adaptation.}
Inductive confidence machines separate proper training and calibration and already include learned residual scaling \citep{papadopoulos2002inductive}. Split conformal prediction supplies economical marginal guarantees and locally weighted residual variants \citep{lei2016distribution}. Coverage, set size, adaptation and calibration variability are separate evaluation questions \citep{angelopoulos2021gentle}. We isolate these choices for simplex-valued responses; neither split calibration nor scaling is introduced here.

\paragraph{Regions for distributions versus label sets.}
The most direct permitted comparison is \citet{javanmardi2024credal}: conformalized predictors from distribution-labeled data construct probability-simplex sets using distance and Dirichlet-likelihood scores, with discretized-simplex size evaluation and distinctions between noisy and clean labels. Thus generic simplex regions, distance scores and simplex-size evaluation are prior contributions. Our empirical scope is the interaction of geometry, calibration variability, observable-group reliability and boundary applicability under controlled mechanisms. APS/RAPS instead uses probability-vector features to predict class-label sets \citep{angelopoulos2020raps}; their cardinality measures a different response space.

\paragraph{Conditioning and calibration families.}
Class-conditional/clustered label calibration illustrates conditioning and calibration scarcity \citep{ding2023classconditional}; batch multivalid methods target stronger criteria over collections of groups and thresholds \citep{jung2022multivalid}. Our fixed-group audit establishes neither multivalidity nor pointwise conditional coverage. Jackknife+ changes the refitting/calibration construction and guarantee \citep{barber2019jackknife}; this split-score study does not compare all conformal algorithms. Statistical group coverage alone establishes neither fairness nor decision benefit \citep{cresswell2024disparate}.

\paragraph{Shift and composition geometry.}
Weighted conformal prediction addresses covariate shift under assumptions including invariant conditional response laws and suitable density ratios \citep{tibshirani2019shift}. Our unweighted prevalence-shift control diagnoses the limits of global exchangeable calibration, not that remedy. The ILR isometry on strictly positive compositions supplies the log-ratio geometry \citep{egozcue2003ilr}; explicit smoothing changes the score when exact zeros occur.
