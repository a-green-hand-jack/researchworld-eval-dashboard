Endpoint Support, Contrast Leverage, and Symmetry

Abstract
Regression estimators of the global average treatment effect (GATE) extrapolate1
from a mixed treatment assignment to two global endpoints. How reliable is that2
extrapolation? We conduct a fixed-graph error audit rather than propose a uni-3
versally superior estimator. A finite-sample decomposition separates conditional4
endpoint bias from covariance-weighted contrast leverage. A support counterexam-5
ple shows why observed fit alone cannot certify accuracy; a complement-symmetry6
result explains why balanced experiments can conceal quadratic misspecification.7
Across 24,000 simulated experiments on controlled synthetic networks and two8
public topologies, larger degree increases variance at fixed size, unnecessary but9
correctly specified features can sharply increase error, and count-versus-fraction10
misspecification is consequential on heterogeneous graphs. A threshold mechanism11
can have essentially zero observed signal residual yet a missing global spillover12
effect. Correlated errors with unit marginal variance invalidate iid regression inter-13
vals; known covariance repairs this only when contrast bias is absent. Clustering14
improves some settings but is not uniformly beneficial. We report Monte Carlo15
uncertainty, estimator availability, and rare-exposure limitations, and release the16
full simulation and reporting code. Conclusions concern the stated response classes17
and fixed graphs, not arbitrary interference or real-platform effects.18
1 Introduction19
A platform often wants the effect of treating everyone, not the direct effect of treating one user while20
most neighbors remain untreated. With network interference, these are different targets. A regression21
plug-in estimator fits outcomes against own treatment and assignment-derived neighborhood features,22
evaluates the fitted response under all-treatment and all-control assignments, and subtracts the23
predictions. This makes the target explicit, but transports a fitted relationship outside much of its24
observed feature support.25
Prior work establishes regression adjustments for this problem and demonstrates the usefulness of26
appropriate interference features (Chin, 2019; Han and Ugander, 2023). Exposure-weighted alter-27
natives trade modeling assumptions for exposure probabilities and dependence conditions (Aronow28
and Samii, 2017; Ugander et al., 2013). These results do not imply that a low training error, a29
large sample, or a correctly included feature is individually sufficient for accurate GATE estimation.30
In particular, dense networks can concentrate neighbor treatment fractions around the allocation31
probability, making an endpoint contrast weakly informed even when outcomes are easy to predict32
locally.33
This study asks a narrower, experimentally answerable question: for fixed-feature linear regressions,34
which failure mechanisms determine endpoint error, and which apparently favorable diagnostics35
can be misleading? We separate three issues: endpoint transport bias, contrast-specific noise36

2 Related work51
Regression adjustment. Chin (2019) fits treatment-arm-specific response regressions using inter-52
ference features and predicts the global endpoints. Linear results require exogeneity and appropriate53
rank conditions, with stronger assumptions for variance formulas. Our arm-specific fixed-feature54
regressions belong to this family; pooled additive regression is a lower-dimensional restriction. We55
do not reproduce the source paper’s complete simulation program or nonlinear procedure. Han and56
Ugander (2023) generates graph/assignment covariates and selects them using ReFeX-LASSO, with a57
selection-aware graph-block bootstrap. The formal consistency result concerns the post variant under58
sparse linear representation and design assumptions; arbitrary feature generation is not unrestricted59
identification. We use known, fixed candidate maps to isolate their costs, not an implementation of60
ReFeX-LASSO or its bootstrap.61
Exposure and experimental design. Aronow and Samii (2017) develops exposure-mapping-based62
randomized inference, requiring correctly mapped exposures and their probabilities. Ugander et al.63
(2013) shows how graph-cluster randomization can improve full-neighborhood exposure probabili-64
ties under graph/design conditions. We include exact marginal-probability full-exposure Horvitz–65
Thompson (HT) and fractional Hájek benchmarks, while distinguishing target misspecification, empty66
denominators, and rare tails. Our bounded-size greedy BFS partition is not their 3-net algorithm. Our67
contribution does not assert that this partition optimizes a design or that clustering uniformly reduces68
GATE risk.69

3.2 Balanced symmetry can hide curvature108
Proposition 3 (Quadratic cancellation). Let Φ(z) = ( 1, x, z, s(z)), with fixed x, and m(z) =109
a1 + bx + z + s(z)2. Under a complement-symmetric assignment law, additive OLS GATE is110
unbiased conditional on full rank, provided this event has positive probability.111
Complementation sends (z, s) to (1 − z, 1 − s) and the regression weight w to −w. The conditional112
bias is w⊤s2 − 1 and changes sign under complementation. Full rank is invariant, so pairing113
assignments proves the claim (Appendix A.1). Both Bernoulli(.5) and independent cluster(.5)114
assignment are symmetric. This does not imply low variance, unbiasedness for general nonlinear115
effects, or validity at allocation probabilities .2/.8. A balanced quadratic benchmark is therefore an116
insufficient stress test of endpoint transport.117

5.2 Hidden endpoint effects and allocation sensitivity201
For the degree-128 threshold mechanism, Add’s mean signal residual RMSE is 0.000, yet its noise-202
integrated conditional bias is -1.000. The observed signal is effectively indistinguishable from no203
spillover; the missing global threshold effect remains one. Supplying the true threshold basis does204
not solve absent support: Oracle succeeds on 0/900 Bernoulli assignments and only 51/900 cluster205
assignments. The latter conditional RMSE must not be compared as if all 900 experiments produced206
estimates.207
Quadratic spillover produces a different lesson. At probability .5, Add often has nearly zero average208
bias despite being misspecified, consistent with Proposition 3. On regular32, bias is −.005 (MCSE209
.012). At probabilities .2/.8 it becomes −.558 (MCSE .019) and .592 (MCSE .020), respectively.210
Figure 3 shows why evaluation only at balanced allocation can miss this vulnerability. Quad removes211
the functional misspecification but can pay a large variance cost; fixed ridge shrinks that cost while212
introducing its own endpoint bias. We do not select whichever estimator wins each scenario using213
known truth.214
Degree-dependent spillover adds another model boundary. On Stanford3, using neighbor fraction215
for a degree-scaled-count mechanism yields Add bias -0.898, whereas the matched Count estimator216
has RMSE 0.033 (Table 1). The reverse substitution is not generally benign either. These results do217
not license Count as a uniformly better feature: it matches a different hypothesized response surface.218

5.3 Clustering and uncertainty address different problems222
Clustering improves exposure spread and some RMSEs, especially on WS, but does not uniformly223
dominate. BA/quadratic Add RMSE increases from .314 to .378 under our fixed cluster design, and224
the direction persists across all three graph seeds. Stanford3/threshold Add bias is not improved225
by this partition (Table 2). Clustering also changes the variability of conditional bias, so average226
unbiasedness need not imply calibrated intervals. We make no claims about alternative optimized227
partitions or maximum block sizes not studied.228
With correlated errors, iid standard errors can be severely anticonservative despite the correct linear229
mean and unit marginal error variances. On ego-Facebook, Add coverage falls to 0.480 (Wilson230
95% interval approximately .424–.536), while using known covariance gives 0.950 (approximately231
.919–.969). Figure 2 distinguishes this covariance failure from endpoint misspecification. Known-232

fractional nonlinear/multihop responses. Full status counts, zero-estimate fractions, and exposure242
diagnostics are released; unreliable rare-event RMSEs are not promoted as true-risk rankings.243
6 Discussion, limitations, and conclusion244
Regression GATE precision requires more than a rich predictor: the endpoint contrast must be245
informed by the assignment, the mean response must transport adequately, and covariance must246
be handled correctly. The audit suggests reporting contrast leverage, exposure range and rank247
availability alongside predictive diagnostics, explicitly varying plausible functional forms, and248
assessing allocation imbalance rather than relying only on balanced quadratic examples. These are249
diagnostics and sensitivity analyses, not data-only identification of the unobserved endpoint residual.250
Scope is intentionally limited. The graph is known, static, unweighted, and undirected; outcome251
mechanisms are controlled functions with unit direct/spillover strengths. We do not study endogenous252
network formation, time dynamics, treatment-induced edges, real outcome heterogeneity, unknown253
networks, or graph-population inference. Covariance is known only for an oracle benchmark. Three254
synthetic graph realizations and two nonrepresentative public social topologies do not establish255
universal rates. Our arbitrary BFS partition and penalty are not tuned or optimal. Rank rejection can256
discard estimable singular contrasts; normal intervals and delta-method risk MCSEs are approximate,257
particularly fragile for rare-event tails. We did not implement data-adaptive ReFeX-LASSO, its258
block bootstrap, GNN estimators, or optimization-based designs; no comparative claim about those259
methods follows. The restrictive source policy prevents an exhaustive novelty assessment.260
All findings are simulated; no real behavioral effect is estimated. Better network evaluation could261
reduce biased platform decisions, but false confidence in a model can also justify harmful global262
rollouts or obscure heterogeneous effects. These diagnostics should not replace domain-specific263
safety/fairness evaluation. Only public anonymized topology is used, with no profile attributes, rei-264
dentification, participants, or new platform data collection. Dataset license and attribution limitations265
are stated in Appendix C. This unattended research draft was developed with LLM assistance for266
hypotheses, proofs, code, analysis, and writing; execution artifacts substantiate numerical claims, but267
no human expert review or conference acceptance is implied. A responsible human author would still268
need to assess novelty, rights, and submission suitability.269
