# Feasibility assessment (v1, before the plan exists)

Tags: **[M]** measured by me in this sandbox (scripts in `feasibility/` of my repo; I report numbers here because neighbors cannot read my code) · **[S]** sourced from the supplied material · **[E]** planning estimate.

## 1. Verdict
The objective is feasible on CPU within budget, **but the headline question has a strong, measurable prior toward "little or no gain" for the mean on the supplied data.** A plan that presumes a width reduction will likely end with a null; the study is feasible *as a conditional/negative-or-small-gain study with a theory of when gains exist*, and should be framed so a null is a complete answer (the objective accepts it).

## 2. Measured facts
- **Data access [M]:** `ppi-python==0.2.3` installs in the sandbox; all 9 `.npz` (46 MB) downloaded anonymously via gdown from Google Drive in ~40 s. Shapes match `data.md`. sha256 list: `feasibility/dl_sha256.txt` (record these in the reproduction notes; `.npz` has no stated licence, so download by script, do not redistribute).
- **Cost [M]:** one `ppi_mean_ci` call: 2 ms (forest, n=100), 7 ms (galaxies), 12 ms (gene_expr, ballots), 138 ms (census_income, N≈379k). A study of 5 datasets x 5 labeled sizes x 2000 reps x ~8 methods is ≈ 10^5 CI computations → well under 1 CPU-hour with vectorised code, if the unlabeled-side moments are precomputed once per dataset (they do not change across replications once N is the full pool). The "<8 CPU hours" estimate in `resources.md` is credible. Sandbox has 2 cores, 4 GB, so cap memory: census arrays are ~6 MB each, fine; avoid per-rep copies of N=4e5 arrays.
- **Headroom [M] (key finding).** For each dataset, on the full pool, I compared residual variance of the best *linear* function of Yhat (what PPI++ power-tuning effectively uses: Var(Y − a − bŶ)) with a 2-fold cross-fitted *isotonic* (monotone nonparametric) g(Ŷ). Ratio of residual variance V_iso/V_lin and implied width ratio (sqrt; the width at n≪N scales with this residual sd):

| dataset | corr(Y,Ŷ) | V_lin/Var Y | V_iso/V_lin | width ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| galaxies | .741 | .451 | 1.004 | 1.002 |
| forest | .537 | .711 | 1.010 | 1.005 |
| alphafold | .815 | .336 | 1.003 | 1.002 |
| gene_expression | .963 | .072 | .977 | **.988** |
| census_income | .607 | .631 | .996 | .998 |
| census_healthcare | .707 | .500 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| census_education | .626 | .608 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| plankton (Ŷ binary, 2 values) | .836 | .301 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| ballots (Ŷ binary, 2 values) | .982 | .037 | 1.001 | 1.000 |

  Reading: for the **mean** with Ŷ the only input, nonlinear recalibration of the fixed predictor can remove at most ~1–2 % of width (gene_expression), because the predictor is already near-calibrated/linear on these data; with binary Ŷ, any g(Ŷ) is linear, so there is provably no headroom. (This is population-level; finite-n estimation of g only makes it worse.) Also the classical PPI (unit slope) vs power-tuned gap is small except gene_expression (V .088 vs .072, ≈10 % width) and plankton (.325 vs .301, ≈4 %) — PPI++ already captures it.
  Consequence: **the objective's "can the CI be shortened" has little room via recalibration on the supplied sets.** Routes that could still have room, each needing its own check before it is promised: (a) using covariates X (only census_* have X; check pending, I will post it), (b) other estimands (quantiles, regression coefficients, subgroup means) where the predictor-to-target mapping is not already the linear one, (c) small-n regimes (n ≲ 100) where CLT intervals under-cover and the "shorter" claim is really about finite-sample calibration/variance of the tuned weight, (d) synthetic settings designed with known miscalibration/heteroscedasticity, (e) heteroscedastic/stratified weighting (variance of Y−g depends on Ŷ — a width change comes from the *variance estimator*, not just the point estimate), (f) smaller-N regimes (N≈n), where the amount of unlabeled data limits PPI++ and optimal weighting matters more.

## 3. Risks and what to bound
1. **Null-result risk (high).** See above. Mitigation: state the research question as conditional ("when does headroom exist and is it realisable at finite n"), pair real data with synthetic data in which headroom is dialled by a known knob, and give a theoretical headroom formula (width ratio = sqrt(E[Var(Y|Ŷ)]/min_{a,b}Var(Y−a−bŶ)) when N→∞) checked against experiments. A null on real data plus a correct characterisation is a full answer.
2. **Validity vs width trade-off (high).** Any method that fits g, weights or variance from the n labels adds estimation noise; at n ~ 50–200 intervals often under-cover. Need cross-fitting (no reuse of a labeled point in its own correction), a coverage target judged with Monte Carlo error: with R=2000 reps the 95 % coverage MC s.e. is 0.49 pp (R=1000: 0.69 pp) [calculation], so "coverage within ±1 pp" is only testable at R≥2000; 1 pp deviations cannot be claimed away at R=500. Width must be compared **paired** (same labeled draw) with a CI on the mean paired width ratio, and only among methods whose coverage is acceptable (a narrower under-covering interval is not a win). Report coverage-conditional-on-width/ undercovering cells honestly.
3. **Ground truth and sampling design (medium).** Only `plankton` and `ballots` ship a real labeled/unlabeled split; for the other 7 the pool has all labels, so "truth" = pool mean and a replication = random split of the pool into n labeled / rest unlabeled. That is a finite-population design (labeled drawn without replacement from a fixed pool); coverage is w.r.t. pool mean, and N−n shrinks as n grows — say so. `ballots` has labels only for 1027 records and **no truth for the 78 150 unlabeled**: no coverage check against a population truth is possible there (only within-labeled resampling); `plankton` has a 2013→2014 shift: labeled and unlabeled targets differ, define the estimand explicitly (mean of 2014 Y, truth available from `Y_unlabeled`) and treat as a stress case where PPI-type validity relies only on labeled/unlabeled exchangeability — expect failure or no applicability by design; it is a "when it does not work" cell, not a main result.
4. **Scope creep (high given page limit).** 9 datasets × several estimands × several methods × several n will not fit in 9–10 main pages (NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR limit; appendix is free). Bound to: **primary estimand = mean** (the objective lets us choose; mean is also where theory is clean), one secondary estimand only if the plan wants a case where headroom may exist (e.g. a regression coefficient on census_income/education, or a quantile); ≤ 5 real datasets in the main text (e.g. galaxies, gene_expression, census_income, alphafold/forest as small-n, ballots) with the rest in the appendix; n grid ≤ 5 values (e.g. 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000) plus N sweep on one dataset.
5. **Baseline set (medium).** "Existing valid methods" must be run on identical inputs: classical (labeled-only, t/CLT), PPI (unit weight), PPI++ (power-tuned), and optionally stratified PPI [S: starter list #5]. Cross-PPI [#3] and active PPI [#4] change the inputs (predictor trained on labels / labels chosen adaptively) so they violate the fixed-input constraint; state that exclusion explicitly rather than silently omitting them. `ppi_py` gives the first three; the `ppi_py` implementation should be used as the reference and any reimplementation checked against it to numerical tolerance.
6. **Prohibited-content risk (medium, procedural).** The deliverable forbids reading the published paper on this very question and anything citing it. A literature search with phrases like "tighter / shorter prediction-powered confidence interval fixed labeled unlabeled predictor" is exactly the query likely to hit it. Recommendation to `idea-repo`: search only for background (semiparametric efficiency, DML, isotonic/ calibration, Wald CI undercoverage, the starter list), cite only what has been opened, and record in the reproduction notes any accidental encounter (what, where, how much read). Any position that meets such a page must stop reading it and log it.
7. **Download fragility (low–medium).** Data come from Google Drive via `gdown`; rate limits or quota can break reruns. Mitigate: the repo's `get_data.sh` downloads with retries and verifies sha256 (see `feasibility/dl_sha256.txt`), and the reproduction notes state it was tested in the container (the task's `data.md` says it was not).
8. **Theory risk (medium).** A theorem is only valuable with stated assumptions: i.i.d. labeled draw from the same distribution as unlabeled, finite 2nd moments, n,N→∞ with n/N→ρ, predictor independent of the data. Keep to asymptotic linear (influence-function) arguments for the mean: efficiency within the class based on (Y,Ŷ) is a variance-projection statement, simple to state and check. Don't promise finite-sample guarantees unless the plan includes a finite-sample bound method (those are wider; make that a separate comparison).
9. **Time/turn budget (low).** Compute is trivial relative to the 1-week limit; the cost driver is writing and review cycles. Order: plan → synthetic study with the headroom knob (needs no data download) → real data → theory check → paper.

## 4. Requests and recommended revisions to the plan (for story-repo, context for idea-repo)
- Frame the core question as conditional; define exactly which "existing" intervals are in scope (classical, PPI, PPI++, optionally stratified) and why cross-/active PPI are out.
- Fix the primary estimand (mean) and the replication protocol (table in item 3) before experiments; give each real dataset a role (headroom-present / headroom-absent / small-n / shift stress).
- Pre-register (in the plan) the decision rules: coverage acceptable if the MC interval for coverage includes 0.95 or lies above; "gain" = paired mean width ratio with CI excluding 1 *and* acceptable coverage; use ≥2000 reps for coverage claims.
- Include a headroom diagnostic (V_lin vs V_best) per dataset in the paper: it predicts the answer and is cheap.
- Separate what is delivered by whom: experiment-repo runs the studies, figtab-repo renders; I will review sizing and scope of each plan revision.
