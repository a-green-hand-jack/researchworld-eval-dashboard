\section{Assessment criteria}
\label{sec:assessment}

For a reference $\phi^\star$ for the same game, let $T_k(a)$ select the $k$ largest absolute coordinates, with original-feature index breaking exact ties. Signed overlap $O_k^\pm$ is the number of shared selected features with matching contribution signs divided by $k$; unsigned overlap ignores signs. We combine value error and signed selection in a one-run event:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{\mathrm{rel}}(\widehat\phi,\phi^\star)
 &=\frac{\|\widehat\phi-\phi^\star\|_1}{\max(\|\phi^\star\|_1,\delta)},\\
 A_{\tau,\eta}(\widehat\phi,\phi^\star)
 &=\mathbf{1}\{E_{\mathrm{rel}}\leq\tau\ \text{and}\ O_k^\pm\geq\eta\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:adequacy}
\end{equation}
The resolution floor $\delta$, cutoff $\tau$, selection requirement $\eta$ and $k$ are predeclared study choices, not validated clinical or lending tolerances. Magnitude error, unsigned/signed selection, top-1 identity and rank diagnostics remain separate components. Absolute error, reference norms and floor sensitivity expose weak-signal effects; deterministic point scores retain tied and near-zero cases with explicit ambiguity diagnostics. Appendix~\ref{app:assessment} gives full formulas and inference details.

For $n$ inputs with $R$ independent seeds each, the observed panel adequacy is the average of per-input success fractions, $\widehat p=n^{-1}\sum_i R^{-1}\sum_r A_{\tau,\eta}(\widehat\phi_{i,r},\phi_i^\star)$. All distinct run pairs are reduced to one repeatability summary per input; shared pairs do not enlarge the independent sample. Pairwise L1 disagreement uses the same reference-norm floor as accuracy; normalization by the runs' own norms is separately labeled. Input-cluster resampling preserves all repeats and matched comparisons. These estimates condition on the fitted model, split, background and implementation, with per-input distributions alongside panel means.

Boundary all-zero/all-one bounded panels retain raw bootstrap endpoints but display positive-width Hoeffding intervals. A separate one-sided bounded-mean guard governs population reliability claims under independent input-level observations. With 60 inputs, even no observed failures cannot certify population success of at least $.95$ using that guard. Thirty seeds likewise do not certify each input. Unresolved references or incomplete runs cannot silently leave denominators; measured negative outcomes and missing evidence remain distinct.
