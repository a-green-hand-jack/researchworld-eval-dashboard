\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Synthetic simplex targets and the exact coverage law}
\label{sec:exp_law}
\paragraph{Design.}
Covariates are $X\sim N(0,I_4)$ and the conditional mean is $\mu(x)=\mathrm{softmax}(Wx+b)$ with fixed population coefficients.
We use $K=3$ and two noise models with known conditional laws: Dirichlet, $Y\mid x\sim\mathrm{Dir}(\phi(x)\mu(x))$, and logistic-normal, with isotropic Gaussian noise in an orthonormal log-ratio basis; in both the scale varies with $x_0$ (heteroscedasticity strength $h=0.5$).
Every replication draws fresh training (500), calibration ($n$) and test (2000) samples, fits the mean by ordinary least squares in clr coordinates, and applies split conformal prediction at $\alpha=0.1$ with 15 scores on the same draws: Euclidean, $L_1$, Hellinger, Aitchison (clr) and forward and reverse KL, each raw and normalised by a fitted local scale, plus quantile-normalised Euclidean and Aitchison and a Dirichlet likelihood score.
We run $R=2000$ replications per setting and calibration size $n\in\{20,50,100,200,500,2000\}$.

\paragraph{Coverage follows the exact law for every score.}
For each replication the observed test coverage is mapped through the randomised probability integral transform under the Beta-binomial law of Proposition~\ref{prop:beta}; under the law the transformed values are uniform.
Figure~\ref{fig:c1} (Appendix~\ref{app:c1}) shows the empirical cdf minus the identity for all 15 scores, three calibration sizes and both noise models.
Across the six panels shown the curves of all scores are close to the zero line and mostly inside the 95\% DKW band of a single ecdf for $R=2000$.
Over all 12 settings and 15 scores (180 Kolmogorov--Smirnov tests, Table~\ref{tab:c1} in Appendix~\ref{app:c1}), 2 rejected at the 5\% level (logistic-normal noise at $n=100$ and $n=200$, $p=0.047$ and $0.045$); the tests are dependent because the scores share draws, so the count has no binomial reference, but it is below the 9 that independent tests of a true law would give.
The ratio of observed to exact standard deviation of coverage is within $[0.988,1.014]$ in every row of the table (medians over the 15 scores).
The calibration-draw spread is therefore the predicted one, whatever the region size.

\paragraph{How large must a calibration set be to certify coverage?}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:beta}, the smallest $n$ with $\Pbb(|V-(1-\alpha)|\le\varepsilon)\ge0.95$ is $3448$ for $\alpha=0.1,\varepsilon=0.01$ and $854$ for $\varepsilon=0.02$, against $3457$ and $864$ from the normal approximation (\texttt{certify\_n.csv}; simulation gives $0.9504$ at $n=854$); the approximation is poor at $\varepsilon=0.05$, $\alpha=0.05$ ($29$ exact vs.\ $73$).

\subsection{Does the geometry of the score matter? (RQ2)}
\label{sec:exp_geom}
\paragraph{Design.}
Experiment~A1 varies one factor at a time around a base configuration (Dirichlet noise, $K=3$, $h=0.5$, precision $20$; 500 training, 500 calibration, $10^4$ test points, $\alpha=0.1$): noise family, $K\in\{3,5,10\}$, $h\in\{0,0.5,1\}$, linear or quadratic scale, precision or $\sigma$, and two-digit rounding with zeros replaced by $\delta=10^{-4}$; 20 configurations, 100 or 200 replications each.
Because the conditional law is known, the quantities that finite data cannot give are computed on a fixed grid of 3000 covariate values (300 per decile of $x_0$, 30 draws of $y$ each): the true coverage of each slab and of ten boundary strata (deciles of the smallest predicted component), and the $\log_{10}$ volume ratio to the oracle highest-density region of the same level (only for $K\le5$).
The worst true slab coverage of the best scores is only $0.86$--$0.89$ because the calibration set is finite, which sets the scale of the differences below.

\paragraph{Marginal coverage does not separate the scores; size and conditional coverage do.}
Across the 20 configurations and 15 scores every estimated marginal coverage lies within $0.0035$ of $0.9$ (within $0.0059$ on the ground-truth grid).
Ranking scores by their marginal-coverage gap is not reproducible: the Kendall $\tau$ between rankings from two disjoint blocks of $R=25$ replications is $-0.03$ on average, against $0.94$ for the volume ratio and $0.89$ for the true worst-slab coverage (\texttt{A3\_rank\_blocks.csv}).
At the same marginal coverage the regions differ widely (Fig.~\ref{fig:geom}a,b; Appendix~\ref{app:c3} for all $h$): in the base configuration the volume is $10^{0.02}$ of the oracle's for the Dirichlet score, $10^{0.12}$ for Hellinger, $10^{0.21}$ for Euclidean and $10^{0.42}$ for Aitchison, and the worst true slab coverage ranges from $0.63$ (Aitchison) to $0.84$ (Dirichlet score).

\paragraph{Normalised and likelihood-based scores come closest to the oracle region.}
On Dirichlet data the Dirichlet score has a volume ratio of $10^{0.02}$--$10^{0.17}$ against $10^{0.18}$--$10^{0.34}$ for Euclidean, with a paired 95\% interval excluding zero in 17 of the 18 configurations with a computed volume; its gain in worst-slab coverage is positive in 18 of 20 configurations but modest (mean $0.07$, $0.78$ vs.\ $0.75$ at $h=1$).
Dividing a score by a fitted local scale mainly repairs conditional coverage: for logistic-normal data at $h=1$ the worst slab rises from $0.53$ (Euclidean) to $0.80$ (Euclidean$/n$) and $0.81$ (quantile-normalised), while the volume ratio falls from $10^{0.78}$ to $10^{0.52}$ and, for the normalised Aitchison score, to $10^{0.30}$.
When the likelihood family is wrong the Dirichlet score keeps small regions on logistic-normal data (volume $10^{0.09}$--$10^{0.53}$) but loses conditional coverage when heteroscedastic (worst slab $0.65$--$0.78$ against $0.84$--$0.87$ for the quantile-normalised Aitchison score).

\paragraph{The best geometry depends on the noise model and on the boundary.}
No score is best everywhere (Fig.~\ref{fig:geom}c,d; Table~\ref{tab:best_full} in Appendix~\ref{app:c5}).
Aitchison scores give a \emph{larger} region than Euclidean in 13 of the 18 computed configurations and a smaller one in 5; on homoscedastic logistic-normal data, whose noise is isotropic in clr coordinates, they are the best ($10^{0.08}$ vs.\ $10^{0.31}$, $K=3$).
On Dirichlet data their worst boundary stratum is covered at only $0.37$--$0.61$ (nine unrounded configurations), against at least $0.76$ for Euclidean, $L_1$, Hellinger, the two KL scores, the Dirichlet score and the normalised Euclidean and Hellinger scores; with two-digit rounding it is $0.81$ and depends on the zero replacement: from $0.59$ at $\delta=10^{-6}$ to $0.86$ at $\delta=10^{-2}$ on logistic-normal data ($0.80$ to $0.86$ on Dirichlet data), whereas Euclidean, $L_1$ and Hellinger move by at most $0.02$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:a1d} in Appendix~\ref{app:a1d}).
Hellinger and forward-KL give smaller regions than Euclidean in all 18 configurations.
A Mahalanobis score on the clr residual covariance adds nothing here (within $0.07$, $0.03$, $0.09$ of Aitchison in volume, worst slab, worst boundary stratum; \texttt{A1m\_summary.csv}), which is expected for isotropic noise.

\subsection{How reliable are the estimators used to compare scores? (RQ3)}
\label{sec:exp_est}
\paragraph{The worst-cell estimate is biased low, and Proposition~\ref{prop:min} predicts by how much.}
Estimating the worst of $G$ cell coverages from a test set of $n_{\mathrm{te}}$ points is biased downwards whatever the score.
In A1 the mean bias of the worst-slab estimate ($G=10$, averaged over 20 configurations and 15 scores) is $-0.026$, $-0.005$ and $+0.002$ at $n_{\mathrm{te}}=500$, $2000$ and $10^4$; for the worst boundary stratum it is $-0.041$, $-0.014$, $-0.001$, and for $G=20$ slabs $-0.063$, $-0.017$, $0.000$ (\texttt{A3\_bias.csv}).
Evaluating the exact formula of Proposition~\ref{prop:min} with each replication's true cell coverages reproduces the observed mean estimate with a mean absolute error of $0.005$ (maximum $0.045$; \texttt{A3\_pred\_bias.csv}).
The Gaussian approximation is accurate only for equal cell coverages: with equal $c_g$ its error is at most $0.012$ at $m=25$ points per cell and $G=20$, with unequal coverages it overestimates the expected minimum by $0.025$--$0.09$ (\texttt{prop2\_equal\_cells.csv}); we therefore use the exact formula (Fig.~\ref{fig:bias} in Appendix~\ref{app:c6}).

\paragraph{The bias depends on the score and can reorder scores.}
Across the 15 scores the biases of the worst-slab estimate span $0.063$, $0.037$ and $0.029$ at $n_{\mathrm{te}}=500$, $2000$, $10^4$, while the true worst-slab coverages span $0.29$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:rank}a).
With the sampling noise averaged out, the Kendall $\tau$ between the mean estimate and the true ranking is $0.83$, $0.88$, $0.92$ (the ranking predicted from the exact formula: $0.91$, $0.95$, $0.98$): mis-ranking is real. How often it reverses a pair falls with the true gap (Appendix~\ref{app:c15}; worst-decile coverage, 105 score pairs, 8 configurations, 1000 replications). For a single replication at $n_{\mathrm{te}}=500$, $2000$, $10^4$, a pair $0.03$--$0.05$ apart is reversed with probability $0.26$, $0.10$, $0.02$ and a pair $0.10$--$0.20$ apart with $0.05$, $0.002$, $0$; reversals stay below $5\%$ only for gaps $\ge0.2$, $0.05$, $0.03$. For the mean of 25 replications a pair $0.03$--$0.05$ apart is reversed with probability $0.03$, $0.005$, $0.001$. A single evaluation at $n_{\mathrm{te}}=500$ thus reverses pairs up to about $0.2$ apart with probability $\ge5\%$; with 25 averaged replications or $n_{\mathrm{te}}\ge2000$ only pairs closer than about $0.01$--$0.03$ are reversed (\texttt{A3\_flip\_threshold.csv}).

\paragraph{Single evaluations rank poorly; replications and paired designs are what matter.}
Ranking the 15 scores by the worst-slab estimate from \emph{one} replication agrees with the true ranking with $\tau=0.50$, $0.60$, $0.66$ and a correct top score in $23\%$, $31\%$, $36\%$ of replications at $n_{\mathrm{te}}=500$, $2000$, $10^4$ (\texttt{A3\_rank\_single.csv}).
Averaging $R$ replications helps: the $\tau$ between two disjoint blocks reaches $0.81$ at $n_{\mathrm{te}}=2000$ and $0.87$ at $10^4$ for $R=25$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:rank}b).
Pairing tightens the comparison (median unpaired/paired standard error $1.51$ for volume, $1.29$ for the worst slab; Fig.~\ref{fig:rank}c), yet 113 of the 1170 contrasts with Euclidean in A1 ($10\%$) still have a paired interval containing zero.

\paragraph{A natural bias correction fails.}
We tried selecting the apparent worst cell on one half of the test set and measuring it on the other (``half-split'', both directions).
It is unbiased for the coverage of the cell it selects (bias $\le0.004$), but it is biased \emph{upwards} for the true minimum ($+0.029$, $+0.013$, $+0.007$ at $G=10$ and $n_{\mathrm{te}}=500$, $2000$, $10^4$, against $-0.018$, $-0.002$, $+0.003$ for the naive estimate), and it does not improve the ranking: $\tau$ for a single replication is $0.56$ against $0.62$ for the naive estimate at $n_{\mathrm{te}}=500$ and $0.75$ against $0.76$ at $10^4$ (8 configurations, 1000 replications; Fig.~\ref{fig:rank}d).
Evaluating Proposition~\ref{prop:min} at cell coverages estimated on the same test set (plug-in) also does not help: it removes the mean bias (residual $+0.0003$ against $-0.018$, worst decile, $n_{\mathrm{te}}=500$) but not the error (RMSE $0.0596$ against $0.0601$) or the ranking (single-replication $\tau$ $0.60$ against $0.62$, mean over 8 configurations), and it predicts only $0.027$ of the $0.070$ spread of the bias across scores (Appendix~\ref{app:c13}).
We therefore recommend reporting the cell size and the size of the bias, not subtracting it.

\subsection{Do the findings carry over to classifier outputs? (RQ5)}
\label{sec:exp_clf}
A softmax vector is a point of the simplex $\Delta^{K-1}$, and the sets LAC, APS, RAPS and class-conditional (Mondrian) LAC are split-conformal regions for the label given the vector.
We use two sources.
\emph{B-synth}: $K=10$, true label probabilities $p\sim\mathrm{Dir}(\beta\mathbf 1)$ with $\beta\in\{0.1,0.3,1\}$, classifier output $\hat p=\mathrm{softmax}(\tau\log p+\varsigma\varepsilon)$ with $\tau\in\{0.5,1,2\}$ and $\varsigma\in\{0,1\}$, calibration size $n\in\{100,1000\}$ (36 cells, 500 replications), so that the exact coverage given $\hat p$ is known.
\emph{B-cifar}: a small CNN trained on 40\,000 CIFAR-10 images, with $20\,000$ held-out images (accuracy $0.869$) with temperature $T\in\{0.5,1,2\}$, $n\in\{100,1000\}$ and $1000$ random splits of that one network (the spread is split variance, not training variance).
The conditional-coverage measure is the minimum coverage over strata of the set size (sizes $1$, $2$, $3$--$4$, $5$--$10$); results are in Fig.~\ref{fig:clf}.

\paragraph{LAC gives the smallest sets, but not always the worst size-stratified coverage.}
LAC sets are smaller on average than those of APS, RAPS and Mondrian-LAC in all 36 B-synth cells (for example $6.04$ vs.\ $6.18$ labels at $\beta=1$, $\tau=0.5$, $\varsigma=0$, $n=1000$); the paired 95\% interval of the size difference lies above zero in all 36 cells for APS and Mondrian-LAC and in 34 for RAPS (\texttt{B\_paired\_vs\_lac.csv}).
The expected order of conditional coverage holds in most cells: the exact worst size-stratum coverage of APS is higher than that of LAC in 24 of 36 cells (paired interval above zero), lower in 10 and unresolved in 2.
The exceptions are sometimes large: at $\beta=0.3$, $\tau=0.5$, $\varsigma=0$, $n=1000$ the worst stratum has coverage $0.865$ for LAC and $0.805$ for APS; at $\beta=1$, $\tau=0.5$, $\varsigma=1$ it is $0.30$ for LAC, $0.59$ for APS and $0.90$ for RAPS.
On CIFAR-10 ($T=1$, $n=1000$) LAC has size $1.089$ and worst size-stratum coverage $0.872$ (largest class-conditional gap $0.104$), APS $1.396$, $0.911$ and $0.024$; RAPS equals APS within $0.003$ in size and $0.002$ in coverage here.
Mondrian-LAC overcovers and inflates the sets at $n=100$ (size $4.2$ against $1.1$, coverage $0.944$).

\paragraph{Small strata bias the estimates of classifier sets in the same way.}
With $n_{\mathrm{te}}=200$, $1000$, $5000$ test points the estimated worst size-stratified coverage of CIFAR-10 is $0.066$, $0.034$, $0.009$ too low for APS and $0.031$, $0.015$, $0.003$ for LAC, measured against the same quantity on the whole pool (\texttt{B\_ssc\_bias.csv}).
Restricting the minimum to strata with at least 30 test points removes most of it (mean bias relative to the same strata at most $0.011$ in absolute value; the minimum cell size of Table~\ref{tab:protocol}).

\subsection{Conditions: covariate shift, rounding, misspecified mean, calibration scheme}
\label{sec:exp_cond}
\paragraph{Covariate shift.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:beta} assumes exchangeability.
When $x_0$ is shifted by $\delta$ standard deviations, unweighted split intervals under-cover (A4; known density ratio, four scores): at $\delta=1$ coverage is $0.72$--$0.82$ on Dirichlet and $0.66$--$0.88$ on logistic-normal data, lowest for the Aitchison score, and density-ratio weighting restores $0.900$--$0.908$. At $\delta=1.5$ (effective sample size about $75$) weighting overcorrects, to $0.904$--$0.925$, because $2.8\%$ of test points receive an infinite threshold (\texttt{A4\_summary.csv}; Appendix~\ref{app:c11}).

\paragraph{Rounding and ties.}
Rounding to one to three digits changes the mean coverage by at most $0.0014$ with a fitted mean; true ties (intercept-only mean) raise it by $0.003$--$0.005$ at one digit: conservative and small, as the remark after Proposition~\ref{prop:beta} says (\texttt{A2\_beta\_law.csv}; Appendix~\ref{app:c2}).

\paragraph{Misspecified mean.}
With a quadratic part in the true logit mean (30\% and 63\% of its variance unexplained by the linear fit; $K=3$; A6), marginal coverage stays within $0.891$--$0.901$ for all 15 scores, but regions are $0.31$--$0.81$ larger in $\log_{10}$ volume and the ranking of scores is not stable: Kendall $\tau$ with the ranking under the correct mean is $0.07$--$0.39$ by volume and $-0.12$ to $0.52$ by worst-decile coverage (Appendix~\ref{app:c14}).
Which score is best is therefore not robust to a misspecified mean.

\paragraph{CV+ and the jackknife+.}
Against split calibration at $n=30$ both cover less in point estimate in all 8 cells (interval below zero in 12 of 16 method--cell contrasts; $-0.006$ to $-0.047$; mostly because split overcovers, e.g.\ $0.94$) and give smaller regions ($\log_{10}$ volume ratio $-0.13$ to $-0.27$); the gain shrinks to $-0.01$ to $-0.03$ at $n=200$ (\texttt{A5\_paired\_vs\_split.csv}; two noise models, four scores, 100 replications; jackknife+ not run at $n=200$; Appendix~\ref{app:c12}).
