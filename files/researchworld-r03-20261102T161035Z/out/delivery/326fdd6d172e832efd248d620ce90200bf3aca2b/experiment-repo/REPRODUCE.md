# Reproduction notes and results summary: predicting a new model's scores on all benchmarks from k of them

Analysis code and results for the study "choose k benchmarks to run, infer the rest". Everything in `results/` is produced by the scripts below from the pinned public data; no number is hand-entered.


## Layout of the delivery
`code/` scripts, `data/` score matrices (built by `build_*.py` from the pinned sources), `results/*.csv` summaries. Files with prefix `ct_` come from protocol B (common target set) and are the ones to use for selector comparisons and k-sufficiency; `curves.csv`, `paired.csv`, `k_needed.csv` (protocol A, selector-dependent targets; `k_needed.csv` relative to kmax and invalid where skill at kmax <= 0) are superseded and kept for the inference/shift/size ablations only. The per-model raw error files (`results/raw_gz/*.csv.gz`, 221 MB) are not copied into the `paper-repo` delivery for size; they are in `world/experiment-repo` `main` under `results/raw_gz/` and regenerate with the commands below.

## Protocol (`code/run_exp.py`)
Model-level split: 25% of models are "new" (test), 75% train. Everything that learns (factor model, imputation, ridge, kNN, benchmark ordering) sees training models only. For a test model, the first k benchmarks of the method's ordering *that the model actually has* are "run"; the target is every other observed benchmark of that model. Error = RMSE of z-scores (error / training sd of the benchmark), pooled over target cells; also raw-score RMSE and MAE. Skill = 1 - (RMSE/RMSE_mean)^2 where mean = no-information per-benchmark training mean. Repeated random splits (MTEB 12, OLB 10, HELM 40/20); additional split types: `group` = whole organisations/families held out (MTEB: prefix of the model name, HELM: first word of name), `time` = OLB train on earlier submissions (2000 sampled) and test on the latest quarter. MTEB uses a dense core of the sparse matrix (`dense_core`, repeatedly drop the least-covered model/task until 95% observed: 145 models x 254 tasks, includes multilingual tasks); ablations at density 0.85/0.98 and with 30/60 training models. Seeds: split `1000+rep`, model init `rep`.

Selection (orderings; `code/bench.py`): `random`, `coverage` (most-observed), `variance`, `corr_mean` (corr with mean score), `qr` (pivoted QR/CUR), `farthest` (farthest-first in 1-|corr|), `greedy_ridge` (forward selection minimising least-squares reconstruction error of all benchmarks, on the factor-model-imputed training matrix), `greedy_bayes` (greedy Bayesian A-optimal design in the latent space of the factor model). Inference: `mean` (baseline), `rank1` (mean z of selected -> per-benchmark regression), `ridge` (multi-output RidgeCV on imputed training matrix), `knn` (K=10 nearest training models in the selected coordinates), `fa` (Gaussian factor model with missing-data EM, posterior mean given the selected scores; rank and noise floor chosen by held-out-cell CV on training data only).

## Protocol A findings (own-target protocol, `run_exp.py`; **superseded for selector comparisons by protocol B below** because the target set depends on the selector — kept for the inference comparison, shift and train-size ablations, whose targets are selector-independent only at fixed selection; (see the CSVs; z-RMSE, `ridge` inference, mean over splits)
1. **Cross-benchmark scores are predictable from very few benchmarks, but with a floor.** MTEB (random model split) with 4/16/48 of 254 tasks: random selection 0.64/0.49/0.42 z-RMSE vs 0.95 for the no-information mean; greedy_ridge 0.54/0.45/0.36 (skill 0.70/0.79/0.87). The curve is still falling at k=48 (19% of tasks); no plateau reached, so "k large enough" is not identified by this data. OLB (6 benchmarks): k=3 of 6 -> 0.49 (random 0.56), k=5 -> 0.27 (random 0.50). HELM Lite (10 scenarios): k=3 -> 0.54 (random 0.67), k=8 -> 0.40.
2. **Informed selection beats random, with a small margin that shrinks with k on MTEB**: greedy_ridge minus random z-RMSE at k=2/8/32 on MTEB: -0.12/-0.05/-0.04 (cluster-bootstrap 95% CIs exclude 0); OLB k=2: -0.05; HELM k=2/8: -0.17/-0.12. Heuristics that ignore redundancy are mixed: on MTEB, variance, pivoted QR and farthest-first are *worse than random* for k=3..16-24 (e.g. k=8: variance 0.62, QR 0.66, farthest 0.63 vs random 0.55; they win at k=1-2 or k=48 for QR/farthest), while correlation-with-mean and coverage beat random only for k<=12 (k=4: 0.63/0.65 vs 0.64, i.e. ~tie) — so no cheap heuristic is reliably better than random; the redundancy-aware greedy methods are (greedy_bayes is close to greedy_ridge on MTEB, 0.47 vs 0.45 at k=16).
3. **Inference**: supervised regression on the selected set (`ridge`) is best or tied at k>=16; kNN is competitive/better at 4<=k<=8 on MTEB and HELM; the Gaussian factor model `fa` (the one with a closed-form theory) is *worse* than ridge on every real dataset (e.g. MTEB k=16: 0.51 vs 0.45) and is unstable for k about equal to its rank (error above the mean baseline at k=3-5 for random selection in the pilot). The rank-1 'average score' predictor is clearly worse than ridge (e.g. +0.11 to +0.23 on MTEB for k>=4).
4. **Shift matters.** Holding out whole organisations (MTEB) raises error at all k (greedy_ridge+ridge at k=16: 0.67 vs 0.45; skill 0.64 vs 0.79); OLB temporal shift (train earlier / test latest quarter) makes even the mean predictor much worse (z-RMSE 1.1-1.2, new models are out-of-distribution) while regression on k=5 still gets 0.33 vs 0.27 under random split. HELM held-out families: greedy_ridge k=8 0.49 vs random-split 0.40.
5. **Training-set size** (MTEB, greedy_ridge+ridge, k=16): 30 train models 0.65, 60 -> 0.51, ~109 -> 0.45. Density 0.85/0.98 changes composition of the benchmark set (k=16: 0.46/0.50) so those numbers are not a controlled missingness effect.


## Protocol B: common target set + full-information reference (`code/run_exp2.py`, `code/analyze2.py`) — use this for selector comparisons and k-sufficiency
Requested by `story-repo`#3 and `figtab-repo`#4. Per split a target set T is fixed for all selectors and k: MTEB (dense core and sparse): a random 25% of the benchmarks per repeat; OLB/HELM: leave-one-benchmark-out, every benchmark in turn (T={j}, selection from the other N-1, k<=N-2; scores pooled over all T). Selectors pick only from candidates (non-T); their objectives still use all training columns (that is what a deployed selector sees). Every method is scored on the observed cells of T of each test model. `k=9999` (`sel=full`) is the **full-information reference**: all observed candidate benchmarks of the model are given (same inference). A benchmark with <8 training observations in a split is dropped for that split (needed for the sparse matrix; a first sparse run without this guard produced a z-score blow-up from a nearly empty column and was discarded and rerun). The factor-model grid was widened (MTEB rank {2,4,8,12,16,24}, noise floor {0.15,0.3,0.5,0.8}): CV still picks the largest rank (24) and floor 0.3-0.8 on dense MTEB, i.e. it keeps asking for more factors/noise — the Gaussian low-rank model is a poor description of MTEB. Output `results/ct_*.csv`; raw per-model errors `results/raw_gz/ct_*.csv.gz` (columns `target` = held-out benchmark index for LOBO runs, `ksz` = number of benchmarks actually run). `curves.n_models` counts distinct test models over all repeats; the per-split test size is 36 (MTEB), 22 (HELM), 150 (OLB, subsampled).

### Findings B (z-RMSE, ridge inference; 95% cluster-bootstrap CI over test models in brackets where given)
1. **Reference and floor (C1).** MTEB random split: no-information mean 1.00; full-information reference (all ~190 other tasks) **0.388** [0.364, 0.414] (skill 0.85); k=4/16/64/128 random 0.64/0.51/0.42/0.39, greedy_ridge 0.56/0.48/0.41/0.40. OLB LOBO: reference 0.547 [0.525, 0.568] (skill 0.70), reached at k=3 (random 0.59 at k=3, 0.57 at k=4). HELM LOBO: reference 0.557 [0.479, 0.646] (skill 0.72). On OLB/HELM kNN beats ridge as full-information predictor (OLB 0.419 vs 0.547; HELM 0.589 vs 0.557, a tie).
2. **How large k (C4), against the reference.** Smallest k with skill >= 0.9 x reference skill (random / greedy_ridge): MTEB 24 / 16 of ~190 candidates; within 10% of the reference error: 64 / 48; within 25%: 24 / 16. HELM 5 / 2 (10% of ref: 6 / 2); OLB 3 / 1. Sparse MTEB (54% observed, k<=32): skill criterion reached at 24 for both, 10%-criterion **not reached** by k=32 (reference 0.394, k=32 0.46-0.47). Under held-out organisations the reference itself is poor (MTEB 0.88 [0.61, 1.16], HELM 0.81), skill-based k is 24/12, but the CIs are wide (few organisations, heavy-tailed model errors) so k-needed is poorly identified there. All of this is for these pools; sufficiency in absolute terms differs by data (3 of 6 for OLB, ~10-25% of tasks on MTEB).
3. **Selection (C2), common targets, MTEB random split** (diff vs random, ridge): greedy_ridge -0.070 [-0.092,-0.050] (k=1), -0.100 (k=2), -0.077 (k=4), -0.049 (k=8), -0.036 (k=16), -0.021 (k=32), -0.010 (k=48), ~0 at k=64-96 and +0.013 [+0.007,+0.018] at k=128 (selection gives nothing near the reference; slightly harmful at the end). greedy_bayes similar for k<=16 (-0.087 at k=2) and equal to greedy_ridge from k=32. Heuristics are never better than random after k>=3 except correlation-with-mean (about random for k 3-8, +0.02 worse for k 12-48); QR, farthest-first, variance and coverage are worse than random (e.g. k=8: QR +0.139 [0.118, 0.162], farthest +0.131, variance +0.054, coverage +0.033), the gap closing by k~64-128. The same ordering holds for the sparse MTEB matrix (greedy_bayes/greedy_ridge best up to k~12, at k>=24 equal to random) and for HELM (greedy_ridge 0.71/0.59/0.58 at k=1/2/3 vs random 0.85/0.75/0.68). On OLB (LOBO, k=1) greedy_ridge/greedy_bayes/corr_mean 0.59 vs random 0.74 and all methods meet by k=4. **The earlier OLB k=5 'selection' differences and the greedy_bayes-gets-worse-with-k pattern were artefacts of the selector-dependent target; there is no bug in `sel_greedy_bayes` (under protocol B it equals greedy_ridge on OLB and HELM k>=3).**
4. **Nested ridge penalty (item 5).** greedy_ridge with selection penalty x{1e-4, 1e-2, 1e-1} of the default (MTEB, only 2 repeats): z-RMSE changes by at most ~0.01-0.02 for k<=16 and the ordering vs random is unchanged (`ct_curves.csv`, data `mteb_pen`); it does not matter at this resolution, but with 2 repeats and 64 distinct test models this is weak evidence.
5. **Controlled missingness (C6).** Fixed 126x238 core, extra MCAR masking of the training/test matrix to 88/69/49% observed: reference 0.418/0.459/0.507 (vs 0.388 on the 95% core), skill 0.83/0.80/0.77. So moving from ~100% to 50% observed costs about 0.1 z-RMSE at the reference and the ridge-on-imputed pipeline degrades gracefully; k-needed (skill criterion) 12-24. Sparse realistic matrix (258x386, 54% observed): reference 0.394, skill 0.86 — not worse than the dense core, the benchmark pool being different.
6. **Theory vs real data (C5).** On the dense MTEB core the correlation spectrum has participation ratio 2.5 (first component 62% of variance, top-3 77%): one strong common factor plus a long tail (HELM: 2.3, 63% first; OLB 1.8, 73% first). The factor model fitted with the FA CV picks rank 24 (MTEB) and the noise floor sits at the imposed lower bound (psi = psi_min = 0.3 for MTEB, 0.15 OLB, 0.3 HELM), so *the Gaussian low-rank model does not identify a noise floor on these data* and its theory curve (`theory_real_curve.csv`; MTEB k=24: 0.67) overpredicts the observed random-selection FA curve (0.51); it matches within ~0.05-0.08 on OLB/HELM. What does hold is the functional form: fitting MSE_z(k)=a(1+r_eff/k) to the observed random-selection ridge curves for k>=4 gives R^2 0.94 (MTEB, floor a=0.169 i.e. RMSE 0.41, r_eff=6.3), 0.98 (HELM, a=0.256, r_eff=2.4), 0.999 (OLB, 0.220, r_eff=1.8) (`theory_fit.csv`, max abs error in z-RMSE 0.036/0.005/0.001); the fitted asymptote on MTEB (RMSE 0.39-0.41) agrees with the measured full-information reference (0.388). Fits for k>=8 give larger r_eff (9.7) and lower floor (0.153): r_eff is not stable to the k range, so it is a descriptive, not an identified, rank. The held-out-organisation fit is poorer for MTEB (R^2 0.95, floor 0.78).
7. **Shift (C6).** Reference error with whole organisations held out: MTEB 0.88 vs 0.39; HELM 0.81 vs 0.56; OLB 0.548 (≈ random split, 0.547); temporal OLB 0.614 (mean predictor itself 1.18). The decrease of z-RMSE with k is not lost under MTEB organisation shift but its level is far higher and the skill saturates near 0.55.
8. **Duplicate task versions (`mteb_dedup`, 5 repeats, `DEDUP=1`).** Collapsing task pairs that differ only by a `.vN` suffix (keep the version with more observations; 254 -> 251 tasks in the dense core) changes nothing at the resolution of the study: reference z-RMSE 0.383 [0.349, 0.420] vs 0.388 [0.364, 0.412]; smallest k with skill >= 0.9 x reference skill: random 24, greedy_ridge 16 (identical to the un-collapsed run); greedy_ridge minus random at k=2/8/32 = -0.124/-0.037/-0.027 (`ct_curves.csv`). The C2/C4 results are not driven by duplicated tasks. (The un-collapsed dedup repeats are 5 of the 12; compare with the same-repeat subset, not the pooled 12, for exact numbers.)
9. **Family split (`mteb_family`, 5 repeats) and time split (`mteb_time`, newest 25% of models by `release_date`, 4 repeats of 36 test models; the split is deterministic so repeats differ only in the FA cross-validation seed/target draw).** `model_meta.json` fields (`data/mteb_meta.csv`, `code/build_mteb_meta.py`): family = `adapted_from` base model where given, else the name stem. Held-out *families*: reference 0.913 [0.610, 1.190] (skill 0.60) vs 0.388 for random models; random 1.13/1.08/1.02/0.99/0.92 and greedy_ridge 1.07/1.05/1.01/0.98/0.93 at k=2/4/8/16/128 against a no-information mean of 1.45 (the mean is itself bad because the held-out family is a cluster). Time split: reference 1.047 [0.767, 1.337] (skill 0.53, mean predictor 1.52). So with families or the newest models held out the full-information reference reaches only z-RMSE 0.91 / 1.05, i.e. no better than an in-distribution no-information predictor would score, though far better than the mean predictor on the shifted set (1.45 / 1.52): k benchmarks recover the *ranking* (per-benchmark Spearman across test models 0.83 at the time split with all candidates, see `rank_corr.csv`) but not the level of new models, and "k needed" is then only relative to a poor reference (greedy_ridge 16 vs random 48 for skill >= 0.9 x ref on the family split; time split 3 vs 6 on 36 test models, CIs very wide, k_needed there is not reliable). Greedy_ridge's advantage over random is largest under these shifts at small k (k=1: 1.13 vs 1.24 on family) and vanishes by k~16-64.
10. **Random-k draws and the low-sd check (item 3 of #6).** `random` fixes one random ordering per split; `random_pm` draws an independent ordering per test model (so a run contributes repeats x test models independent draws: MTEB random 6 x ~36, group 4 x ~36, sparse 4 x 64, OLB 5 x 150, HELM 20 x 22; all >= 100). `random_pm` vs `random`: paired difference within +-0.01 almost everywhere with 95% CI covering 0 (e.g. MTEB random split k=2/8/32: -0.002/-0.009/+0.015 [CIs -0.03..0.03]; OLB k=1..4 <= 0.007; HELM <= 0.024), so the random baseline is not an unlucky ordering and the greedy-minus-random gaps of finding 3 are real draws-averaged gaps. These runs cover only a subset of the repeats (MTEB random 6 of 12, group 4, sparse 4 of 8): they are reported in the separate views `<data>|sub` of `ct_*.csv` where *every* selector is restricted to the same repeats (mixing repeat subsets would bias the comparison; `analyze2.py` does this automatically). `lowsd_check.csv`: the 10% of tasks with the smallest overall sd carry 16-26% of the squared z-error on MTEB (11% of cells) but removing them lowers z-RMSE by only 0.02-0.04 (on the family and time splits they are not harder than average: z-RMSE without them is 0.03 higher) and leaves the ordering of selectors and the shape of the curves unchanged (e.g. MTEB random split greedy_ridge/random at k=16: 0.484/0.526 with, 0.452/0.499 without); HELM and OLB error shares are close to the cell share (0.10-0.15 vs 0.10; 0.13-0.15 vs 0.17).
11. **Per-model rank correlation (item 4 of #6; `rank_corr.csv`, from the `*.pred.csv.gz` runs; PRED=1 only for random/random_pm/greedy_ridge/full with ridge).** Mean over test models of the Spearman correlation between predicted and true z-scores over the model's target benchmarks (95% CI by bootstrap over test models). MTEB random split: full-information 0.835 [0.812, 0.847]; greedy_ridge k=1/2/4/16/64 0.28/0.51/0.60/0.74/0.81, random 0.22/0.28/0.43/0.69/0.81. Across benchmarks (per-benchmark Spearman over test models) it is higher (reference 0.88, greedy k=4 0.79). OLB (only 6 benchmarks, 1 target): per-model rank correlation is low (reference 0.20, greedy_ridge k=2 0.23, random k=1/2 *negative*, -0.10/-0.05), because with one target benchmark and a one-factor structure ranking a model's benchmarks is almost un-identifiable; the per-benchmark ranking of models is high (0.8). HELM: reference 0.35 (k=4: greedy 0.28, random 0.20). Family/time splits: reference 0.76 / 0.63. Read: aggregate z-RMSE vs the reference answers "how well is the level predicted"; model-specific profile ordering (which benchmark a model is relatively good at) needs far more benchmarks than the model-ranking per benchmark.
12. **MMTEB-style selector (item 5 of #6; `backelim`, `sel_backelim` in `code/bench.py`)**: backward elimination, drop the benchmark that the others predict best (ridge-regularised inverse correlation matrix on the imputed training matrix), selection = survivors. It is *worse than random* at small k on every MTEB view: MTEB random split minus random at k=2/8/32: +0.230/+0.188/+0.054 (CIs exclude 0 up to k=32; vs greedy_ridge +0.32/+0.24/+0.07); sparse MTEB +0.31/+0.15/+0.06; OLB +0.11/+0.08; HELM random it is better than random (-0.08 at k=2, -0.04 at k=3) but still worse than greedy_ridge. Reason (not tested separately): eliminating the best-predicted benchmark keeps the *least* redundant, i.e. idiosyncratic, tasks, which are poor predictors of the rest, whereas in the MMTEB use the criterion was applied to a correlation-clustered, much larger final set. It is an approximation of that selector (no clustering, our imputed matrix, no task-type balancing), not a reproduction of MMTEB's released subset.
13. **Train-size under protocol B (item 6 of #6; `mteb_ntrain30`, `mteb_ntrain60`, 4 repeats each; same test sets as `mteb_random`).** Reference z-RMSE 0.507 [0.465, 0.554] (30 train models), 0.446 [0.404, 0.487] (60), 0.388 (~109); at k=16 greedy_ridge/random: 0.557/0.561 (30), 0.492/0.525 (60), 0.476/0.512 (109). Hence more training models mostly lower the *reference* (the achievable floor) and the gap between selectors; with 30 training models informed selection is no better than random at k>=16 (0.557 vs 0.561) and greedy_ridge is worse than random at k=64-128 (0.553 vs 0.518 and 0.522 vs 0.497 at 30; 0.469 vs 0.453 and 0.463 vs 0.428 at 60; +0.013 at 128 even with ~109), which suggests (not tested separately) that the in-sample selection objective overfits when the training set is small relative to the number of candidates. Skill-based k_needed is noisy here (4 repeats).

## Theory check (synthetic; `code/theory_synth.py`)
Under x = Lz + e, z~N(0,I_r), e~N(0,diag(psi)), unit-variance columns, the Bayes-optimal predictor of benchmark j from set S has MSE_j = psi_j + l_j' (I + L_S' Psi_S^-1 L_S)^-1 l_j (exact). For isotropic random loadings and k >> r: mean MSE ~ psi_bar (1 + r/k), i.e. a noise floor psi_bar plus an r/k term; the design problem is minimising the reducible part tr(L'L P_S) (Bayesian A-optimal; `greedy_bayes`). Checked: the exact oracle formula and psi(1+r/k) agree for k>=~1.5 r (r=5: k=8: 0.65 vs 0.66; k=16: 0.53 vs 0.53). With *estimated* parameters (M=150 or 600 train rows) the FA-based predictor matches the oracle only for k >= ~2.5 r (r=5: 0.70 vs 0.57 at k=12 with M=150; at k=3..5 the estimated model is worse than the unconditional mean, 1.04-1.07 vs 1.0), and the gap does not close when M grows from 150 to 600, so it is a conditioning/misspecification-of-noise effect and not only small-sample. This predicts, and the real data show, that factor-model inference needs k well above the rank. Lines in `results/theory_summary.csv`.
Real-data tie-in is qualitative: the real-data MTEB curves are not of the psi(1+r/k) form with small r (rank chosen by CV is at the grid edge, 8, with noise floor at the upper edge 0.3, i.e. the Gaussian low-rank model is a poor description).

## Limitations / not done
- FA CV grid saturates at its edge even after widening (rank 24, floor 0.3-0.8): FA hyperparameters not identified on MTEB; FA results there describe a regularised model, not a tuned one.
- Protocol B: `mteb_mask*` 4 repeats, `mteb_pen` 2 repeats; one random 25% target draw per repeat on MTEB; single-benchmark targets on OLB/HELM only (no leave-two-out).
- `greedy_ridge` selects on the in-sample reconstruction error of an *imputed* matrix; no nested selection of the ridge penalty during selection.
- Wide MTEB task pool is heterogeneous (multilingual); I did not stratify by task type (not in the result JSONs).
- OLB organisation hold-out: see above (done).
- No LiveBench/BigCodeBench/AlpacaEval data.
- Cluster bootstrap resamples test models only, not training-set draws; splits within a dataset reuse the same models, so repeated-split SEs describe split noise, not population uncertainty.
- Score scales are not comparable across datasets; z-RMSE is relative to training sd.
