# Results hand-off (analysis position)

All numbers below are read from `results/` (CSV) and `results/tables/`; every table is produced by the script named next to it
(`make all` runs everything; see `Makefile` for run times). Environment: Python 3.11.2, numpy 2.4.6, scipy 1.17.1 (HiGHS MILP), pandas 3.0.6.

## Definitions (what the experiments measure)
* **Leaderboard** `X` (M models x n tasks, scores in 0..100). **Target** model i may raise its score on task t to any y_t in [x_t, cap_t]; all other models are fixed.
  "First" = strictly ahead of every other model under the rule (margin eps = 1e-3 points; ties count 1/2 in the rank rules).
* **Investment** (three measures, all reported): `L1` = sum of score gains; `K` = number of tasks whose score is raised; `Linf` = largest single-task gain (equals the uniform "general improvement" needed).
* **Cap scenarios** (the main modelling assumption): `ceil` (100), `front0/front2` (best other model on the task, +0/+2), `gain5/10/20` (at most +h points per task), `emp95/emp99` (95th/99th percentile of fine-tune-minus-base gains observed on the OLLB, 540 pairs).
* **Rules**: mean, geometric mean, harmonic mean, median, min, trimmed mean (1 off each end, OLLB sample only), Borda (= mean rank), Condorcet-winner requirement (`condorcet_winner` in `results/final`: target must beat every rival on a strict majority of tasks. This is *sufficient* for first place under any Condorcet-consistent rule (Copeland, Schulze, ...) but not necessary, so its cost is an **upper bound** on the cost under those rules; it is not comparable in order to Borda, which is not Condorcet-consistent), **Copeland** (exact MILP; wins minus losses over pairwise majorities, ties 1/2; run on a rank-stratified subset only, finding 10), plurality (first-place count, ties split).
  Exact solvers (closed form / water-filling / MILP) in `src/mti/`; `tests/` check them against brute force (`python3 -m pytest tests`; 7 tests, ~40 s).
  Every "inf" in the ordinal results was re-solved with a strict solver that raises on a MILP time-out: all are proven infeasible (`results/recheck_inf.csv`, `src/recheck_inf.py`).
* **Data** (`data/processed/manifest.json`): OLLB `contents` @ 9c09a7ca… (parquet sha256 f13a7061…; 4576 rows, 79 duplicate `fullname` dropped -> 4497, 1 of them flagged and excluded -> 4496 targets), 6 tasks; HELM MMLU v1.13.0: 79 models with all 57 subjects (manifest sha256 830bdb10…). Licences: not declared for either data set (see TASK data.md); we redistribute only derived score matrices. **Leaf board** (`data/processed/ollb_leaf.csv`, `ollb_leaf_manifest.json`): Open LLM Leaderboard `results` @ aa81ecc3… (10 509 run files, ~1.2 GB download (the data notes' 13.6 MB is wrong), newest complete run per `fullname`, 39 leaf tasks, 4506 models, 10 of them without a row in `contents`), validated against the official raw benchmark columns (correlation 0.89-1.00, see the manifest).
* **Rank groups**: targets are grouped by their rank under the mean (2, 3-5, 6-10, 11-30, 31-100, >100). Sampled runs (`sample`: all of ranks 2-21 plus 150 log-uniform ranks) are rank-stratified, so **only per-group medians are meaningful for them, not pooled medians**.

## Findings (each with its table; CIs are 1000-resample bootstraps over targets, seed 0)
1. **Mean: cost is exactly n x gap (verified).** `L1 = n(gap+eps)` to 1e-12 on all 78 HELM and 4495 OLLB targets (`results/theory_checks.txt`, P1). `Linf >= gap+eps`, with equality only when the uniform raise hits no cap (3/78 HELM, 4346/4495 OLLB targets). With `ceil` headroom K is 1-2 tasks on the OLLB (median target; n=6) and ~21 of 57 on HELM for rank 31-100 (P2).
2. **Rule ordering (HELM, ceil, L1 medians, rank 11-30 / 31-100):** plurality 25/72 < median 38/207 < min 46/200 < Condorcet 107/312 < Borda 286/858 < geometric 412/992 ~ harmonic 392/965 < mean 435/1021 (`tables/group_medians_helm.csv`, `tables/summary_helm_main_groups.csv`). Rank rules and order-statistic rules are 2-30x cheaper to game than the mean; the ranking among the *mean-like* rules (mean, geometric, harmonic) is within 6-10% of each other.
3. **Scaling with the gap is rule dependent** (`tables/elasticity_*.csv`, log-log slope of L1 on own-rule gap; HELM ceil): geometric 1.05 [1.04,1.06], harmonic 1.10 [1.07,1.11], mean 1.00, median 1.63 [1.57,1.67], min 1.69 [1.54,1.92]. Median/min cost grows super-linearly in the gap: they are cheap only for targets that are already close.
4. **Breadth vs depth:** the Condorcet-winner requirement forces K ~ n/2 tasks even when L1 is small (HELM: K=28-29 of 57 for ranks 11-100), while min/mean need few tasks under `ceil`. Under `gain10` caps and rank 31-100 on HELM, all rules except plurality are infeasible for most targets (median reported inf when >50% infeasible; infeasible shares in `tables/group_medians_helm.csv`), and at rank 11-30 the min rule already is: *whether a rule can be gamed at all depends on the headroom cap*.
5. **Cap scenarios change conclusions** (`tables/group_medians_*`): the OLLB under `ceil` has K=1-2 for the mean rule; under `gain10` most targets of rank >100 cannot reach first under the mean at all, and under `gain5` neither can most targets of rank >10 (`tables/group_medians_ollb.csv`). Headline claims must name the cap.
6. **Robustness - score normalisation:** on raw (unnormalised) OLLB scores the advantage of the median over the mean shrinks (rank 11-30: 20.8 vs 23.0 vs 4.2/32.2 normalised) and min becomes costlier (`results/ollb_raw_score.csv` vs `ollb_closed.csv`). **Slices:** removing merged models (`ollb_nomerge_score.csv`) changes group medians by 0-20% (rank>100: mean 185 vs 182; rank 31-100 median 21.4 vs 17.9) - ordering unchanged. **Leaderboard depth:** the top-500 slice (`ollb_top500.csv`) gives the same ordering among score rules, but the ordinal rules move: Borda is much cheaper than on HELM and is the cheapest rule at rank 3-5 (0.4) and close to the median/min rule elsewhere (rank 11-30: 6.7 vs median 4.2, min 9.7, mean 32.2); the Borda/Condorcet cost depends strongly on the leaderboard size and the task count (M, n).
7. **Robustness - evaluation noise and task choice** (`tables/robust_noise.csv`, `robust_taskboot.csv`; HELM, gain10 cap, targets of ranks 2-11, 100 draws): rules that are cheap in the observed data stay cheap (share of draws cheaper than the mean: plurality 0.94, median 0.93-0.95, Condorcet 0.94-0.95, Borda 0.90-0.92), but the cost of the **min** rule is unstable (within-target CV 1.2-1.3, 30-44% of draws infeasible) and the median's is noisy (CV 0.58-0.68). Mean/geometric are the most stable (CV 0.17-0.26).
8. **Task-subset scaling** (`tables/tasksub_*.csv`, HELM random subsets of n = 3..48 tasks, 200 draws, target = rank-2/5 under the rule's own ranking; rank-5 target, ceil, L1 medians for n = 3/6/12/24/48): mean 7.0/15.5/30.7/65.6/129 and geometric 6.8/15.1/28.2/60.4/117 grow ~ linearly in n; Borda grows more slowly (3.3/5.2/9.2/18.3/41.7); median (2.8/4.3/3.8/3.2/1.9) and min (4.0/7.2/6.7/6.6/2.4) are flat or *fall* with n; plurality is 2-3 for n >= 6 but **20.8 at n = 3** (with 3 tasks a rank-5 target must win several tasks outright; under gain10 it is infeasible for 66% of these targets at n=3). Rank-2 targets show the same pattern at a lower level (`tables/tasksub_ceil.csv`, `results/final/robust_tasksub.csv`).
9. **Rules agree on who is cheap** (`tables/spearman_helm.csv`, gain10): Spearman of L1 across targets 0.92-0.99 among mean/geometric/harmonic/Borda/Condorcet/median; min is the outlier (0.67-0.78).

10. **Copeland is exact and the Condorcet-winner bound is tight on HELM, loose on OLLB** (`results/final/copeland_vs_condorcet.csv`, `src/run_copeland.py`, 34 target x cap cases; L1): on HELM (ranks 2-70, ceil/gain10) Copeland's minimum equals the Condorcet-winner cost minus 0.001 (one eps) in every feasible case (ratio 1.000-1.001; the cheapest-way-to-top-Copeland is to become a Condorcet winner), and the cases infeasible for the Condorcet winner (gain10, ranks 50/70) are infeasible for Copeland too. On the OLLB top-500 slice (n = 6) Copeland is 1.4-8.1x cheaper than the Condorcet-winner requirement (ranks 2-11: 1.10 vs 6.70, 0.69 vs 5.29, 6.34 vs 9.72, 1.39 vs 11.17, 9.78 vs 13.70) and is always cheaper than the mean rule (4.77, 5.11, 23.9, 27.7, 29.1); there, with six tasks, pairwise ties/majority structure leaves room to top the Copeland score without winning every duel. Copeland <= Condorcet-winner holds in all 34 cases (as it must). So on HELM the Condorcet-winner column can be read as the Copeland cost; on the OLLB it is only an upper bound. Schulze and Kemeny are not implemented.
11. **Targeting is what makes ordinal rules cheap: random allocation control** (`results/final/random_control_summary.csv`, `src/run_random_control.py`; HELM, targets of ranks 2-20 per rule with L1* >= 0.05, 100 draws per target; `spread` = Dirichlet-random split over all 57 tasks, `ktasks` = same number of tasks as the optimum, chosen at random, equal split; factor = smallest multiplier of the optimal budget at which the random allocation makes the target first). Median over targets of the per-target median factor, ceil: **mean 1.00** (any split works as long as no cap binds), geometric 1.16, harmonic 1.35, Borda 2.8, Condorcet winner 4.0, plurality 7.7, median 12.2, min 38.4 (gain10: 1.00, 1.09, 1.19, 2.4, 3.7, 7.7, 12.2, 43.4). So the rules that are cheapest *with optimal targeting* (min, median, plurality) are the ones that gain most from targeting (random spending needs 8-40x the budget), whereas under the mean targeting buys nothing in total points. With the random choice of only K* tasks (`ktasks`) the target typically cannot become first even with every chosen task raised to the cap (median factor inf for all rules; share of targets for which the median draw is infeasible 0.90-1.00 for every rule including the mean, see the table): which tasks are raised matters, not only how many.
12. **Granularity: same models, 39 leaf tasks vs 6 benchmark aggregates** (`results/final/leaf_vs_six_groups.csv`, `leaf_top500_groups.csv`, `leaf_tasksub.csv`; Open LLM Leaderboard `results` dataset, 4506 models, raw accuracy x 100, one board of 39 leaf tasks and the item-weighted 6-benchmark aggregates of the same runs; per-task units L1/n and K/n; ceil, rank groups by the rank under each board's own mean): the **mean rule is invariant to granularity** (per-task L1 25.3 vs 26.0 for rank > 100; 3.0 vs 4.9 at 11-30, where the group composition differs between the boards), but **median and min become 5.5-20x cheaper per task at ranks 11-100** on the finer board (median 19.8x and 8.9x, min 8.9x and 5.5x at ranks 11-30 and 31-100; 2.2x and 3.1x at rank > 100; higher still at ranks <= 10, where a few targets make the ratios noisy): rank 11-30 median 0.22 vs 4.44 points per task, min 0.27 vs 2.40; rank 31-100 median 0.61 vs 5.44, min 0.47 vs 2.59; the share of tasks that must be raised falls (median 9% vs 33%, min 5% vs 33% at rank 11-30). Geometric mean sits between (1.67 vs 3.62 at 11-30). Under `gain10` the 39-task board makes the median/min rules *feasible* where the 6-task board is infeasible (rank 11-30: median infeasible 0% vs 80%; rank 31-100: 34% vs 100%). Ordinal rules on the top-500 slice of the leaf board (`leaf_top500_groups.csv`, sampled targets; ranks 11-30 / 31-100, ceil, per-task L1; the comparison column is `ollb_top500` on the *normalised* 6-task scale, so only the direction and the ratio between rules are comparable, not levels): Condorcet-winner 0.75 / 1.55 vs 2.9 / 4.6, Borda 0.86 / 1.85 vs 1.1 / 2.9, plurality 1.97 / 2.76 vs 2.4 / 3.1 (Borda is only ~1.5x cheaper and plurality hardly changes, while median 0.22 / 0.61 vs 0.71 / 2.8 and min 0.27 / 0.48 vs 1.6 / 2.0 fall 3-6x). Under `gain10`, plurality becomes infeasible for 80% (ranks 11-30) and 100% (31-100) of the leaf-board targets (vs 10% / 63% at n = 6) - with many tasks a plurality win needs first place on many tasks, which a +10 cap forbids - while the median and Condorcet-winner rules stay feasible for most targets. Random task subsets of the leaf board (`leaf_tasksub.csv`, rank-5 target under the rule's own ranking, ceil, median per-task L1 at n = 6 / 24 / 39; 100 draws, n = 39 is a single subset): mean 3.25 / 1.56 / 1.64, geometric 2.11 / 0.73 / 0.70, median 1.19 / 0.20 / 0.055, min 0.54 / 0.080 / 0.050, i.e. the median-to-mean cost ratio falls 0.36 -> 0.13 -> 0.034: the number of tasks, not the number of models, drives the divergence between order-statistic rules and the mean (the OLLB main board confounds the two: n = 6 with M = 4496, HELM n = 57 with M = 79). Caveat: leaf scores are raw accuracies (not normalised for chance level), which matters for min/median (finding 6: raw scores shrink the median/mean advantage on the 6-task board).

13. **HELM near-tie (wording for rank 2-3)** (`results/final/helm_near_tie.csv`, `src/helm_neartie.py`): the leader (Claude 3.5 Sonnet 20241022) leads DeepSeek v3 by 0.070 points (0.0007 on the 0-1 scale) in the 57-subject mean, which is 0.14 binomial standard errors of the gap (SE 0.48 points under independent cell errors; the models share items, so the paired SE is smaller and 0.48 is an upper bound); Gemini 1.5 Pro (002) trails by 0.39 (0.8 SE), Claude 3.5 Sonnet (20240620) by 0.73 (1.5 SE), ranks 5-8 by 2.7-3.1 points (5.4-6.2 SE). So the *rank-2 and rank-3* investments on HELM (mean rule 4.0 and 22.5 points in total, or 0.07 and 0.40 per task) are statements about a margin that is inside the evaluation noise; read them against `results/final/robust_helm_noise_gain10_by_target.csv` (cost distribution under noise for ranks 2-11) and do not interpret ranks 2-3 as a ranking of rules. Ranks >= 5 are well outside the noise.

## Final figure-ready files (`results/final/`, built by `src/finalize.py`; schema in its docstring; hashes in `results/manifest.csv`)
* `status` in {`ok`, `infeasible`, `already_first`}: infeasible rows have `L1=K=Linf=inf` (proven by the solver); already-first rows keep their raw cost (0 or a tie-break margin) in `L1/K/Linf` and have NaN `*_net` columns. Use `*_net` for "investment needed", and always report `share_infeasible` next to a feasible-only median (`group_summary.csv`; the plain `L1_med` is `inf` when >50% are infeasible).
* `L1_per_task`, `K_frac` normalise by n (57 HELM, 6 OLLB). The rank group of a *variant* file (`raw`, `nomerge`, `top500`) is the target's rank under that variant's own mean on that variant's own board (not under the full-board mean), so `variant_vs_main.csv` compares targets at the same rank *position*, not the same models.
* `theory_per_target.csv.gz`: solver vs closed form for the mean (all 8 caps: feasibility, L1 = n(gap+eps), K greedy by headroom, Linf water level), the min rule (all caps) and the median rule (HELM, n odd, all caps); 0 mismatches over 108k comparisons (`logs/finalize.log`).

## Caveats that must travel with the numbers
* "Targeted training raises a score to any value up to the cap" is a modelling assumption; the cap scenarios are the sensitivity analysis. The `emp*` caps use fine-tune-vs-base gains observed on the OLLB (540 pairs), not causal estimates.
* Other models are held fixed (no strategic response); the target's own score noise is not modelled in the main runs (only in the MC robustness).
* HELM has 79 models, so rank groups 2/3-5/6-10 contain 1/3/5 targets: their CIs are wide and some are degenerate (single target).
* The `rank_rule` column was dropped from the `helm_noise/taskboot` CSVs (it was a placeholder for Condorcet); in `results/final` `rank_rule` is NaN for `condorcet_winner`.
* Ordinal OLLB runs use sampled targets (rank-stratified) and gain caps only; `ceil/front` ordinal runs on all 4496 OLLB models were not done (a single Borda MILP at M=4496, ceil takes minutes; the top-500 slice covers `ceil`).
* Trimmed mean: sample of OLLB targets only. Copeland: 34 target x cap cases (MILP, 0.2-50 s each), not all targets.
* Random-allocation control: HELM only, ranks 2-20, targets with L1* < 0.05 skipped (near-ties make the ratio meaningless).
* Leaf board: raw accuracies, 4506 models (not the 4496 of the main OLLB board: it keeps models absent from `contents`); its rank-group cohorts differ from the 6-benchmark board's because the means differ (unweighted over 39 leaves vs 6 item-weighted benchmarks). Ordinal rules were run only on the top-500 slice of the leaf board (sampled targets) and in the task-subset runs.
