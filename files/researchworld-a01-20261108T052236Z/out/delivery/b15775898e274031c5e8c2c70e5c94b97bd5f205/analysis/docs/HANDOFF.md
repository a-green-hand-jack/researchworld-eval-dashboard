# Handoff: what each result file means, what may be plotted, and how to read the claim tables

Provenance: every file under `results/` is produced by the command in `run_all.sh`; `results/MANIFEST.csv` gives for every file the row count, grid check
(`complete` = full caps × rules × targets product, no duplicates, no NaN), sha256 and the producing script; `results/input_hashes.json` hashes the inputs.
`results/infeasibility_summary.csv` = share of infeasible targets per file × cap × rule × measure.

## Conventions (all result files)
- **Target set.** Every model except the leader under the plain arithmetic mean (`rank_mean > 1`): 78 targets on HELM (79 models), 4495 on OLLB (4497 models in the processed table, 1 model flagged by the leaderboard removed ⇒ 4496 models in the grids). The CSVs contain *all* models; the mean leader appears with `rank_mean = 1` and cost 0 under the mean: **filter `rank_mean > 1`** in any figure/table (all `claims/` tables do, with denominators in every row). A target that is already first under another rule (cost 0) is kept.
- **Measures** (columns `K`, `L1`, `Linf`): tasks touched / total points / largest single-task raise, **each the exact minimum of its own problem** (the minimiser of one is not the minimiser of another; a figure must not draw them as one strategy). Scores are in OLLB-normalised points (0–100) on OLLB and accuracy points (0–100) on HELM. Margin ε = 1e-3 (strict win); ε sensitivity in `results/sens/` → `claims/robust_eps.csv`.
- **Infeasible = `inf`** (no raise within the cap reaches first place), never dropped; **NaN = unresolved solver status** (none in the delivered files; `MANIFEST.csv` column `n_unresolved_nan`). `front0/front2` are feasible by construction; `ceil` is feasible for every rule on both datasets (see `infeasibility_summary.csv`); gain caps are infeasible for a large share of targets, so **medians are over feasible targets only and must be shown together with `share_feasible`** (`claims/cap_rule_summary.csv` has both, with Wilson 95 % intervals on the share).
- **`gap`** (score rules) = best rival's rule value − target's rule value; NaN for ordinal rules (benign).
- **Ordinal rules** use a discrete candidate-level model (tie with a rival = half credit; strict pass = rival score + ε). Linf for ordinal rules was corrected on 2026-11-08 (issue #4: earlier it lacked the ε margin so Linf could be < L1/n); L1 and K were unchanged. `claims/measure_consistency.csv`: Linf ≤ L1 ≤ n·Linf, K ≤ n and identical feasibility across the three measures hold in every row.

## Interval types (never interchangeable)
1. **Wilson 95 %** — a *share of targets* (feasible / claim holds).
2. **Bootstrap over targets** (B = 2000, seed 20261108, percentile) — sampling variability of a median/quantile over the *target models*; not evaluation noise.
3. **Draw quantiles** (`*_q05/_q50/_q95`) — spread over replicates *for one target* (evaluation noise / task bootstrap / task subsets / weights): the quantity of interest, not a confidence interval.

## Files
Main grids (per target): `helm_main.csv` (6 caps × 8 rules: mean, geomean, harmonic, median, min, Borda, plurality, Condorcet; all 79 models), `ollb_closed.csv` (ceil) + `ollb_scores_caps.csv` (7 other caps) for mean/geomean/harmonic/median/min on all 4496 targets, `ollb_trimmed1.csv` (trimmed mean, all caps, with K), `ollb_ordinal_sample.csv` (Borda/plurality/Condorcet on a rank-stratified sample: ranks 2–21 plus 150 log-uniform ranks, same sample for every cap; **sample, not all targets** — label as such; the score rules on the same sample are used in `claims/c4_*`).
Robustness slices: `ollb_nomerge_scores.csv`, `ollb_top500_scores.csv`, `ollb_raw_scores.csv` (raw accuracy instead of normalised), `ollb_dedupe_worst_scores.csv`, `ollb_powmean.csv`/`helm_powmean.csv` (p = 0.5, −0.5, −2, −5), `sens/` (ε, offsets).
Replicate files: `*_noise_gain10.csv` (evaluation noise: binomial SE per cell for the whole leaderboard, then the minimum **re-optimised on each draw**), `*_taskboot_gain10.csv` (task bootstrap, re-optimised), `tasksub_helm.csv` (n-sweep: random task subsets of n = 3…57, with `shuffled=True` control that permutes each task column over models), `weights_*.csv` (Dirichlet(1) weights, B = 50).
**Re-optimised ≠ fixed strategy.** Noise/bootstrap rows give a *cost distribution*; they cannot be read as the probability that a given intervention works. For **F4 (first-place probability versus fixed investment)** use `replay/*.csv` (columns `strategy` ∈ {uniform, greedy}, `d`, `L1_spent`, `p_first` over R = 200 noise draws, strategy fixed *before* noise: raising by `d` on every task up to cap, or spending the same L1 on largest-headroom tasks first) and its summary `claims/robust_replay_summary.csv`. Information assumption: the rival scores and the target's scores are re-drawn with the same SE model; the investment is chosen on the observed leaderboard.
`condorcet_existence.csv`: whether a Condorcet winner exists on each dataset, observed (`draw = -1`) and under noise.
Theory: `docs/PROPOSITIONS.md`, `theory_validation.csv` (per target), `theory_validation_summary.json`, `theory_checks.json`: numerical agreement between closed forms and solver, not an independent proof. The blanket statements "median needs ⌈n/2⌉+1 tasks" and "min needs all n tasks" are **false** (tasks already above the winning threshold count). Even-n median: only K has a closed form (checked); L1/Linf for the median are solver values validated by brute force in `tests/`.
Claim tables (`claims/`): `cap_rule_summary.csv` (feasibility + cost quantiles per dataset × cap × rule: the cap map), `c2_order_statistics.csv`, `c3_rule_ordering_by_cap.csv`, `c4_ordinal_vs_mean.csv`, `pairwise_rule_comparison.csv`, `measure_consistency.csv`, `robust_*.csv`, `robust_slices.csv` (data-slice / normalisation / duplicate-rule robustness: the L1 order of the rules min < median < harmonic < geomean < mean at `ceil` is the same in every slice).

## Claim verdicts (plan C1–C6), all from the files above; `n` = targets with `rank_mean > 1`
- **C1 (mean closed form; also min/median).** Confirmed numerically: 0 mismatches between closed forms and solver in every (dataset, rule, measure) cell (`theory_validation_summary.json`). Blanket median/min task-count statements are false.
- **C2 (order of K: mean ≤ trimmed ≤ median ≤ min).** Holds only partly. OLLB `ceil`: K(mean) ≤ K(trimmed1) ≤ K(median) for 100 % of 4495 targets, K(median) ≤ K(min) for 89 %. It fails once caps bind or on HELM: HELM `ceil` K(mean) ≤ K(median) 86 %, K(median) ≤ K(min) 9 %; HELM `front0` K(mean) ≤ K(median) 4 %. OLLB `front0`: 74 %/61 %/82 %. Cause: each rule has its own rival threshold, so the count of tasks to raise is not ordered by the rule's "strictness". The verdict is: **refuted as a general order, true only for OLLB `ceil` (mean ≤ trimmed ≤ median)** (`c2_order_statistics.csv`, with the numbers of targets infeasible for one rule only).
- **C3 (caps and feasibility).** `ceil`, `front0`, `front2`: 100 % feasible. Gain caps: HELM gain10 feasible for 18 % (min) to 73 % (plurality) of targets; OLLB gain5 ≤ 2.3 %, gain10 2.5–9.9 %, gain20 13.5–63.9 % (min highest), emp95 1.4–7.5 %, emp99 6.1–12.1 %. Costs at `ceil` are not a guide to costs under realistic caps (`cap_rule_summary.csv`, `c3_rule_ordering_by_cap.csv`).
- **Headline at `ceil`, median over targets (K / L1 / Linf).** HELM: mean 16 / 696 / 13.2, geomean 16 / 669 / 14.2, harmonic 16 / 644 / 15.3, median 21 / 112 / 9.8, min 11.5 / 98 / 22.2, Borda 47 / 534 / 15.1, Condorcet 29 / 213 / 10.2, plurality 9 / 47 / 6.2. OLLB: mean 2 / 181 / 30.1, geomean 3 / 153 / 27.8, harmonic 3 / 125 / 24.9, trimmed1 3 / 141 / 33.7, median 3 / 106 / 36.2, min 3 / 43 / 17.9. The ordering of rules differs across the three measures (the mean has the highest L1 but the smallest K on OLLB; min has the smallest L1 and Linf on OLLB but the largest Linf on HELM).
- **C4 (rank rules vs mean).** At `ceil` the ordinal rules are cheaper in L1 than the mean for 99–100 % of HELM targets (median ratio ordinal/mean L1: Borda 0.78, Condorcet 0.30, plurality 0.07) and for 98–100 % of the OLLB sample (0.46, 0.66, 0.42). Under tight caps ordinal rules are often infeasible where the mean is feasible (e.g. OLLB sample gain10: plurality infeasible where the mean is feasible for 54 of 136 targets) (`c4_ordinal_vs_mean.csv`). A Condorcet winner exists on the observed HELM data (it is the mean leader) and in 86 % of the HELM noise draws (in 57 % of those draws the winner is that draw's mean leader) and does not exist on OLLB (`condorcet_existence.csv`): for OLLB the Condorcet column is the *requirement to beat every rival by a task majority* (Copeland-maximal), not a winner that exists today.
- **C5 (near the top, with noise).** Noise/bootstrap files give the cost distribution; the fixed-strategy replay gives the success curve: for the uniform raise at the nominal minimum Linf the per-target probability of being first has median 0.2–0.5 across rules (range 0.09–0.89 over the 5 target ranks), and the further uniform raise needed to reach 90 % is a median over the 5 targets of 0.75–1.97 points for most rules, 3.45 for OLLB harmonic, and 6.38 (OLLB) / 8.26 (HELM) for min (`robust_replay_summary.csv`; `uniform` strategy, targets of ranks 2, 3, 5, 10, 20).
- **C6 (n-sweep, shuffle control).** K/n falls with n and L1 grows roughly linearly with n for the mean in the observed matrix; the shuffled-column control (cross-task correlation broken) changes the level and the slope (`robust_nsweep.csv`, columns `shuffled`, `K_over_n_*`, `L1_over_n_*`); read it as a comparison of the two curves, not as a fitted exponent.

### #7 smallest uniform gain cap g* (`src/gstar.py` → `results/gstar_per_target.csv`, `claims/gstar_summary.csv`, `gstar_curves.csv`, `gstar_validation.csv`, `gstar_anchor_finetune_gains.csv`)
- **Definition.** g*(target, rule) = Linf under the `ceil` cap = the smallest g such that raising every task by at most g (capped at 100) can make the target strictly first (ε = 10⁻³). Feasibility is monotone in g, so "feasible under `gain g`" ⇔ g* ≤ g; `gstar_validation.csv` checks this against the stored gain5/10/20 runs: 0 disagreements in 1,872 HELM and 82,134 OLLB (target, rule, cap) cells. g* is finite for every target and rule at `ceil`.
- **Statistic.** The feasible share at cap g is the empirical CDF of g* (`gstar_curves.csv`, g = 0, 0.5, …, 100, Wilson 95 % bands). The cap where the share crosses 50 % is the lower median of g* (`crossing_cap_50`, bootstrap `crossing_lo/hi`).
- **Sets.** HELM (78 targets, 8 rules); OLLB census (4,495 targets; mean, geomean, harmonic, median, min, trimmed1); OLLB 136-target rank-stratified sample (all nine rules; **not** a population sample: it over-represents ranks 2–21). Read the census row for "typical OLLB model" and the sample row only for rule comparison on the same targets.
- **Numbers (50 % crossing cap, [95 % CI]).** HELM: plurality 6.1 [5.3, 7.5], median 9.8 [7.5, 12.7], Condorcet 10.1 [7.7, 12.7], mean 12.6 [10.2, 16.4], geomean 13.8, Borda 15.0 [11.0, 17.9], harmonic 15.2, min 22.2 [19.3, 24.5]. OLLB census: min 17.9 [17.7, 18.1], harmonic 24.9, geomean 27.8, mean 30.1 [29.7, 30.4], trimmed1 33.7, median 36.2 [35.8, 36.5]. OLLB sample: Borda 8.2 [6.7, 9.7], min 9.9, mean 10.1 [8.9, 11.1], geomean 10.3, median 10.3, harmonic 10.0, trimmed1 12.5, Condorcet 13.7 [12.3, 14.4], plurality 17.0 [15.3, 18.2]. Shares feasible at g = 10 / 20: HELM mean 37 % / 72 %; OLLB census mean 3 % / 18 %, min 10 % / 64 %, median 5 % / 14 %.
- **Reading.** The rule ordering by g* is dataset-dependent (the median rule is among the cheapest on HELM and the dearest on the OLLB census; plurality is cheapest on HELM and dearest of the sample rules on OLLB), and differs from the ordering by L1. ### #8 replay robustness (`src/run_replay2.py` → `results/replay2/*.csv`; `src/replay2_summary.py` → `claims/replay2_summary.csv`, `replay2_per_target.csv`)
- **Design.** Uniform strategy only (every task +d, capped at the cap; at d = Linf* it is the exact Linf-minimiser), chosen on the observed leaderboard; budget grid = Linf* + {−2, −1, −0.5, 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16} points; p_first = share of R = 200 draws (draws shared across rules, targets and budgets) in which the target is strictly first. **36 targets per dataset** (HELM 36, OLLB 36): ranks 2–11 under the mean plus 26 log-spaced ranks up to the last (deterministic grid; `*_randtargets_seed*` runs redraw the tail ranks log-uniformly). Rules: HELM all eight, OLLB the five score rules (ordinal rules on all 4,496 OLLB models are in the 136-sample only).
- **Metrics (per rule, medians over the 36 targets, bootstrap over targets).** `p0` = p_first at the nominal Linf-optimal raise; `x90` = further uniform raise beyond Linf* until p_first ≥ 0.9 (first crossing, linear interpolation; the grid ends at +16, so x90 is censored at +inf for targets that never reach 0.9 — `x90_censored`). Also by rank band (2–5, 6–20, 21+).
- **Headline (obs, SE × 1, seed 20261108, R = 200).** Median p0: HELM mean 0.24 [0.20, 0.28], geomean 0.22, harmonic 0.24, median 0.22, min 0.24, plurality 0.14, Condorcet 0.29, **Borda 0.80**; OLLB mean 0.43 [0.42, 0.46], geomean 0.40, harmonic 0.34, median 0.26, min 0.14. Median x90 (points): HELM mean 1.3 [1.0, 1.3], median 2.0, Condorcet 1.2, plurality 1.9, **min 9.0 [7.6, 9.6]**, Borda 0.3; OLLB mean 1.3, geomean 1.7, harmonic 2.5, median 1.5, **min 5.6**. The 5-target numbers of C5 (p0 0.2–0.5; x90 0.75–1.97, min 6.4/8.3) are inside these ranges. The CI over targets is narrow on OLLB (the cost is a smooth function of rank) and wider on HELM.
- **Overdispersion (SE × 1.5 and × 2).** The factor stands for the ratio of total evaluation noise SD to the binomial item-sampling SD that `standard_errors` assumes. No data in hand estimates it (no repeated evaluations of the same model on either board), so 1.5 and 2 are sensitivity multipliers, not estimates; no factor below 1 is justified. Median p0 falls monotonically (HELM mean 0.24 → 0.19 → 0.17; OLLB mean 0.43 → 0.38 → 0.34; OLLB min 0.14 → 0.08 → 0.06) and the extra raise for 90 % rises roughly in proportion to the factor (HELM mean 1.3 → 2.0 → 3.0; OLLB mean 1.3 → 2.0 → 2.9; the min rule reaches 9.2 (OLLB) and ≥13 (HELM) at × 1.5; at × 2 it is 12.0 on OLLB and censored (> 16 points) for 17 of 36 HELM targets). The min rule keeps by far the largest x90 and the mean/Condorcet the smallest; only close pairs swap (HELM harmonic vs median x90: 1.47 vs 1.98 at × 1, 3.68 vs 3.62 at × 2), and the p0 values of the score rules converge (0.14–0.17 on HELM at × 2).
- **Regression to the mean (`--center shrunk`, a model, not a measurement).** The observed leaderboard is read as one noisy draw around a shrunk truth T = m_t + λ_t (X − m_t), λ_t = max(0, 1 − mean SE_t² / var_t(X)), m_t the column mean (λ median 0.93 on HELM (min 0.34), 0.994 on OLLB (min 0.78)); the target's row gets the same raise d (a true gain), the investment d is still the one chosen on the observed data, and noise is drawn around T. Assumptions: homoscedastic Gaussian noise with the item-count SE, a common prior mean per task, no other noise source. Result: the probability **rises**, not falls (median p0: HELM mean 0.84, geomean 0.80, Condorcet 0.60, median 0.37, min 0.46; OLLB mean 0.82, geomean 0.72, harmonic 0.60, median 0.26, min 0.30), a likely reason being that a raise designed to *just* pass the observed best rival (by 10⁻³) leaves the target marginally ahead of a rival whose true score has regressed, while the target's own (lower) baseline regresses upward. So the observed-centred replay is the conservative of the two readings, and p0 at the nominal minimum is knife-edge: on OLLB the shrunk centring moves rival scores by (1 − λ)(X − m), under 1 % of their distance from the column mean (≤ 0.3 points), yet raises the mean rule's median p0 from 0.43 to 0.82. That is why x90, not p0, is the number to quote for "how much more than the minimum".

### #11 rule-normalised costs (`src/normalised_costs.py`, `src/nozero_diag.py` → `claims/normalised_summary.csv`, `rank_matched.csv`, `abstract_ratio_check.csv`, `min_zero_diagnostic.csv`, `min_zero_bars.csv`, `normalised_per_target.csv`)
- **(a) Normalisation.** For rules with a score in task-point units (mean, geomean, harmonic, median, min, trimmed1): gap = best rival's rule score − target's rule score (the rule's own leader gap; 0 if already first), IQR = interquartile range of the rule score over all models. `nL1_gap = L1/gap`, `nLinf_gap = Linf/gap`, `nL1_iqr = (L1/n)/IQR`, `nLinf_iqr = Linf/IQR`. Why: L1 and Linf are in task points, but each rule's score scale differs (the min rule's scores are compressed towards the floor, its gaps are small); the gap says how far the target must climb *in that rule's units*, the IQR how spread the board is in them. Borda points are not in task-point units and plurality/Condorcet have no cardinal score, so those rules appear in the raw view and in (b) only.
- **(b) Rank-matched** (`rank_matched.csv`): among targets whose rank *under that rule* falls in a bin, the median K / L1 / Linf to first and the ratio to the mean rule's median in the same bin (independent bootstrap for the ratio's interval). Bins: HELM 2, 3–5, 6–10, 11–20, 21+; OLLB sample 2–5, 6–10, 11–21, 22–100, 101–1000, 1001+; census adds finer bins.
