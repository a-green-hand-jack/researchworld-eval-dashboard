\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:disc}
\paragraph{What the evidence supports.}
On score matrices that look like the training population, a few well-chosen benchmarks recover most of what the column mean misses: on MTEB-56, $k=2$--$4$ informed benchmarks close $80\%$ of the gap to the full-information predictor and $k=4$--$16$ close $90\%$ ($5$--$8$ and $12$--$16$ for random ones; Table~\ref{tab:kstar}), and a single aggregate score does not (Table~\ref{tab:cross}). The gain from \emph{choosing} the benchmarks is largest at $k\le4$ and $\le0.03$ nMSE from $k\approx16$; the inference rule matters at least as much.

\paragraph{Failure cases.}
(i) \emph{Extrapolation.} In the temporal split the newest MTEB-56 models lie outside the range of older ones: the column mean has $\nmse=4.6$, even the full-information floor is $1.4$, and no $k\le32$ gives an error below $1.2$; release-date terms and unshrunk least squares do not remove it. A Mahalanobis check on the $k$ revealed scores flags these models ($39$--$48\%$ of the test models, with $3$--$6\times$ the error of the unflagged ones; $7$--$8\%$ of models in random folds, with $4$--$8\times$), so we recommend reporting the flag rate; it is weak where the shift is mild (MTEB-275). Leave-family-out raises the floor from $0.15$--$0.18$ to $0.27$--$0.33$. (ii) \emph{Spectral heuristics.} Leverage-score, pivoted-QR and highest-variance selection are worse than random at small $k$, apparently through rank mis-specification (Section~\ref{sec:exp}). (iii) \emph{Theory.} The Bayes curve stays $20$--$30\%$ below the cross-validated error on MTEB even with a train-only covariance; it checks the shape of the curves, not their level.

\paragraph{Limits of the design.}
Targets are a random $25\%$ of benchmarks, so prediction of a different \emph{kind} of benchmark is not tested. The evidence is mostly MTEB: the five generative-LLM matrices have $N\le57$ (short $k$-curves); random folds are the optimistic reading of ``new model''. $k^\star$ is relative to an uncertain floor far from the noise level ($0.03$--$0.06$); the sparse result is one matrix.
