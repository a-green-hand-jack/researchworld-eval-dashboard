# Results index (experiment-repo)

Every file is produced by a script in `analysis/` from the cached/synthetic data named below; none is edited by hand. Status of each file is stated.

## Synthetic study (Gaussian-copula item pool; Kp=128 configs, N=20000 items, `analysis/synth.py`)
- `synth_plan_summary.csv` — **plan grid** (`analysis/run_synth3.py`, summarised by `analysis/make_synth_results.py`). One row per (cell, est_name). Complete: 106 cells = OFAT around the reference cell (229 rows), an n x K x tau x procedure (argmax, sh) interaction grid (540 rows) and stress cells (40 rows); sr and greedy appear in the OFAT cells. Files with `sr`/`greedy` have M=2000 only in OFAT/stress cells.
  - Cell factors: `f_kind` (ofat / inter / stress), `f_n` tuning items, `f_K` configurations searched, `f_tau` sd of true accuracies across configs, `f_rho` latent item correlation between configs, `f_m` mean accuracy, `f_proc` search procedure (argmax, sh = successive halving, sr = successive rejects, greedy), `f_eps` generation noise (correctness flipped with prob eps), `f_leak` number of leaked items, `f_outlier` outlier-config fraction; `is_reference` marks the reference cell (n=200, K=16, tau=.01, rho=.5, m=.3, argmax, eps=0, leak=0).
  - `est_name`: reporting method (naive, split, crossfit, boot_opt = Harrell optimism, boot_oob = bootstrap out-of-bag, boot_632, simul = naive point + simultaneous interval, cond = conditional-on-selection median-unbiased estimate/CI for argmax only).
  - Metrics (per replication the reported value is compared with the *true* score of the selected config): `bias` (mean reported − true), `bias_se` (MC s.e.), `rmse`, `cover` (95% interval covers the truth) with `cover_se`, `width`, `lb_viol` (reported lower bound above truth), `mu_sel` (mean true score of selected config), `regret` (best-in-candidate-set minus mu_sel), `claim` (rate of claiming tuned > baseline config at one-sided z .05 using the reported estimate), `fp_rate` (claim rate when the selected config is truly not better), `ship_*pp`/`false_ship_*pp` (claim that the config beats baseline by ≥1/2/3 pp, and false ones), `n_nonpos` (replications with a truly non-better config), `reps`. M = 2000 for ofat/stress cells, 500 for the interaction grid.
- `synth_theory_prediction.csv` — Gaussian-max prediction of the gap (`analysis/theory.py`, M=5000 draws, from pools generated with seed 7) on a tau x rho x n x K grid (`pred_gap`, `pred_gap_mc_se`), joined to the empirical naive gap of the plan grid where a matching cell exists (`emp_gap`, `emp_gap_mc_se`, `emp_reps`). On the 55 matched cells |emp − pred| averages 0.0009 and is at most 0.0047 (accuracy units); 8 of 55 differ by more than 2 combined MC s.e. (interaction cells have M=500).
- `synth_compare_summary.csv` — two-system comparison (tuned with K_A vs K_B configurations; `analysis/run_synth2.py`): claim / false-claim / wrong-sign rates and reported vs true difference per (tau, K_A, K_B, n, est_name); `bias_diff` = mean reported − mean true difference with `bias_diff_se`, `mean_reported_diff_se` its MC s.e.
- `synth_ladder_summary.csv` — Ladder-type thresholded reporting vs the other methods (`analysis/run_synth_ladder.py`), same columns as the plan summary, per (tau, proc, n, K, est_name).
- `synth_hybrid_summary.csv` — conditional (`cond`) vs hybrid (`hybrid`, Andrews–Kitagawa–McCloskey-type: projection interval at beta=.005 plus conditional test on the restricted truncation set; `analysis/lib.py::est_hybrid`) vs `naive` and `simul` intervals for the argmax winner, over tau in {0,.01,.03} x n in {100,200,500} x K in {4,16,64} (rho=.5, m=.3; M=500; `analysis/run_hybrid.py`). Columns as in the plan summary. Over the 27 cells hybrid coverage is 94.6–97.0% (mean 95.7%), cond 94.0–96.2%, simul 96.6–99.2%, naive 51–93%; mean widths hybrid .17, cond .59, simul .20, naive .13. (The `bias` column of cond/hybrid is the bias of the conditional median, which is heavy-tailed; do not use it as a point-estimate bias.)
- `synth_ranking_summary.csv` — ranking among 5 tuned systems evaluated on the same tuning items (`analysis/run_grid.py::run_rank`, `analysis/run_rank_synth.py`; designs: five systems with K=16 each, or K = 4/8/16/32/64; tau in {0,.01,.03}; n in {100,200,500}; M=500). Per (design, tau_gen, n, est_name): `tau_mean` Kendall tau between reported and true ranking of the systems (true value of a system = true score of its selected config), `top1` (reported best is truly best), `wrong_order_1pp` (share of discordant pairs among pairs with a true difference of at least 1 pp; NaN-averaged, noisy at tau=0), `big_reported_best` / `big_truly_best` (how often the largest-budget system ranks first by reported / true score), `range_true` (mean spread of true scores), `reps`.

### Reliability criterion, claim test and false-claim rates (synthetic; `analysis/make_cell_tables.py`)
- `synth_cell_pass_fail.csv` — one row per (cell of `synth_plan_summary.csv`, est_name) with the plan §6 criterion applied: **bias_pass** = |bias| <= max(2·bias_se, 0.5 pp); **cover_pass** = coverage inside the 95% Monte-Carlo band 0.95 ± 1.96·sqrt(.95·.05/reps) (`band_lo`, `band_hi`; reps differ by cell: 500 or 2000); `under_cover` / `over_cover` = coverage below / above the band; `pass` = both; `bias_dir` = over-report / under-report when the bias fails.
- `synth_pass_fail_by_method.csv` — counts of the above per est_name, for all 106 cells and per `f_kind` (ofat / inter / stress): cells, pass_both, bias_fail (split into over-/under-report), cover_fail, under_cover, over_cover, median/min coverage, median width. (`cond` exists for the 67 argmax cells only.) `hybrid` is not in the plan grid (see `synth_hybrid_summary.csv`).
- `synth_theory_vs_sim.csv` — the 55 cells where the Gaussian-max prediction of the naive gap can be compared with the simulated naive bias: `z` = (pred − emp)/sqrt(se_pred² + se_emp²), `differs_2se`, `direction`. 8 cells have |z| > 2: seven with the theory *below* the simulation (by 0.1–0.5 pp; z from −2.0 to −3.2) and one with the theory above it (tau .03, rho .5, n 1000, K 64: +0.23 pp, z = +3.4).
- `synth_claim_margin_summary.csv` (plan grid, per cell × est_name) and `synth_compare_margin_summary.csv` (two tuned systems, `run_synth2.py`) — claim and margin-based false-claim rates.
  - **Claim test of the plan grid** (`analysis/run_grid.py::summarize`, `analysis/lib.py::_pair`): the claim is "the shipped configuration is better than the baseline". Baseline = pool configuration 0 (a fixed default, never tuned), scored on the same tuning items as the winner (for `split`: on the report half). `base` = its accuracy there, `dse` = sd of the paired per-item differences (shipped − baseline) / sqrt(n). A claim is made iff (reported score of the shipped config − base)/dse > 1.645 (one-sided 5% z-test); the *reported score* is the method's own estimate, so a method that debiases the winner's score is tested with its debiased score. The baseline may be among the K candidates (K random configs of the 128; probability K/128); if it wins, the paired difference is 0 and no claim is made. The true difference is mu(shipped) − mu(baseline) with exact pool means; at tau = 0 all configurations have the same generating accuracy but their pool means differ by sampling (sd ≈ 0.3 pp), so "true difference <= 0" holds in about half of the replications, not all.
  - Columns: `claim` = P(claim); `false_claim_0` = P(claim and true diff <= 0), `false_claim_0.5pp` / `_1pp` the same with true diff <= 0.5 / 1 pp (claims that are not worth the margin); `claim_given_le_*` = the same conditional on the true diff being <= margin (count `n_true_diff_le_*`); `wrong_sign_claim` = P(claim and true diff < 0). `fp_rate` in `synth_plan_summary.csv` equals `claim_given_le_0`. The older `ship_*pp` / `false_ship_*pp` columns of the plan summary are a different (oracle-baseline) decision: ship iff reported score >= TRUE baseline score + margin, false when the shipped config's true score is below that bar.
  - Two-system comparison: claim iff (dhat_A − dhat_B)/dse > 1.645, dse = paired sd on the same tuning items (split: the report half); true diff = mu(A's winner) − mu(B's winner); the two systems draw their K configs from the same 64-config pool, so when A and B ship the same configuration the true difference is exactly 0 (the bootstrap-optimism method can still claim there because the two optimism estimates use independent bootstrap draws; naive/split never do).

### Conditional and hybrid winner inference: definitions and validation (`analysis/lib.py`, `analysis/validate_cond.py`, `analysis/validate_cond_lead.py`)
- **Definitions** (`lib.py::est_hybrid`). x = in-sample accuracies of the K configurations, Sigma = `np.cov` of the 0/1 item matrix / n (estimated, never clipped to [0,1]); winner k = argmax of x (after one dither draw U(-.5,.5)/n added to x to break ties; the same draw is used for every quantity of one replication). Truncation set of the polyhedral lemma: conditional on the selection event, x_k lies in [V-, V+] given the component of x orthogonal to x_k (`winner_truncation`; V- = max over j with a_j<0 ..., standard Lee et al. form), sk = sd of x_k. `cond` = median / 95% equal-tailed CI of the truncated normal N(theta, sk^2) on [V-, V+] (`cond_cdf`, `cond_ci`, root-finding over x ± 80 sk). `hybrid` = Andrews–Kitagawa–McCloskey hybrid: projection (simultaneous) interval x ± c_beta·sk with beta = .005, c_beta = the 1-beta quantile of max_j |Z_j| for Z ~ N(0, estimated correlation) with Bc = 1000 draws, plus the conditional test on the *restricted* truncation set [max(V-, x-c_beta sk), min(V+, x+c_beta sk)] at level (alpha-beta)/(1-beta) (gamma = 1-(1-alpha)/(1-beta)); CI by inverting on a 161-point grid; point estimate = the median of the truncated normal on the restricted set (hybrid median-unbiased estimator; always within x ± c_beta sk). `cond_est` in the code is the unrestricted conditional median.
- `validation_cond_hybrid.csv` (long format: check, stat, value): (1) K=2 independent closed form vs mpmath: max |cdf difference| 1e-15. (2) Known-Sigma exact Gaussian draws, 3000 reps per case, K=2..16, tau 0–.05, n 200–1000: PIT of the conditional cdf at the true theta_k is uniform (KS p-values .27–.88) and both cond and hybrid coverage are inside 94.2–96.1%. (3) Reference cell (K=16, n=200, tau=.01, rho=.5): the conditional CI keeps nominal coverage (.957 with known Sigma, .952 with estimated Sigma from Bernoulli draws) with mean width .60–.64 pp-units (x100), median width .33–.34: the interval is very wide because the selection event leaves little information when the winner's standardised lead (x_k - V-)/sk is small (median lead 0.45 se; 54% of draws below 0.5). (4) AKM replication-code port (Python port of the authors' MATLAB conditional/hybrid routines, `analysis/validate_cond.py` part 5; the authors' replication files, MATLAB, from the AKM paper's supplement), 361 random Sigma (K=2..16; 39 skipped where the port's truncated-normal quantile saturates): Vm/Vp agree to 1e-12, conditional limits and median to ~1e-6 se; hybrid limits agree to within 0.96 of one step of the 161-point grid used in `lib.py` (the port inverts on a 4001-point grid, so a one-step difference is discretisation) and the hybrid median to 0.04 of a step. (5) Reference cell, known Sigma, point estimates minus true score: hybrid median mean -0.6 pp, rmse 5.3 pp, median abs error 4.1 pp; conditional median mean -12 pp, rmse 43 pp, median abs error 4.5 pp; hybrid CI coverage .947.
- **Diagnosis of the heavy-tailed conditional median.** It is not an implementation fault (checks 1, 2, 4): the conditional median is unbiased in the median but its mean error is large because when the lead is small the truncated normal is nearly flat over a long left tail in theta. The hybrid restriction to x ± c_beta sk removes the tail (rmse 43 → 5.3 pp) at the cost of a small conservative bias. `validation_lead.csv` (`validate_cond_lead.py`; known Sigma, exact Gaussian draws, 2500 reps per case) bins replications by the winner's standardised lead (x_k − V−)/sk: in the reference cell 32% of draws have a lead below 0.25 and in that bin the conditional median's error has median −15 pp and mean −38 pp (the heavy tail), the conditional CI has median width 105 pp, while hybrid has mean error −7 pp and width 14 pp; for leads ≥ 0.5 the conditional median is within 3–8 pp of naive and its CI is 14–24 pp wide. With one clearly better configuration (+5 pp, K=16, tau 0) the lead is typically ≥ 1 (n=500: 64% of draws ≥ 1; n=1000: 70% ≥ 2): the winner is the truly best config in 85–98% of draws, conditional and hybrid intervals have the width of the naive interval (n=1000: 6 pp) and at n=1000 the conditional median differs from naive by < 0.5 pp on average. Coverage within a lead bin is not a validity statement (the lead is a function of the data and of x_k); the overall coverage per case is .947–.952. `synth_hybrid_summary.csv` was regenerated with the hybrid point estimate = hybrid median (`analysis/run_hybrid.py`; hybrid bias −0.2 … −1.7 pp, mean −0.8 pp across the 27 cells).

### Fresh-evaluation-set protocol and equal-item-budget comparison (`analysis/run_fresh.py`, `analysis/make_fresh_results.py`)
- **Protocols.** `insample` (items consumed = n_tune): tune and report on the same n_tune items with naive / boot_opt / boot_632 / boot_oob / simul / hybrid / 5-fold crossfit / 50-50 split (split reports on half of the n_tune items, so the config it ships is chosen on the other half). `fresh` (items = n_tune + m_fresh): tune on n_tune items, ship the winner, report its accuracy on m_fresh *new* items (Wilson interval); n_tune/m_fresh pairs 50/150, 67/133, 100/100, 133/67, 150/50 (N_tot = 200) and 100/300, 133/267, 200/200, 267/133, 300/100 (N_tot = 400). Equal-total-items comparison: compare `fresh` rows with `insample` rows at the same `items` (200, 400).
- **Truth per row.** Every row is scored against the true accuracy of the configuration *that protocol shipped* (`mu_ship`; differs by protocol because the search saw different data): synthetic = exact pool mean, real = accuracy on the pool items the protocol did not use (disjoint arm). `bias` = mean(reported − that truth) with `bias_se`, `rmse`, `medae` = median absolute error, `cover`/`cover_se`, `width`, `regret` = best candidate's true accuracy minus `mu_ship`, `reps`.
- `fresh_protocol_summary.csv` — synthetic, 18 cells (tau {0,.01,.03} x K {4,16,64} x rho {.5,.9}; m = .3; M = 600 replications per cell/protocol). Reference cell (tau .01, K 16, rho .5), N_tot = 200: boot_oob rmse 2.9 pp, bias −0.1 pp, coverage .975; fresh 100/100 rmse 4.5 pp, bias −0.2 pp, coverage .95, width 17.6 pp; split rmse 4.6 pp; naive bias +4.5 pp, coverage .76. All-in-tuning protocols ship a slightly better config (`mu_ship` .3046 vs .3034 for fresh 100/100) and report it with a smaller error; the fresh-set protocol has no bias by construction but its error is the sampling error of m_fresh items, so more items for tuning (which also lowers the gap) give a smaller rmse at the same N_tot.
- `fresh_protocol_real.csv` — same on the real correctness matrix (K = 1, 2, 4 and all cached configurations; disjoint arm; M = 400–500). Regenerated with each real matrix refresh (K column).
- `ranking_equal_budget_summary.csv` — ranking of 5 tuned systems (K = 16 each, or K = 4/8/16/32/64) at equal item budgets 200 and 400: Kendall tau_b between reported and true ranking, top-1 accuracy, share of pairs ordered wrongly among those >= 1 pp apart, true range of the five systems. In the synthetic setting (true spread of the five systems ~2.6 pp) every protocol ranks poorly (tau_b .13–.33); the fresh-set protocol is not better than the in-sample ones at equal budget (it consumes items for the fresh half). In the unequal-K design (K = 4…64) naive and simul have the highest tau_b (.33 at 400 items). At tau_gen = 0 (no true differences) all tau_b are ≈ 0 (−.03…+.09).

## Real LLM x MMLU-Pro (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, fp32 CPU; MMLU-Pro revision b189ec76…)
Scale: **K = 60 of 60 planned configurations** (instruction wording x demonstration set x layout, `code/config_pool.json`), each complete on all 2000 questions (`matrix_composition.csv`, `compute_log.json` state the count and items). All analysis files below are computed from this one matrix (`matrix.npz`: mean accuracy .182, sd across configurations 1.16 pp, range 15.2–20.8%, mean pairwise item correlation phi .35). The files that earlier carried the interim 21-configuration snapshot (K = 21: sd 1.45 pp, phi .36) were regenerated; numbers quoted from that snapshot elsewhere are superseded.
- `matrix.npz` — `Y` (configs x items, 0/1 correct), `logp` (float16 log-probabilities of the option letters, original option indices), `cfg_idx` (index into `code/config_pool.json`), `qidx` (test row positions), `answer`, `category`. Produced by `analysis/assemble.py` from `code/score.py` output.
- `matrix_composition.csv` — one row per complete configuration: instruction wording, demo set, layout, pool accuracy and s.e. `matrix_spread.csv` — sd/min/max of accuracy across configurations and mean pairwise item-level correlation.
- `real_summary.csv` — replay of tuning searches on the cached matrix (`analysis/run_real.py`, summarised by `analysis/make_real_results.py`; columns as in the plan summary, per (pool, truth_arm, proc, n, K, est_name)). `truth_arm = disjoint`: tuning items T drawn without replacement from the 2000 items, truth = the selected configuration's accuracy on the remaining items (primary, n <= 400); `truth_arm = iid`: T drawn i.i.d. with replacement, truth = accuracy over the whole pool (check, n up to 800). Procedures: argmax (exhaustive), sh (successive halving), sr (successive rejects); greedy coordinate search (all 60 configurations, a 6 x 5 x 2 grid of instruction x demonstration set x layout levels) is included. 1000 replications per cell; the baseline for claims is configuration 0/instr 0, demo set 1, plain layout when present, else the first configuration.
- `fresh_protocol_real.csv` is computed from the same matrix (K = 1, 2, 4, 60; disjoint arm; 500 replications). At N_tot = 200 (K=60): naive bias +4.5 pp (coverage .65), boot_oob −0.05 pp (rmse 2.7 pp, coverage .944), boot_opt +1.2 pp, hybrid −0.5 pp (rmse 5.0 pp, coverage .966), fresh 100/100 bias +0.05 pp (rmse 3.8 pp, coverage .95), split rmse 3.9 pp; as in the synthetic study the fresh set is unbiased but its error is the sampling error of the fresh items, so the in-sample out-of-bag bootstrap has the smaller rmse at equal item budget.
- **Real replay, headline (K = 60, argmax, disjoint arm, n = 200, 1000 reps; `real_summary.csv`)**: naive gap +4.2 pp (bias_se .07), coverage .709, claim rate .29 (false-claim rate .245); boot_oob −0.3 pp, coverage .966; boot_opt +1.0 pp, coverage .964; boot_632 +1.3 pp; crossfit −0.1 pp but coverage .843; simul coverage .988; hybrid −0.85 pp (rmse 5.2 pp, coverage .966, the hybrid median-unbiased estimate); cond coverage .953 but rmse 40 pp (heavy tail, see the validation section). Naive gap vs K at n = 200: +0.0 (K=1), 1.1 (2), 2.1 (4), 2.8 (8), 3.4 (16), 3.8 (32), 4.2 (60) pp (bias_se <= .09 pp); at K = 60 the gap is 9.3, 6.4, 4.2, 2.8 pp at n = 50, 100, 200, 400 (iid arm 9.0, 6.2, 4.1, 2.6, 1.6 pp at n = 50…800). The shipped configuration's true accuracy is 18.85% against 18.2% for a random configuration (+0.6 pp) and 2.0 pp below the best configuration of the pool (`regret`). Adaptive searches at n = 200, K = 60: successive halving gap +3.0 pp (naive coverage .83, claim rate .18), successive rejects +3.65 pp (.76), greedy coordinate search +3.4 pp (.78), against +4.2 pp (.71) for exhaustive argmax — the cheaper searches shrink the gap but do not remove it.
- `real_gaussian_max_check.csv` (`analysis/real_theory.py`) — the Gaussian-max prediction of the naive best-of-K gap computed from the real matrix's empirical covariance (Sigma = cov of the 0/1 item matrix / n; M = 5000 draws; K = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 60 random subsets, n = 50/100/200/400 (+800 iid)) next to the replayed argmax gap: `pred_gap` (`pred_mc_se`), `replay_gap` (`replay_se`), `diff`, `z`. disjoint arm: truth = accuracy on the pool items not used, prediction with the finite-population factor N/(N−n) applied. 63 cells; |diff| <= 0.36 pp; 6 cells have |z| > 2 (z −2.5 … +2.9; at K = 60 the prediction is below the replay in the cells that differ, e.g. n = 50: 8.9 vs 9.3 pp); over the 45 cells with K >= 4 the prediction is on average 2.1% below the replay (range −6.9% … +2.4%). At n = 200, K = 60: 4.17 pp predicted, 4.18 pp replayed.
- `generator_*.csv`, `synthetic_phi_map.csv` (`analysis/calibrated_generator.py`) — a synthetic generator matched to the real matrix and its gap against the real replay. `synthetic_phi_map.csv`: mean pairwise 0/1 item correlation phi of two configurations of the Gaussian-copula generator as a function of the accuracy m and the latent rho (exact): at m = .195, rho = .3/.5/.7/.9 give phi = .16/.29/.45/.69; at m = .3, .18/.32/.48/.70. The plan grid's rho = .5 and .9 therefore bracket the real matrix (mean phi .36, latent rho ≈ .59; pairwise phi .01–.92). `generator_calibration.csv`: real vs generator accuracy mean/sd (the generator uses the real accuracies as thresholds; .182 and 1.16 pp vs .182 and 1.17 pp), mean pairwise phi (latent rho .58 reproduces .348 vs .344), sd of item difficulty (.232 vs .231) and the item-count profile (the generator puts too little mass on items solved by 0 or by all 60 configurations: 23% vs 27% and 0.2% vs 1.2%); `generator_pairwise_fit.csv`: pairwise phi — the one-rho copula cannot reproduce the heterogeneity of the real pairwise phi (max abs error .57, rms .28) — and the variant `tetrachoric` that matches every pair (max error .053, rms .011; 19 eigenvalues of the tetrachoric matrix had to be clipped to make it positive semi-definite). `generator_vs_real_gap.csv`: 10 pools x 300 replications per generator, argmax naive gap at n = 50…800, K = 1…60, per truth arm, z against the real replay: the single-rho copula overstates the real gap by 6.8% on average (K >= 4; range −9.7 … +16.4%; 31 of 63 cells |z| > 2, all with the generator above the real gap), the pairwise-matched copula understates it by 1.3% on average (range −14 … +4%; 4 of 63 cells |z| > 2, three of them with the generator below). What the Gaussian copula cannot match: heterogeneity of pairwise dependence (near-duplicate and near-independent configuration pairs) and the item-count profile at the extremes.
- `compute_log.json` — package versions, seeds, matrix scale, job running times (seconds of world running time as measured by the resource; cpu-hours reported as an upper bound).
