# Timed development pilot (not target evaluation)

The local-compute job `job-dca1acc15c` ran `bash scripts/run_pilot.sh` successfully. Three prechosen datasets cover a small binary instruction matrix, a small HELM set, and the largest supplied matrix: IFEval, GSM and MMLU-Pro. Budgets were 10, 30 and 100, one uniform item draw per budget (nested in the same ordering). Ridge and crossfit-difference used a development grid of regularization 0.1, 1, 10, 100 and 1000. No locked target rows were scored. Raw validation metrics and timings are in `results/pilot/metrics.csv`; split row indices and IRT convergence are in `results/pilot/diagnostics.json`.

Both the source-mean difference and cross-fitted correction are implemented. The exact finite-population design-unbiasedness check passes exhaustive ordered samples at budgets 2, 3 and 4 on a deliberately mismatched source/target case. This is a mathematical implementation check, not empirical performance evidence.

Measured fitting/selection times: response-vector k-means across three budgets took 0.231 s on IFEval, 0.154 s on GSM, and 8.818 s on MMLU-Pro. The MMLU-Pro Rasch fit plus prediction took approximately 17 s (the pilot CSV includes the same fit cost in each budget's row, so summing those rows triple-counts it); its optimizer converged in 125 iterations. Ridge and crossfit-difference calls across all three budgets and five alphas totaled 0.049 s and 0.103 s respectively on MMLU-Pro. These are observed pilot timings in the shared one-thread CPU environment, not guaranteed final runtimes.

The pilot does not establish an advantage for the candidate. For example, at 30 items validation MAE on MMLU-Pro is 4.557 percentage points for the best candidate alpha, versus 3.071 for the best ridge alpha and 3.930 for cluster anchors. At 100 items on IFEval it is 2.176 versus 2.011 for ridge and 1.974 for regularized Rasch. These grid minima are optimistically selected on validation outcomes; final tuning must use separate development item draws and target evaluation must remain untouched. Small GSM validation samples show larger variability and little consistent correction benefit. No method has been abandoned; a null or negative candidate result remains scientifically useful.

A full grid of 13 datasets, 3 budgets, at least 20 item draws and the implemented baselines is computationally practical at the measured timings (planning estimate, not yet run). Stronger literature-faithful IRT comparisons, validation-selected blending/ablation, model-family shift, source size and response-deduplication sensitivities remain to be settled with the plan and context owners before the full protocol is frozen.
