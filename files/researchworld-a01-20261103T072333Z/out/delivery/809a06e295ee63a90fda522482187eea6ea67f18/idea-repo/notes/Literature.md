## The supported positioning

The permitted literature already supplies multi-task benchmarks, task-selection fragility, ordinal/voting-based evaluation, contamination mechanisms, leaderboard distortion mechanisms, and strategic developer models. Therefore avoid any statement that this study introduces social choice to benchmark evaluation, first discovers incentives to target benchmarks, or establishes that score inflation can happen.

The defensible contribution description is: **an exact, reproducible comparison of the minimum breadth of target-only task-score interventions needed for first place under explicitly stated aggregation rules and gain/ceiling/tie assumptions, on two public score snapshots, with paired cohorts and composition/representation sensitivity**. This describes what the organization actually computes; it makes no priority claim. The independence restriction precludes a comprehensive novelty comparison with the withheld prior work, and we must not infer what it found. “First,” “unprecedented,” “previously unstudied,” and “no prior systematic analysis” are not justified by this restricted review.

The most useful distinctions for related work are:

- **Benchmark Lottery → composition, not intervention optimization.** Task-selection fragility motivates analyzing composition sensitivity; our estimand conditions on an observed task set and asks how selected target entries can change the winner.
- **Voting-as-Evaluation → rule framework, not proof of sparse-edit resistance.** Aggregators encode different preferences/information and must be evaluated under the actual intervention. Ordinal invariance to monotone per-task transforms does not imply resistance to a target moving upward in many task rankings. Borda is not a proxy for all social-choice methods.
- **Contamination papers → possible score inflation, not a cost calibration.** Score-space headroom cannot establish the examples/compute needed for a chosen gain, independent attainability, no forgetting, or that any leaderboard entrant has trained on test data.
- **Leaderboard Illusion → other strategic mechanisms.** Selective variant publication and uneven pairwise sampling are distinct from holding rival score rows fixed while editing a target's task scores.
- **Leaderboard Incentives → endogenous competition versus fixed-rival reachability.** Our exact support minimization is a conditional best-targeting calculation, not a repeated competitive game or equilibrium prediction.
- **Voting bribery → useful formal analogy.** It supplies terms for preferred-candidate, constrained-edit and budget goals, without providing the study's empirical optima or proving its complexity class.

## Analysis and presentation conventions these sources support

1. Give the full score-to-rank map, weighting and tie rules. Plot selected-task count with the task denominator; keep 57-subject versus six-family interpretations distinct. Distinguish equal-task recomputations from published site rankings and the original MMLU weighted summary.
2. Explain why the rules were selected. Cardinal mean/median/geometric/minimum and one ordinal Borda rule give a manageable comparison; they are not an exhaustive social-choice survey and do not test the maximal-lottery recommendations in Lanctot et al.
3. Report score-menu sensitivity with immutable target denominators. Separate attainability, conditional reachable-only comparisons, zero-budget incumbents and common challengers. Result values must come from experiment-repo's actual outputs, not literature tables or these notes.
4. Identify the source of every uncertainty interval: repeated random task orders versus task-composition resampling versus item/model-evaluation error. The latter is not available merely because a leaderboard table was resampled. Do not use significance rhetoric to disguise a descriptive finite-cohort comparison.
5. Put dependence and collateral effects near the intervention definition, not solely in a final caveat. The contamination experiments and strategic-effort model demonstrate why an independently selectable unit-cost score gain is a conditional abstraction, not an observed learning process.
6. A high task-count budget is resistance to this particular sparse intervention; it need not preserve a benchmark's intended values, measure general intelligence, or prevent another manipulation mechanism. No universal best aggregation rule follows.
