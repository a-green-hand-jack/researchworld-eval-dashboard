## Research question and scope

For mu=E[Y], can prespecified nonlinear functions of a frozen scalar score improve confidence-interval width over available scalar prediction-powered methods using exactly the same observed labels and unlabeled scores? How does the answer depend on nonlinear calibration, signal strength, sample size, finite unlabeled information, and validity regime?

The mean is directly relevant to binary prevalence and continuous scientific outcomes; it permits a transparent variance decomposition and direct PPI/PPI++ comparisons. This is a study of score-only controls and their estimation costs, not a general solution for every parameter, feature representation, valid interval, or possible use of fixed inputs. No universal optimality or novel-method claim follows from the chosen route.

Within each replicate all procedures receive identical labeled pairs and unlabeled scores from a fixed predictor. Across replicates observations are resampled from the stated population; coverage is not a probability conditional on a single immutable observed dataset. Inferential transformations of existing scores are allowed; no predictor weights are trained or changed, and no external/additional labels or observations are used. Any train/held-out split consumes the original label budget. Evaluation truth and unselected archive labels are unavailable to estimators and tuning.

## Questions, comparisons, and evidence gates

Q1: Does nonlinear score information reduce width beyond scalar PPI++? Compare cross-fitted cubic vs the pinned implementation of PPI++, alongside label-only and unit PPI. Include unrestricted scalar and cross-fitted linear controls so scalar coefficient clipping or nuisance estimation cannot be mistaken for nonlinear information gains. Keep degree fixed in advance; evaluation labels do not select methods or degree. Analysis supplies formulas, assumptions, implementations, diagnostics, uncertainty, and measured results. Context supplies source-checked positioning. Its completed assessment says these baselines suffice for the narrow cubic-versus-affine question; see the explicit StratPPI scope decision below.

Q2: When is there no population gain, and when can finite-sample fitting erase a population gain? Include affine conditional mean and no-signal cases, nonlinear cases, increasing n, high-degree and non-cross-fitted diagnostics, heavy tails, and rare outcomes. Diagnostics are not automatically admissible competing valid methods. Present failures and no-gain cases regardless of the sign of measured results. The scalar no-loss assertion is limited to the specified nested score-control variance class; finite-sample equal width is not promised.

Q3: Can shortening also be established when nominal coverage has a finite-sample guarantee? Compare bounded-data empirical-Bernstein (EB) label-only, full-label fixed unit-PPI, split-trained scalar and cubic controls, with matched split fixed-PPI diagnostic and exact Clopper-Pearson label-only for binary outcomes. State boundedness, independence, training conditioning, ranges, confidence allocation and clipping requirements. Test both realistic binary examples and the deliberately designed bounded nonlinear high-signal case. Report when conservative bounds and training costs prevent improvement. Do not use a shorter Wald interval as evidence of improvement among guaranteed finite-sample intervals.

Q4: Does the proposed variance explanation agree with experiments? Analysis supplies and checks the deterministic-control variance decomposition, nested projection gain, oracle h*=E[Y|S]/(1+n/N), and finite-N term. Compare calculable leading-order widths with measured large-n behavior and expose deviations at small n. These are class-restricted asymptotic statements. The completed follow-up uses linear/quadratic/null at n=N=2000, 3000 paired repetitions per cell, with seeds/results in results/ratio1/. It checks the finite-N mechanism against the N=10n grid. This is a documented follow-up after initial real/bounded results, not an original-grid preregistration. Claims remain limited to these tested ratios or the explicitly stated variance theory.

## Experimental design and fixed scope

The analysis owner's declared design is adopted: 5000 paired repetitions per cell; n=100,500,2000; N=10n; nominal coverage .95. Seven synthetic regimes: linear Gaussian, quadratic Gaussian, sigmoid Gaussian, no signal, quadratic t3 noise, quadratic Bernoulli, rare Bernoulli. Three real frozen-score archives: galaxies, forest, alphafold from PPI v0.2.3. Additional bounded-quadratic supplement: 3000 paired repetitions, n=2000,10000, N=10n. The subsequent finite-N follow-up adds linear/quadratic/null at n=N=2000, 3000 repetitions each. Preserve scenario IDs, deterministic seeds, all trial outcomes, actual elapsed times and failures. A pilot is a timing/checking milestone, not main evidence. Record whether additions preceded or followed inspection of main outcomes; do not describe already-started work as a preregistration.

Real-data trials draw labeled and unlabeled indices independently with replacement from each finite archived list, with the archived-label average used solely as evaluation truth. They establish behavior for empirical populations of fixed record/score pairs, not natural-population coverage, dataset representativeness, covariate shift robustness, or unseen predictor performance. The archive's available labels outside each draw do not increase any estimator's budget. State this prominently in the paper and captions.

All draws remain in the summaries, including zero-width intervals, failures, undefined quantities and unexpected negative findings. Analysis determines legitimate handling and logs it; presentation cannot silently discard inconvenient results. Missing planned runs remain unfinished requirements, not negative evidence.

## Validity and uncertainty standard

Coverage and width are joint evidence. Coverage is hits/R with a named binomial Monte Carlo interval; width is mean absolute width with Monte Carlo uncertainty. Main relative comparison is ratio of paired mean widths, with paired uncertainty, not the average of replicate ratios. Define every baseline and percent reduction. Preserve replicate alignment. Analysis also provides exploratory undercoverage diagnostics with its declared multiplicity convention.

A Monte Carlo interval containing .95, or a nonsignificant undercoverage test, does not prove validity. A narrow interval with detectable undercoverage is a failure, not a successful width gain. Wald results must be described as asymptotic with finite-sample empirical behavior and diagnostic limits. Finite-sample guarantees require an explicitly justified theorem under the data model; empirical coverage checks the implementation and informativeness but cannot establish a universal guarantee. Conservative overcoverage may reflect a real width cost; show absolute widths as well as ratios. Distinguish target confidence intervals from Monte Carlo uncertainty throughout.
