\section{Problem setting and measures}
\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Practice under study.}
A deployer explains the prediction of a trained model $f$ for an instance $x$ by running a randomised Shapley-value estimator once, with a finite budget, and presents the resulting vector $\hat\phi\in\mathbb{R}^d$ as the reason for the prediction. We call one call of the explainer, with its own random seed, the same model, instance, background set and budget, a \emph{run}.

\paragraph{Value function and reference.}
Throughout, $v(S;x)=\frac1{|\mathcal B|}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B} f(x_S,b_{\bar S})$ is the marginal (interventional) expectation of the predicted probability of the positive class over a fixed background set $\mathcal B$ of 50 training rows \citep{chen2020true}; features are the $d$ original attributes (a one-hot group is masked jointly). The target of every run is the Shapley value $\phi^\ast$ of this game. Where $d\le16$ we compute $\phi^\ast$ exactly by enumeration; for German Credit ($d=20$) we use the mean of $10$ high-budget Kernel SHAP runs with its standard error, checked against enumeration on a subset (Section~\ref{sec:exp}). Because a deployer has no $\phi^\ast$, we also report \emph{run-vs-run} agreement, which needs no reference. The budget is counted in model rows actually evaluated by one explainer call, which is comparable across explainers; wall-clock time is reported separately.

\paragraph{Measures.}
Per instance we compute, over $R$ runs: the relative $\ell_2$ error $\lVert\hat\phi-\phi^\ast\rVert_2/\lVert\phi^\ast\rVert_2$; the probability $p_i^{(k)}$ that the top-$k$ set (by $|\phi|$) of a run equals that of $\phi^\ast$ (``match'') and the analogous probability for two distinct runs; top-$k$ Jaccard; Kendall's $\tau$; rank-biased overlap (RBO, $p=0.9$) \citep{webber2010rbo}; the chance-corrected stability of the $R$ top-$k$ sets \citep{nogueira2018stability}; and the fraction of the reference's top-$k$ attributes whose sign a run flips. Metric names for agreement follow \citet{krishna2022disagreement}. The instance's \emph{margin} is $(|\phi^\ast|_{(k)}-|\phi^\ast|_{(k+1)})/|\phi^\ast|_{(1)}$, the relative gap between the last attribute shown and the first one omitted.

\paragraph{Reliability criterion.}
A per-instance statement such as ``the top-3 set is correct with probability $\ge 0.95$'' cannot be made from $R=30$ runs: even 30/30 matches give a one-sided 95\% Clopper--Pearson lower bound of only $0.905$. Our headline quantity is therefore population-level: the mean of $\hat p_i^{(k)}$ over instances with an instance-cluster bootstrap CI, together with the fraction of instances with $\hat p_i^{(k)}\ge 0.8$ and $\ge0.9$, reported on the grid $k\in\{1,3,5\}$ rather than at one threshold. Headline cells and $k=3$ were fixed in the plan in force when the main grid was launched, which is part of the delivery (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}). Ties in $|\phi|$ are broken by attribute index; a single value $k=3$ is used for every $d$, so cells are compared by what a user reading three attributes would see, not at a fixed fraction of $d$, and the chance level of a match (one over $\binom{d}{3}$: 0.018 at $d=8$, below 0.001 at $d=20$) is far below the values we discuss.

\paragraph{Comparison standards.}
A measure of run-to-run variation is judged against three standards, each computed with the same measure on the same scale: (i) \emph{model-induced} variation, between models retrained on random 80\% subsamples of the training set, explained with the exact value function where $d\le16$ and, for German Credit ($d=20$), with a single Kernel SHAP run of 10\,000 coalitions, in the spirit of predictive multiplicity \citep{marx2020predictive}; (ii) \emph{background-induced} variation under redrawn backgrounds of 50 and 200 rows; (iii) a \emph{null} floor: attributions of random other instances and chance-level random $k$-subsets. Disagreement between different explanation methods \citep{krishna2022disagreement} was planned as a further standard (Kernel versus permutation versus tree-based attributions) but was not run; Kernel and permutation SHAP are compared only through their error and match curves against the same reference. In addition, a \emph{compute-matched} comparison asks whether one run at budget $mB$ beats the mean of $m$ runs at budget $B$.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
The instance is the unit of inference; runs are nested in instances and are never bootstrapped alone. Cells (dataset $\times$ model) are never pooled silently. CIs are percentile intervals from an instance-cluster bootstrap (1000 resamples), and Wilson intervals for fractions of instances. Within a cell the model has one training seed, so statements are conditional on that model; training-seed variation appears only in the retrain standard.
