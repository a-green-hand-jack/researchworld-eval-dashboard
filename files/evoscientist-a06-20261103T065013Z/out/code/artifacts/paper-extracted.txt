REPORTING A SELECTED PROMPT :
WINNER OPTIMISM , F INITE -POOL TARGETS , AND
CONDITIONAL AUDITS
Anonymous authors
Paper under double-blind review
ABSTRACT
A benchmark score used to choose a prompt is a selection statistic, not auto-
matically an honest estimate of the chosen system. We study the discrepancy
with known-truth experiments and a newly collected response cache: eight frozen
prompts, 600 MMLU-Pro questions, and one supplied LLM endpoint. The central
distinction is between a selected configuration’s population accuracy, its accuracy
on a fixed cohort, and its accuracy on the unselected remainder. These targets re-
quire different reference quantities. In the primary equal-quality simulation, win-
ner optimism is 6.51 percentage points and a nominal 95% Wilson interval covers
only 83.95%; a fresh exact audit covers 96.60%. In the real fixed-cohort replay,
optimism is 2.32 points and the corresponding coverages are 95.90% and 96.35%:
optimism does not force undercoverage in every setting. A finite-population audit
can be mapped exactly back to cohort accuracy and paired baseline improvement.
We give its conditional guarantee, including joint confidence allocation, and doc-
ument a boundary setting in which a correlation-aware multiplier approximation
fails. The empirical evidence is a fixed-family case study, not a population guaran-
tee for this endpoint or a validation of arbitrary adaptive optimizers. All responses,
seeds, and analyses are released.
1 I NTRODUCTION
Automatic prompt selection creates a statistical dependency that ordinary benchmark reporting often
hides. A researcher evaluates several configurations, chooses the highest-scoring one, and reports its
score on the same questions. Even if every candidate has exactly the same true accuracy, selection
can turn sampling noise into an apparent gain. The chosen system may genuinely improve, but a
reused score alone cannot establish the size or even the sign of that improvement on new questions.
There are two distinct problems: estimating accuracy and deciding whether the selected system im-
proves over a baseline. A confidence interval for one fixed prompt need not cover a prompt chosen
using those same answers. Conversely, replacing a reused score with an independent-looking hold-
out score is insufficient unless the holdout’s target and access history are specified. In a finite bench-
mark, an audit drawn without replacement from the unselected questions estimates the remainder,
not the original cohort. The distinction matters most when selection consumes a substantial fraction
of the available questions.
This paper is an estimand-explicit statistical case study rather than a new general theory of post-
selection inference. Its contributions are: (i) a controlled measurement of optimism, interval
coverage, and false promotion under prompt dependence, candidate count, sample size, and true-
quality gaps; (ii) a finite-pool reporting recipe that combines exact conditional auditing, a known-
contribution transformation, and paired baseline comparisons; and (iii) an independently collected
real-LLM cache exposing both the practical scale and limitations of these procedures. The under-
lying statistical ingredients are standard. The useful contribution is their explicit sampling contract,
executable comparison, and stress testing rather than a claim to have invented sample splitting or the
winner’s curse.

one endpoint whose model alias cannot be independently authenticated. Its complete cache supports

2 R ELATED WORK
Prompt optimization and evaluation.Shi et al. (2024) connect fixed-budget prompt selection to
best-arm identification, emphasizing selection quality under a budget. That optimization objective
differs from inference about the accuracy of the configuration ultimately selected. Polo et al. (2024)
estimate the distribution of performance across prompt variants, whereas the target here is the par-
ticular data-selected configuration. Sclar et al. (2024) document sensitivity to meaning-preserving
prompt-format changes and motivate reporting performance over plausible formats. Sensitivity and
selection bias are different: true prompt-quality variation can exist without reused-score optimism,
and optimism can exist with identical true qualities. MMLU-Pro expands answer choices and em-
phasizes more challenging reasoning questions (Wang et al., 2024); it provides the real item frame
for this case study.
Adaptive reuse and simultaneous inference.Dwork et al. (2015) study validity under adaptively
chosen analyses, and Blum & Hardt (2015) address adaptive leaderboard reporting. Our fixed-
family selection-only bound is much narrower than those general adaptive settings. A family-size
bound cannot be applied merely by counting candidates generated after seeing the selection data. In
contrast, a properly isolated fresh audit can condition on an arbitrarily complicated pre-audit search
history. We do not implement or claim a replacement for privacy-based reusable holdouts or the
Ladder.
Correlation-aware approximation. Gaussian multiplier methods approximate maxima of vector
sums under conditions studied by Chernozhukov et al. (2013). This motivates sharing a resampling
weight across the full vector of prompt outcomes for one item. It does not establish finite-sample
validity for small, nearly degenerate correctness tables. We therefore compare the approximation
to conservative simultaneous concentration and exact audit inversion, and explicitly retain its fail-
ures. Related-work statements here are grounded in the directly consulted abstracts of the permitted
sources, not a comprehensive literature survey.

4.2 P AIRED IMPROVEMENTS AND TWO HEADLINE CLAIMS
On the same audit items, count D+ = 1{YJ = 1, Y0 = 0} and D− = 1{YJ = 0, Y0 = 1} against
the immutable baseline. Their marginal proportions q+, q− satisfy ∆ = q+ − q−. Given two exact
marginal intervals each with failure budget β/2, an interval for improvement is
I∆ = [L+ − U−, U+ − L−]. (6)
The discordance counts need not be independent: Bonferroni suffices. In the finite cohort, apply
the analogue of equation (5) using the observed selection difference. For a self-comparison the
difference and its interval are exactly zero. Promote only if the lower endpoint for the named target
exceeds zero. False promotion when the true selected-minus-baseline effect is nonpositive implies
noncoverage, and is therefore controlled by β under the audit contract.
Two separately valid 95% intervals do not constitute a joint 95% report. Our final replay report
card assigns αa = 0.025 to accuracy and α∆ = 0.025 to improvement. Each discordance marginal
consequently uses failure budget 0.0125. The two headline claims have joint coverage at least 95%.
Method-comparison experiments instead assess separate marginal 95% accuracy and improvement
claims; they do not assert a joint 95% guarantee for every entry or grid cell.
4.3 A PPROXIMATE AND NO -AUDIT ALTERNATIVES
We compare six accuracy procedures: naive winner Wilson; simultaneous Hoeffding; a Gaussian
row-multiplier maximum; a guarded multiplier; audit Wilson; and exact audit inversion. The mul-
tiplier centers each selection column, shares Gaussian weights across items’ prompt vectors, and
takes the 95th percentile of the maximum absolute mean deviation. It uses sample-variance scaling
and, for a fixed cohort, finite-population factor
p
1 − n/N. A covariance eigendecomposition gen-
erates the identical conditional Gaussian distribution efficiently. Its radius isapproximate, including
with the finite-population factor. The guard uses Hoeffding if n <64 or any column has fewer than
five successes or failures; otherwise it uses the multiplier. This is a heuristic, not a new coverage
theorem. Both audit intervals are mapped to cohort accuracy in real-cache comparisons, ensuring
like-target comparisons.

wider but does not require post-selection approximation.

6.3 D ECISIONS AND THE REPORTING CONTRACT
Under the all-equal primary simulation, a naive paired normal interval computed on selection an-
swers falsely promotes a winner in 10.85% of repetitions; the exact paired audit rule falsely promotes
in 0.10%. This is an unconditional false-promotion probability against the predeclared baseline, not
false discovery rate. The exact paired construction is intentionally conservative: low false promo-
tion can coexist with low power. In the real-cache primary setting, the analogous false-promotion
frequencies relative to exact finite-cohort effects are 0.00% and 0.00%; these are conditional replay
quantities and depend on which winners have nonpositive true effects. In fact P0 is the lowest-
accuracy prompt on this cache: the nonpositive-effect cases are self-comparisons. The zero real
false-promotion frequency is therefore not a real-data type-I stress test; the equal-quality simula-
tions supply that evidence.
A single precommitted replay illustrates the report format. Its winner is P5, selection score 87.50%,
audit score 83.59%, and exact cohort reference 84.50%. The joint-95% report-card accuracy inter-
val is [77.33,89.17]%, and its paired cohort-improvement interval is [-3.67,9.50] pp (true cached
difference 2.33 pp). Audit discordances are 6 positive and 2 negative. This is explicitly a replay
illustration: the investigator could access the complete cache. It is not evidence that a prospec-
tively blinded audit was operationally enforced. A guarantee for future deployment requires that
enforcement, not just reuse of this report format.
7 D ISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS
What can be concluded. A selected score and an honest performance report answer different
questions. The studied gap can change decisions even when there is no true improvement. Its
size depends jointly on search breadth, prompt-error dependence, item count, true-quality gaps, tie
handling, and the target. Our reporting recommendation is to disclose the reused score as a selection
statistic, identify the frozen winner and complete search history, publish a fresh audit with a named
sampling target, and use a paired comparison if claiming improvement. If no new items are available,
a conservative simultaneous interval is defensible only for an independently fixed encompassing
family; a narrow approximate interval should not be substituted without qualifications.

8 C ONCLUSION
Reusing the data that selected a configuration changes the interpretation of its score. Known-truth
simulations demonstrate optimism, undercoverage, and false promotion; one real LLM response
cache confirms a finite-cohort discrepancy under a modest fixed-family search. Conditional ex-
act auditing offers a transparent alternative, provided the access contract, finite-versus-population
target, and joint confidence budget are respected. It trades additional evaluation and potentially
conservative intervals for interpretable evidence, rather than promising a universal correction to an
automatically optimized score.
