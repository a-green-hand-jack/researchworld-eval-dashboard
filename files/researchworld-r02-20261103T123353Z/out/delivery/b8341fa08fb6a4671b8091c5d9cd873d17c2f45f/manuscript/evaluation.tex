\section{Target and evaluation framework}
\label{sec:framework}
\subsection{Whole-vector coverage and finite-rank calibration}
The observed response lies in
\begin{equation}
\simplex=\{y\in\R^K:y_j\geq0,\ \textstyle\sum_{j=1}^K y_j=1\}.
\end{equation}
Separate training data determine a predictor $m(x)$ and a measurable scalar nonconformity score $s(x,y)$, without fitting or tuning on calibration responses. For calibration scores $S_1,\ldots,S_n$ and error level $\alpha\in(0,1)$, define
\begin{equation}
 k=\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil,\qquad
 q=\begin{cases}S_{(k)},& k\leq n,\\+\infty,& k=n+1,\end{cases}
 \qquad C_\alpha(x)=\{y\in\simplex:s(x,y)\leq q\}.
 \label{eq:rank}
\end{equation}
Under exchangeable calibration/new scores, this gives marginal whole-vector coverage at least $1-\alpha$ \citep{angelopoulos2021gentle}. Ties use $\leq$; an infinite threshold denotes the full domain. Conditional on independent training, the guarantee averages over calibration and a future observation; it does not hold for every realized calibration set or feature value. Appendix~\ref{app:rank} derives the bound and the narrower IID continuous-score calibration-variability reference.

Coordinatewise events are separate. Calibrating each coordinate at error $\alpha$ supplies no joint $1-\alpha$ guarantee; allocating $\alpha/K$ gives a Bonferroni joint guarantee by the union bound, without coordinate independence. Intersecting an ambient region with the simplex preserves coverage because the target lies there.

\subsection{Reliability, efficiency, and uncertainty}
For independent fitted/calibrated trial $r$ with $M_r$ test observations, let $I_{ri}=\mathbb1\{Y_{ri}\in C_{\alpha,r}(X_{ri})\}$. Report
\begin{equation}
\widehat c_r=M_r^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{M_r}I_{ri},\qquad
\overline c=R^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^R\widehat c_r,
\label{eq:coverage}
\end{equation}
and the corresponding group estimates with their actual denominators. Mean precision and across-trial spread describe different properties: a precise average does not imply stability of one calibration. Tests sharing a fit and threshold are not independent replicates for an unconditional pooled binomial interval \citep{angelopoulos2021gentle}. Methods share partitions and use paired trial differences; Section~\ref{sec:methods} specifies the outer-trial and numerical uncertainty procedures.

Efficiency uses a common original-space measure,
\begin{equation}
 a_r(x)=\frac{\operatorname{vol}_{K-1}(C_{\alpha,r}(x))}
 {\operatorname{vol}_{K-1}(\simplex)},\qquad
 A=\mathbb E_r\mathbb E_{X\sim P_{\rm test}}[a_r(X)].
 \label{eq:area}
\end{equation}
Ambient $K$-dimensional volume is zero for all simplex regions; raw radii in different geometries are also incomparable. Compare common area alongside achieved joint coverage: an under-covering smaller region does not establish better calibrated efficiency. Location subsets must represent the declared test-$X$ distribution and use test-group weights, especially under shift. Location sampling and integration error remain distinct uncertainty sources. Zero integration hits do not establish zero area, and unresolved numerical distinctions do not support rankings. Full-dimensional area assigns zero measure to faces, so it cannot alone describe informativeness for face-supported or atomic responses.
