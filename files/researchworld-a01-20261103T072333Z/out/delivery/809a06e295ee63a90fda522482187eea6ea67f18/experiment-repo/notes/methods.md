# Counterfactual analysis specification (working)

Input is a complete matrix X in [0,1], one row per evaluation and one column per task. Rivals' scores are held fixed. A target selects a subset S; selected scores become x_j + alpha (u_j - x_j), other scores remain x_j. Unit task cost gives k=|S| and k/n. The primary idealized upper envelope is alpha=1, u_j=1. Sensitivities use alpha=0.5, 0.25, 0.1 and u_j=max_i X_ij. These are explicit score interventions, not measured contamination effects, train examples, monetary costs, or causal training estimates. Task costs and cross-task transfer are unobserved.

Primary outcome is minimum k for co-first, permitting ties within absolute tolerance 1e-10. Strict-first is separate. Rules give equal task weight: arithmetic mean, conventional median (even n averages the middle pair), geometric mean (actual product root, zero permitted), minimum score and Borda (sum across tasks of the average ascending rank minus one, ties get half a point in pairwise comparisons). Higher is better for all. Open LLM primary uses published normalized score columns /100, raw scores form a sensitivity. HELM uses EM. Same target cohort is used for paired comparisons; zero-cost existing winners remain in the denominator and will also be separated from challengers. Infeasible means no allowed subset succeeds and differs from solver failure or an unresolved optimum.

For n<=10 all 2^n subsets are evaluated, with rivals' Borda scores updated when the target crosses them. For odd-n median count how many scores already meet the strongest rival's median and how many can be raised above that threshold; the deficit is the optimum. Minimum requires every below-threshold score to be upgraded. Arithmetic and positive geometric means reduce to sorting additive score or log-score gains; zeros are forced upgrades for a positive geometric threshold. Borda uses binary MILP constraints, one per relevant rival: sum_j [g_j+d_ij]z_j >= B_i-B_t, where d_ij is the increase in target pairwise task points against rival i, and g_j=sum_i d_ij. MILP status and bounds are retained. Rivals initially farther than n points below the maximum cannot become the strongest rival, since no rival can lose more than n points. Every returned subset is independently checked against the actual aggregation outcome.

Random-selection comparison chooses tasks in a uniformly random order until co-first is reached. For six tasks it is computed exactly over all subsets: the probability at each k is the fraction of k-subsets that succeed, and E[min(T,n+1)]=sum_{k=0}^n P(T>k). For 57 tasks it is estimated with seeded independent random permutations; output records the replicate count. The n+1 value is an explicit censoring code for infeasible runs, not a cost. For every requested random baseline, provably infeasible targets contribute the exact stopping value n+1 to the full-cohort expectation, with no Monte Carlo sampling required. The stored `random_requested` and `random_baseline_method` distinguish exact infeasibility, exhaustive subsets, Monte Carlo permutations and unrequested baselines. Success probability at the target-specific optimal k is undefined on infeasible targets: its stored value remains null, and summaries explicitly label this quantity reachable-only and report its nonnull denominator. Summary `random_expected_censored_k_n` must equal the full cohort for a requested baseline; `random_success_at_optimum_n` equals the reachable denominator. Unrequested strict/raw/unique baselines remain null and have denominator zero. Primary descriptive fractions refer to the finite observed cohort, not a random sample of all models. Task-deletion or resampling analyses describe dependence on benchmark composition, not test-example confidence or population generalization. The data do not provide sampling uncertainty of model scores; no fake test-set standard errors will be inferred from model-to-model dispersion.
