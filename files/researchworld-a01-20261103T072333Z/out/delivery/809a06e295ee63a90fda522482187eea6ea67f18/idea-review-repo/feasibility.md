## Risks that must bound the claims

1. **Investment is an intervention-menu cost.** For a target coordinate `x_j`, selection changes it to `x_j + alpha (u_j - x_j)`; rivals and unselected coordinates stay fixed. Cost is the number selected, with equal task cost. A score table supplies no training dose-response, task acquisition cost or evidence that these independent changes are attainable. Transfer could help several tasks or damage unselected ones. Therefore the computed task count is neither a measured real-world training cost nor a general lower bound on it. Necessary correction: use “minimum selected-task budget under the intervention model” and state the omitted mechanisms prominently.
2. **Perfect scores are optimistic and scale-dependent.** `alpha=1, u=1` is an idealized menu; attainability is not measured. Normalized and raw Open LLM coordinates imply different menus as well as different cardinal aggregates. The supplied raw-score sensitivity changes the minimum rule's median budget from 3 to 5 tasks. Necessary correction: retain bounded-gain and observed-best ceilings, report unreachable targets on the original fixed cohort, and interpret normalization results as representation/intervention sensitivity rather than an isolated causal rule effect.
3. **The task unit is not portable.** A HELM subject and an Open LLM task have different granularity, size and dependence. Dividing by the task count does not convert them into equal training costs. Necessary correction: describe these as controlled alternative-rule recomputations, not necessarily deployed leaderboard policies; distinguish equal-weight HELM subject aggregation from the site’s own aggregate weighting; compare rules within each frozen benchmark; identify counts and fractions explicitly; qualify any cross-benchmark pattern. Heterogeneous training costs are unobserved. A weighted-cost extension is optional and must not use the equal-cost greedy proof unchanged.
4. **The rival population matters.** First place is relative to the fixed observed rival matrix. Borda additionally depends on the entire rival cohort and must update rivals' points when the target improves. Necessary correction: use common challengers (remove the union of rule-specific incumbents), keep the deduplicated-evaluation sensitivity, and state that published entries are evaluations rather than necessarily independent models. No universal robustness ordering or recommendation follows from cohort medians.
5. **Winning and computation are different events.** Co-first permits ties; strict first can fail under a ceiling. Missing/timeout/unresolved optima are not mathematically unreachable targets, and zero-cost incumbents are not attacks requiring no effort for a challenger. Necessary correction: retain the tie/tolerance definition, strict sensitivity, status audit and fixed-cohort reachability curves. A Borda solver certificate and full-reranking check are needed; freezing opponent ranks is invalid.
6. **Uncertainty has a specific source.** These are descriptive statistics of frozen published matrices. Random-order intervals quantify Monte Carlo error; task resampling/deletion quantifies composition sensitivity and has a weak exchangeable-task interpretation for correlated subjects. Neither supplies test-example uncertainty or population-model confidence. Necessary correction: label these separately, avoid score confidence intervals inferred from model dispersion, and acknowledge unmeasured uncertainty of the upstream scores.
7. **Novelty and reporting are not established by solver completion.** The public score study is feasible even if its empirical ordering is conditional or mixed. Necessary correction: ground claims in the context owner's verified readings, never seek the withheld paper or its discussion, keep an access/incident statement, and avoid unverified “first” claims. Source scores have no separately declared data license; do not treat model licenses or code licenses as data permissions.
