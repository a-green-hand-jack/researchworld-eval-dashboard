## Reproduction and validity

`README.md` contains the complete commands, environment versions, seeds and source map. `src/validate_outputs.py` checks 296,064 full primary/sensitivity attack rows and the completion of 63,200 task-subset attacks. Full attack rows have only certified-optimal or infeasible statuses; there are no bounded or unresolved rows. Per-target witnesses are re-evaluated against all competitors. Small-instance tests independently enumerate all subsets, including zeros, ties, partial uplift, observed frontiers, odd/even median and Borda's changes to opponents. Input and result hashes are in `data/manifest.json` and `results/analysis_validation.json`. Pilot data, completed run times and resource records are retained. No failed numerical run or abandoned fitting attempt occurred; no model training or inference was performed. During implementation, the task-resampling diagnostic was changed from a draft with replacement to distinct-subject subsets before executing it, because duplicate copies should not be independently attackable training tasks.

Primary population: every complete entry in HELM MMLU v1.13.0 (79) and every complete unflagged Open LLM entry in the pinned contents snapshot (4,575). Tasks are respectively 57 subjects and six published task aggregates, which are different units of task coverage. Multiple entries from the same model family are not independent experimental replications. All 57 duplicated HELM subject-table representations agree; no score imputation is used. The Open LLM flagged, repeated-score and repeated-fullname variants are explicitly retained as sensitivities.

## Main supported statements

1. **Under perfect-score saturation, strategic task selection can reach first with limited task coverage under cardinal rules, while Borda often requires broad coverage.** For HELM median task counts across entries are mean 16/57, median 21/57, geometric 16/57, Borda 46/57; for Open LLM they are 2/6, 3/6, 3/6, 6/6. These are deterministic finite-snapshot optima. `primary_summary.csv` also reports means, quantiles and rule-specific nonwinner versions; `budget_success_curves.csv` gives full cost-success distributions. `paired_rule_comparisons.csv` prevents different target samples from explaining comparisons; `rank_strata.csv` fixes the same mean-rank strata across rules.
2. **The ordering is conditional on attainable scores.** With each selected score set to the observed task frontier, average K for mean/median changes from 16.6962/19.3165 to 34.0886/19.3165 on HELM, and from 2.36284/2.84962 to 3.87847/3.23847 on Open LLM. Thus the mean-versus-median ordering by average cost reverses in both boards. This does not make observed-frontier scores attainable by every target. `sensitivity_summary.csv`, `paired_sensitivity.csv`, and `sensitivity_budget_curves.csv` show the complete ceiling/uplift/strictness results.
3. **Attainability can dominate optimization.** At half remaining-headroom uplift toward 1, only 40/79 HELM targets can win under mean, 41 under median, 42 under geometric and 32 under Borda. Open LLM fractions differ substantially: 4,423/4,575, 3,428, 4,575 and 1,973 respectively for tied first. Full-frontier and quarter-uplift variants are also evaluated. Do not compare conditional feasible means as if the feasible sets were the same; the paired sensitivity table gives both-feasible/only-one-feasible/both-infeasible counts.
4. **Selecting tasks strategically matters.** For HELM, the average optimal K versus expected K under a uniformly random task order is mean 16.6962 vs27.2314, median 19.3165 vs21.8963, geometric 16.9241 vs29.9356, Borda 42.2278 vs47.8901. For Open LLM the corresponding pairs are 2.36284 vs2.73881, 2.84962 vs3.01891, 2.98317 vs3.94913 and 5.51038 vs5.83079. Six-task random-prefix success probabilities are exact over all subsets. HELM uses 2,000 shared random task orders per target, seed 519733; per-target Wilson intervals quantify Monte Carlo error for success probabilities only. The follow-up `RANDOM_BASELINE_MC_ERROR.md` and `random_mean_mc_summary_helm.csv` quantify the four across-target cost means separately using retained stopping-cost variances; approximate marginal 95% intervals are [27.2213,27.2415], [21.8890,21.9036], [29.9233,29.9480], [47.8834,47.8968]. These concern random-order simulation error for these fixed targets, not model/task/accuracy uncertainty. Source: `random_baseline_*.csv`.
5. **The primary result is stable to substantial subject omission in the declared perturbation model.** Across 200 shared random subsets of 45 distinct HELM subjects, the central 95% ranges for the across-target mean K are mean [12.6690,14.2411], median [14.5437,16.7215], geometric [12.8684,14.5699], Borda [32.7592,34.3304]. The paired mean-minus-median range is [-3.49494,-1.25253] and mean-minus-Borda [-20.7500,-19.2655]. These are sensitivity ranges over random subsets of these 57 subjects, not confidence intervals for future tasks, true accuracies, or independent model samples. Sources: `task_subsets_helm.csv`, `_summary.json`.

## Additional boundary checks

- Strict first versus tied first is computed in every ceiling/uplift condition. Changes are generally small for full saturation but may alter feasibility under partial uplift; report actual configuration-specific results.
- Reintroducing the flagged Open LLM entry changes no shared target's K under any primary rule. Dropping repeated score vectors changes Borda K for 11/4,521 retained targets (all +1); dropping repeated fullnames changes it for 7/4,496 retained targets (3 +1, 4 -1). Cardinal K is unchanged for retained targets in these variants. This establishes stability to these specific removals, not immunity to arbitrary model cloning. Source: `variant_comparisons.csv`.
- Raw versus official-normalized Open LLM columns change K for 1,190/4,575 geometric targets, 458 median targets, 268 mean targets and 64 Borda targets. Maximum absolute changes are one task except median, which reaches two. The published Raw-to-normalized table transformation is not order-preserving within all task aggregates: `normalization_rank_audit.json` finds changed average ranks for BBH, MUSR, GPQA and some MATH entries. Therefore a theorem about invariance to strictly increasing per-column transforms does not apply to this empirical comparison. We do not infer the normalization mechanism from these tables alone.
- Weighting HELM arithmetic mean by evaluation-item counts changes average K from 16.6962 to11.1266 under perfect scores and from34.0886 to33.3165 under observed frontiers, while retaining task-count attack cost. Subject sizes range from100 to1,534. This is an aggregation-weight sensitivity, not a second measurement of training effort. Source: `weighted_mean_helm.csv`.
- Algebraic three-task counterexamples in `DATA_AND_MODEL.md` disprove a universal mean-versus-median resistance ordering. They are mathematical boundary examples and must not be presented as real-board data.

## Claims the evidence cannot support

The analysis does not estimate training data quantities, money, computation, model contamination, or a causal response of scores to training. It assumes no cross-task transfer, no collateral decreases, unilateral action, fixed competitor raw scores and explicit independent task ceilings. Saturation gives an optimistic lower bound on affected-task count only within those assumptions. It is not a lower bound on the number of training datasets if one dataset can improve other tasks. Geometric outcomes depend on score origin/scale; rank aggregation depends on competitor composition. Equal-task and item-weighted rules are different choices. Snapshot populations contain related and merged models; we do not attach model-sampling significance tests to their census summaries. No universal ranking of aggregation-rule resistance, external validity to future benchmarks, or actual exploit effectiveness is established.
