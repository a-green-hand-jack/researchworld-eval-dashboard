# Statistical claims and conditions

Let K configurations be fixed independently of iid search questions X_1,...,X_n. Let Y_ij in [0,1] be the score of configuration j on question i, with mean theta_j, and let j_hat maximize empirical means with a predetermined random tie rule. Within-question scores can be arbitrarily dependent.

Writing M_j=n^{-1} sum_i Y_ij, expectation over independent question draws gives

0 <= E[M_j_hat - theta_j_hat] <= sqrt(log K / (2n)).

Lower bound: E[max_j M_j] >= max_j E[M_j] = max_j theta_j >= E[theta_j_hat]. Upper bound: M_j_hat-theta_j_hat <= max_j(M_j-theta_j). Hoeffding's bounded-variable moment-generating-function bound and log-sum-exp give E[max_j(M_j-theta_j)] <= log(K)/lambda + lambda/(8n); optimize lambda. For K=1 the expectation is zero. This is an expectation bound, not a realized confidence bound, and it can be very loose when candidates' errors are correlated. It does not say optimization fails to improve true quality.

For Bernoulli scores, construct two-sided Clopper–Pearson intervals C_j at marginal error alpha/K. By a union bound, P(all theta_j in C_j)>=1-alpha. Consequently P(theta_j_hat in C_j_hat)>=1-alpha for any rule selecting among that predeclared bank, including a rule that sees the whole search matrix. No independence across candidates is required. The simultaneous bound used for a comparison with the frozen default is valid since both means are covered jointly. A naive selected CP interval only has its fixed-candidate guarantee and need not cover a winner.

For an independent audit of size m scored only after the configuration is frozen, the audit accuracy has conditional mean theta_j_hat, conditional variance theta_j_hat(1-theta_j_hat)/m, and its ordinary CP interval has at least 1-alpha conditional coverage. Thus it is unbiased also after averaging over search datasets. This guarantee requires a fresh iid question sample; deciding to change the configuration after inspecting audit outcomes invalidates that argument.

Real fixed finite-pool resampling is a separate target. When drawing with replacement from a fully scored reference pool, Bernoulli draws are iid conditional on that matrix and the exact reference-pool mean is the truth for that resampling experiment. It is not the model's population accuracy. When comparing winners on a disjoint finite audit pool, observed search-minus-audit differences estimate generalization gaps with audit sampling uncertainty; empirical overlap with an audit interval is not coverage of unknown population accuracy. We will not label such overlap as coverage. If using without-replacement resampling, either report finite-population coverage with appropriate intervals or avoid iid CP claims.

These statements cover a fixed predeclared bank, not an optimizer constructing previously unspecified configurations using the same question outcomes. Counting only the adaptively queried configurations does not justify replacing K with that count in a fixed-bank union bound. A genuinely independent audit remains valid after such adaptive search once the final system is frozen.

## Exact remaining-benchmark intervals for the primary real selection

Let the benchmark have N_total questions and the full search pool be a uniform sample of s questions without replacement. For a fixed bank candidate j, the observed search count x_j is hypergeometric conditional on its total benchmark successes M_j. Invert hypergeometric tails with per-candidate error alpha/K to obtain intervals [L_j,U_j] for M_j/N_total. The union bound covers all bank M_j simultaneously. The unknown accuracy outside the entire search pool is (M_j-x_j)/(N_total-s), so transforming each interval by this known affine map preserves the simultaneous event. The selected configuration can therefore receive the transformed interval with unconditional coverage at least1-alpha over search-pool sampling and selection. Unlike the iid CP statement, this interval exactly matches finite without-replacement sampling and the remaining-question target. It can be conservative and is not asserted to have conditional-on-a-particular-search-pool coverage.

The disjoint audit interval instead conditions on the search pool and frozen winner. Its success count is hypergeometric from the remaining N_total-s questions; exact inversion supplies at least1-alpha conditional coverage. For a paired selected-minus-default difference, count wins and losses on the same audit questions. Each category count is marginally hypergeometric. Intervals at error alpha/2 for each category and subtraction of opposite endpoints give conservative coverage at least1-alpha for the paired contrast, with no independence assumption between categories. Bank-adjusted paired contrasts divide alpha additionally by the number of inspected nondefault contrasts.

For conditional repeated selections sharing the audit pool, the simultaneous candidate audit intervals also bound any weighted mixture of their unknown remaining-benchmark accuracies when the nonnegative weights sum to1 and are determined without audit outcomes. Subtracting that bound from the recorded mean report bounds the mean true gap for that finite Monte Carlo mixture. It does not pretend that an arbitrarily small Monte Carlo SE removes audit uncertainty or that the finite mixture exactly equals the infinite-repetition procedure expectation.

## Runtime-triggered choice of prespecified designs (B/C fallback)

A timing rule can depend on item difficulty and response length even without inspecting correctness. Thus outcome-blind tier selection alone does not justify fixed-tier95% coverage conditional on that trigger, or audit unbiasedness conditional on the trigger. The delivered design uses a conservative union across the two prespecified possible completed designs, B and C. For each fixed design d, form the relevant exact interval at error .05/2=.025; for a bank-simultaneous interval allocate .025/K within that design. Each fixed design has its stated sampling guarantee before conditioning on the time trigger. The probability that either design's event fails is at most .025+.025=.05, with arbitrary dependence between designs. Consequently the interval from whichever design the runtime rule selects has at least95% **unconditional** coverage. No conditional-on-trigger guarantee is asserted. This adjustment applies separately to the selected audit/gap endpoint, paired contrast endpoint, and simultaneous-bank/mixture endpoint; the collection of all scientific endpoints is not jointly95% protected.

For Tier C, retain the fixed even-position subset of the original uniform-permutation partition and first4 predeclared candidates, without using omitted search labels. Conditional on these256 retained search identities, the512 retained audit identities are a uniform sample from the11776-question complement after integrating out the256 omitted original search identities. One way to see this is to view retained search, omitted search and retained audit as fixed disjoint positions in one uniform permutation; after fixing the first group every ordering of the other positions is equally likely. For every fixed retained audit set the number of ways to fill the omitted search positions is the same. Conditional on the larger original512-item search set the audit has a different sampling frame; the delivered11776 target does not condition on that omitted set. The runtime union above avoids claiming that this marginal argument also establishes coverage conditional on selecting C.

Naive/binomial-simultaneous95% search intervals remain labelled comparators for the without-replacement experiment. Known-empirical-cache iid calibration continues to use its original95% methods and exact empirical truth, conditional on the final cache; it is a different experiment. Bootstrap sensitivity intervals remain approximate and make no exact or conditional-on-trigger coverage claim.
