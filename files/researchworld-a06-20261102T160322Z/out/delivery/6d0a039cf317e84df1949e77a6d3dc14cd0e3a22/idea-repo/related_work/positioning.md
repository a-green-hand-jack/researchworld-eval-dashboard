# Related work and positioning (idea-repo, v1)

Provenance: abstracts/metadata of the cited items were read on 2026-11-02 (arXiv API, OpenAlex); full texts not yet. No search was made for the withheld paper or for 2025–2026 work on the reporting of auto-tuned LLM systems (Brief §5); nothing resembling it was met.

## What the established literature says (abstract level)
- **Selection/optimism bias after choosing by validation error.** Guan (arXiv 1801.02817): the minimum validation error "can be biased downward"; proposes refit-free estimates of test error after selection and bootstrap CIs. Same mechanism as our reported-score problem in a model-selection setting; a direct baseline family (post-selection test-error estimators).
- **Adaptive reuse of one dataset.** Dwork et al. (1411.2664; Science 2015): validity of inference fails when analyses adapt to earlier outcomes on the same data; reusable holdout (Thresholdout) restores it. Blum & Hardt (1502.04585): leaderboard "accuracy" of the best submission under adaptive resubmission, Ladder algorithm. These supply (i) a theory vocabulary for adaptivity (number of adaptive queries, stability), (ii) *reporting mechanisms* to compare against as Q2 baselines.
- **Empirical counterweight.** Recht et al. (1902.10811): on fresh test sets accuracy drops 3–15% (CIFAR-10) and 11–14% (ImageNet) but they attribute it to distribution shift to "harder" images, "not caused by adaptivity", and model ranking is roughly preserved. This tells us how to separate the *selection* gap (our object) from *distribution shift* (fresh questions are drawn from the same pool in our design), and that "does ranking survive" is a natural question for Q1(b).
- **Inference on winners.** Andrews–Kitagawa–McCloskey (QJE 2024): estimates and CIs for the best-looking of many; conditional and hybrid (projection/conditional) intervals, median-unbiased estimators. Candidate corrected-reporting method; K candidates with correlated noise match their setup if scores are approximately normal, which for accuracy on n questions is approximately true but needs checking at small n and accuracies near the 10% chance level.
- **Max-type inference.** Chernozhukov–Chetverikov–Kato (1212.6906): Gaussian approximation/multiplier bootstrap for maxima of high-dimensional sums, valid with K ≫ n under conditions; supports a bootstrap over *questions* that preserves the candidates' correlation (paired candidate scores on the same questions).
- **Prompt/program optimisers and their evaluation.** DSPy (2310.03714) and MIPRO (2406.11695): compile programs by optimising a metric on a training/validation set; MIPRO's abstract mentions a stochastic mini-batch evaluation function — i.e. noisy, adaptively chosen evaluations (check full text for their train/dev/test protocol before characterising it). Shi et al. (2402.09723): prompt selection = fixed-budget best-arm identification; PromptEval (2405.17202): estimating performance distribution over many prompt variants from limited evaluations; Sclar et al. (2310.11324): large performance spread from formatting alone. They show the candidate pool has a real spread (so selection matters) and give search algorithms (successive-halving-like, TRIPLE) to include as "search procedures" in the factor study. None of these abstracts addresses the reliability of the *selected* configuration's reported score.
- **Benchmark.** MMLU-Pro (2406.01574): 10 options, 14 categories — use categories for heterogeneity/shift ablations; chance 10%.

## Baselines and remedies the experiments should compare (from the above; plan owner to choose)
1. naive (search-set score); 2. disjoint report split (cost: n_search shrinks); 3. K-fold / nested-CV style; 4. optimism/bootstrap correction (e.g. resample questions, re-run selection, estimate bias; cf. Guan); 5. max-aware / conditional intervals for the winner (Andrews et al.; CCK multiplier bootstrap); 6. reusable-holdout / Ladder-style reporting (Dwork et al.; Blum–Hardt) for adaptive searches.

## Gap our study addresses (as far as the allowed literature shows)
The cited work treats classical model selection, leaderboards or econometric winners; the prompt/program-optimiser papers evaluate their methods but, per their abstracts, do not quantify the reporting gap for auto-tuned LLM systems as a function of search design, nor test remedies for it. A claim of novelty must stay limited to this combination and must be checked against full texts; novelty cannot be asserted relative to the withheld paper, which we do not know.
