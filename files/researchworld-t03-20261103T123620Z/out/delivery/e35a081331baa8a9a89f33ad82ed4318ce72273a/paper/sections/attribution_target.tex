\section{Attribution target and reliability}
\label{sec:target}

Let $f$ be a trained classifier's probability for the selected class, $x$ a held-out input, $N=\{1,\ldots,d\}$ the original-feature players, and $B=(z^{(1)},\ldots,z^{(m)})$ a fixed training-data background. We use the empirical marginal game
\begin{equation}
 v_{x,B}(S)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{b=1}^{m}
 f\!\left(x_S,z^{(b)}_{N\setminus S}\right),\qquad S\subseteq N.
 \label{eq:game}
\end{equation}
Hybrid records pass through the fitted preprocessing pipeline; an entire categorical feature remains one player even if encoded in several columns. The game's Shapley vector $\phi$ allocates $f(x)-v_{x,B}(\emptyset)$ in probability units \citep{lundberg2017unified}. Appendix~\ref{app:definitions} gives the full definition and diagnostics. Exactness means this finite empirical game, up to numerical precision. It does not make the background representative or resolve the application-dependent choice between marginal and observational games \citep{chen2020true}.

Write $\widehat\phi_{q,r}$ for an approximation with computation setting $q$ and seed $r$. Holding the model, input, background, preprocessing, output and feature groups fixed isolates explainer randomness. \emph{Repeatability} compares independent runs; \emph{accuracy} compares a run with a validated same-game reference; \emph{adequacy} asks whether errors satisfy a specified numerical use criterion. Two runs can agree while sharing error. The efficiency residual checks total allocation, not coordinate accuracy. Neither exactness nor repeatability establishes causal validity, fairness or deployment safety. Cost includes executed model-input rows after background expansion and measured time, alongside requested work.
