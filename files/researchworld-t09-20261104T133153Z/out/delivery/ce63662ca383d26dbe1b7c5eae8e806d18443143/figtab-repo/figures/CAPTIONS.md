# Suggested captions (for `paper-repo`)

Table and figure numbers here are internal; the paper numbering is in `MANIFEST.md`. Verdict wording follows `results/FINDINGS.md` of `experiment-repo`. Truth is the exact GATE of the simulated potential-outcome model; error = estimate − GATE; units are those of the direct effect τ = 1. Every error bar is ±1.96 Monte-Carlo SE over 4 worlds × 100 assignments per cell unless stated. "Oracle spec." is the correctly specified exposure regression (`ORACLE_D`, with log-degree).

**Fig. 1 (`fig1_strength`; C2, C3 / H6).** Bias (top) and RMSE (bottom, log axis) of the GATE estimate vs interference strength γ, linear spillover, Bernoulli(0.5) design, on SBM, BA and Stanford3. The difference in means has bias −γ (slope −1.000…−0.999 on all six graphs, appendix Fig. A1) and wins only for γ ≲ 0.5; the regression that includes the neighbour treated fraction is unbiased and its RMSE does not depend on γ. At γ = 0 its SD is 5.5–18.6× that of the difference in means. The 11-feature regression is noisier than either.

**Fig. 2 (`fig2_bias_theory`; C1 / H1, H2).** (a) Simulated noiseless bias of OLS (z, f) against the closed-form (exact binomial) prediction for 144 cells; the line is y = x, max |deviation| 0.04, all within 3 MC SE. (b, c) Bias vs treatment probability p for concave and convex spillover (curvature 3 solid, 8 dotted; closed form as thin lines). The large-degree shortcut γ(h′(p) − 1) is not supported (median deviation 0.09, up to 3.3).

**Fig. 3 (`fig3_bias_vs_n`; C1 / H3).** Concave spillover (γ = 2), SBM. Top: bias vs n on a symmetric-log axis (linear within ±0.1): the misspecified regressions' bias does not shrink from n = 1k to 20k (OLS (z, f): +2.53 → +2.49 at p = 0.1, −0.57 → −0.52 at p = 0.5), while the correctly specified regression is unbiased. Bottom: SD falls as n^{−1/2} (dotted guide). The γ-dependence of the misspecification bias is not tested (e1 varies γ only for linear truth).

**Fig. 4 (`fig4_variance_scaling`; C1, C2 / H4).** (a) SD vs n for mean degrees d = 5…80 on ER, Bernoulli(0.5); dotted: fitted C√(d/n). (b) SD/√(d/n) is flat in d for the regression on (z, f) (exponents of n −0.501 ± 0.005, of d +0.495 ± 0.006, Table 5) and not for the 11-feature regression (n −0.61, d +1.18). (c) SD relative to p = 0.5 vs p against √(0.25/(p(1−p))): SBM and Stanford3 follow it, ego-Facebook (hubs) does not. The scaling does not carry over to cluster designs (SBM + Louvain: n −0.55, d +0.37).

**Fig. 5 (`fig5_cluster_design`; C4 / H5).** Louvain clusters (solid) vs random clusters of the same sizes (dashed) across cluster resolution, concave spillover; dotted: Bernoulli(0.5). Left: share of units with a neighbour-treated fraction f ≥ 0.9 (exposure support under the design). Middle/right: bias (symmetric-log axis) and RMSE. Random clusters leave the difference in means at the Bernoulli bias (−2.0); Louvain clusters reduce it in every matched cell. For the regression the evidence is mixed — partly supported: Louvain RMSE is lower in 72 % of matched cells (SBM, ego-Facebook) but **reversed on Stanford3** (dense, mean degree 98). Linear-spillover version: Fig. A2.

**Fig. 6 (`fig6_coverage`; C5 / H7).** Coverage of nominal 95 % intervals (dashed), γ = 2, by variance estimator (HC0; cluster-robust CL-r at analysis resolution r, or at the design clusters; graph-HAC with 1- and 2-hop kernels), filled: OLS (z, f), hollow: correctly specified regression. Bars: ±1.96 binomial SE. HC0 under-covers everywhere; cluster-robust and HAC approach 0.95 only for linear truth with several analysis clusters; under concave truth (bias ≈ −0.5) none is near nominal. Omitted: BA and ER at analysis resolution 0.5, which have a single analysis cluster (SE ≈ 10⁻¹³, degenerate).

**Fig. 7 (`fig7_failure_map_*`; C3, C6 / H8).** Relative RMSE (RMSE/|GATE|, log colour scale) for each estimator under each interference mechanism and design (B.5/B.1 Bernoulli 0.5/0.1; Lv Louvain clusters; Rd random clusters of the same sizes). Hatched: no estimate (Hájek with fewer than 5 estimable replicates; no correctly specified model for the propagation mechanism). `fig7_failure_map_biasshare_sbm`: share of MSE that is squared bias. Two-hop interference degrades OLS (z, f) (bias −0.9…−1.0 on GATE 3) and the correctly specified one-hop-plus-two-hop model has *larger* RMSE; strong propagation (ρ = 0.9) defeats every estimator; the heavy-tail hypothesis (BA, Stanford3) is refuted on RMSE of the regression.

**Fig. 8 (`fig8_factor_ranking`; C3).** RMSE of OLS (z, f) (filled) and the correctly specified regression (open) over one-factor sweeps from each sweep's default (row label: experiment and base scenario). Bars span the tested range, so the ordering ranks effect sizes over *these ranges*, not factors in general; ratios equal `t11_factor_ranking.csv`.

**Fig. 9 (`fig9_gamma_dependence`; C1, C3 / H3, post-freeze grid e11).** Bias of the GATE estimate vs interference strength γ with a non-linear outcome model (concave or sigmoid 1-hop spillover; Bernoulli(0.5) and (0.1); SBM, BA, Stanford3, ego-Facebook; marker = graph). The difference in means has bias −γ; the regression on (z, f) has bias proportional to γ whose sign and size are set by shape and p (proportional in 14 of 16 graph × shape × p groups; the two exceptions are ego-Facebook); the correctly specified regression is unbiased (|bias| ≤ 0.17 at γ = 4). OLS quad is omitted: its bias at p = 0.1 does not depend on γ (−2…−10; instability, not spillover; see `t14_gamma_dependence.csv`).

**Fig. 10 (`fig10_hajek`; C2, C6 / H6, post-freeze grid e12).** Bias relative to the GATE of exposure-mapping (Hájek) estimators, the difference in means, OLS (z, f) and the correctly specified regression on low-degree graphs (ER, WS, BA, mean degree 4 and 8, n = 3000), by interference mechanism and design (marker = graph). Filled: the exposure mapping (or model) is correct for the mechanism; open: it is wrong. Exposure-mapping baselines are unbiased only under a correct mapping and are not more accurate than the plain regression in most cells; on Bernoulli(0.8) they rest on 200–400 of 400 replicates (control-exposed units are rare) and HajekKappa is undefined for cluster designs. Cells with fewer than 100 estimable replicates are not drawn.
