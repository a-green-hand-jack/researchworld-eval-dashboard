\begin{abstract}
Regression GATE estimators predict outcomes under all-treated and all-control assignments from a mixed network experiment. We study their accuracy through conditional bias--variance and estimability diagnostics, using 102 simulation cells with 36 graphs, eight response mechanisms, two assignment designs and 400 repetitions per cell. Correctly matched linear regression tracks endpoint-contrast variance: at 2000 nodes, mean graph RMSE rises from 0.101 to 0.412 as regular degree increases from 4 to 80. Better experimental-distribution prediction, correct exposure features and increased support each have measured counterexamples to guaranteed risk improvement. A coupled hidden-tail response demonstrates a support obstruction, while correlated noise reduces working-independence coverage to about 0.59 on the fixed public topology. Exact full-exposure HT risk also exposes extreme tails missed by finite simulation. These are conditional findings for specified synthetic outcomes and tested topology/design regimes, with graph and Monte Carlo uncertainty distinguished; they introduce no new estimator or feasible oracle inference procedure.
\end{abstract}
