\section{Setting, Theory and Reporting Methods}
\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Setting.}
A \emph{system} is a configuration $\theta$ from a finite set $\Theta$ ($|\Theta|=K$) of prompts / few-shot demonstration sets / pipeline variants, scored per item by $y(\theta,x)\in\{0,1\}$ (we use correctness). Its true performance is $\mu(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim D}\,y(\theta,x)$ for a task distribution $D$.
A \emph{benchmark} is a sample $S$ of $n$ items. A \emph{search procedure} $\mathcal{A}$ (random search, successive halving, coordinate ascent, surrogate-guided search, \dots) interacts with $S$ only by evaluating configurations on (subsets of) $S$ and returns $\hat\theta=\mathcal{A}(S)$. The usual \emph{reported score} is $\hat R=\frac1n\sum_{x\in S}y(\hat\theta,x)$.
We call $\mathrm{opt}=\hat R-\mu(\hat\theta)$ the \emph{optimism} of the report; the natural quantities of interest are its mean (bias), whether intervals around $\hat R$ cover $\mu(\hat\theta)$, and whether decisions (``$A$ beats $B$'') based on reported scores are correct.

\paragraph{A closed-form benchmark for the size of the effect.}
Let $\bar Y_c=\mu_c+\varepsilon_c$ be the accuracies of $B$ fully evaluated configurations, with $\mu_c\sim\mathcal N(m,\tau^2)$ i.i.d.\ (true spread $\tau$) and configuration-specific evaluation noise $\varepsilon_c\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)$ i.i.d. Then $\bar Y_{\hat c}=m+\sqrt{\tau^2+\sigma^2}\,Z_{(B)}$, with $Z_{(B)}$ the maximum of $B$ standard normals, and since $\mathbb{E}[\mu_c\mid \bar Y_c]=m+\kappa(\bar Y_c-m)$, $\kappa=\tau^2/(\tau^2+\sigma^2)$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[\hat R-\mu(\hat\theta)]=\frac{\sigma^2}{\sqrt{\tau^2+\sigma^2}}\,\mathbb{E}Z_{(B)},\qquad
\mathbb{E}[\mu(\hat\theta)-m]=\frac{\tau^2}{\sqrt{\tau^2+\sigma^2}}\,\mathbb{E}Z_{(B)} .
\label{eq:opt}
\end{equation}
Hence (i) the optimism grows with the search budget like $\mathbb{E}Z_{(B)}\approx\sqrt{2\ln B}$ and, because $\sigma^2\propto 1/n$ (for binary scores $\sigma^2\approx(1-\bar r)\,p(1-p)/n$, where $\bar r$ is the average correlation between configurations' per-item outcomes: shared item difficulty cancels in comparisons and does not inflate the optimism), shrinks with $n$ like $n^{-1/2}$ when $\tau\ll\sigma$ and like $n^{-1}$ when $\tau\gg\sigma$; (ii) the share of the reported improvement over an average configuration that is noise is $\sigma^2/(\tau^2+\sigma^2)$ --- when configurations truly differ little compared with the evaluation noise ($\tau\ll\sigma$), \emph{all} of the reported gain is optimism and the optimism is at its maximum $\sigma\,\mathbb{E}Z_{(B)}$; (iii) without the Gaussian-prior structure the maximal inequality gives the bound $\mathbb{E}[\hat R-\mu(\hat\theta)]\le\sigma_{\max}\sqrt{2\ln B}$ for exhaustive search over $B$ configurations. Equation~\eqref{eq:opt} is an idealisation; Section~\ref{sec:exp} tests it, and the other regimes, by simulation.

\paragraph{Reporting methods compared.}
All methods see only $S$ (size $n$) and the search procedure.
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item \textbf{Naive} (status quo): $\hat R$ with a Wald interval.
\item \textbf{Hold-out}: search on a random $(1-\rho)n$ items, report the accuracy of the winner on the remaining $\rho n$ with a Wilson interval; exact for $\mu(\hat\theta_{1})$ but spends $\rho n$ items on reporting instead of search.
\item \textbf{Cross-fit}: $F$-fold: search on $F\!-\!1$ folds, score on the held-out fold, pool. Estimates the performance of the \emph{procedure}, using $F$ extra searches.
\item \textbf{Replay bootstrap} (generic, our default recommendation when the full score matrix of explored configurations is available or evaluations are cached): re-run the \emph{entire} search $\mathcal{A}$ on $b=1..100$ bootstrap resamples $S^{*b}$ of $S$, set $\widehat{\mathrm{opt}}=\frac1b\sum_b[\hat R^{*b}-\hat R_S(\hat\theta^{*b})]$ where $\hat R^{*b}$ is the winner's score on $S^{*b}$ and $\hat R_S(\hat\theta^{*b})$ its score on the original $S$, and report $\hat R-\widehat{\mathrm{opt}}$ (Efron-style optimism correction applied to the whole procedure). A \texttt{.632} variant uses out-of-bag items.
\item \textbf{Empirical-Bayes shrinkage} (random/grid search over $B$ fully evaluated candidates): estimate $\tau^2$ from the between-configuration variance after subtracting the paired item-level noise, shrink the winner toward the grand mean by $\kappa$ (eq.~\ref{eq:opt}, the Tweedie/normal-normal correction).
\item \textbf{Worst-case analytic correction} (random/grid search): subtract $\hat\sigma\,\mathbb{E}Z_{(B)}$, eq.~\eqref{eq:opt} with $\tau{=}0$ and $\hat\sigma$ the paired item-level noise of the configuration contrasts: the maximal optimism for given $(n,B)$, needing no prior on candidate quality; conservative whenever candidates truly differ.
\item \textbf{Conditional (winner) inference} (random/grid search): the polyhedral-lemma truncated-normal interval and median-unbiased estimate for $\mu_{\hat c}$ given that $\hat c$ won, with the covariance of the $B$ accuracies estimated from the paired item-level data \citep{lee2016exact,andrews2024inference}.
\item \textbf{Simultaneous interval}: max-$t$ bootstrap band over the $B$ evaluated configurations \citep{chernozhukov2013gaussian}; valid for any selection rule among them.
\item \textbf{Ladder} \citep{blum2015ladder} and \textbf{Thresholdout} \citep{dwork2015generalization}, run over the same candidate sequence, as adaptive-data-analysis baselines.
\end{itemize}
