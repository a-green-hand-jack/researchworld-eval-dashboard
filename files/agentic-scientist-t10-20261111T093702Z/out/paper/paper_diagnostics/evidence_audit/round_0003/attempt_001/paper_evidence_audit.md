## Summary
The numbers match the sealed final metrics, the headroom file and the search metrics, and the qualified outcome is reported accurately. Two causal or explanatory statements go beyond what the evidence trail supports.

## Findings
### 1. headroom-explains-overclaim-1 — Conclusion; Discussion (What the evidence supports); Title
- claim: Conclusion: "A closed-form headroom analysis explains why the gain is small and regime-dependent" and "a cross-fitted recalibration added as a second control variate to PPI++ shortened the mean interval by 2.2%"; Discussion: "a few percent where the PPI++ clip binds (census income) or where the calibration is nonlinear"; the title attributes the gains to "Recalibrated Control Variates".
- issue: The only controlled comparison is the whole evaluated configuration against the ppi-python PPI++ baseline. Recalibration, the Student-t quantile, the 1% fallback and the variance estimator were not ablated, so the gain cannot be attributed to recalibration or to the clip. The paper's own results show observed narrowing above the population ceiling in four of five families. The census-income observed value of 0.0287 is above its ceiling of 0.0213. The plankton observed value of 0.0359 has a ceiling of exactly zero. The paper also says the mechanisms are untested hypotheses and that the decomposition is "a diagnostic of what is possible, not an attribution". "Explains" and the component attribution contradict those caveats.
- evidence: paper_data.json ablation_studies=[] and evidence_sources.ablations status=missing; analysis/headroom_derived.json observed_vs_ppi_pp against oracle_vs_ppi_pp (income 0.0287 against 0.0213; plankton 0.0359 against about 2e-16); paper.tex Section 5.2 ("We did not run a diagnostic that isolates its source") and Limitations item (3); source_evidence/frozen_winner_estimator.py _best_interval, which bundles recalibration, Student-t and fallback.
- requested_fix: Say that the evaluated configuration (cross-fitted recalibration with Student-t and a PPI++ fallback) was 2.2% narrower than ppi-python PPI++. Replace "explains" with wording such as "a population-level diagnostic that matches the observed margin only for synthetic data and roughly for census income, and does not account for the observed gain in four of five families". Drop the claim that the margins arise where the clip binds, and soften the title's attribution to recalibrated control variates.

### 2. search-stack-headroom-link-1 — Results, Design-space contrast on the search pools
- claim: The more elaborate stack did not beat the simpler recalibrated interval on the aggregate, "which is consistent with the headroom analysis: when the ceiling is small, richer calibrators have little to gain and pay an estimation cost."
- issue: The headroom analysis was computed only for the sealed final pools (census income, insurance, education, plankton and the sealed synthetic regimes). No ceilings were computed for the search pools (galaxy, forest, gene expression, alphafold, search synthetic). The stack's shortfall against the recalibrated variant is largest on forest (0.0054 against 0.0168) and alphafold (0.0038 against 0.0104), whose ceilings are unknown. On the search synthetic family, which has a nonlinear regime, the two variants are close (0.0707 against 0.0757). The paper also states that these are single evaluations that differ in several components and cannot isolate a mechanism.
- evidence: analysis/headroom_derived.json keys cover only real.census_income, census_healthcare, census_education, plankton and the sealed synthetic regimes; source_evidence/search_metrics/recalibrated_metrics.json and stacked_metrics.json per-family log ratios; paper.tex Section 5.4 and Table tab:search.
- requested_fix: Delete the "consistent with the headroom analysis" explanation. State only that the stacked variant scored lower on the aggregate (0.0223 against 0.0271) in single, uncontrolled evaluations, and that no search-pool ceilings were computed.
