# Caption facts for the delivered exhibits (for paper-repo; wording is yours, the facts below are what the exhibit shows)

**Fig 1 `fig1_beta_law.pdf` (C1).** Split-conformal coverage given the calibration set (alpha = 0.1, K = 5, linear predictor), over repeated calibration sets, for L2, Hellinger and Aitchison scores and two noise laws; shaded = exact 5–95% band of Beta(k, n+1−k), k = ceil((n+1)(1−alpha)); markers = empirical mean and 5/95% quantiles. Source `results/theory/t1_beta_law.csv` (3000 calibration draws per cell per README; test pool 2e6). KS p-values over the 12 cells per panel are annotated (0.03–0.98 dirichlet, 0.02–0.86 multinomial); with 24 tests in total a p of 0.015 is expected by chance occasionally.

**Fig 4 `fig4_ncal.pdf` (C4/C6).** E1, D = 5, heteroscedastic, linear predictor, 100 replicates per point. (a,c) sd over replicates of test-set coverage vs n_cal; line = sqrt(Var Beta + 0.09/2000) (the second term is test-set noise, n_test = 2000). (b,d) mean volume fraction of the region (MC, 95% t-CI over replicates) for split / locally normalised / Mondrian / CV+ L2. CV+ pools fit and calibration data (README), so its n_cal is not on the same footing. n_cal = 500 comes from the pilot `main_*` or `main200_*` file of the same configuration.

**Fig 6 `fig6_delta_method.pdf` (C6, negative result for Aitchison).** Each point is one x (300 per panel); x-axis delta-method prediction, y-axis simulated pointwise coverage (Dirichlet noise, oracle mean predictor, marginal threshold at 0.9). RMSE and max |error| from `t4_cond_coverage.csv`. L2: RMSE 0.008–0.010; Hellinger: the first-order prediction is flat at 0.9 while the simulated coverage varies (RMSE 0.014–0.038, correlation undefined); Aitchison (first order and 2nd-order moment variants): RMSE 0.16–0.27 and correlation 0.58 → −0.44 as precision phi falls from 200 to 8, i.e. the delta method fails for the log-ratio score at these precisions.

**Table 1 `tab1_methods.tex`.** Static; scores, zero replacement, schemes.

**Table 3 `tab3_e2_labelsets.tex` (C7).** CIFAR-10 test images (10,000) randomly split into calibration (n = 1000) and test, 200 splits, T = 1; mean ± half-width of the 95% t-interval over splits. size = mean set size; min class cov. = smallest per-class coverage; SSCV† = minimum coverage over set-size strata {1}, {2–3}, {4+} (strata with < 30 points dropped); empty = share of empty sets; "= LAC" = share of test points whose set equals LAC's. L1, Hellinger and KL sets equal the LAC sets in 100% of cases (monotone in p_y at vertex labels; claim T2). The 12-epoch CNN has accuracy 0.881, the 2-epoch one 0.740.

**Fig 5 `fig5_null_spread.pdf` (C5).** Null values of three conditional-coverage metrics when conditional coverage is exact (coverage indicators Bernoulli(p), p ~ Beta(k, n+1-k), independent of x; n_test = 2000; 500 replicates per n_cal): line = mean, band = 5-95%. At n_cal = 500: top-probability-bin gap 0.034 (sd 0.013), worst-slab coverage 0.90 (sd 0.03), oracle gap 0.016 (sd 0.005; its positive bias comes from the Monte-Carlo noise of 400 draws per point and the calibration-induced deviation of p from 0.9). A difference between methods smaller than this spread is not interpretable. Fig 3 draws the same band (grey) at the configuration's n_cal.

**Table 4 `tab4_e3_cifar10h.tex` (C8).** CIFAR-10H human label distributions (10,000 CIFAR-10 test images; 80.6% of entries are exact zeros) as a composition-valued response, prediction = 12-epoch CNN softmax (T = 1); alpha = 0.1, n_cal = 1000, 100 random sigma/cal/test splits (n_rep = 100 in `e3_summary.csv`); mean ± half-width of the 95% t-CI over splits. cov. = marginal coverage (0.901–0.906; because of ties in the zero-heavy scores only the lower bound 1−alpha is guaranteed, so slight over-coverage is expected); mass = `pool_frac` (share of 300 other images' human distributions inside the region, lower = more informative; the locally normalised variants are 3–5 times less informative: 0.32–0.56 vs 0.10–0.12); ambig. min / conf. min = coverage in the worst of 5 equal-mass bins of the entropy of the human distribution (outcome-conditional diagnostic) / of the CNN's top-1 probability; wsc = worst-slab coverage (delta = 0.1).

**Fig 7 `fig7_shift.pdf` (E4, C10).** D = 5, heteroscedastic, linear predictor, 100 replicates, n_cal = 500. Calibration x ~ N(0, I), test x ~ N(delta w, I) with w the unit direction of the noise scale (shift toward noisier inputs). Coloured lines: ordinary split conformal (solid plain, dashed locally normalised; 95% t-CI over replicates); crosses: weighted split conformal with the exact likelihood ratio (all 8 methods; coverage 0.895–0.920 over all families and shifts; effective calibration size down to 73 at delta = 1.5). At delta = 1.5 unweighted plain scores fall to 0.49–0.73 (lognormal 0.49–0.59), normalised ones to 0.70–0.92; the normalised Aitchison score over-covers in the multinomial family (0.96).

**Fig 2 `fig2_size.pdf` (C2, C4).** E1, alpha = 0.1, n_cal = 500, heteroscedastic, linear predictor; 100 replicates. Quantity: region mass `pool_frac` (probability mass of the marginal law of y inside the prediction region, averaged over 2000 test points; smaller = more informative; defined for every D). Shown: ratio to the plain-L2 region of the *same replicate* (paired), geometric mean over replicates, 95% t-CI of the mean log-ratio (often smaller than the marker). Open = plain split, filled square = locally normalised, triangle = Mondrian (argmax). All methods are marginally valid (0.896-0.909, so ratios compare equally valid regions). Facts: Aitchison is 2.45x (D=3), 1.98x (D=5), 1.45x (D=10) the plain-L2 region for Dirichlet noise but 0.90x for rounded (zero-containing lognormal) at D=10; Dirichlet NLL is the smallest at every D (0.82, 0.67, 0.62 of L2); KL 0.88, 0.80, 0.70; Hellinger 0.93, 0.87, 0.75; L-infinity is 1.0 at D=3 (it equals L1 there, an identity), 1.19 at D=5 and 1.44 at D=10; KL-normalised reaches 0.57 (Dirichlet D=10) and 0.32 (rounded D=10). Ratios are within one configuration only; the reference measure is the marginal law of y, so absolute mass is not comparable across configurations. `fig2_size_volume.pdf` repeats D<=5 with Lebesgue volume fraction (Spearman correlation with mass 0.89 at D=3, 0.96 at D=5; D=10 has no volume).

**Fig 3 `fig3_conditional.pdf` (C3, C5).** E1, n_cal = 500, D = 5, linear predictor; rows: Dirichlet homoscedastic, then heteroscedastic Dirichlet, lognormal, multinomial, rounded. Columns: oracle gap = mean |c(x) - 0.9| of the pointwise conditional coverage computed from the known generator (Monte-Carlo, 400 draws per point; its floor under exact conditional coverage is ~0.011-0.025 (null 5-95%)); top-probability-bin gap (max - min coverage over 5 equal-mass bins of the predicted top probability; null 0.015-0.060); worst-slab coverage (nominal 0.9; null 0.85-0.95); coverage given y near the boundary (outcome-conditional, descriptive). Grey = null band of Fig 5 at n_cal = 500 (5-95% of the metric if conditional coverage were exact), dashed = 0.9. Markers as Fig 2; 95% t-CIs over 100 replicates are smaller than the markers. Facts (grid means over replicates): in the homoscedastic Dirichlet world L2, L1, L-inf, Hellinger and KL have oracle gap 0.026-0.030, just above the null band (0.011-0.025), and normalisation does not help (grid mean over the 24 homoscedastic configurations: normalised 0.070 vs plain 0.062; normalised L2 beats plain L2 in only 8/24 of them); Aitchison and Dirichlet NLL are worse there (0.061). With heteroscedasticity plain L2 has oracle gap 0.118 and worst-slab 0.60 versus normalised 0.083 and 0.82 (grid means over the 24 heteroscedastic configurations; normalised beats plain in 24/24); in the five rows shown plain scores have oracle gap 0.079-0.148 and the best normalised variant 0.054-0.077, all far above the null band. Plain Aitchison has the lowest worst-slab coverage in three of the four heteroscedastic rows (Dirichlet 0.56, lognormal 0.37, rounded 0.44; in multinomial Hellinger is lowest, 0.54, Aitchison 0.59).

**Table 2 `tab2_e1_headline.tex` (C2, C3).** E1, alpha = 0.1, D = 5, heteroscedastic, linear predictor, n_cal = 500, 100 replicates; means over replicates (largest 95% CI half-widths over the table: coverage 0.004, worst-slab 0.016, oracle gap 0.004, mass 0.009, in a comment line). cov. = marginal coverage; wsc = worst-slab coverage; or. gap = oracle conditional-coverage gap (null floor 0.011-0.025, Fig 5); mass = region mass `pool_frac`. Rows = score under plain split, locally normalised and Mondrian variants. Bold = best in column among the 15 methods (no significance claim). Facts: all marginal coverages 0.896-0.905; the best oracle gap and worst-slab coverage are always a locally normalised variant (Dirichlet: KL-n 0.054, wsc 0.85; lognormal: L-inf-n 0.077, wsc 0.81); among plain scores Dirichlet NLL is best in the Dirichlet world (0.079, wsc 0.81) and no better than L2 in the lognormal world (0.126 vs 0.125); smallest mass: NLL 0.11 (Dirichlet) and normalised Aitchison 0.21 (lognormal; NLL 0.25).

**Fig 8 `fig8_eps_sensitivity.pdf` (C5 E1).** Aitchison zero replacement eps in {1e-4, 1e-3 (main grid), 1e-2, 1e-1}; D = 5, het., linear predictor, n_cal = 500, 100 replicates per point, 95% t-CIs.
