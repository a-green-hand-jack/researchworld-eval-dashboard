Simple Predictions of Uniform-Start Random-Walk Cover Times on Small
Graphs: Accuracy and Family Dependence
Autonomous Research Agent 1
Abstract
Can a few inexpensive graph statistics predict
random-walk cover times more closely than a
general upper bound? We generate 133 connected
graphs with 8–30 vertices from ten families and
estimate their uniform-start mean cover times
with 4,000 independent walks each. A fixed log-
linear ridge model using vertex count, edge count,
maximum degree, and diameter achieves a family-
balanced test mean absolute percentage error of
20.1%, with a Monte Carlo 95% interval of [19.8,
20.5]%, compared with 75.9% for a fitted size-
only model. Accuracy depends strongly on fam-
ily: errors are about 5% on stars but 50–55% on
grids and dense random graphs. Leaving an en-
tire family out of training raises overall error to
31.3%. These results support a useful empirical
approximation on this graph collection, without a
uniform accuracy guarantee over small connected
graphs.
1. Introduction
For a simple random walk on a finite connected undirected
graph, the time to visit all vertices varies substantially with
topology. General bounds, including the classical edge-
based bound associated with random-walk traversal (Aleliu-
nas et al., 1979), give guarantees but need not approximate
individual instances. Tight extremal bounds (Feige, 1995)
address a different objective from typical-instance predic-
tion. The survey of Lov´asz (1993) provides background on
random walks on graphs.
We test four basic statistics on held-out sizes and unseen
families, with saved raw trajectories and propagated simu-
lation uncertainty. Under the task’s access restriction, no
research literature or discussing pages were retrieved; we
make no novelty claim. The supplied references serve as

4. Discussion and Limitations
Four elementary statistics provide a substantially better ap-
proximation than the chosen general bound and a fitted size
baseline on this collection. Nevertheless, the family-wise
errors are far larger than the simulation uncertainty, indi-
cating model mismatch rather than insufficient walks. The
large grid and dense-ER errors are consistent with the lim-
ited flexibility of a single multiplicative rule. Diameter and
maximum degree do not encode all spatial organization or
bottleneck structure. Our experiment does not isolate which
omitted property causes each error.
This is a connectivity smoke test, not an exhaustive bench-
mark: it includes only seven sizes from 8 upward, a few
instances per random family, and specific lollipop/barbell
proportions. In particular, nothing here establishes a guar-
antee for every graph with at most 30 vertices, for n < 8,
for different starting distributions, or for worst-start cover
time. Family balancing is a deliberate evaluation choice, not
a natural distribution on all connected graphs. Small Monte
Carlo intervals should not be mistaken for confidence in
out-of-distribution predictions.
The predictor is a rough estimate for represented families
and sizes; applications should validate on their own graph
class. Predictions are not certified upper bounds. More
flexible models remain untested. Code, raw outcomes, seeds,
and compilation instructions accompany this report.
