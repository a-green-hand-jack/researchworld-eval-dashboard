# Prospective resource and convergence bound

Measured first pilot: 12 development MAP-MIRT fits, 10 relative-objective convergence and 2 iteration-capped MMLU-Pro fits. The second development-only job `job-4801f9828b` tested the fixed continuation rule. Both capped fits converged after continuation: dimension 10 needed 500+146 iterations and 113.909 seconds total; dimension 15 needed 500+535 iterations and 177.669 seconds total. Source fit process high-water RSS was 408,380/421,180 KiB (the maximum is 411.31 MiB). These are process peaks, not isolated per-fit incremental allocation. All diagnostics/raw records are in `results/pilot_comparators/records.json`.

On MMLU-Pro, constructing paper-AIPW linear statistics for 3 budgets x 5 alphas took 0.0390 seconds summed, and batch predictions for the 60 development models took 0.000974 s summed. The exact pinned predict body and direct sklearn formula agree with the optimized implementation to 1e-12 in independent checks. Its algebra permits constructing a source/item-dependent weight vector once per draw and applying it across targets, rather than redundantly fitting sklearn for every target. The two formulas share the same orientation and alpha-10 source structures when their settings match. Per-target API cost will still be measured in the complete study.

The executable metadata grid has 45 benchmark/scenario cases: 13 main, 26 reduced-source, and 6 eligible name-proxy family exclusions; ARC has no eligible family by frozen counts. Source fitting eligibility gives 168 development MIRT fits (all eligible dimensions), at most 71 selected-dimension historical refits, hence at most 239 fits. A looser every-dimension refit bound is 336; that is not the implemented caching count. Primary cells are 13x3x20=780 per method; source/family extensions are 32x20=640 k30 cells per uniform method. MIRT ability inference is shared by gp-style mixtures. At most 2,200 target batches are required for random/anchor p-IRT, before optional caching of identical anchor predictions; each batch has the fixed cohort's 17–26 (family) or 24–129 (main) targets. These are workload counts, not completed target runs.

Using the largest measured 177.669 s source fit for every one of 239 fits gives 11.8 CPU hours of fit time; doubling this reference for larger refits/shared-host variability gives 23.6 hours. This is a deliberately conservative planning extrapolation, not a guaranteed bound or a measurement of total study time. Most matrices are much smaller, and source-size reductions shrink fits. Additional linear/cluster/inference work was seconds-to-minutes in pilots. Two independent single-thread benchmark jobs are suitable if host room is available; never fit the same cache entry concurrently. The measured ~0.41 GiB fit process is well below the 4 GiB per-job ceiling; target inference adds a roughly 129x12,032 float64 probability array (~12 MiB), so a <1 GiB working-memory expectation is plausible but remains a projection before target runs. No N-by-N item matrix is constructed.

Source-only develop/refit can proceed while prospective feasibility is judged; final target prediction requires independent judgement plus the recorded delivery ownership and immutable executable freeze. Numerical failures remain explicit. The predeclared continuation/exclusion/stop rules are not selected based on target error. Actual end-to-end source/target timings, process RSS, failure counts and file sizes will replace these estimates in the final evidence package.
