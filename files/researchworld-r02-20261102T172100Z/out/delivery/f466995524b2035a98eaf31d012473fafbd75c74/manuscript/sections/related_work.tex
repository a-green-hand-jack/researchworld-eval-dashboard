\section{Related work and positioning}
Split conformal prediction separates fitting from calibration and gives marginal coverage under exchangeability; its computational simplicity motivates holding the predictor fixed while varying scores and calibration schemes \citep{lei2017distribution,angelopoulos2022gentle}. Full conformal and refitting-based alternatives make different computational and statistical tradeoffs. Jackknife+ uses leave-one-out fits and has a general worst-case $1-2\alpha$ lower bound in its ordinary construction \citep{barber2020jackknife}. We do not benchmark every conformal family or claim a new rank guarantee.

The closest permitted work is \citet{javanmardi2024credal}. They already split-conformalize distribution-vector labels into simplex regions, compare scores including total variation, KL divergence, Wasserstein distance and model-based alternatives, and evaluate coverage and relative simplex size across repeated splits. Thus conformalizing a composition, varying distributional scores and measuring a fraction of the simplex are established ideas. Their distinction between noisy observed distribution labels and latent clean probabilities is also directly relevant: coverage for one does not automatically establish coverage for the other. Our focus is a shared clr-regression predictor under controlled heterogeneity, coordinate simultaneity, calibration variation, a known group-mixture intervention and boundary sensitivity. The contribution must be assessed through those completed comparisons, rather than through a claim that simplex regions are new.

Classification prediction sets instead cover labels, even when their scores use a softmax vector \citep{angelopoulos2022raps}. Class-conditional and clustered calibration illustrate how marginal guarantees can hide class failures and how fine strata increase calibration demands \citep{ding2023class}. Our strata are observed features, not response classes. Multivalid conformal methods address richer collections of groups and thresholds \citep{jung2022multivalid}; evaluating two groups or calibrating within them does not establish such a general property.

Weighted conformal prediction handles specified covariate shift when the conditional response law is invariant and appropriate likelihood ratios are known \citep{tibshirani2020shift}. The oracle baseline here isolates the effect of exact ratios under a deliberately transparent shift. It is not evidence that estimated weights work without additional assumptions. Finally, coverage parity does not itself establish equitable downstream decisions: human-subject classification evidence makes this distinction concrete \citep{cresswell2025disparate}. No human-utility or fairness conclusion follows from this numerical benchmark.
