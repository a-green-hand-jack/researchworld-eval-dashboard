## Findings (open at this head)
F1 (claims vs evidence) The text says the correction and stratification are "what survives" and that the P3 gain is "most attributable to stratification alone". The delivered per-prediction table already contains the ablations `random+lowrank_dr` (correction without stratification) and `strat+lowrank` (stratification without correction), which Section 5.5 says are not part of this version. From it at n=50: extrapolation MAE B1 4.54, B2 3.77, random+lowrank_dr 3.55, P3 DR 3.43, strat+lowrank 8.08; random split: 4.60, 3.72, 3.47, 3.40, 3.06. So the correction alone (random items) delivers ~0.99 of P3's 1.10-point gain, i.e. more than stratification alone (0.77), and the model-only part of P3 is what fails. The abstract/Sec.5.2/Sec.6 attribution must be rewritten from reported ablations (a table with paired CIs), not asserted.
F2 (abstract accuracy) "every source-informed method beats the random subset mean" and "falls from 4.6 to about 3.0-3.4": Anchor points (4.30 at n=50; +0.21 vs B1 at n=100) and B2 (3.72) are source-informed and outside that range/claim. Qualify.
F3 (uncertainty) All intervals are bootstrap over 30 repetitions with the target set fixed (extrapolation targets are a deterministic partition; random split reuses the same models), so +-0.05 bands describe selector/subsample noise, not the 20-107 target models. "13/13 benchmarks better" and tight CIs overstate precision. Add a bootstrap over target models (clustered by model, paired across methods) at least for the headline paired differences, and say in the text (not only in the checklist) what the bands cover.
F4 (metrics required by the task) RMSE and Kendall are announced as metrics (Sec. 1, 3) but no RMSE/Kendall result is shown or summarized; add a table (appendix is fine) or state per-method that conclusions agree, with numbers from summary.csv.
F5 (internal inconsistency) Sec. 3 says "Hyperparameters of every method, including the baselines, are tuned by cross-validation across source models"; Sec. 4 and REPRODUCE.md say IRT/low-rank/embedding sizes were fixed a priori and only ridge alpha / P3 design were chosen on sources. Make Sec. 3 match.
F6 (Table 3 text) "estimation floor of 30 draws": on the random split a given target model is a target in only ~25% of the 30 repetitions, so the floor differs from the extrapolation case; state the floor per split (B1 random bias^2 5.1 vs variance 29.9).
F7 (novelty/positioning) P3's difference/residual-correction estimator is the standard model-assisted survey estimator (the checklist says so) and the data repository's own summary names a doubly-robust estimator; the contribution list presents P3 as a contribution without citing survey-sampling / prediction-powered-inference literature and without saying that the design-based correction is not new. Add the citations (survey sampling difference estimator; PPI; IRT basics), reposition P3 as a tested design, and cite starter items 7-9 (Balkir et al., ONEBench, MMLU-Pro) or say why not. Related work is currently thin (9 references).
F8 (figure) Fig. 2: panels are tiny (tick labels unreadable in print), the "macro-avg." title collides with the axis; use a larger layout (e.g. macro panel full-width + per-benchmark in appendix at readable size). Figure PDFs embed Type 3 fonts (format checker WARN): set matplotlib pdf.fonttype=42.
F9 (hygiene) `figures/__pycache__/*.pyc` are committed; remove and add .gitignore.
F10 (related-work claims) The paper states it read the cited works only at abstract level; keep statements about their results to what the abstracts say, and avoid characterizations of their evaluation protocols that were not read (e.g. "typically held-out models resemble the source models").
