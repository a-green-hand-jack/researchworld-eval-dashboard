# Findings per claim and hypothesis (experiment-repo)

All grids e1–e10 are complete at the freeze (`results/COMPLETENESS.csv`: every cell index has a raw file; `results/CELLS_NOT_RUN.csv` is empty). Each verdict below is computed by `code/scripts/findings.py` from the decision rule written in `results/hypotheses.csv`; **the rules were NOT pre-registered**: the hypotheses and their predicted directions come from the plan (written before any data), but the numeric thresholds in `findings.py` (first committed 2026-11-05 06:33, with the later 06:55 revision) were written after the e1–e10 runs had finished and after I had looked at the claim tables `t1`–`t10` (made since 2026-11-04) — they are descriptive decision criteria stated explicitly, not a test fixed in advance; I did not tune a threshold after reading its verdict, but that cannot be verified from the history. Treat the H1–H8 verdict words as readings of the numbers, which are the evidence. Rules for the post-freeze grids are different, see below. Numbers are cell values (400 estimates = 4 worlds × 100 assignments per cell and estimator unless stated); Monte-Carlo SEs are in the summaries (`bias_se`). One run of each cell (one seed), so cell-to-cell differences smaller than a few MC SEs are noise. "Truth" is the exact GATE of the simulated potential-outcome model, not an observed effect. Estimators: **DM** difference in means; **OLS_f** regression on (1, z, neighbour-treated fraction f1) — the plain regression-based GATE; **OLS_fd/OLS_quad/OLS_cubic/OLS_big** richer features; **ORACLE/ORACLE_D** correctly specified exposure model (`_D` adds log-degree, which matters because baselines depend on degree in the noisy grids); **GBM** gradient boosting; **HajekFull/HajekKappa** exposure-mapping estimators.

## Claims (plan §5)
- **C1 (error = bias + variance as in §2)** — *supported for linear/low-order exposure regression*: noiseless bias equals the exact-binomial prediction (H2); bias does not vanish with n while SD ∝ n^−1/2 (H3, H4). The closed form is verified numerically, not derived for general graphs.
- **C2 (correct spec ⇒ small bias, variance far above DM; misspecified ⇒ bias dominates)** — *supported*. e1 γ=0: SD(OLS_f)/SD(DM) = 5.5–18.6 (ORACLE_D has the same SD as OLS_f, ratio 0.84–1.00: the variance is the cost of any exposure regression, not of misspecification). e2, OLS_f: median \|bias\|/GATE = 0.01 (linear), 0.14 (concave), 0.14 (convex), 0.34 (sigmoid); squared bias exceeds half the MSE in 0% / 33% / 38% / 67% of cells. Over 96 e2 cells OLS_f has lower RMSE than DM in 85%, but ORACLE_D is better than OLS_f in 85%, and neither OLS_quad (better in 14%) nor GBM (18%) usually beat OLS_f: more flexibility costs variance and extrapolates badly (e10: OLS_quad bias −9…−11 at p = 0.1, `t4`).
- **C3 (factor ranking)** — `t11_factor_ranking.csv` (OLS_f RMSE, max/min over one-factor sweeps from the default; the ranges are not equally wide, so this ranks effect sizes over the *tested* ranges only): n 1k→20k 4.6×, spillover shape 4.5× (bias share 94% at the worst level), mean degree 5→80 3.9×, p 0.1 vs 0.5 3.6× (bias share 92%), graph family 3.2×, design 2.0×, noise σ 0.25→4 1.5×, strength γ (linear truth) 1.1×. Interference strength matters for DM, not for correctly-linear OLS_f.
- **C4 (clusters help only through quality)** — *partly supported* (H5): random clusters of the same size do nothing for DM bias; good clusters remove most of it; for the regression estimator the effect on variance depends on graph (helps on SBM/ego-FB, hurts on dense Stanford3).
- **C5 (coverage fails in stated regimes)** — *supported* (H7): fails for HC0 everywhere and for all intervals under misspecification bias; HAC/cluster-robust at best nominal (0.9–0.95) in unbiased cells with several analysis clusters.
- **C6 (failure map)** — e2 sigmoid truth, OLS_f, Bernoulli(0.5): bias +2.0 on SBM/ER/WS (GATE 3) and *negative* (−1.4…−1.5) at p = 0.1 — the sign of the extrapolation bias flips with p; Louvain clusters remove it on SBM/WS (bias 0.03/0.08) but not on ER (1.10, no community structure). e7, Bernoulli(0.5), mean over the 6 graphs: relative bias of OLS_f for neighbour-count −0.22, 2-hop −0.29, heterogeneous direct effect ≈ 0; propagation ρ = 0.9 (equilibrium propagation, near-singular): mean RMSE 7.8 for OLS_f, DM 8.9, GBM 8.7, OLS_big 9.8 — no estimator tested is safe there; ρ = 0.3 is benign (mean RMSE 0.56, DM 0.43). Real graphs: Stanford3 and ego-FB were run in every grid; ego-FB has the largest errors (hubs, n = 4k) except in cluster designs.

## Caveats and negatives that must be stated in the paper
- Not tested in e1–e10 (both were run afterwards, see "Post-freeze additions": H3g supported, H6c/H6d unclear by the pre-stated rules): dependence of the bias on interference strength γ in a misspecified grid (H3), Hájek bias under a wrong exposure model (H6); still untested: derivation of the bias beyond the closed form for the linear neighbour-fraction regression under Bernoulli designs. GNN/other learners and staggered rollout were cut as planned.
- ORACLE in e7 lacks the degree term and is therefore *not* correctly specified there (baselines depend on degree); use ORACLE_D. For the propagation mechanism no oracle exists (NaN).
- e8/e9 are noiseless; no coverage claim from them. e6 BA/ER analysis-resolution-0.5 cells are degenerate (above).
- 4 worlds per cell: for synthetic graphs the graph is redrawn per world, for real graphs only the baseline and assignment vary, so real-graph cells understate between-graph variability.
- Single seed per cell; `bias_se` is the larger of the replicate-level and the between-world SE.
- Host stalls (cells with `secs` ≥ 10⁴) affect timing only, not results.

## Checks that were actually run (for the AI-use / verification statement)
A unit-test suite exists since the second freeze, part 3 (`code/tests/`, 16 tests; see "Delivery reproducibility" below). Before it, and in addition, the following checks were run and are reproducible from the scripts:
1. **Closed-form check (H2).** The exact-binomial bias formula (`theory_bias.py`) was compared with the simulated noiseless bias of OLS_f in all 144 e9 cells: correlation 1.0000, max deviation 0.041, all within 3 MC SE (`theory_vs_sim_e9.csv`).
2. **Noiseless-oracle check (H1).** The correctly specified regression in the noiseless grid e8 recovers the GATE to 2.4e-11 in all 120 cells, which verifies the exposure computation, the potential-outcome function and the GATE truth against each other.
3. **Scaling check (H4).** Fitted exponents −0.501 ± 0.005 and +0.495 ± 0.006 against the analytic −1/2, +1/2.
4. **Reproducibility check.** `code/scripts/package_delivery.py` was run to a clean directory (`code/`, `results/` layout); `aggregate.py`, `make_tables.py` and `findings.py` run from there gave identical tables (only whitespace differences in printed output). Aggregation does not change under sharding (cells are independent; resumable runs).
5. **Bug found by inspection, fixed, and rerun:** a Louvain-partition cache keyed only by graph family gave wrong labels across synthetic worlds; the affected launch was discarded and every grid was rerun (see `REPRODUCE.md` §2).
The estimators themselves were not re-implemented independently; the HC0 and cluster-robust variance estimators are checked against statsmodels and the one-hop HAC by hand in `code/tests/` (second freeze, part 3); the two-hop HAC and the Hájek variance have no external check.

## What was not done
- GNN / other learners and staggered rollout (cut per the plan's order); no grid cell was cut: `COMPLETENESS.csv` shows 1622/1622 cells (806 in e1–e10, 389 in e11–e13, 427 in e14–e21).
- (First freeze) γ not varied under a misspecified outcome and Hájek baselines unestimable at the e1–e10 degrees; both addressed by the post-freeze grids e11/e12; no explicit comparison to an external package for variance estimators (added later, see part 3); single seed per cell (the second seed e13 covers H2, H4, H5 only); no outcome data other than simulated.

## Post-freeze additions (second freeze: part 1 e11 and e12, part 2 e13, part 3 e14–e21)
These grids were run **after** the first freeze and after seeing its results, at the request of `story-repo` (experiment-repo#7). The decision rules (H3g, H6c, H6d) were written in `code/scripts/post_freeze.py` before any e11 cell and before the e12 bias values were read (my account; the script was first *committed* at ac8dfe8, after the results existed, so the history cannot prove it; the later edits to the script only added the labelled post-hoc stratification rows and a guard for the missing e13 summary, no threshold changed); the predictions are those of the plan (H3: bias grows with strength × curvature; H6: exposure-mapping baselines are unbiased only under a correct mapping). The first-freeze summaries (`e1`–`e10`) are unchanged. New files: `summary/e11.csv`, `summary/e12.csv`, `tables/t14_gamma_dependence.csv`, `tables/t15_hajek.csv`, `hypotheses_postfreeze.csv` (the frozen `hypotheses.csv` is untouched). Cells: e11 64, e12 108 (4 × 100 each); the frozen total of 806 refers to e1–e10.

**H3g — bias of the misspecified regression vs interference strength γ (e11): supported.** SBM, BA, Stanford3, ego-FB × {concave, sigmoid} × Bernoulli(0.5/0.1) × γ ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 4}, noisy, 400 estimates per cell. Prediction (pre-stated): E[bias] ∝ γ, because the baseline/noise do not depend on γ and OLS is linear in y. Rule: weighted line bias = a + b·γ, proportional if R² ≥ 0.99 and |a| ≤ 3·max(SE_a, 0.02); supported if ≥ 75% of the groups with |bias(γ=4)| > 0.2. Result for OLS_f: **14/16 groups proportional**; slopes per unit γ (SE of slope 0.005–0.037, median 0.010): SBM concave −0.25 (p=0.5) / +1.26 (p=0.1), sigmoid +1.01 / −0.71; BA concave −0.23 / +1.20, sigmoid +0.89 / −0.68; Stanford3 concave −0.08 / +0.51, sigmoid +0.29 / −0.22; ego-FB concave −0.12 / +0.88, sigmoid +0.48 / −0.40 (`t14_gamma_dependence.csv`, rows est_name = OLS_f). The sign and size depend on shape × p exactly as in e2/e9, only the scale is γ. The two failing groups are both ego-FB (concave, p = 0.5: R² 0.96 with a small bias, −0.12γ; sigmoid, p = 0.1: intercept +0.20 ± 0.06, 3.1 SE). Correctly specified ORACLE_D: |bias| ≤ 0.17 at γ = 4 in all 16 groups (max |intercept| 0.12); DM bias = −γ·(1.00 ± 0.003) in all 16 groups. **Negative:** OLS_quad does *not* follow the prediction at p = 0.1: it has a γ-independent bias of −9…−10 (SBM, BA), −6 (ego-FB), −1.9 (Stanford3) at every γ ≥ 0.5 (intercepts in `t14`), the extrapolation/finite-sample instability already seen in e10, not a spillover effect. The plan's "grows with strength × curvature" is now tested for strength (linear in γ) and curvature (e9, first freeze).

**H6 — exposure-mapping (Hájek) baselines under correct and wrong exposure mappings (e12).** ER (mean degree 4, 8), WS (k = 4, 8) and BA (m = 2, 4), n = 3000 (largest component), Bernoulli(0.5), Bernoulli(0.8) and Louvain-cluster designs; mechanisms: threshold `step` with κ = 1 and κ = 0.75 (HajekFull's mapping is correct for both, HajekKappa(0.75)'s only for κ = 0.75), linear and sigmoid 1-hop shape, two-hop, propagation (ρ = 0.6); noisy; GATE = 3 for the step/shape mechanisms (γ = 2, τ = 1). The estimators are **estimable** here: share of cells with n_rep ≥ 200 is 0.89 (HajekFull) and 0.92 (HajekKappa); all non-estimable cells are Bernoulli(0.8), where control-exposed units are rare (n_rep as low as 203 of 400). HajekKappa is not defined for cluster designs. Pre-stated rules: H6c "unbiased under a correct mapping" = |bias| ≤ max(3 SE, 0.1) in ≥ 75% of estimable cells; H6d "biased under a wrong mapping" = |bias| > max(3 SE, 0.2) in ≥ 75% (HajekFull on two-hop/propagation; HajekKappa on linear/sigmoid shape and on step κ = 1).
- **H6c: unclear by the rule (31/43 cells, 72%)**; the failures are all Bernoulli(0.8). Post-hoc stratification (same cells): Bernoulli(0.5) 18/18, Louvain clusters 12/12, **Bernoulli(0.8) 1/13** — HajekFull has bias +0.5…+0.7 and HajekKappa +0.28 there (GATE 3), a finite-sample ratio bias from the few fully control-exposed units (probability 0.2^(d+1)). So: unbiased under a correct mapping when both exposure types are common, not otherwise.
- **H6d: unclear by the rule (44/65, 68%).** Post-hoc stratification: Bernoulli(0.5) 24/30, Bernoulli(0.8) 12/23, clusters 8/12. At Bernoulli(0.5) HajekFull is biased on two-hop (−0.88 mean, 6/6) and propagation (−0.65, 6/6), HajekKappa on linear spillover (−0.57, 6/6) and on the κ = 1 step (−1.38, 6/6); **HajekKappa on the sigmoid shape is not biased** (−0.13, 0/6) because f ≥ 0.75 already lies on the saturated part. HajekFull is *not* wrong on 1-hop shapes (its full-exposure contrast equals the GATE for any h): bias 0.03 (linear), −0.01 (sigmoid) at Bernoulli(0.5).
- **H6e (descriptive):** Hájek has lower RMSE than OLS_f in 32% (HajekFull, 96 cells) and 30% (HajekKappa, 66 cells) of the estimable cells; the correctly specified regression with the degree term (ORACLE_D, `t15_hajek.csv`) is unbiased (|bias| ≤ 0.04 in every cell) for the step, shape and two-hop mechanisms (ORACLE without the degree term is biased, as in e7) (it contains the 2-hop term).
Reading for the paper: the exposure-mapping baselines work as advertised only when the mapping is right *and* both extreme exposures are well populated; with a wrong mapping they are biased by the size of the mismatch, and they are not more accurate than the plain regression in most cells. All numbers: `results/summary/e12.csv` (rows est_name HajekFull/HajekKappa), `results/tables/t15_hajek.csv`.

**#10(a) Variance law across graphs (`t17`, `t17b`, `t17c`).** Prediction for OLS_f under independent errors and Bernoulli(p): Var(τ̂) ≈ σ_r²(1 + d_h)/(n p (1−p)), d_h = 1/mean(1/degree) of the largest component, σ_r² = Var(baseline + noise). *Negative for the default outcome:* with the full default outcome (graph-smooth baseline, log-degree baseline, noise) neither d_h nor the mean degree d orders the six graphs: observed/predicted SD is 1.29 (ER), 1.44 (BA), 2.40 (SBM), 2.60 (Stanford3), 2.59 (WS) and 5.1 (ego-FB); max/min 3.97 with d_h and 2.96 with d; Spearman rank correlation of prediction and observation 0.03 for both. *The law holds when the errors are independent* (e21, γ = 0, noise only): observed/predicted[d_h] = 1.00 ± 0.04 on all six graphs (max/min 1.12; Stanford3 SD 0.064 against 0.064 predicted), whereas the mean degree gives max/min 2.82 (Stanford3 0.185 predicted, 0.064 observed). Within the ER and BA families of e3 the law holds with a log-ratio SD of 0.058 (d_h) and 0.046 (d) over 25 ER + 12 BA cells. The excess comes from the graph-smooth baseline (SD of OLS_f with it ÷ without it: ego-FB 7.2×, SBM 3.7×, WS 3.9×, Stanford3 3.6×, ER 1.8×, BA 2.1×) and, for ego-FB and Stanford3, from the log-degree baseline (obs/pred 4.2 and 1.6 with it alone; `t17c`). **Why Stanford3 equals ER at SD ≈ 0.26:** its d_h is 10.8 (mean degree 98; many low-degree nodes), which alone would give a *lower* SD than ER (d_h 19), and its graph-smooth baseline inflates the SD by 2.6× instead of ER's 1.3×; the two effects cancel. A statement "SD ordered by mean degree" or "by d_h" is therefore not supported for the default outcome; d_h is right only for independent errors.

**#10(b) Stanford3 cluster-design mechanism (`t18_exposure_variation.csv`).** The explanation given in H5 ("good clusters make exposure ≈ treatment, so the regression has little exposure variation") is **supported for SBM and ego-FB and not supported for Stanford3**. Stanford3, Louvain clusters: within-arm Var(f) is 0.021–0.028 (resolutions 1–20), i.e. about the Bernoulli value 0.0232, even though corr(z, f) is 0.70–0.88 and Var(f) is 0.055–0.093; random clusters of the same size leave within-arm Var(f) at 0.020–0.023. So the regression has the same residual exposure variation as under Bernoulli; the SD inflation (0.26 → 0.49–0.60 at resolutions 1–10) comes from there being few clusters (10 at resolutions 0.5 and 1; 630 at resolution 20: SD 0.33) and from cluster-level dependence of the treated share. SBM: within-arm 0.0046 vs 0.013 under Bernoulli, corr 0.985; ego-FB 0.0035–0.040 vs 0.0225, corr 0.84–0.99 — there the mechanism holds. The H5 sentence on line 12 should be read with this correction: the Stanford3 reversal is real (H5s) but the stated reason is not.

Linear truth, γ = 2: OLS_f SD 0.259 (BA), 0.243 (cmba), 0.263 (ER), 0.236 (regular), 0.147/0.157/0.163 (power laws), bias |·| ≤ 0.017 everywhere. **BA vs its degree-matched configuration model: no difference (SD 0.259 vs 0.243, within MC noise; concave bias −0.452 ± 0.012 vs −0.487 ± 0.018), so no BA-specific structure effect beyond the degree sequence.** Heavier tails at matched mean degree *lower* the SD of OLS_f (ratio power law/ER 0.56–0.62, in the direction of d_h; observed/predicted 1.2–1.6, as expected from the baseline), and under concave truth they lower the bias too (−0.12…−0.14 against −0.45…−0.52 for BA/cmba/ER/regular). **H8a (heavy-tailed graphs have higher RMSE; refuted at the first freeze on graphs that differed in n and d, `hypotheses.csv`) is confirmed as refuted by a test that holds n and the mean degree fixed: no heavy-tail penalty for the SD of OLS_f.** Caveat: the power laws also change the share of low-degree nodes, so the bias reduction is a property of the whole degree distribution (not tested separately).

**#14(1) Crossover γ* and the variance ratio (e17, `t21`, `t21b`).** SBM, Bernoulli(0.5), linear truth, n ∈ {1k, 5k, 20k} × mean degree ∈ {10, 20, 40} × σ ∈ {0.5, 1, 2} × γ ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2}. **#15(1,2) Uncertainty with 4 worlds (`t20`, `t20b`, `t20c`; e18 = 20 worlds × 25 for six graphs × linear/concave).** Between-world variance of the error of OLS_f is small: ICC median 0.000–0.004, max 0.035 (e18 concave) and 0.016 (e2); ORACLE_D has larger ICC (max 0.165 e18, 0.105 e2 concave); DM ≈ 0.
