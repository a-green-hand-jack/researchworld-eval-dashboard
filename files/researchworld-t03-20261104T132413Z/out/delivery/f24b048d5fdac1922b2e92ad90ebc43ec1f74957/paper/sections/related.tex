\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Estimating Shapley-value attributions.}
Kernel SHAP \citep{lundberg2017unified} estimates Shapley values by a weighted regression on sampled coalitions, and is exact once all coalitions are enumerated.
\citet{covert2020improving} analyse its estimator, propose paired sampling as a variance reduction, and give convergence detection and uncertainty estimates for it; \citet{slack2020reliable} give a Bayesian variant of Kernel SHAP and LIME with credible intervals;
\citet{mitchell2021sampling} study permutation sampling for Shapley values and \citet{musco2024leverage} give a leverage-score variant with non-asymptotic guarantees.
These works establish that the estimator is random and how its error can shrink with the budget; we take the error--budget relation as expected (Section~\ref{sec:exp}) and ask a different question: whether \emph{one} run at a budget a practitioner actually uses supports the explanation that is shown, judged by decision-level quantities (top-$k$ sets, signs), against exact references and against other sources of variation.
For tree ensembles the interventional value function admits fast exact computation \citep{lundberg2019trees}; we use this only as a labelled comparison standard.

Instability under random sampling has been studied most for LIME: repeated explanations of the same instance disagree, and stability indices and deterministic variants have been proposed \citep{visani2022stability,zafar2021deterministic}. For Shapley-value estimators the corresponding evidence is the variance analysis above; what is new here is the top-$k$ reliability of one run at the budgets a user selects, compared with the variation caused by retraining and by the background.

\paragraph{What is being estimated.}
The attribution depends on the value function, e.g.\ interventional versus observational conditional expectations \citep{chen2020true}, and on the background set \citep{yuan2022background}, who report fluctuation of SHAP values and rankings across random backgrounds for deep models on one clinical dataset.
We fix the marginal (interventional) value function and a fixed background, so that estimator noise is separated from definitional choices, and treat background redraws as a separate source of variation.

\paragraph{Disagreement, robustness and multiplicity.}
\citet{krishna2022disagreement} study disagreement between different explanation methods on the same model and define rank-, sign- and feature-agreement measures, which we reuse. \citet{alvarezmelis2018robustness} study robustness of explanations to input perturbation, and \citet{slack2019fooling} show that LIME and SHAP can be fooled by classifiers that behave differently on the off-manifold samples these explainers query. Predictive multiplicity \citep{marx2020predictive} documents that equally accurate models can disagree on individual predictions; we use retrained models as a yardstick for how much attribution variation a user already tolerates.

\paragraph{Stability measures.}
We compare top-$k$ sets across runs with the chance-corrected stability of \citet{nogueira2018stability} and rankings with rank-biased overlap \citep{webber2010rbo}.
