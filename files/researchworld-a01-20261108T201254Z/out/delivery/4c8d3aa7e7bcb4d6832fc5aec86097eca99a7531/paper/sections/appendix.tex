\section{Reproducibility and Provenance}
\label{app:repro}

Everything in the paper is produced by the code in the delivery (directory \texttt{code/}, with \texttt{code/README.md} as the full reproduction notes, and \texttt{figtab/} for figures and tables). Every number quoted in the text comes from the compact result files in \texttt{code/results/analysis/}, and every figure and table of the paper is rebuilt from them (\texttt{figtab/scripts/make\_all.sh}). The per-instance solver results (one row per target, cap and rule; about 209~MB, 12~MB gzip-compressed) are in the delivery as \texttt{code/results/instances/*.csv.gz}, except the one large file \texttt{ollb\_powmeans\_weighted.csv} (131~MB), which \texttt{reproduce.sh} regenerates. A manifest (\texttt{code/results/MANIFEST.md}) lists the SHA-256 and a one-line convention of every result file. \texttt{bash code/reproduce.sh} (offline, CPU only) unpacks the instance files, regenerates the large file, rebuilds all of \texttt{code/results/analysis/}, runs the closed-form checks and compares every file with the manifest; on a 2-CPU container it took 1844~s wall (1575~s CPU), and 89 of 91 files were byte-identical, the other two differing only in a measured CPU-time column that the manifest hash excludes. This path rebuilds the analysis tables and checks from the per-instance results; it does not re-run the ordinal mixed-integer, HELM, noise and bootstrap solver jobs that produced them, which \texttt{make experiments} does. \texttt{paper/scripts/check\_numbers.py} checks that the numbers quoted in the text agree with the compact result files.

Table~\ref{tab:data} gives the versions and full checksums of the inputs. The evaluation-noise model on OLLB needs only per-task item counts, which we took from one results file because every model is evaluated on the same items of each task (one count vector in 60 randomly sampled models; the file is pinned in the table).

\paragraph{Environment, seeds and commands.}
Python 3.11.2, NumPy 2.4.6, SciPy 1.17.1 (bundled HiGHS), pandas 3.0.6, pyarrow 26.0.0; CPU only. All runs are deterministic given the seeds: rank-stratified target sample seed 0 (ranks 2--21 plus 150 log-uniform in rank, 136 distinct targets), Dirichlet weights seed $1000+k$, Monte Carlo draws seed 0 (100 draws for evaluation noise and task bootstrap, 50 for task subsets), bootstrap over targets $B=1000$ seed 0. The commands are \texttt{make tests}, \texttt{make experiments}, \texttt{make analysis}, \texttt{make theory} and \texttt{make manifest} (see \texttt{code/README.md}); the ten solver unit tests compare the solvers with brute force on small random instances.

\paragraph{Compute.}
The measured solver CPU time, summed over 260{,}572 solves, is 3.25 CPU-hours (OLLB score rules on all 4496 targets and eight caps 0.26; OLLB power and weighted means 0.06; OLLB Borda and Condorcet on the 136-target sample 1.78; plurality 0.13; HELM 0.27; slices, $\varepsilon$ and tie variants 0.77). There were no solver time-outs, so no censoring correction was needed. The scheduler's accounting of all jobs of the study, including data builds, tests, aborted and repeated runs, is about 7.7 CPU-hours, above the rough 4 CPU-hour estimate in the task statement; the required grid fits in the estimate, the robustness extras and development reruns do not. The job logs sum to about 4.9 hours of wall time on a shared, oversubscribed host, so wall time and CPU time are not comparable; re-running all jobs with \texttt{make experiments} is a matter of hours (the scheduler's total is 7.7 CPU-hours), not days.

\paragraph{Checks of the closed forms.}
The closed forms of Propositions~\ref{prop:mean}--\ref{prop:median} were coded independently of the solvers and compared with solver output on every instance (\texttt{code/results/theory\_checks.txt}). Feasibility and $K$ agree on all instances of the mean, minimum and median (HELM) and the mean and minimum (OLLB); the largest absolute difference in $L_1$ is $2.3\times10^{-13}$. The bounds $L_\infty\le L_1\le nL_\infty$ and $1\le K\le n$ hold on all 98{,}148 instances, and the identity $L_1=n(\text{gap}+\varepsilon)$ for the mean holds to machine precision over 499 random task subsets.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
All code, experiments, analyses and this manuscript were produced by AI agents (Claude models) working autonomously in separate roles (context, planning, analysis, presentation, writing); no human wrote or ran any of it. The study plan from which the questions of Section~\ref{sec:results} come was written after pilot runs existed and is not a pre-registration.

\paragraph{Licences.}
The data are public leaderboard scores. The HELM storage bucket and the OLLB \texttt{contents} and \texttt{results} datasets declare no dataset licence (the HELM code is Apache-2.0; the OLLB \texttt{contents} card has a per-model licence column only). We used only aggregate scores for research analysis and did not redistribute raw files; the derived score matrices committed with the code carry their sources and checksums. We treat this as permissible research use of public data; this is our judgement, not a legal opinion, and anyone redistributing the processed matrices should check the upstream terms.

\paragraph{Statement on withheld material.}
The task statement names a published paper on this question that must not be searched for, accessed or used. We did not search for it, and no one involved reported having met it. The analysis made no literature or web search (only the scripted data downloads from the two hosts above). Literature searches for the related work (social-choice bribery, manipulation and control, leaderboard sensitivity, benchmark aggregation) returned no item identified as that paper, and no hit was opened beyond a title or abstract snippet except the full text of the starter-list item of \citet{chen2026incentives}; for a work on the sensitivity of language-model leaderboards that a search returned we saw only its title, authors and an abstract snippet, did not read it, and do not cite it. We cannot rule out that a page we did not read is related to the withheld paper.

\section{Checks Added After Review}
\label{app:checks}
\paragraph{Composition of the empirical cap.}
Of the 540 pairs, 154 are typed chat (RLHF, DPO, IFT), 346 domain-specific fine-tunes, and 40 other (24 pretrained, 13 continuously pretrained, 2 merges, 1 other); the self-declared \texttt{Type} does not say whether a ``domain'' fine-tune is an instruction conversion, so we also drop pairs whose model name contains a chat-like token (263 pairs; 207 remain after removing both). The \texttt{emp95} values per task are, for all pairs / without chat-typed pairs / without chat-typed or chat-named pairs: IFEval 46.0 / 44.6 / 55.7, BBH 7.3 / 7.0 / 7.9, MATH Lvl~5 21.3 / 19.4 / 18.8, GPQA 3.8 / 3.9 / 3.1, MuSR 7.2 / 7.2 / 6.9, MMLU-PRO 3.2 / 3.2 / 3.2 (\texttt{cap\_composition\_pairs.csv}). Table~\ref{tab:capvar} gives the share of targets that can become first. The ordering of the rules and the confinement of feasibility to roughly the top 500 targets hold under every variant; the absolute shares do not: across the definitions of Table~\ref{tab:capvar} they range from 1.4 to 12.2\% for the mean, 1.7 to 10.0\% for the median, 0.4 to 16.0\% for plurality and 0.8 to 6.1\% for the minimum, which reaches 100\% under \texttt{head0.25}. Not done: a cap calibrated on pairs verified from model cards as base-to-domain fine-tunes (no such data are supplied).

\paragraph{Fits for the growth with rank.}
For each rule and dataset we fitted the median $\LL$ at \texttt{ceil} against rank with a power law, a linear form and a logarithmic form, to the group medians and to the targets (\texttt{rank\_dependence\_fits.csv}). By the relative error (rmse of $\log_{10}\LL$) the power law is preferred in 25 of 32 OLLB and 16 of 18 HELM group-level fits, and in 21 of 32 OLLB and all 31 HELM target-level fits; by $R^2$ on the $\LL$ scale a linear form is preferred in 14 of 32 OLLB and 17 of 18 HELM group-level fits. For the mean on OLLB the exponent is 0.44 ($R^2$ 0.954, against 0.980 for the linear form). The HELM fits rest on four or five groups and the exponent of 3.1 for the Condorcet requirement comes from near-zero costs at ranks 2--5. Group medians are monotone in rank in all 31 HELM cells and in 19 of 32 OLLB cells; for the median rule on OLLB the decrease (9.6 and 8.4 points at ranks 2 and 3--5, 0.3 at ranks 6--10) lies outside the bootstrap interval, for the minimum, harmonic mean, plurality and Condorcet requirement it lies within it. The first two groups hold one and three to five targets.

\paragraph{Duplicate model names.}
All 79 duplicated names are float16/bfloat16 pairs (36 share a submission date). Re-running the mean, median and minimum at \texttt{ceil} and the mean at \texttt{emp95} with the first, the latest or the worst-scoring variant instead of the best one leaves the leader and the runner-up unchanged for every rule and moves the group medians of $\LL$ by at most 5.3\% (largest: minimum at ranks 31--100, 13.8 to 14.5 points; \texttt{latest} at most 2.2\%); the share of infeasible targets at ranks 101--500 under the mean with \texttt{emp95} is 41\% (best), 38\% (first), 45\% (latest) and 43\% (worst) (\texttt{duplicate\_rule\_sensitivity.csv}, \texttt{duplicate\_rule\_leaders.csv}). The other robustness cells were not re-run.

\paragraph{HELM test-set sizes.}
The 57 subjects have 100 to 1534 test items (10 subjects have 100, the median is 173, the total 14\,042; identical for all 79 models), so one item is 1.0 point on a 100-item subject and 0.07 on the largest; the binomial standard error of a subject accuracy of 50\% is 5.0, 3.8 and 1.3 points for 100, 173 and 1534 items (\texttt{helm\_subject\_sizes.csv}). A jump on one subject can thus be within evaluation noise for small subjects.

\paragraph{Score-cap defence.}
Clipping every model's per-task score at the leader's score plus $h$ points ($h=5,10,20$) leaves the share of targets that can become first at 100\% for the mean, median and minimum on both datasets and the cost unchanged (median $\LL$ at $h=5$: OLLB 180.5, 109.1, 43.2; HELM 696, 113, 98), because reaching the clip on every task is still enough. At $h=0$ nobody can become strictly first, a tie rather than a higher price. Clips at the 99th, 95th or 90th percentile of each task also clip the leader; the share falls to 0 for the median and minimum on OLLB at all three levels, for the same rules on HELM at the 95th and 90th, and for the OLLB mean at the 90th (the HELM mean stays at 100\%), but this is the strict-first rule meeting tied scores, not a higher cost of first place in points, so we do not use these rows for cost statements. Per-model clips and tie-allowed first place were not run (\texttt{score\_cap\_defence.csv}).
