\section*{AI-use statement}
This research was carried out end to end by a system of cooperating large-language-model agents (models of the Claude family), each holding one function: literature and data context, study planning and feasibility checking, analysis code and experiments, figures and tables, venue formatting, report writing, and independent internal and external review. The tasks for which ICLR requires disclosure were all done with generative AI: the research questions and hypotheses, the methodology, the theoretical derivations and proofs, the implementation, data cleaning and matrix construction, the generation of the synthetic matrices used for theory checks, the interpretation of results, and the drafting of this paper. The figures, code edits, literature search and polishing were also AI-assisted. No task was performed without generative AI assistance. No human wrote or edited text or code in the work reported here, and no human checked its content before this version; the verification that exists is mechanical and by other agents: every number, table and figure is generated by the shipped scripts from the pinned data, review agents independently read the code, the results and the draft against the task requirements, and cited works were looked up in public bibliographic records. These checks cannot exclude errors. No human author reviewed or takes responsibility for this work; the checks above are the only verification. This document is a research deliverable prepared in the ICLR 2027 format; it was not submitted to ICLR, and a real submission would require human authors who review the work and accept the responsibility that the ICLR AI policy places on authors.

\section*{Reproducibility statement}
All results come from the code shipped with the submission (\texttt{code/}, run from the repository root) on the pinned data versions listed in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}. Seeds, the commands for each run group, environment versions, measured wall-clock times and the script that produces every table and figure are listed in the repository \texttt{README.md} and in \texttt{results/README.md}. All computation is CPU-only. Raw per-model result tables are not in git: they are published as the release \texttt{raw-results-1} of the experiment repository (compressed parts with checksums) and can be regenerated by the launch scripts; the summaries under \texttt{results/summary} are the inputs of every table and figure, and \texttt{code/check.sh} reproduces a small run end to end.

\section*{Ethics statement}
The study uses only public leaderboard results and synthetic matrices; no human subjects or personal data are involved. Several of the leaderboards used declare no data licence (Open LLM Leaderboard, LiveBench); we use their scores for research and redistribute only derived score matrices of small size. Predicting benchmark scores from a few evaluations could be misused to avoid evaluations that matter for safety or contamination checks; our results concern average score prediction on the leaderboards studied and say nothing about tail behaviour of individual models, and we recommend running the benchmarks that matter for a decision rather than inferring them.
