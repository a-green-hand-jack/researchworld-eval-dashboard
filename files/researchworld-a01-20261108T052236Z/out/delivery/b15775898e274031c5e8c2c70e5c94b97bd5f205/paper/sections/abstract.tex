A developer can try to put one model first on a multi-task leaderboard by improving it on some of the sub-datasets. How many score points does that take, and does the answer depend on how the leaderboard aggregates?
We define investment as points added on chosen sub-datasets with all other models fixed and each score capped, measure it three ways (tasks touched, total points, largest single gain; each minimised exactly), and solve it for six score rules and three rank rules on every non-leading model of HELM MMLU (\num{helm.ceil.mean.ntar} targets, 57 tasks) and the Open LLM Leaderboard (\num{ollb.ceil.mean.ntar} targets, 6 tasks).
The answer depends on the rule, the cost measure and above all the per-task cap. With no cap the median total cost on HELM is \num{helm.ceil.mean.L1} points under the mean and \num{helm.ceil.median.L1} under the median; on HELM the median and minimum rules are five to ten times cheaper than the mean in total points, on the OLLB census only the minimum is several times cheaper (about two times after normalising by each rule's score scale), and in the largest single gain none is. The smallest uniform gain per task at which half of the targets can reach first place is \num{gs.helm.crossmin}--\num{gs.helm.crossmax} points on HELM and \num{gs.ollb.crossmin}--\num{gs.ollb.crossmax} on OLLB depending on the rule, in a different order on the two leaderboards. With 10 points per task only \num{ollb.gain10.mean.feas}\% (mean) of OLLB targets can reach first place. Raising every task by the nominal minimum puts a target first in a median of only \num{r2.ollb.obs1.p0min}--\num{r2.ollb.obs1.p0max} of noise draws on OLLB and \num{r2.helm.obs1.p0min}--\num{r2.helm.obs1.p0max} on HELM (\num{r2.helm.obs1.ntar} targets per dataset, noise with the published sampling error); reaching 90\% takes a further \num{r2.ollb.obs1.x90min_nomin}--\num{r2.ollb.obs1.x90max_nomin} points per task, and more for the minimum rule.
The caps are illustrative and not calibrated to what data buy in points. Results hold for these two leaderboards, one deviating developer, fixed rivals and costs in leaderboard points.
