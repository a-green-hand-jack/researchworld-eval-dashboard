\paragraph{Licences.}
Neither source declares a licence for the scores: the \texttt{contents} dataset card has no licence field (it carries each model's own hub licence as a column), \texttt{results} has no card, and we found no separate statement for the HELM published results (the HELM code is Apache-2.0). \section{Closed forms and their check}\label{app:theory}
For the mean, with $R=nb-\sum_t x_t$ and $h_t=c_t-x_t$: $L_1=R$ when $\sum_t h_t\ge R$ (and does not depend on how the points are placed); $K$ is the least $k$ for which the $k$ largest headrooms sum to at least $R$; $L_\infty$ is the level $d$ with $\sum_t\min(d,h_t)=R$. For the minimum: $K=|\{t:x_t<b\}|$, $L_1=\sum_t(b-x_t)^+$, $L_\infty=b-\min_t x_t$, infeasible iff some $x_t<b$ has $c_t<b$. For the median at the ceiling cap, raising the $k$ lowest tasks to 100 is optimal, and for odd $n$ with $c=|\{t:x_t\ge b\}|$, $K=\max(0,(n+1)/2-c)$. $K$ equals $\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1$ for only a small share of targets, so the blanket statements ``the median needs $\lceil n/2\rceil+1$ tasks'' and ``the minimum needs all $n$ tasks'' are false. Table~\ref{tab:theory} compares these with the solvers on every target at the ceiling cap. The agreement is numerical: \num{theory.mismatch} mismatches in \num{theory.cells} cells. The closed forms are validated only at \textsf{ceil}; with general caps one replaces 100 by $c_t$, and the solver is what we ran. For even $n$ (OLLB) only $K$ of the median has a closed form.

\section{Additional analyses added after external review}\label{app:newanalyses}
\paragraph{Definitions.} \emph{Leader gap}: the best rival's rule score minus the target's rule score (zero if the target already leads); \emph{IQR}: the interquartile range of the rule score over all models; normalised costs are $L_1/\text{gap}$ and $(L_1/n)/\text{IQR}$, defined only for rules with a score in task-point units (mean, geometric, harmonic, median, minimum, trimmed mean). \emph{Rank-matched}: the ratio of a rule's median cost to the mean's among targets whose rank under that rule falls in the same bin. \emph{Replay}: uniform raise of every task by $d$, capped, against $R=200$ noise draws shared across rules, targets and budgets; $p_0$ is the share of draws in which the target is strictly first at the nominal $L_\infty$, and $x_{90}$ the further raise until that share reaches 0.9 (censored beyond 16 points). \emph{Shrunk centring}: noise is drawn around $T=m+\lambda(X-m)$ with $m$ the column mean and $\lambda=\max(0,1-\overline{\mathrm{SE}^2}/\mathrm{Var}(X))$, with homoscedastic Gaussian noise and one prior mean per task. \emph{Cluster}: the \texttt{Base Model} field of the OLLB table; a model whose base is a placeholder or itself is its own cluster. Intervals are percentile bootstrap over targets (B=2000, seed 20261108) unless the caption says Wilson, draw quantile or cluster bootstrap.

\paragraph{Sizes of the noise runs.} The plan fixed 200 draws per run; some runs are smaller, and none of them is a sample of the whole leaderboard. Replay (Section~\ref{sec:noise}): 200 draws, 36 targets per dataset; Figures~\ref{fig:replay-ollb} and \ref{fig:replay-helm} use the earlier 6 targets (ranks 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20). Re-optimised noise runs at \textsf{gain10}: 100 draws, the 10 best-ranked non-leaders on HELM and the 20 best-ranked on OLLB, not a stratified sample; re-run for the score rules with 200 draws and the study seed, the median costs move by at most \num{nsc.maxabs.ollb} (OLLB) and \num{nsc.maxabs.helm} points (HELM), inside the bootstrap intervals. HELM ordinal rules were not re-run with 200 draws since each draw costs about 155\,s of exact optimisation. Bootstrap over tasks: 100 draws on HELM and 200 on OLLB. Task-subset and random-weight runs: 50 draws. The first noise and bootstrap runs used the script's default seed 0 (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
\paragraph{Number of tasks and cross-task correlation.} On HELM we draw 50 random subsets of $n\in\{3,6,12,24,48,57\}$ subjects and recompute the cost for a target of rank 5 within the subset, for the observed matrix and for a control in which each task column is permuted over models, which removes cross-task correlation (Figure~\ref{fig:noise}a; the $L_1/n$ and $K/n$ curves for ranks 2 and 5 are in Figure~\ref{fig:taskscale}). In the observed matrix the mean's $L_1$ rises from $\num{nsweep.obs.mean.3.L1}$ at $n=3$ to $\num{nsweep.obs.mean.57.L1}$ at $n=57$, roughly in proportion to $n$, as the closed form predicts for a gap that persists across tasks. In the shuffled matrix it rises only to $\num{nsweep.shuf.mean.57.L1}$ at $n=57$: with correlation removed the average over many tasks concentrates and a target's gap shrinks with $n$. The median behaves in the opposite way: its $L_1$ falls with $n$ in the observed matrix ($\num{nsweep.obs.median.12.L1}$ at $n=12$, $\num{nsweep.obs.median.57.L1}$ at $n=57$). The number of tasks $K$ grows slowly (mean: $\num{nsweep.obs.mean.3.K}$ at $n=3$ to $\num{nsweep.obs.mean.57.K}$ at $n=57$), so $K/n$ falls. We read these as a comparison of curves with and without correlation, not as fitted exponents, and as a within-benchmark proxy for $n$, since subsets of MMLU subjects are not independent leaderboards.
