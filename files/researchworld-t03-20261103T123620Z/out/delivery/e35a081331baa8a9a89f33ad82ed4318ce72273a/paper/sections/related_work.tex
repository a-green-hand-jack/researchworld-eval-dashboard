\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Finite-budget Shapley estimation.}
The SHAP framework connects additive feature attribution to Shapley values and provides model-agnostic sampling and weighted-regression approximations \citep{lundberg2017unified}. Its axiomatic properties characterize a specified target, whereas a finite stochastic estimate also has numerical error. Its original computational evaluation already compares estimates with reference values as model-evaluation count grows. \citet{covert2021improving} analyze regression-based Shapley estimators, derive an unbiased alternative, and develop paired sampling, uncertainty estimation and convergence detection. \citet{musco2025} propose leverage-score sampling with a regression-objective guarantee and a derived coefficient-error bound under their assumptions. Such guarantees do not directly establish signed top-feature recovery for every prediction. Our comparison covers Kernel SHAP and paired permutation sampling; it does not rank all newer estimators. Budget dependence, estimator variability and uncertainty estimation are established concerns. The present study targets the probability that one run meets explicit magnitude and salient-feature criteria at measured cost.

\paragraph{Different sources of disagreement.}
\citet{chen2020true} distinguish observational and marginal attribution games and show why their suitability depends on the application. \citet{krishna2024} study disagreement among distinct explanation methods, using practitioner-motivated feature, rank and sign comparisons. \citet{yuan2023} vary DeepExplainer background samples and examine local magnitude variation and global ranking stability. These comparisons change the method or background; our primary repeated-run question holds both fixed. A background sensitivity is a separate target comparison. Similarly, predictive multiplicity concerns competing fitted models \citep{marx2020}, rather than explanation randomness for a frozen prediction. Several model families broaden the tested settings without providing uncertainty over model training.

\paragraph{Metrics and benchmark scope.}
\citet{nogueira2018} formalize feature-subset stability and its uncertainty, emphasizing the sampling unit and metric properties. \citet{webber2010} give a top-weighted rank comparison with explicit finite-prefix and tie conventions. Membership, ranks, signs and value precision capture different properties; none alone supplies a universal explanation-adequacy cutoff. In particular, overlap divided by $k$ differs from Jaccard overlap, and its chance behavior varies with feature count. We retain value error and signed selection as separate components rather than comparing a single stability score indiscriminately across dimensions. Dataset identity also matters: the ACS tasks of \citet{ding2021retiring} explicitly specify prediction targets and population filters. We fix state, year, task and weighting policy and limit conclusions to that benchmark. The contribution sought here is a reproducible conditional assessment of one-run numerical adequacy; no priority claim about discovering SHAP variability is made.
