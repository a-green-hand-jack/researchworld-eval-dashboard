**Table C (Q2a, Q2b; T0).** Bias, RMSE (points), coverage of the nominal-95% interval with Wilson 95% interval, median interval width (points) and items used for the report, per method; τ ∈ {0, 0.1}, K=200, n ∈ {100, 500}; 1000 replicates per cell. **Table D (Q1c, Q2a; T0).** Claim "tuned beats untuned baseline by more than m=0.02" at K=200, n=100: false-claim rate (true gain below m) and power (true gain ≥ m), % with Wilson 95% intervals. "point": the method's estimate minus the baseline's estimate exceeds m; "lower bound": the method's 95% lower bound exceeds the baseline's estimate plus m. At τ=0 the true gain is 0, so there is no power column. Cells at τ=0.1 pool the false-claim regimes (`gain<=0`, `0<gain<m`) and contain fewer replicates than 1000: 162 false-claim and 608 power replicates at τ=0.1 (per-cell counts in `table_d_all_cells.csv`), so their intervals are wide.

**Table E (Q1c, Q2a; T0).** Claim "system A beats system B" from reported scores where A and B are tuned with different K or n. False-winner rate = share claiming A > B when A is not truly better; "A truly better" = detection rate. Wilson 95% intervals. Naive reports (M0) declare the K=200 system the winner over the K=5 system 86.7 [84.5, 88.7]% of the time at τ=0 (true difference 0).

## T1 (real MMLU-Pro outcomes, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct Q8_0; 24 base configs + 276 pairwise ensembles, 2000 items)

T1 replicates draw search and fresh items from one fixed 2000-item pool, so replicates are not independent. Every T1 uncertainty below is mean ± 1.96 × the delete-a-block SE (SD of the 10 block means / √10), never the replicate SE or a Wilson interval.

**Fig A-T1 (Q1a, Q1b; `fig_a_t1.pdf`).** Optimism of the naive report on the real outcome matrix, reported − fresh accuracy (points) against search-set size n, K candidates searched exhaustively (K=2, 5, 24, 100, 300; 1000 replicates per cell). Bars: mean ± 1.96 × block SE. Dashed: the closed form fed with the matrix's own σ, r, τ (`gap_theory_from_matrix`). Readings: K=300 8.74 [8.43, 9.05] points at n=50, 5.75 [5.52, 5.97] at n=100, 1.88 [1.71, 2.04] at n=500; K=2 1.29 [0.87, 1.71] at n=50 and 0.09 [−0.06, 0.24] at n=500; K=24 6.97 [6.52, 7.42] at n=50. The closed form tracks the observed gap (mean difference −0.04 points, largest 0.51, over 35 exhaustive cells) but not within the intervals everywhere: 13 of 35 cells differ by more than 1.96 block SE, mostly small K and n=50, where it over-predicts (K=2, n=50: 1.80 predicted, 1.29 observed).

**Fig B-T1 (Q1b; `fig_b_t1.pdf`).** Same quantity, by search procedure: exhaustive, successive halving, greedy (greedy only at K=24); panels K=24, 100, 300. Halving and greedy give slightly lower optimism than exhaustive at small n (K=24, n=100: exhaustive 4.55, halving 4.02, greedy 4.06 points) and the same at n=500 within the intervals for K=300 (1.88 vs 1.89).

**Table G (Q2a, Q2b; `table_g_t1_calibration.tex`, compact `table_g_main.tex`).** Bias and RMSE (points, against each method's own target), coverage of the nominal-95% interval ± 1.96 block SE, median width (points) and items used for the report, per method, real matrix, exhaustive search over K=300, n=100 (and 500), 1000 replicates per cell. M3 rows come from the `|m3` cells of the same design. At n=100: M0 0.771 ± 0.032 (bias +5.75 ± 0.22), M1_f0.5 0.954 ± 0.013, M4_simult 0.988 ± 0.005, M4_cond 0.944 ± 0.008, M4_hybrid 0.953 ± 0.008, M2 0.923 ± 0.016, M5_eb 0.109 ± 0.011. M4_cond has a heavy-tailed point estimate (RMSE 20.8 points), M5_eb's interval is far too narrow (width 1.0 point): both as in T0.

**Table H (Q1c, Q2a; `table_h_t1_pairs.tex`, `table_h_t1_decisions.tex`, `table_h_t1_decisions_n500.tex`).** Ranking two tuned systems A and B tuned exhaustively on the same search set from disjoint candidate subsets of K_A and K_B configs (75 vs 5, 75 vs 75, 24 vs 24, 5 vs 5), 1000 replicates per cell, ± 1.96 block SE. True order: sign of the fresh-set difference. Pairs table: true and reported mean difference, share of replicates where A is truly better, where the report claims A > B, and where the reported order differs from the true one (a tie in the reported scores counts as a flip, so equal-K pairs with near-zero true difference have flip rates near 0.5 by construction). 75 vs 5 at n=100: true difference +0.50 ± 0.10 points, reported +3.01 ± 0.10, A claimed better in 0.90 ± 0.02 of replicates and truly better in 0.62. Decisions tables: false-winner rate (A not truly better) and detection rate (A truly better) per method. "point": estimate A > estimate B; "lower bd": A's lower bound > B's estimate. Tuned-vs-baseline decisions are not shown: the baseline is a weak config, nearly all replicates have a true gain, so false-claim rates are not estimable from T1.

**Fig T1-subjects (Q1a; appendix, `fig_t1_subjects.pdf`).** Gap of the selected config within each MMLU-Pro subject, exhaustive, n=500, K=24 and K=300; mean over replicates ± 1.96 × replicate-mean SE. A subject holds 16-63 of the 500 search items and replicates share items, so these bars understate the uncertainty; the figure shows that the optimism is present in every subject, not subject-level differences.

**Fig T1-check (Q1a, Q1b; appendix, `fig_t1_check.pdf`).** (a) Observed T1 gap against the closed form from the matrix, all 35 exhaustive cells; (b) observed T1 gap against the T0 cells with the same (n, K) at τ=0.1, ρ=0.6, baseline accuracy 0.3 (T0 not calibrated to the matrix); dashed: y = x. The synthetic world over-predicts the real gap (mean difference +0.80 points; up to about 3 points at n=50).
