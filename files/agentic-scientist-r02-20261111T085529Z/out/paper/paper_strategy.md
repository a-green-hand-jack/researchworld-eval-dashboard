# Paper Strategy (paper_standalone, ICLR 2026)

**Status:** ready  
**Title (provisional):** Gated Weighted-Mondrian Split Conformal Prediction for Softmax Outputs: A Multi-Axis Reliability Evaluation on Synthetic Simplex Families

## Central question
On synthetic softmax-output (simplex-valued) families, does a gated, shift-aware Mondrian split-conformal procedure give a better coverage/slice-reliability/set-size trade-off than standard LAC, APS, RAPS and class-wise split conformal under a single evaluator-owned multi-axis reliability protocol, and where do its guarantees visibly stop holding?

## Central thesis
Under a fixed multi-axis protocol (marginal under-coverage, worst-slice under-coverage, set-size ratio, negative controls), a Mondrian LAC procedure with a gated domain-classifier reweighting step attains a higher composite reliability score than the best of four standard conformal baselines on sealed final data (qualified; positive margin with a bootstrap interval excluding zero), while remaining exact-in-marginal-coverage on exchangeable cases and leaving non-trivial residual under-coverage on shifted cases and worst slices, so the result is conditional on the declared synthetic families and must not be extrapolated.

## Narrative rationale
The only completed, qualified evidence is a sealed final evaluation (5 seeded runs, 0 failed episodes, all 3 negative controls passed) of the frozen search winner against evaluator-implemented baselines, plus a 3-variant, 3-seed development comparison. This supports a focused empirical paper that (i) states the evaluation protocol as the first-class object (what a reader should measure when judging conformal sets on compositional outputs, including one-sided under-coverage metrics, stratified slices, and negative controls), (ii) presents one method (Mondrian LAC + gated weighting) as the studied procedure, and (iii) reports headline margin with uncertainty and the residual failure modes honestly. Thesis-first, not chronology: the three search variants appear only as a nested design comparison. No real-data (e.g. CIFAR-10) results, no per-baseline breakdown and no per-case results exist in the artifacts, so claims stay inside the synthetic families; that scope limitation is itself part of the story. The final outcome is 'qualified' (final.qualification.outcome), so no failed-qualification disclosure is needed, but the unverified diagnostics trust and the limits of the evidence must be stated.

## Method groups
- **Standard split-conformal baselines (LAC, APS, RAPS, class-wise)** (baseline): Final evaluator scores them on identical data with the same loss; only the best baseline score (final.metric.best_baseline_score) and the paired margin with bootstrap CI are retained. Individual baseline scores and identity of the best one are NOT in the artifacts and must be stated as unavailable. Trivial references (uncalibrated top-mass, full set, argmax only) exist in the evaluator but have no retained scores; mention at most as protocol components.
- **Mondrian LAC split conformal with confidence-bin/class-conditional groups (development variant 1)** (ablation): Base procedure of the nested variant family; its search-stage metrics (combined score 0.868, shift gap 0.0613, worst-slice gap 0.0497, set ratio 0.166) give the reference point for what the weighting step changes.
- **Gated cross-fitted domain-classifier weighted Mondrian conformal (frozen selected procedure)** (proposed): Search winner (tied top search score 0.9192 with variant 3) and the object of the sealed final evaluation; exact unweighted fallback when no shift is detected keeps exchangeable coverage and negative controls safe by design.
- **Group-indicator quantile-regression thresholds on top of variant 2 (development variant 3)** (ablation): Produced search metrics and per-seed scores identical to variant 2 in all retained fields; report this as a null difference. Do not attribute the cause (e.g. gate fallback) unless the program log/source is read and cited; otherwise cause is unknown.

## Notes for writer

Say 'search-stage score' for combined_score and 'sealed final score' for final_score; never reuse one label for different thresholds. No thresholded rate is needed.
- Final outcome is 'qualified' (final.qualification.*), status completed, correct true. Do not imply failure; but state that candidate diagnostics trust is 'unverified' and that all numbers are aggregates retained by the evaluator.
- Unknowns to state as unknown, not infer: identity and individual scores of baselines, per-case/per-family results, alpha and sample sizes of final cases (sealed), cause of search-to-final score drop, why variant 3 equals variant 2, which shift family drives the shift gap. Do not claim a statistical-vs-validity cause for any residual gap.
- The marginal and slice gaps are one-sided under-coverage shortfalls (max(0, 1-alpha-coverage)); over-coverage is penalised only via set size. Say so, since a small gap does not show coverage is tight. The exchangeable gap 0.0017 is compatible with the finite-sample guarantee but is not a test of it.
- Shift-case coverage is descriptive per the task contract; the shift gap 0.0536 (and worst-slice gap 0.0675) are residual failures, not successes. Report them alongside the headline margin. Positive margin means higher composite score under this specific loss weighting (0.5/0.25/0.25), which is evaluator-defined; do not present it as dominance on each axis, since per-axis baseline values are not retained.
- Interpret the margin CI carefully: it is a bootstrap over seeded runs within each case, equal-weighted over cases; it does not capture variation across case designs or data-generating families beyond those evaluated.
- Search variants are nested development steps (variant 2 adds gated weighting to 1; variant 3 adds QR thresholds to 2), 3 seeds each, on different cases than the final; present as a design comparison, not an ablation with significance. All 3 attempts were scored; no unscored attempts.
