\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{Leaderboard.}
A leaderboard is a matrix $X\in[0,100]^{M\times n}$: $M$ models, $n$ tasks, $X_{jt}$ the score of model $j$ on task $t$. An aggregation rule maps $X$ to a ranking of the models. \emph{Score rules} assign each model a value $f(X_j)$ from its own row: mean, geometric and harmonic mean, median, minimum, trimmed mean. \emph{Rank rules} use comparisons between models: Borda (the sum over tasks of the number of models beaten, ties counting one half), plurality (the number of tasks on which the model is best), and the Condorcet requirement (the target beats every other model on a majority of tasks).

\paragraph{Targeted investment.}
A developer holds a target model $i$ with scores $x=X_i$. Training on data related to task $t$ raises its score to any $y_t\in[x_t,c_t]$; all other models are fixed. The \emph{cap} vector $c$ is the modelling assumption on what training can buy. We study caps of the form ceiling ($c_t=100$), frontier ($c_t$ is the best score of any other model on $t$ plus $0$ or $2$ points), additive ($c_t=x_t+h$, $h\in\{5,10,20\}$) and empirical (the 95th or 99th percentile of per-task fine-tune-minus-base differences measured on the Open LLM Leaderboard). Target $i$ is \emph{first} under rule $f$ if its aggregate exceeds every other model's by at least $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$ points.

\paragraph{Investment measures.}
For a raise $y$ that makes the target first we report three measures: the total score gain $L_1=\sum_t(y_t-x_t)$, the number of tasks touched $K=|\{t:y_t>x_t\}|$, and the largest single-task jump $L_\infty=\max_t(y_t-x_t)$, each minimised separately. If no $y\le c$ makes the target first, the target is \emph{infeasible} under that rule and cap, and we report infeasible counts rather than dropping those targets.

\paragraph{Minimum cap.}
To see how feasibility depends on the cap rather than only at a few scenarios, we also compute for each rule and target $h^\ast$, the smallest uniform per-task gain cap $h$ (with $c_t=\min(x_t+h,100)$) under which first place is feasible.
