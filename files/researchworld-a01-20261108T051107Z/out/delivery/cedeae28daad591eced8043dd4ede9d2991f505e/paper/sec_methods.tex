\section{Data and methods}
\label{sec:methods}
\paragraph{Frames.}
\emph{HELM MMLU} (release v1.13.0, dated 2025-01-10): 79 models, each with all 57 per-subject exact-match accuracies (the 57 subject tables, not the aggregate). \emph{Open LLM Leaderboard} \texttt{contents} (commit \texttt{9c09a7c}\ldots, 2025-03-20): 4{,}576 rows; after removing repeated model names (keeping the largest reported average, 79 repeated names) and one flagged model, 4{,}496 models on six normalised tasks. A fixed top-500 frame (selected once by the normalised mean) is used for ordinal rules, a no-merge frame (3{,}789 models) and the raw (unnormalised) scale as sensitivity checks. These are censuses of two snapshots, not samples of models, and they are never pooled. Source licences are undeclared for the scores (Appendix~\ref{app:data}); checksums and retrieval dates are recorded in the supplement.

\paragraph{Targets and comparisons.}
Every model in a frame is a target. For rule comparisons we pair targets across rules; since the mean's unique leader has cost zero under the mean by convention, it is excluded when conditioning on non-leading targets, and targets already first under another rule keep cost zero. We report costs against the target's initial rank under the mean, as well as over all targets.

\paragraph{Solvers.}
Score rules use closed forms where available (mean, minimum, odd median), water-filling for concave power means, and exact enumeration of threshold pairs for the even median. Trimmed means (supplementary; $L_1$ and $L_\infty$ only) are continuous score rules, solved by a mixed-integer program with sorting assignments ($L_1$) and by bisection ($L_\infty$); a rerun with an explicit zero solver gap reproduced the supplementary output exactly (500 rows, 100 of them trimmed means). Only the ordinal rules (Borda, Condorcet, plurality) are solved exactly over the finite per-task level set ``unchanged / tie a rival / pass a rival by one margin step'' (only rival scores at or above the target's original score on the task are candidate levels), with zero requested optimality gap. A solver limit or numerical failure raises an error and is never recorded as infeasibility. The solvers are tested against exhaustive search and independent LP/convex oracles on small random instances, and a result checker verifies, on the full outputs, the mean-gap identity, monotonicity in the cap, agreement of feasibility across $K$, $L_1$, $L_\infty$, and the cap-threshold equivalence. For Borda a dynamic program is used, checked by a witness test with a full mixed-integer fallback; on the actual Open LLM Leaderboard frame it agrees with an independently built full-rival mixed-integer program in all 24 checked cases (six target ranks, ceiling and 10-point cap, $L_1$ and $K$), and across the 4,948 Borda objective instances of the HELM and top-500 mains none required the fallback (an audit re-run, not telemetry of the original runs).

\paragraph{Uncertainty.}
Cost summaries over targets describe the fixed frame. To probe sensitivity we (a) resample tasks with replacement (100 shared draws; original-mean ranks 2--11 followed under every rule), (b) perturb scores with Gaussian noise scaled by plug-in binomial standard errors, clipped to $[0,100]$, with per-task shock correlation 0 and 0.8, and (c) vary the number of HELM subjects ($n\in\{3,6,12,24,48,57\}$, 100 subsets). The resulting percentile ranges are sensitivity intervals under stated assumptions, not population confidence intervals.

\paragraph{Margin and rival-set checks.}
On the 20 targets of original mean rank 2--21 per frame we change $\varepsilon$ from $0.001$ to $0.1$; on ranks 2--11 we recompute all rules with the mean's leader removed and under ten random half-frames of the rivals (the target is always kept).

\paragraph{Controls.}
For every feasible non-leading target we compare the optimal $K$ and $L_1$ with four policies: raising tasks to their caps until the target is first in order of lowest original score, of largest headroom, or in random order (median over 200 orders, seed 7), and the uniform improvement that attains the optimal $L_\infty$; the ratio ranges come from 1000 target resamplings shared by all policies. The earlier 20-target random-order and uniform comparison (original mean ranks 2--21) is kept in the appendix.
