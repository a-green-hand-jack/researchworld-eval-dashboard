\section{Reproduction and provenance}
\label{app:reproduction}
The accompanying repository contains the complete numerical analysis in \texttt{analysis/}, rendered presentation assets and their generator in \texttt{presentation/}, and the official conference style files in \texttt{venue/}. The root \texttt{README.md} gives the build entry point and links the analysis and presentation instructions. Raw data are downloaded to ignored \texttt{analysis/data/raw/}; raw conversations and full source datasets are not redistributed with this paper. Minimal processed outcome rows, including participant labels and available question/judge identifiers, are distributed as exact attack witnesses.

Arena is pinned to dataset commit \texttt{18c298340948c0e7f7727399fd459cca6ce0ca6f}; MT-Bench to \texttt{f7d2896d2cc5d80f8b55c2bbc722613555233c25}; and the NBA source to repository commit \texttt{4c1ff5e3aef1816ae04af63218015066e186c147}. Exact file URLs, licenses, byte counts and SHA-256 hashes are in \texttt{analysis/results/provenance.json}. The parsers preserve canonical participant order, invert labels when swapping participants, validate source label categories, and remove duplicate NBA game representations. All human MT annotations are included in the primary human fit; the expert-only subset is a separately named control.

The recorded primary environment is Python 3.11.2, NumPy 2.4.6, SciPy 1.17.1 and pandas 3.0.6; the dependency file also pins PyArrow 26.0.0. Runs use one BLAS/OpenMP thread on CPU, with base seed 20261102 and explicit dataset/replicate offsets in the runner. \texttt{run.json} records each workload's arguments and package versions; per-ranking audits record measured elapsed times and optimization diagnostics. The primary workload totals 99.4 seconds of measured wall time on the shared host. This is a measured workload summary, not a universal runtime guarantee; downloads and other controls are additional. The robustness, human-label selection and joint matched-bootstrap workload sums are 33.1, 5.3 and 34.0 seconds, respectively. Jobs ran under a two-CPU, 4 GB memory ceiling with no GPU; these are allocation limits, not peak-use measurements. Detailed commands and timing records, including preliminary and orchestration failures, are in the analysis README and execution log.

The source-to-claim chain is explicit. \texttt{scripts/fetch\_data.py} obtains pinned data; \texttt{datasets.py} parses them; \texttt{bt.py} fits and evaluates the model; \texttt{run\_study.py} creates primary, robustness and matched analyses; \texttt{matched\_fixedpair.py} implements the post-hoc reference contrast; \texttt{nba\_home.py} fits the home control; \texttt{diagnostics.py} produces adjacent-pair checks; and \texttt{summarize.py} aggregates the recorded refits. These script paths are relative to \texttt{analysis/}. The rendering manifest identifies the source CSVs and generator for each displayed figure and table. Reported numerical summaries can be inspected without downloading raw data; rerunning fits requires those inputs.

\section{Exact witnesses and approximation checks}
\label{app:witnesses}
For each primary found budget, \texttt{analysis/results/witnesses/} records distinct processed-row indices, participant names, original labels and reversed labels. \texttt{verify\_witnesses.py} reconstructs the data and refits the full objective after applying those rows. The Arena witness reverses two original wins by \texttt{gpt-4-1106-preview} over \texttt{gpt-4-0125-preview}, identified by battle IDs 175888324 and 244123213. Its exact leading contrast changes from $0.008134$ to $-0.000871$. The human and GPT-4 MT witnesses give post-edit margins $-0.007132$ and $-0.006431$, respectively. These identify concrete changes sufficient for the stated fitted endpoint; they do not show that all smaller subsets fail.

The one-edit approximation follows by differentiating the stationarity equation. Reversing $y_i$ changes the gradient by $-(1-2y_i)x_i$ at the original fit; applying the inverse Hessian gives $\Delta\theta\approx H^{-1}(1-2y_i)x_i$, yielding Equation~\ref{eq:influence}. The Hessian uses all observations, including ties. For each primary ranking, representative influence-ordered and random decisive rows are checked by actual single-row refits. The delivered per-row checks and full-budget curve outputs retain both the linear prediction and exact contrast, so the approximation can be assessed rather than assumed valid for a large prefix. Explicit numerical checks compare an independent SciPy optimizer, orientation invariance, infinitesimal-label influence finite differences, and duplication/penalty scaling at $\lambda\in\{0.1,1,10\}$. Parser assertions validate source IDs, allowed labels and canonical participant order.

At a found budget, the neighboring smaller prefix is also refitted and recorded. Binary search is a heuristic even within this candidate ordering because no monotonicity result is assumed. Its successful output remains a valid witness regardless of whether a different prefix or subset succeeds earlier. Attack fractions in matched bootstraps likewise concern this algorithm and cap. The fixed-reference control assigns cost zero when the unedited resample already has the opposite reference ordering; treating those draws as failed searches would change the estimand.

\section{Analysis sequence and scope}
The empirical plan was developed during the task rather than preregistered. A measured pilot sized the full CPU runs; pilot outputs are retained separately and do not substitute for the final primary results. Primary fixed-pair bootstrap margins and the matched control's reselected-pair statistic were initially distinct. After inspecting frequent human runner-up changes in the latter, we added the explicitly post-hoc fixed GPT-4--Claude-v1 analysis. The report preserves both quantities and does not reinterpret a reselected-pair range as uncertainty for a single fixed contrast. The supplied analysis reports all planned analyses executed and no searches censored in the final paired control.

The study does not include deletion or injected-vote experiments, global optimal-budget certificates, online defenses, a dynamic sports model, or comparisons of present-day production leaderboards. These are outside the defined intervention and claims. No missing experiment is represented by illustrative or synthetic measurements.

\section{Research assistance, data use, and independence}
AI assistance was used for literature synthesis, implementation, presentation, manuscript drafting and checking. The experimental labels are the original public human, GPT-4 and sports observations; no model was queried to generate replacement evaluation labels for this study. Outcome reversals are deterministic transformations for sensitivity analysis.

We use public data subject to their recorded licenses (Apache-2.0 for the selected Arena snapshot and CC-BY-4.0 for MT-Bench and the NBA source), cite their sources, and exclude raw text and full source files from the artifact while retaining minimal processed witness rows for verification. The paper studies aggregate sensitivity, not individual voters or annotators. A historical sensitivity witness is not evidence of misconduct by any participant.

Literature consultation was limited to permitted supplied sources and direct methodological context, without seeking, identifying or following the withheld study or its discussions. The context supply records the sources and sections actually read, and the analysis independence note records its direct dataset-only access. No prohibited encounter is reported by those supplies. This restriction limits the breadth of the related-work survey; it does not license a first-discovery or comprehensive-novelty claim.
