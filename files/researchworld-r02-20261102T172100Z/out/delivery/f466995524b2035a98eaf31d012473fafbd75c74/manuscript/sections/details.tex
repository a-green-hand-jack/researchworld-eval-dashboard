\section{Executable settings and estimands}
\label{app:details}
\paragraph{Generator and fitted scale.}
For dimension $K$, NumPy generator seed $1729+K$ produces the entries of $B$ independently from $N(0,0.32^2)$; each row is then centered across components. The same $B$ is retained in all repetitions of that dimension. Let $\Phi$ be the training design matrix and $R=\operatorname{diag}(0,10^{-3},\ldots,10^{-3})$. The fitted coefficient matrix is
\begin{equation}
 \hat B=(\Phi^\top\Phi+R)^{-1}\Phi^\top\operatorname{clr}(Y_{\rm train}).
\end{equation}
The scale regression uses targets $t_i=\log\max(\|\operatorname{clr}(Y_i)-\phi(X_i)\hat B\|_2^2,10^{-12})$, coefficient vector $\hat d=(\Phi^\top\Phi+R)^{-1}\Phi^\top t$, and
\begin{equation}
 \hat s(x)=\exp\{\operatorname{clip}(\phi(x)\hat d/2,-12,12)\}.
\end{equation}
These in-sample residuals can bias the scale level. Calibration absorbs a constant multiplicative error, but does not correct arbitrary errors in the covariate-dependent shape. No true noise scale is given to the fitted method. Predictor and scale parameters are fixed before calibration; there is no test-based tuning.

Main repetition seeds are 901000 through 901099. Methods share streams within a cell; cells can reuse seed numbers but remain separate estimands. Pilot seeds 900000 and 900001 are excluded from confirmatory inference. The data are newly generated for each full repetition, rather than repeated test draws around one fixed calibration sample. Both environments share the trained model and unweighted thresholds; the oracle rule additionally knows the target-environment density ratio. The 32 area anchors are newly drawn independently of coverage test points in each environment, with 8,192 candidates per anchor.

\paragraph{Area precision.}
With anchor hit fraction $\hat v_a$ and $M$ independent candidates per anchor, the recorded candidate-only standard error is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\mathrm{SE}}_{\rm MC}
 =\sqrt{\frac{A^{-1}\sum_{a=1}^A\hat v_a(1-\hat v_a)}{AM}}.
\end{equation}
This conditions on the fitted regions and feature anchors. It does not include variation across anchors or across training/calibration datasets. Independent full-repetition intervals do include these sources. Common candidates make method comparisons paired. A finite candidate experiment that returns no hits does not prove zero area.

\paragraph{Mean and paired intervals.}
For independent repetition estimates $V_1,\ldots,V_R$, the pointwise mean interval is $\bar V\pm t_{0.975,R-1}\,s_V/\sqrt{R}$. A paired method contrast first forms $D_r=V_{r,a}-V_{r,b}$ and applies the same formula to $D$. A paired environment contrast is shifted minus iid within the same trained/calibrated repetition. These are descriptive intervals for mean performance, using a finite-variance normal approximation for the mean; they are not familywise intervals, proof of equivalence, or a guarantee for each realized calibration set. With infinite radii the complete-cell radius mean is omitted and denominators/status are retained; coverage and area retain those full-region outcomes.

The distribution of finite-test coverage estimates, including standard deviation and empirical percentiles, describes repeated-calibration variability. The fraction below nominal minus $0.05$ is an empirical tail diagnostic, with a Wilson interval across independent repetition indicators. Its events depend on noisy finite test estimates, so it does not estimate a certified event about exact conditional population coverage.

\paragraph{Rank sensitivity.}
The focused rank grid is $n\in\{2,9,19,99,499\}$ and $\alpha\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2\}$, with 20,000 independent calibration/test trials per cell and continuous uniform or Bernoulli-$0.5$ scores. The continuous case checks the rank mean and beta variability; ties are included without randomization. With a Bernoulli score, the conditional covered probability is $0.5$ if the threshold is zero and one if it is one or infinite. This makes conservatism directly checkable. Cell seeds follow the recorded rule $310000+$cell index. These score simulations isolate calibration behavior from predictor fit and compositional geometry.

\paragraph{Censoring and replacement.}
For boundary checks, components of the heterogeneous generator below $0.025$ are set to zero and the vector is reclosed. Replacement is the fixed map
\begin{equation}
 T_\epsilon(y)_j=\frac{\max(y_j,\epsilon)}{\sum_\ell\max(y_\ell,\epsilon)}.
 \label{eq:replacement}
\end{equation}
The implementation uses this clipping-and-closure map, not an additive pseudocount. Each $K\in\{3,10\}$ uses 50 fresh seeds 902000 through 902049, $n=499$, $\alpha=0.1$ and 5,000 iid test observations. Original datasets are identical across the three replacement values. The center and scale are trained on transformed responses. Original-target raw methods calibrate their raw residual scores on censored $Y$ and test on censored $Y$. Transformed-target methods consistently calibrate and test on $T_\epsilon(Y)$. An additional original-target clr diagnostic uses the transformed-calibrated rule: finite clr regions reject original responses with any zero and score only positive responses; an infinite threshold invokes the explicit whole-simplex fallback.

The transformed event $T_\epsilon(Y)\in C_\epsilon(X)$ can equivalently define an original-space inverse-image region $\{y:T_\epsilon(y)\in C_\epsilon(X)\}$. Original-response membership in this inverse image equals the transformed membership observation by observation, so its measured coverage and interval equal the transformed estimates, including at zeros; invertibility is unnecessary. This is an exact reinterpretation of existing events, recorded in \texttt{results/boundary\_summary/pullback\_equivalence.csv}, not a new simulation. It is distinct from directly testing $Y\in C_\epsilon(X)$. The reported direct original clr diagnostic uses the latter rule, and inherits no transformed-target guarantee. No original-simplex area of the inverse image was estimated, so its efficiency is unresolved.
