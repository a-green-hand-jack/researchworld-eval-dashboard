# Caption drafts (paper-repo may shorten; the definitions and the stated uncertainty must stay)

Common definitions: *budget* $k$ = number of comparisons removed (or added/relabelled) by the adversary; always a **verified upper bound** (an exact refit confirms that the claim flips), not a proven minimum. *Adjacent-pair claim* C2: rank $r$ above rank $r{+}1$ in the base fit. $z$ = score gap / bootstrap SE of the gap. Colour and marker encode the evaluation design in every figure (blue circle crowd votes, orange square expert labels, green triangle LLM judge, pink diamond sports).

**Table 0 (`tables/table0_headline.tex`, E3).** Headline: verified adversarial removal budgets per dataset (exact ties excluded). C1: top-1 claim, count and share of $N$ (NBA single seasons: median over the 68 seasons with a strictly ordered top-1); C2: adjacent-pair claims, median [min, max] in comparisons and median share of $N$ (single seasons: all claims of those seasons); the two 95% intervals are percentile intervals over dataset-bootstrap resamples (`s5_budget_intervals.csv`, 100--200 resamples; the claim is re-defined on each resample's own ranking; not computed for the NBA single seasons); $z$ = gap / bootstrap SE, median shown for the iid resampling of comparisons and for the cluster bootstrap (MT-Bench question, Arena identical prompt, NBA week of the season or season for the pooled fit; `s5_cluster_se.csv`); \% of pair's comparisons = median budget as a share of the comparisons involving either participant of the claim (the noise-equivalent "leverage" of `adv_budgets.csv` is not shown: it is approximately $z\sqrt{(N-k)/k}$ and so adds nothing to $z$ and $k/N$); verified = share of claims for which an exact refit confirmed the flip. Budgets are upper bounds on the minimum.

**Table 1 (`tables/table1_datasets.tex`, E1).** The four datasets. $K$: participants (in brackets: size of the claim window for Arena and NBA pooled, the 20 top ranks of the fit; the top-1 and top-$k$ claims treat every participant as a possible challenger); $N$: comparisons; Ties: share of tied comparisons; Pairs seen: participant pairs compared at least once out of those possible (over all $K$); last column: comparisons per compared pair (min / median / max). Source `results/dataset_summary.csv`.

**Figure 1 (`fig1_leaderboards.pdf`, E1; appendix `fig1b_nba.pdf`).** BT scores (Elo scale) with 95% percentile bootstrap CIs (B=1000), sorted by score; the number at the right is the 95% bootstrap rank interval. Left: Arena 55k, the 20 top-ranked participants of the fit of all 64 (rank intervals are over all 64 participants); right: MT-Bench, human experts (top) and GPT-4 judge (bottom) on the same six models, shared axis. **Figure 2 (`fig2_survival_remove.pdf`, E2; appendix `fig2_survival_flip.pdf`).** Upper part of each panel: probability that a base-fit claim still holds after removing a random fraction $f$ of the comparisons (200 seeds per $f$; random removal evaluated from $f=10^{-3}$), for C1 (top-1), C2 (adjacent pairs: line = mean over pairs, band = min–max over pairs) and C3 (top-$k$ set, mean over $k\in\{3,5,10\}$). The seed-level Wilson 95% intervals of single claims are in `random_survival.csv` and not drawn. Lower strip: verified adversarial removal budgets as a fraction of $N$ on the same horizontal axis, one marker per adjacent pair (vertical position = rank, rank 1 at top), star = C1. `fig2_survival_flip.pdf`: label flips replace removal and the strip shows the adversarial relabelling budgets. The appendix grid versions `fig2b_survival_{remove,flip}_grid.pdf` add a narrow panel with the bootstrap resamples of all $N$ comparisons (1000; C1, C2 mean, C3 mean from left to right). Claims with a near-zero base gap sit near 0.5 by construction.

**Figure 3 (`fig3_budgets.pdf`, E3).** Verified adversarial removal budget of each adjacent-pair claim against its rank, as a fraction of $N$ (a) and as a fraction of the comparisons that involve either participant of the pair (b). The noise-equivalent "leverage" is not drawn: it is approximately $z\sqrt{(N-k)/k}$ (measured ratio 0.98--1.04), i.e. a re-expression of $k/N$ and $z$. Lines: the four main datasets; band: NBA single seasons (69 seasons pooled by rank; median and interquartile range, ranks with at least 10 seasons). Claims with an exactly tied base fit are excluded.

**Figure 4 (`fig4_budget_vs_z.pdf`, E4).** Verified removal budget of each adjacent-pair claim against its statistical margin $z$ (log-log). Solid lines: pooled fit $\log k = a_{\rm dataset} + b\log z$ with a dataset-specific intercept (slope $b=0.78$, cluster-bootstrap 95% interval [0.66, 0.85]); dashed: NBA single seasons fitted alone ($b=0.48$ [0.44, 0.52]). Arena 55k alone gives $b=0.84$ [0.69, 1.01] (Table 2). NBA all seasons is plotted but not in the pooled fit. (b) The parameter-free first-order prediction $\hat k$ (ceiling of the linearised estimate of the number of removals, `s5_khat.csv`; no fitted parameter) against the verified budget; line: equality, band: factor 2. Same claims as (a). Five season claims with $z<0.002$ lie below the x-limit. Budgets are integers $\ge1$, hence the floor. Dataset intercepts are descriptive: design is confounded with dataset identity (one data set per non-sports design).

**Table 2 (`table2_regression.tex`, E4; full set `table2b_regression_full.tex`).** OLS of $\log k$ (adjacent-pair claims, strictly ordered) on the listed log regressors, 95% stratified cluster-bootstrap intervals (B=2000); SNR is the influence signal-to-noise ratio from `s3_influence.csv`; $n_{\rm pair}$ comparisons involving either participant. The main table lists M0 ($\log z$), M1 ($+\log n_{\rm pair}$) and their cluster-SE versions M0c, M1c, with $z_{\rm cl}$ = gap divided by the cluster-bootstrap standard error (`s5_cluster_se.csv`); the slope on $\log z$ is stable (0.77--0.78 pooled) and $n_{\rm pair}$ adds nothing identifiable (intervals include 0). The full table also lists the SNR models M2--M4: $\log z$ and $\log$ SNR are collinear (correlation 1.000 on Arena, VIF up to $10^4$; `s5_collinearity.csv`), so their coefficients are not separately identified and should not be interpreted.

**Figure 5 (`fig5_matched.pdf`, E5).** The 15 MT-Bench model pairs under two labellers at equal size ($N=1814$ matched keys). x: GPT-4 judge; y: one human judgment per key (median over 20 draws; bars: min–max over draws). (a) margin $z$; (b) verified removal budget. Diagonal: equal. All 15 pairs keep their order in every human draw. The 1,232-key order-matched robustness variant and the all-judgments variant are in `matched_pairs.csv` and not drawn.

**Table 3 (`table3_method_validity.tex`, E6).** Budget found by each method divided by the smallest verified budget among the methods, on the claims where the method was run. *Equal to best* includes the used method itself, so for it 100% is by construction; the independent check is the exhaustive search on small instances (Table 3b). The influence estimate is a prediction, not a witness, so ratios below 1 are possible.

**Table 5 (`table5_additions.tex`, E8).** Verified budgets to reverse adjacent-pair claims by adding, relabelling or removing comparisons (medians; ratio to removal, median and interquartile range).

**Table 3b (`table3b_exhaustive.tex`, E6).** The search against exhaustive enumeration: 900 adjacent-pair claims on 180 small MT-Bench-human subsamples, every removal set of at most 5 comparisons enumerated. For the 652 claims whose exact minimum is at most 5, number of claims where each method returns exactly that minimum (no method can return less); for the 248 claims where no set of $\le5$ removals flips the pair, the exact minimum is only known to exceed 5, and the cells give the median [min, max] budget the methods returned. The check therefore covers budgets of up to 5 comparisons on six-model data; larger budgets remain verified upper bounds.

**Table 4 (`table4_ablations.tex`, E7).** Median verified removal budget (comparisons) of adjacent-pair claims under modelling variants; in brackets the 95% percentile interval of the median budget as a % of $N$ (claim-level bootstrap, B=2000; claims within a data set are not independent, MT-Bench has five per judge). $\dagger$: median over all single-season claims with the home-advantage parameter, no interval available. `--`: variant not run on that data set. The rows "window of 10 ranks" and "old claim set" change the claim window: all Arena claims are defined on the top-20 ranks of the fit with every participant as challenger (the default), the old set takes the 20 most-compared participants as window and challengers (`*_top20count` units; it is the set of the first-round results). The Arena eligible-set rows change the claim set (39 and 63 claims) and are not comparable claim by claim with the 19 baseline claims.

**Figure 6 (`fig6_controls.pdf`, E4 controls).** (a) Median budget as a share of $N$ for synthetic data (outcomes drawn from the BT fit of the real data with the real pair counts and tie shares; bars: min--max of the per-run medians over 10--20 runs) against the real data; filled: the four main data sets, open: seven synthetic NBA seasons against the real season. Diagonal: equal. (b) Median budget share of $N$ against $N$ for random subsamples of each real data set (line; bars: min--max of the per-run medians, 10 runs), the full data set (outlined marker), and Arena restricted to the six MT-Bench models (open triangles; $N$ about 700, 10 runs); at $N=1{,}367$ the open triangle (Arena restricted to the six MT-Bench models) and the open square (MT-Bench human subsampled to the same size) are each the median over 40 independent draws with its 95% interval (`s5_control_summary.csv`; median of the five adjacent-pair claims per draw); the ratio of the two medians is 1.02 [0.94, 1.13], and ratios outside [0.93, 1.07] are distinguishable from the sampling null (`min_detectable_ratio`). All controls: adjacent-pair claims, removal budgets, greedy search with exact refit (verified upper bounds); the synthetic runs are labelled `synthetic = True` in `controls.csv`.

**Table 5, extended.** The last two columns give the constrained adversary (`s5_constrained.csv`): every added or relabelled comparison involves the target participant, whose opponents follow its observed pair frequencies, so no third-party votes and no free choice of opponent; best of pushing the leader down or the rival up. Same claims as the unconstrained columns. Last three columns (`s5_noise.csv`): the smallest number of *uniformly random* removals that flips the claim in at least 5% of 200 draws (median over claims; "$>98\%\,N$" = never up to 98% of $N$), the share of claims for which this never happens, and the median ratio of that number to the adversary's verified removal budget of the same claim: how many random removals equal one targeted removal.

**Figure 7 (`fig7_noise.pdf`, E2/E3).** Targeted verified budget $k/N$ (x) against the number of uniformly random removals, as a share of $N$ (y), needed to flip the same claim in at least 5% (a) or 50% (b) of 200 random draws per size (`s5_noise.csv`; sizes on a geometric grid with log-linear interpolation). Points on the dashed top line never reach that level up to 98% of $N$. Diagonal: equal. Claims: C1 and adjacent pairs C2, exact ties excluded; grey dots: the NBA single seasons.

**Table 6 (`table6_published.tex`, E4/E7).** Left: share of adjacent pairs with margin $z<1$ under the iid bootstrap SE and under a cluster bootstrap SE (clusters: MT-Bench question; Arena identical prompt text; NBA week of the season, season for the pooled fit); SE ratio = median cluster/iid. Right: the claim "the 95% bootstrap interval of the score difference excludes 0" (the way a published leaderboard would be read): share of pairs for which it holds at base, and for those pairs the median removal budget after which it fails when the perturbed data are re-bootstrapped, against the point-gap budget of the same pairs (seven sampled NBA seasons, every tenth from 1950; the margin block on the left covers all 69 seasons, 1,371 pairs, the right blocks the 151 pairs of the seven sampled seasons). Last two columns, C1$^\prime$: "the top participant's 95% bootstrap rank interval is $[1,1]$" (probability of rank 1 at least 0.975): number of data sets (seasons for the last row) where it holds at base, and the median number of removals after which it fails under re-bootstrapping, over those where it holds.

**Figure 8 (`fig8_window.pdf`, E3 window choice).** Verified budgets of Arena 55k (blue) and pooled NBA (pink) against the size $w$ of the claim window (`s5_window_sweep.csv`). (a) C1 budget; (b) median budget of the $w-1$ adjacent-pair claims. Solid: window = the $w$ top participants by BT score, with every participant of the fit as challenger for C1 (the rule used in the paper, $w=20$, vertical line); dashed: window = the $w$ participants with the most comparisons, challengers restricted to them (the first-round rule). The last score window contains every participant (64 models, 53 teams). By score, the C1 budget does not depend on $w$ (3 for Arena, 10 for NBA); the median adjacent-pair budget falls from 21 (Arena) and 9 (NBA) at $w=10$ to 2--4 for $w\ge30$ and is 7 and 5 at $w=20$. By count, the C1 budget is 455 (Arena) and 90 (NBA) at $w=20$, because the by-count window can miss the leader's actual runner-up; it drops to 3 for Arena at $w=40$ and to 26 for NBA, once the window contains the actual runner-up.

**Table 7 (`table7_estimator.tex`, E7).** Budgets under alternative estimators (`s5_estimator.csv`, `s5_estimator_rank.csv`): $\lambda=10^{-6}$, tied comparisons dropped, a tie counted as one win and one loss. Greedy removal search with exact refit on the variant's own ranking (claims re-defined on it); C2 = median [min--max] over adjacent pairs of the window; "top-1 unchanged" compares the leader with the base fit; $\tau$ = Kendall's $\tau$ between the variant's and the base fit's ranking on the base window.

**Table 8 (`table8_oos.tex`, E4).** Out-of-sample accuracy of two predictors of the verified removal budget of adjacent-pair claims (`s5_oos_summary.csv`). Left: the line $\log k=a+b\log z$ fitted on the NBA single seasons alone; right: the first-order estimate $\hat k$, which has no fitted parameter. "predicted / verified" = geometric mean of prediction over verified budget with a claim-resampling 95% interval; RMSE in natural-log units. The seasons line overpredicts every other data set by a factor of 3--10, $\hat k$ is within 0.5--1.0 of the verified budget.

**Table 9 (`table9_restarts.tex`, E6).** Fifty randomised restarts of the removal search on every headline claim (`s5_restarts_summary.csv`): no restart found a smaller verified budget than the deterministic search used in the paper on any claim; the largest ratio of a restart's budget to the deterministic one is the column "max restart / search".
