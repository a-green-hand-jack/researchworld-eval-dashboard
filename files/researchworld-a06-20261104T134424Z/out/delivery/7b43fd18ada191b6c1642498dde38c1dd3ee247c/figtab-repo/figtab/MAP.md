# Exhibit → script map (figtab-repo)

All scripts read committed files of `results/` (delivery root; override with `RESULTS_DIR`) and write to `paper/figures/` (`FIG_OUT`).
Run from `figtab/`: `python3 <script>.py [args]`. Requires numpy, pandas, matplotlib. `style.py` holds the shared rendering conventions
(NeurIPS text width 5.5 in, fonts >= 7 pt, Okabe-Ito colours plus marker/line style so it reads in black and white, vector PDF).

## Caption facts (what the plot shows, so the manuscript caption matches)

Common: synthetic study = Gaussian-copula pool of 128 configs, N=20000 questions, mean accuracy .30 (`results/README.md`); one repetition = whole pipeline
(draw tuning set -> search -> select -> report -> compare with the exact true score of the selected config). tau = sd of true config accuracies; rho = latent
correlation between configs; n = tuning questions; K = configs searched; pp = percentage points. Repetitions per point are in the results files (`reps`):
2000 in OFAT/stress cells, 500 in the interaction grid (E1 markers, E4a, E5 use 500 except where stated). Numbers in captions must be read from the final files.

- **E1** - markers: mean(naive report - true score of the selected config) for best-of-K, rho=.5, one panel per tau, colour/marker per n; bars 1.96 x MC s.e.; lines: Gaussian-max prediction (M=5000 draws, pools of seed 7; its own MC s.e. is below line width). The prediction line extends to K=128 (no simulation there). Prediction and simulation agree closely: over the 55 matched cells the observed gap exceeds the prediction in 41, by +0.07 pp on average (range -0.23 to +0.47 pp); 8 of 55 cells differ by more than 2 combined MC s.e. (largest: tau=.01, n=100, K=4, predicted 3.70 vs observed 4.17 pp; tau=.03, n=1000, K=64 the observed gap is 0.23 pp *below*). The interaction cells have 500 repetitions (MC s.e. about .15-.2 pp), so the deviations are small and partly sampling noise; there is no systematic overshoot (an earlier version of this note said so; it was a misreading and is retracted). K=1 has no search and no gap.
- **E2** - panels vary one factor, all others at the reference cell (n=200, K=16, tau=.01, rho=.5, mean acc .3, best-of-K, no generation noise, no leakage); open orange ring marks the reference cell; dotted line its value; bars 1.96 x MC s.e. (M=2000). Search-procedure panel: best-of-K, successive halving, successive rejects (greedy ran at K=128, not comparable, not drawn).
- **E4a** - among repetitions where the selected config is truly not better than the untuned baseline (config 0), the share in which the method still licenses "tuned beats baseline" (one-sided 5%). n=200, rho=.5, best-of-K. Bars 1.96 sqrt(p(1-p)/m), m = repetitions in that denominator (few at tau=.03, hence wide bars); grey band = binomial 95% band around nominal 0.05 for the smallest m in the panel. Winner-aware (conditional) method is above nominal at K>=16 (about .10-.20).
- **E4b** - systems A, B tuned with K_A, K_B configs; top: share of repetitions claiming "A beats B" (one-sided 5%) while A is truly not better; n=200; bars 1.96 sqrt(p(1-p)/reps), reps=500; line = nominal 0.05. Bottom: bias of the reported A-B difference (pp) with bars 1.96 x bias_diff_se; naive is biased by about 2-6 pp when budgets differ and not when they are equal; split and bootstrap-optimism stay within about 1.5 pp. Equal budgets (16/16) give ~0 for naive; unequal budgets inflate it (tau=0: about .20 at 64/1).
- **E5** - top: bias of the reported score (method - true score of selected config), bars 1.96 x bias_se; y axis clipped at -4 pp, the conditional method lies below (range printed in the panel); simultaneous bound reports the naive point so no bias curve. Middle: coverage of the nominal 95% interval for the true score of the selected config, bars 1.96 x cover_se, grey band = binomial 95% MC band (500 reps). Bottom: mean interval width (pp), log axis. n=200, rho=.5, best-of-K. Methods: naive (Wilson interval, same items), held-out split, 5-fold cross-fit, bootstrap optimism (Harrell), bootstrap out-of-bag, simultaneous (max-statistic) bound, conditional-on-selection (median-unbiased; intervals 40-80 pp wide).
- **E4c** - five systems tuned on the same items with K = 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 configs (best-of-K), M = 500; top: share of repetitions in which the K=64 system is ranked first by each method's reported score vs how often it is truly best (black dashed; bars 1.96 sqrt(p(1-p)/500)); bottom: mean Kendall tau between reported and true ranking, bars 1.96 sd/sqrt(500). Naive ranks the K=64 system first in about 35-40% of repetitions at all tau, against 14-32% truly (tau=.01, n=200: 39% vs 21%); split and bootstrap out-of-bag track the truth. The equal-K16 design (chance level .2) is in the file, not drawn.
- **E5b** - argmax winner, M = 500, rho=.5, one panel per tau, n=200 (n=100, 500 via argument); coverage of the nominal 95% interval for the true score of the selected config (bars 1.96 x cover_se; grey band binomial MC band) and mean width (pp, log axis). Over the whole file (27 cells) hybrid coverage is 94.6-97.0% at width about 17 pp, conditional 94.0-96.2% at about 59 pp, simultaneous 96.6-99.2% at about 20 pp, naive 51-93% at about 13 pp.
- **Tables** - each `paper/tables/tN_*.tex` is a `booktabs` tabular wrapped in a group (`\footnotesize`, `\tabcolsep` 3.5 pt) that fits the 5.5 in text width in 10 pt Times (measured: T1 377 pt, T2 387 pt, T3 382 pt, T4 191 pt of 397 pt); no `table` environment, no caption. `tN_*_note.tex` holds the table note with the numbers of repetitions, ready to `\input` after the tabular. Cells show mean +- Monte Carlo s.e. (pp for bias/width). T1: reference cell, 2000 repetitions; the conditional method has RMSE 40 pp and mean bias -10 pp (reported as measured). T2: five stress cells, 2000 repetitions; the conditional method's mean interval width reaches 138 pp in the n=50, K=128, chance-level cell, i.e. its intervals are not clipped to [0,1] (stated, not hidden). T3: n=200, 500 repetitions. T4: 500 repetitions per cell.
- **E3** (K=60, the final matrix of 60 configs; from `real_gaussian_max_check.csv` and `real_summary.csv`; 1000 replications per point) - Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct on 2000 MMLU-Pro questions, replayed from the cached response matrix. (a) disjoint truth arm (truth = accuracy of the selected configuration on the pool questions outside the tuning set), n = 50-400; (b) iid arm (tuning set drawn i.i.d. with replacement, truth = accuracy over the whole pool), n = 50-800. Markers = replayed naive best-of-K gap with 1.96 x MC s.e., colour = n; lines = Gaussian-max prediction from the real matrix's empirical covariance (disjoint arm with the finite-population factor), K in {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 60}. Prediction and replay agree: max |difference| 0.36 pp (disjoint, 28 cells) and 0.28 pp (iid, 35 cells); 6 of 63 cells have |z| > 2 (z from -2.5 to +2.9; the K=60 cells at n=50 and 100 and the K=32 cell at n=200 have the prediction below the replay by 0.1-0.3 pp; K=1, n=100 disjoint: replay -0.37 pp vs prediction 0.0, z=2.9); on average the prediction is 2.1% below the replay for K>=4. The gap grows with K and flattens (K=60, best-of-K, disjoint: 9.25 +- .13 pp at n=50, 6.38 at 100, 4.18 at 200, 2.82 +- .06 at 400), shrinks roughly as 1/sqrt(n), and K=1 (no search) is within 0.4 pp of 0 (0.2 pp except n=100 disjoint). The two truth arms agree within 0.43 pp in every cell (K=60: iid 9.02/6.24/4.08/2.64 pp vs disjoint 9.25/6.38/4.18/2.82 at n=50/100/200/400; iid n=800: 1.59), no systematic sign. (c) best-of-K / successive halving / successive rejects / greedy at K=60, disjoint arm, n=50/100/200/400: best-of-K 9.25/6.38/4.18/2.82 pp, halving 5.60/4.30/3.01/1.89, rejects 7.48/5.30/3.65/2.33, greedy 7.44/5.04/3.36/2.30 (greedy drawn in grey, only exists at K=60); successive rejects is not monotone in K (n=50: 4.8 pp at K=2, 7.6 at K=4, 7.6 at K=8, 6.9 at K=16, 6.6 at K=32, 7.5 at K=60). Pool: all 60 planned configurations, true spread sd 1.16 pp (accuracy 15.2-20.8%), mean pairwise item correlation .35.

- **E7** (K=60) - T6: model, MMLU-Pro revision (first 12 characters in the table), items, configs complete vs planned (60 of 60; 120,000 of 120,000 forward passes scored), accuracy mean 18.2% / range 15.2-20.8% / sd 1.16 pp across configs, mean pairwise item correlation (.35), scoring jobs (10 chunks of 6 configurations, all finished; wall-clock 20.7-34.7 h per job from `scoring_wall_clock`, elapsed time on a shared host including frozen-container time; the resumed chunk 0.7 h), replay job times of the K=60 replays (85, 109 min), seeds, package versions. - **T7/T8** - each row is scored against its own truth `mu_ship` (true accuracy of the configuration that protocol shipped; differs by protocol because the search saw different data), 600 repetitions (synthetic), 400 (real). T7 at budget 200: naive bias +4.53 +- .10 pp, RMSE 5.19, coverage .762; bootstrap OOB bias -0.12, RMSE 2.85, coverage .975; 50/50 split RMSE 4.57, coverage .952; fresh 100/100 bias -0.23 +- .18, RMSE 4.53, coverage .952; fresh 50/150 RMSE 3.87; hybrid RMSE 5.63 (above naive 5.19), coverage .940. So at equal total items OOB has the smaller RMSE and (slightly conservative) near-nominal coverage; split and fresh-set are unbiased but pay the sampling error of their report items. So at equal items OOB has the smallest RMSE; fresh-set 50/150 is next and unbiased.
- **T9** (K=60) - mean pairwise phi, accuracy mean/sd and item difficulty of the real matrix vs the one-rho Gaussian copula (accuracies as thresholds); the one-rho copula cannot match the spread of the real pairwise phi (real -.007 to .926, generator .321-.366, max abs error .572) while the pairwise (tetrachoric) copula matches every pair (max error .053, 19 eigenvalues clipped). Naive best-of-K gap, disjoint arm, n=200: real 1.09/2.07/2.81/3.39/3.81/4.18 pp at K=2/4/8/16/32/60; one-rho 1.14/2.04/2.95/3.57/4.30/4.85 (z -0.4 to +7.8), pairwise 1.09/1.97/2.67/3.31/3.83/4.14 (z within -1.6 to +0.2). Over all 63 cells (10 pools x 300 replications per generator): one-rho overstates by +6.8% on average (K>=4; range -10% to +16%; 31 of 63 cells |z|>2), pairwise understates by -1.3% (range -14% to +4%; 4 of 63). What the copula cannot match: heterogeneous pairwise dependence (near-duplicate and near-independent configuration pairs) and, for the pairwise version, higher-order structure.
