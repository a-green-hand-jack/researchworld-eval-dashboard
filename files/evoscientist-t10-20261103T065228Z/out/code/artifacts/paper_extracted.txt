Fixed-Score Confidence Intervals:
Conditional Gains and Finite-Sample Costs
Anonymous Authors1
Abstract
Can confidence intervals become narrower when
labels, unlabeled observations, and a supplied
predictor cannot change? For binary popula-
tion means, we separate nonlinear information
in an immutable score from the cost of exploit-
ing it with valid inference. A control-variate
decomposition characterizes the oracle variance
improvement beyond affine PPI++, while pilot-
conditioned binary component intervals provide
finite-sample coverage without retraining the base
predictor. In a prespecified low-disagreement syn-
thetic setting, the latter intervals reduce mean
width by 29.14% (paired 95% Monte Carlo in-
terval: 29.03–29.25%) against full-budget exact
label-only intervals. The gain disappears with a
smaller unlabeled pool, higher disagreement, and
two real empirical populations; a larger 48.63%
Wald reduction instead exhibits undercoverage.
These are conditional-gain and negative-result
findings, not a new general PPI principle, an ex-
haustive literature comparison, or a claim of uni-
versally shorter valid intervals.
1. Introduction
Expensive gold-standard measurements and plentiful model
scores arise in remote sensing, astronomy, and scientific
model evaluation. Prediction-powered inference (PPI) uses
a labeled residual correction to turn these scores into valid
inference rather than accepting their predictions as truth
(Angelopoulos et al., 2023). The practical question here is
narrower than improving the predictor: after both datasets
and their predictions have been collected, can a different
inferential use of exactly those inputs produce a shorter
confidence interval?

Three distinctions determine the answer. First, PPI++ al-
ready optimizes the affine correction to a supplied score
(Angelopoulos et al., 2024); comparing only with untuned
PPI overstates remaining room. Second, low variance and
valid coverage are different objectives. A narrow plug-in
normal interval need not achieve its nominal level at a sci-
entifically relevant sample size. Third, no realized dataset
has a frequentist repeated-sampling guarantee conditional
on every possible observed input. We keep inputs identical
across methods within a repetition, and assess unconditional
repeated-sampling coverage under an explicit model.
We study binary prevalence, θ = EY , because it directly
represents quantities such as the proportion of deforested
plots or galaxies with spiral arms. Binary labels also per-
mit exact finite-sample probability inversion, so the strict
coverage question need not be answered by a normal ap-
proximation alone. We only use functions of the immutable
score Z = f(X) as inference controls. This permits fitting
a small correction using existing labels but never changes
f, adds a label, or enlarges the unlabeled sample. If im-
mutability were instead interpreted as forbidding even fitted
inference controls, only prespecified score maps would be
admissible; that is a stricter problem.
Our contribution is an evidence-backed conditional-gain
analysis. A variance identity isolates the conditional-mean
information not represented by an affine score. Nested finite-
dimensional controls test this headroom without weakening
the PPI++ baseline. A pilot-conditioned, signed compo-
sition of three exact binomial intervals then tests whether
a gain survives finite-sample coverage requirements. The
resulting tradeoff is concrete: low disagreement and a suf-
ficiently large existing unlabeled pool can help, whereas
affine conditional means, conservative uncertainty composi-
tion, and rare labels can eliminate an apparent improvement.
On the supplied real-data empirical populations, additional
gains beyond affine PPI++ are small, and the exact construc-
tion is wider than label-only exact inference.
These methods use standard control-variate projection and
PPI rectification ideas. No algorithmic priority claim is
made. The study deliberately restricts its literature access to
directly supplied starter papers and an older concentration
reference, under an independent-research access constraint.

5. Experiments
5.1. Protocol, datasets, and comparisons
All methods receive identical labeled values, scores, and
unlabeled scores in each repetition. No model training,
prospective allocation, additional labeling, or sample en-
largement occurs within a comparison. Varying n, Nacross
experiments is a sensitivity analysis, not an additional re-
source given to one method. All nominal intervals use
α = .05. Intersecting with [0, 1] preserves coverage; an
empty intersection is retained as empty, with zero width and
no coverage.
Known-truth synthetic laws. Except for the explicitly
invalid shift stress test, Z, eZ are iid uniform on [0, 1], and
Y | Z ∼ Bernoulli(m(Z)). Central laws are linear m =
.1 +.8Z, anticorrelated m = .9 − .8Z, uninformative m =
.5, U-shaped m = .05+.91{Z < .25 or Z ≥ .75}, and rare
m = .002 + .041{Z ≥ .9}. Their means are .5 except rare,
whose mean is .006. A separately prespecified finite-sample
supplement uses sharp U-shaped m = .005 + .991{Z <
.25 or Z ≥ .75}. The shape and error probabilities are
frozen generators, not fitted using hidden unlabeled labels.
Oracle controls use the known formula only as explicitly
infeasible diagnostics.
Real empirical populations. We use galaxies and for-
est from the pinned ppi-python==0.2.3 example data:
respectively 16,743 and 1,596 paired labels/precomputed
scores. Both label arrays are binary; no missing values occur,
and scores lie in [0, 1]. The empirical means are approxi-
mately .259273 and .151629. Forest has only 252 distinct
scores; duplicated score values are retained, not mistaken for
iid sampling violations. We independently sample labeled
and unlabeled indices with replacement from each fixed em-
pirical distribution. Methods never receive labels at sampled
unlabeled indices. Coverage targets that known empirical-
law mean; it does not establish iid sampling, gold-standard
quality, or external scientific validity of the original data
collection. Raw-data licensing is not separately specified
by the pinned package; download provenance and hashes
are provided, without claiming its MIT code license covers
data.
Repetitions and chronology. The main suite has 10,000
repetitions per central synthetic law at n = 1000 , N=
10000; 3,000 per sample-size, finite-ratio, and real-data set-
ting; and 20,000 for rare n = 200, N= 2000. Independent
ablations have 3,000 per law. The finite-sample supple-
ment has 5,000–10,000 per setting. Totals are 103,000 main,
12,000 ablation, and 65,000 supplementary repetitions; de-
terministic reruns are not extra evidence. Small pilot runs
tested execution/runtime only. The binary supplement was
motivated by EB’s concentration cost and preregistered be-
fore its own results were examined; its chronology is distinct
from the original primary experiment. No confirmatory con-
figuration was tuned to gain or coverage afterward.
Uncertainty and validity assessment. We report Wilson
95% Monte Carlo (MC) intervals for marginal coverage and
paired delta-method 95% MC intervals for ratios of mean
widths. Ratios are not averages of within-repetition ratios.
Four prespecified nonlinear Series/PPI++ contrasts and four
sharp CPRectHist/label-CP contrasts form separate Holm-
corrected width-test families. Coverage intervals and other
comparisons are descriptive, not simultaneous. All four tests
in the first family and three in the second support smaller
mean width after correction; the N = n sharp setting is
wider. Width significance does not rescue undercoverage.
In particular, a coverage MC interval containing.95 is not
proof of exact validity, and all covered repetitions do not
prove coverage one.
5.2. Nonlinear headroom does not certify finite-sample
validity
Table 1 and Figure 1 separate the large synthetic opportu-
nity from modest empirical gains. In the U-shaped law,
raw-score covariance is zero, so oracle affine PPI++ cannot
exploit the conditional mean. Series4 reduces mean width
by 48.63% and approaches the score-function oracle, as the
projection calculation predicts. Yet its coverage is 94.43%,
with MC interval [93.96, 94.86]%, below the nominal tar-

5.3. Finite-sample gains are conditional and smaller
Table 2 and Figure 2 use only the separate binary supple-
ment. In the low-disagreement sharp law at n = 1000, N=
10000, CPRectHist has mean width .04455, versus .06287
for full-budget label-only CP: a reduction of 29.14% (95%
MC interval 29.03–29.25%). All 10,000 CPRectHist repeti-
tions covered; the Wilson lower endpoint is 99.96%, while
the theorem supplies the distribution-wide 95% guarantee
under its hypotheses. The observed overcoverage is substan-
tial conservatism, not exact nominal calibration.
The same construction is only 13.90% narrower at n =
200, N= 2000, and 32.28% narrower at n = 4000, N=
40000. With n = N = 1000 it is 54.37% wider. With
the moderate U-shaped disagreement level, it is wider than
label-only CP despite the large normal-oracle headroom. It
is also wider on both real empirical laws. Raw-threshold
exact PPI composition, even using all labels, cannot exploit
the sharp U shape; its failure shows the value of the map,
not a new general rectification principle.
EB makes the conservatism particularly visible. In the

These failed routes remain in the report rather than being

5.4. Ablations and failure mechanisms
Nested basis, bins, and fitting. We fix B = 4 before
confirmation and ablate B ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16} on independent
repetitions, never selecting the best observed interval. Se-
ries1 is affine-only; adding indicators retains the affine class.
In the U-shaped law, two bins do not encode the symmetric
extremes, whereas four aligned bins do. Beyond four, fur-
ther bin splitting supplies no additional conditional-mean
signal and can increase estimation cost. On the linear law,
histogram-only binning discards within-bin signal, whereas
the nested series retains it. Full results include real-data
ablations and a same-label histogram fit, so cross-fitting is
not credited with creating nonlinear information. Figure 3
presents the main mechanism; sample-size/oracle sensitivity
is in Appendix B.
Rare outcomes defeat fitted Wald intervals. For iid
Bernoulli mean p >0, all labeled outcomes are zero with
probability (1 − p)n. Label Wald, zero-covariance PPI++,
and the tested mean-fitted controls then return [0, 0]. Their
coverage is at most 1 − (1 − p)n. At p = .006, n= 200
the analytic ceiling is 69.99%, and PPI++ achieves only
69.89%. More unlabeled scores do not change that event.
Exact CP and EB avoid zero-width collapse. Appendix Ta-
ble 3 reports coverage and widths; even the synthetic oracle
normal interval need not be reliable with rare labels.
Mismatched score populations invalidate rectification.
In the shift stress test, labeled scores remain uniform, but un-
labeled scores are Beta(2, 5); the target remains the labeled-
law mean. Matching marginals fails, so score-mean differ-
ences are not mean-zero. Series4 has zero observed cov-
erage; many support intersections become empty, yielding
near-zero mean width. This is an invalidity signal, not an
efficiency gain. Label-only CP remains applicable. Appar-
ent EB robustness in one shift setting does not extend its
theorem beyond matching populations.

6. Discussion, Limitations, and Conclusion
Answer to the fixed-input question. There can be shorter
valid intervals without more labels or a different base pre-
dictor. The gain comes from a better use of existing
score information, not a resource change. In this study
a theorem-backed binary composition is narrower in a low-
disagreement nonlinear setting, but finite-sample protection
costs much of the normal-oracle advantage. There is no
universal shortening: affine conditional means have zero
nonlinear score-only oracle headroom, a finite basis may
miss nonlinear structure, and even informative scores may
not offset splitting and uncertainty-composition penalties.
These no-gain statements have different scopes and should
not be conflated with a minimax impossibility theorem.
Practical implications. Prespecify the inference control
and guarantee class before inspecting the remaining labels
or interval widths. Compare against tuned, unconstrained
finite-ratio PPI++, not merely raw PPI, and against full-
budget exact label-only inference when strict binary cov-
erage matters. A strong coverage theorem does not imply
near-nominal tightness; a small MC width error bar does not
imply statistical validity. When labels are rare, the all-zero
event provides a simple analytic warning before optimizing
coefficients.
Limitations. This is a restricted independent study of bi-
nary means and score-only controls. It does not establish
novelty relative to unsearched literature, global optimality,
or superiority to every available exact PPI construction. The
positive exact result uses a deliberately favorable low-error
synthetic mechanism. Real-data analyses are resampling
experiments on supplied empirical laws, not new remote-
sensing or astronomy conclusions; the original predictor-
training independence and annotation provenance were not
independently established. Known-weight designed strati-
fication, additional features, finite-population corrections,
quantiles, regression parameters, growing control classes,
and distribution shift require separate analysis. Numerical
CP inversion is tested in ordinary floating-point arithmetic,
not formally verified rounding. Conservative CP composi-
tion can be improved in principle, but post-hoc choosing
the shortest reported interval would require selection-safe
coverage not supplied here.

Impact Statement

is explicit separation of validity from apparent precision.

policy decisions about the original populations. Source data

independent licensing is not established by a package’s code
license. The manuscript and reproducibility artifacts were
prepared with an AI research assistant; mathematical audits
and deterministic reruns reduce, but do not eliminate, the
need for human scientific review.
