\begin{abstract}
Historical item-level responses can reduce the cost of evaluating a new language model, but representative selection, score reconstruction and model ranking are different objectives. We study a cross-fitted generalized-difference estimator of a finite binary benchmark mean. Independent residual sampling gives exact design-unbiasedness, without assuming source--target distribution agreement; this does not imply low predictive risk. We compare eighteen estimators and selection adapters on thirteen public matrices at budgets of 10, 30 and 100 items, with source-only tuning, twenty paired item draws and smaller-source and name-proxy exclusion checks. Crossfit reduces equally weighted benchmark MAE relative to random means, but exceeds random-subset score ridge by 3.030, 1.074 and 0.292 percentage points at the three budgets. At 30 items its excess is 1.074 [0.999, 1.150] points, using a conditional 95\% paired interval. Ranking also depends on the metric: at this budget crossfit slightly increases Spearman correlation over random means while reducing Kendall correlation. Learned anchors and item-response adapters provide further useful but heterogeneous comparisons. The evidence favors separating an expectation guarantee from score and ranking performance; it does not establish a universally best method or transfer to future model families.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Evaluating a model on every item of a benchmark can be expensive when many checkpoints or inference configurations must be compared. If historical models have already answered every item, a new target model may need to answer only a subset. The historical responses can guide which items to retain, how to reconstruct the full score, or both.

These choices should be distinguished. A representative subset can produce useful rankings while shifting absolute scores. A learned score predictor can reduce error within a familiar collection but depend on relationships that change for new models. Even an unbiased estimator can have large realized errors. The practical question is therefore how much each use of historical data improves score error and within-benchmark ranking at a fixed target-item cost, and where it fails.

This study evaluates a cross-fitted generalized-difference proposal against simple and relevant existing methods. Residual correction and source-response imputation are established prior art \citep{zhang2025misses}; we make no novelty claim for those ingredients. The candidate instead motivates a precise finite-population expectation identity and a comparison of its variance and prediction-error costs. Its imputer uses half the observed target responses in each direction. That can sacrifice predictive information even when the expectation identity is valid.

We ask three questions: whether source assistance and learned selection improve equal-cost prediction; whether cross-fitting's guarantee justifies its realized error relative to close correction and regression comparators; and how conclusions change with less history, name-proxy family exclusion and response collisions. All thirteen supplied matrices and all planned conditions were evaluated. The candidate reduces macro MAE relative to random means, yet learned-score ridge has lower macro MAE and better rank correlations at every budget. Matched-imputer controls show that residual correction can help at larger budgets without overcoming the stronger learned controls. These conditional and heterogeneous findings, the explicit finite-population derivation, and reproducible comparisons are the contribution; new residual-correction ingredients and universal superiority are not claimed.

\section{Related work}
Anchor Points selects representatives using correlations of source-model class confidences and uses cluster-weighted confidence scores or item prediction \citep{vivek2024anchor}. Binary correctness clustering is a distinct adaptation explicitly described in tinyBenchmarks \citep{maiapolo2024tiny}. That work also selects items from multidimensional item-response representations, preserves observed responses in p-IRT, and blends imputed and observed estimates in gp-IRT. Selection and estimation are thus separate ingredients; a correctness-anchor baseline is not an exact reproduction of confidence-based Anchor Points.

The permitted study of \citet{zhang2025misses} already compares random-subset ridge score prediction and same-sample ridge AIPW. Its AIPW fits target correctness across sampled items using source responses as features and corrects the aggregate using sampled residual information. Our comparison distinguishes its paper formula from the return expression in the supplied pinned implementation. It also distinguishes that item-regression orientation from historical-model regression, avoiding attribution of every crossfit--AIPW difference solely to splitting. Published result values are not reused as our measurements; all empirical comparisons below use the supplied matrices and delivered execution artifacts.

The bounded experiment concerns fixed reusable subsets of one binary matrix. It does not evaluate every efficient-evaluation family or establish superiority over response-adaptive, text-dependent or continuous-score approaches. The source dossier records broader permitted context and the exact versions and reading scopes; the present method discussion relies on the three directly consulted papers above.

\section{Problem and methods}\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Finite-matrix estimand}
Let $X\in\{0,1\}^{M\times N}$ contain complete historical-model responses. A new target has fixed binary responses $y_1,\ldots,y_N$ and score $\mu=N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^N y_j$. A method observes $y_S$ at $k$ distinct items and predicts $\mu$ using $X$ and those responses. Full target outcomes are judging data, not training or tuning inputs. The complete matrices are physically available for audit; the separation is controlled estimator use, not inaccessible labels. The budget counts unique target observations per benchmark. Historical fitting costs are reported separately.

The estimand is the unweighted mean of supplied matrix columns. Without verified subject/subtask weights, this does not reconstruct an official macro-averaged benchmark or multi-benchmark leaderboard score. At $k=100$, the observed fraction ranges from 0.83\% to 22.88\%; this budget is not always a tiny subset. Appendix~\ref{app:pools} gives every benchmark fraction.

\subsection{Simple and learned estimates}
For a uniform sample $S$ without replacement, the random mean is $\bar y_S$. Let $\bar x_j=M^{-1}\sum_m X_{mj}$ and $\bar x=N^{-1}\sum_j\bar x_j$. A fixed source-difference control is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-difference}
 \widehat\mu_{\mathrm{SD}}=\bar x+\frac1k\sum_{j\in S}(y_j-\bar x_j).
\end{equation}
It is design-unbiased because $\bar x_j$ is fixed before sampling. Improvement over the random mean depends on the across-item variance of $y_j-\bar x_j$, which can increase under mismatch. The zero-observation historical mean is a separate control; its constant predictions can have undefined ranking correlations.

Random-subset ridge regresses historical full scores on responses at $S$, then inserts $y_S$. Ridge minimizes a sum of squared residuals plus $\alpha$ times squared coefficients, with an unpenalized intercept and no feature standardization. Eligible $\alpha\in\{0.1,1,10,100,1000\}$ minimizes mean development MAE over five independent item draws before historical refitting. The primary predictions are not clipped. A full-sample imputation control instead regresses the historical mean on unsampled items on the selected responses, and includes observed target items exactly.

\subsection{Cross-fitted generalized difference}\label{sec:crossfit}
Draw a uniformly ordered sample of $k$ distinct items and partition it into $A$ and $B$, of sizes $\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ and $\lceil k/2\rceil$. Conditional on $A$, $B$ is a uniform sample from $U=\{1,\ldots,N\}\setminus A$. An imputer fitted using $X$ and $y_A$ supplies $f_j(A,y_A)$ on $U$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crossfit}
 \widehat\mu_{A\to B}=\frac1N\left[\sum_{j\in A}y_j+\sum_{j\in U}f_j+
 \frac{N-|A|}{|B|}\sum_{j\in B}(y_j-f_j)\right],\qquad
 \widehat\mu_{\mathrm{CF}}=\frac{\widehat\mu_{A\to B}+\widehat\mu_{B\to A}}2.
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:unbiased}
For fixed finite $y,X$, positive fold sizes, and an imputer independent of the residual sample conditional on its fitting fold, $\mathbb E[\widehat\mu_{\mathrm{CF}}]=\mu$ over the declared sampling design. No agreement of source and target response distributions is required.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditional on $A,y_A$ and the fixed imputer, each $j\in U$ belongs to $B$ with probability $|B|/|U|$. The expected expanded residual sum is therefore $\sum_{j\in U}(y_j-f_j)$. Substitution in Eq.~\eqref{eq:crossfit} leaves $N^{-1}\sum_j y_j$. The exchanged direction has the same property, so averaging preserves the expectation.
\end{proof}
The implementation fits multi-output historical-model ridge from responses at $A$ to other items and inserts $y_A$. Linearity permits computing aggregate predictions without materializing an item-by-item output matrix. Although the optimized aggregate subtracts the fitted mean at $B$, it is algebraically identical to evaluating a previously fixed full imputer at a random residual sample. This computation does not fit that imputer using $B$.

Tuning on the same realized residual-item identities or outcomes could invalidate the proposition. Regularization is fixed using independent development draws before final sampling. A source-only choice fixed before sampling is allowed. The guarantee concerns expected signed error, not MAE, RMSE, ordering or confidence-interval coverage. Predictions may leave $[0,1]$; clipped predictions are a separately named biased ablation. Controls remove residual correction with the same half-sample imputer (both matched and independently selected regularization), and compare full-sample same-orientation imputation.

Figure~\ref{fig:crossfit-design} summarizes the sampling and information flow; its historical matrix $H$ is $X$ above. Both directions reuse the same $k$ observed target responses.


\subsection{Close same-sample AIPW comparators}
AIPW regresses target responses across sampled items, using source-model responses as features, whereas the candidate regresses across historical models using sampled items as features. Let $m_S,m_U$ be the fitted means on sampled and unsampled items. The paper-formula adapter \citep[Eq.~3]{zhang2025misses} and supplied pinned-code reproduction are respectively
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aipw}
 \widehat\mu_{\mathrm{paper}}=\bar y_S+(1-k/N)(m_U-m_S),\qquad
 \widehat\mu_{\mathrm{code}}=m_U+(1-k/N)(\bar y_S-m_S).
\end{equation}
The code uses fixed $\alpha=10$ and an unpenalized intercept; the paper adapter uses development-selected regularization. The primary variants differ in regularization as well as return formula, so their empirical contrast cannot isolate the effect of the formula discrepancy. At identical fits their difference is $(k/N)(m_U-\bar y_S)$. The intercept makes the sampled residual mean numerically zero: the paper form equals the fitted population mean and the code form the fitted unsampled mean. Independent analysis checks compare the optimized forms with the exact pinned predict body and direct scikit-learn ridge. The word ``AIPW'' here identifies the prior comparator; no crossfit design guarantee is asserted for these same-sample fits.

\subsection{Selections and item-response adapters}
Correctness anchors cluster $X_{\cdot j}$ with $k$-means, select the nearest real member to each centroid and weight its observed outcome by cluster mass \citep{maiapolo2024tiny}. A ridge score estimator on the same selected items separates estimation from selection. Selection uses historical responses only, with three initializations, at most 100 iterations, and frozen seeds.

MAP-MIRT fits $p_{mj}=\operatorname{sigmoid}(\theta_m^\top a_j+b_j)$, with a fixed Gaussian MAP penalty and development-selected dimension in $\{2,5,10,15\}$. The p-style estimate preserves observed outcomes exactly and imputes only unobserved probabilities. The gp-style adapter blends it with the observed estimate using a development-selected mixture weight. IRT anchors cluster $(a_j,-b_j)$, retaining cluster weights. Random and IRT-anchor comparisons share the selected dimension. These are explicit MAP/inference/validation-mixture adapters, not exact tinyBenchmarks variational hierarchical fitting. Appendix~\ref{app:implementation} gives all optimization, continuation and mixing rules.

\section{Experimental design}\label{sec:protocol}
\subsection{Audit and separation}
All thirteen supplied matrices were acquired at the fixed upstream revision recorded in the reproduction instructions. The measured audit finds finite binary entries and no missing cells (Appendix~\ref{app:audit}). Exact repeated OpenLLM names have identical response vectors and are collapsed before splitting. Distinct names with identical responses remain distinct in the primary evaluation. All HELM rows use opaque row IDs because matching lengths do not establish name alignment; LegalBench has fewer rows than the supplied name list.

The original development pilot's locked target membership is preserved, with deterministic seed 20261103. Approximately 30\% of deduplicated rows are targets; historical rows are divided approximately 80/20 into fitting/development pools. Exact primary counts are in Table~\ref{tab:protocol-counts}; row IDs and identity maps are committed. No pilot-evaluated development model becomes a final target. Item selection and all dimension, penalty and mixture choices use only historical data; eligible components are refitted on all history after development.

\subsection{Matched budgets, ablations and stress conditions}
An independent prospective assessment accepted the comparison and robustness scope before target execution; its decision and continuing conditions are recorded in \path{study/execution_gate.md}. A subsequent pre-target scoring-only amendment pairs macro rank differences on common-defined benchmark cells; it leaves estimators, tuning, partitions and item draws unchanged. The feasibility owner extended its decision to the amended scorer. The original authorization concerned the unchanged predictor scope; the formal exact amended-scorer-hash extension was published after prediction launch. Original timestamps are preserved (Appendix~\ref{app:numerics}). The primary protocol covers all matrices at $k\in\{10,30,100\}$ with twenty final draws, distinct from the five development draws. Budgets are nested within each independently drawn ordering. Uniform methods share the same draw across targets, allowing within-benchmark ranking and paired method comparisons. Source-selected anchors are fixed across final repeats; copied anchor outputs do not create independent fit uncertainty. Target predictors receive only their declared items through an observation-accounting guard. A separate scoring step computes full target truth only after every required prediction exists. The guard prevents accidental budget violations; it is not a cryptographic isolation mechanism.

Selection controls compare mean versus ridge on correctness anchors, random versus IRT anchors with the same MIRT estimator, and observed versus p-/gp-style estimation on IRT anchors. Correction controls include source difference, the uncorrected half-fit imputer, full-sample imputation and clipped crossfit. Source-size sensitivity uses nested historical fractions $.25,.5,1$ at $k=30$ with the original targets. Name-proxy family exclusion is restricted to verified per-benchmark OpenLLM lists: the largest eligible target proxy group is selected from names/counts alone, requiring at least ten target members and forty remaining historical rows. It compares the same target cohort with ordinary and proxy-excluded history. It does not verify ancestry. Metadata eligibility is frozen before scoring (Table~\ref{tab:family-cohorts}).

After predictions are fixed, exact source--target response collisions and target-pattern-weighted errors are retrospective diagnostics. They do not determine training data or items. Full-response deduplication would change the population and can leak target information into fitting, so it is not substituted for the primary experiment.

\subsection{Metrics, uncertainty and failures}
Primary metrics are per-benchmark MAE/RMSE in percentage points; secondary metrics are within-benchmark Spearman and Kendall $\tau_b$, retaining ties and explicit undefined counts. Signed error, tail error, out-of-range rates and close-pair inversions are diagnostics. True ties are distinct from the predefined one-percentage-point close-pair bin. No pooled cross-benchmark rank correlation substitutes for within-benchmark ranking. Final rank/tie calculations round truth and predictions to twelve decimals; errors retain raw predictions. This dated post-scoring repair, triggered by a violated mathematical rank invariant, is disclosed with its nonzero precision sensitivity in Appendix~\ref{app:numerics}.

Primary 95\% paired $t$ intervals for the mean of twenty independent item-draw differences quantify Monte Carlo uncertainty in expected performance conditional on the fixed source/target pools. Repeats are not independent new models; these intervals are not prediction intervals for a future model or draw. A prespecified sensitivity uses 5,000 paired draw-bootstrap replicates and percentile endpoints, with seed 817. Macro summaries weight the thirteen finite matrices equally within each repeat before computing intervals; undefined correlations retain their denominators. A method--reference macro contrast first takes per-benchmark paired differences where both metrics are defined, then averages; differing defined subsets are never subtracted as if they were the same population. Each contrast records the common-defined counts, and a rank summary of fewer than thirteen benchmarks is labeled as that subset. Any benchmark-bootstrap summary would be descriptive of these heterogeneous matrices. Per-benchmark outcomes and unfavorable candidate comparisons must accompany macro-averages. Numerical failures, capped fits, continuation attempts, timing and memory are retained, with no target-error-selected fallback.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
All 45 source/target scenarios completed: thirteen main matrices at three budgets, twenty-six reduced-source cases and six proxy-exclusion cases. There are 1,611,440 saved predictions in 22,360 method cells. Access/cell audits and independent full-truth, baseline and error checks passed. All 219 MIRT source fits met the declared relative-objective stopping criterion; thirteen needed the allowed continuation. This certifies optimizer stopping, not global optimality: the largest recorded gradient component is 1.250. No primary fit or inference failure was replaced by another method.

\subsection{Source assistance and selection}
Table~\ref{tab:absolute-summary-k30} reports all eighteen methods at $k=30$. Figure~\ref{fig:paired-crossfit-mae-pp-k30} retains all thirteen per-benchmark contrasts against four references, and Appendix~\ref{app:empirical} supplies the complete all-budget tables and diagnostics.
At $k=10,30,100$, crossfit's macro MAE is respectively 8.563, 4.836 and 2.498 percentage points (pp), compared with 10.616, 5.989 and 3.248 for random means. The paired crossfit-minus-random differences are $-2.054$ [$-2.353,-1.754$], $-1.153$ [$-1.325,-0.981$] and $-0.750$ [$-0.849,-0.651$] pp. These and subsequent brackets are conditional 95\% paired $t$ intervals. Source difference also improves score error, but adds a common constant to every target's random mean within a draw, so its rankings are exactly the random-mean rankings.

Stronger learned comparators change the conclusion. Random-subset score ridge has macro MAE 5.533, 3.761 and 2.206 pp. Crossfit's excess is 3.030 [2.885, 3.175], 1.074 [0.999, 1.150] and 0.292 [0.242, 0.341] pp; macro RMSE is also larger at each budget. The macro result hides benchmark differences. At $k=30$, crossfit-minus-ridge MAE ranges from $-0.558$ pp on HELM Math [$-0.775,-0.341$] to $+3.831$ pp on GPQA [3.554, 4.107]. This is not a claim that ridge wins every benchmark or that a tally of unadjusted intervals establishes family-wide significance.

Selection and estimation both matter. At $k=30$, correctness-anchor mean MAE is 4.225 pp and ridge on those same items is 3.242 pp, compared with 3.761 pp for random-subset ridge. The IRT-anchor mean, p- and gp-style adapters give 4.759, 4.553 and 3.479 pp; random p-/gp-style adapters give 4.847 and 4.084 pp. These controls keep the representation/dimension or selected items shared while changing the other ingredient. At $k=100$, IRT-anchor gp has macro MAE 2.027 pp versus correctness-anchor ridge's 2.250 pp; the latter has higher Spearman correlation (0.943 versus 0.935). These are point comparisons of the fixed selections, not selection-seed confidence statements or a universal winner claim.

\subsection{The error cost of cross-fitting}
Against development-tuned paper-AIPW, crossfit-minus-reference MAE is 0.094 [$-0.189,0.377$] pp at $k=10$, 0.295 [0.207, 0.384] at $k=30$, and 0.070 [0.040, 0.100] at $k=100$. The lowest budget gives no resolved macro MAE advantage in either direction. The item-regression orientation and regularization differ from crossfit, so these contrasts do not isolate splitting.

The matched-half-imputer control is more diagnostic. Adding crossfit's residual correction changes MAE by $+0.738$ [0.575, 0.900] pp at $k=10$, then $-0.130$ [$-0.215,-0.046$] and $-0.335$ [$-0.394,-0.277$] at $k=30,100$. Correction helps the same imputer at the larger budgets, but the separately tuned uncorrected half-fit aggregate remains stronger: crossfit exceeds it by 2.706, 1.027 and 0.190 pp. Full-fit ridge imputation is also stronger, at 5.532, 3.720 and 2.160 pp. Regularization and using all observed items thus remain relevant explanations; design-unbiasedness alone is insufficient.

Clipping lowers crossfit's MAE by 0.112 [0.077, 0.147] pp at $k=10$, 0.025 [0.013, 0.037] at $k=30$, and 0.002 [0.001, 0.003] at $k=100$. It changes the estimator and its expectation guarantee. The saved per-target twenty-draw variance/squared-mean-error decomposition is descriptive: finite Monte Carlo squared mean error is not a population-design-bias estimate.

Ranking does not follow MAE. At $k=30$, crossfit has macro Spearman 0.799 and Kendall $\tau_b=0.652$, versus 0.793 and 0.665 for random means. Its paired Spearman gain is 0.0058 [0.0007, 0.0109], but its Kendall change is $-0.0134$ [$-0.0185,-0.0083$]. Relative to ridge, both decrease: $-0.0477$ [$-0.0573,-0.0382$] and $-0.0428$ [$-0.0498,-0.0357$]. The corresponding primary contrasts use all thirteen common-defined benchmarks and twenty draws. Constant zero-observation source-prior predictions have undefined correlations and are retained as such. Ties, close-pair inversions and every other undefined cell remain in the full results.

An additive retrospective same-fit AIPW diagnostic, prompted by independent review after primary predictions were fixed, reconstructs paper-$\alpha10=(1-k/N)\,\mathrm{code}_{\alpha10}+(k/N)\bar y_S$; 55,520 actual preselected-$\alpha10$ predictions agree with maximum discrepancy $4.44\times10^{-16}$. Code-minus-paper MAE at this matched fit is $-0.0062$ [$-0.0083,-0.0042$], $-0.0022$ [$-0.0061,0.0016$] and $+0.0498$ [0.0392, 0.0604] pp. The macro MAE effect is small and budget-dependent here; it is distinct from comparing the differently tuned primary variants. Individual prediction differences reach 2.569 pp at $k=100$, so the small macro effect does not establish individual equivalence.

\subsection{Less history, proxy exclusion and response collisions}
At $k=30$, reducing historical rows to one quarter increases crossfit MAE by 0.236 [0.196, 0.276] pp and ridge MAE by 0.648 [0.527, 0.768] pp, macro-averaged over thirteen matrices. With half the history, the increases are 0.181 [0.135, 0.227] and 0.248 [0.175, 0.322] pp. Excluding the selected name-proxy family increases MAE by 0.067 [0.014, 0.121] pp for crossfit and 0.220 [0.174, 0.266] for ridge over the six eligible fixed target cohorts. Smaller degradation does not establish lower resulting error or robustness to unseen lineages. Random mean is unchanged because targets and item draws are shared.

Retrospective collisions are concentrated unevenly: OpenLLM Math has 129 target rows, 119 distinct response patterns and thirteen source-matching targets; GPQA and MMLU-Pro each have 129 rows and 127 patterns. On OpenLLM Math at $k=30$, equal weighting of target patterns changes crossfit MAE from 2.156 to 2.281 pp and ridge MAE from 1.770 to 1.820 pp. Restricting to the 116 targets without an exact historical response match gives 2.313 and 1.839 pp. These descriptive populations do not remove related training or verify ancestry, and were not used for fitting or selection.

\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:limits}
The expectation identity separates a sampling guarantee from predictive usefulness. Learned imputation can reduce variance only when residual structure is favorable; splitting also reduces the observed information available to each fit. The completed comparisons show this distinction in practice: macro MAE improves over random means but remains above stronger learned estimators. Neither the sign of the expectation bias nor one ranking statistic substitutes for the requested score and ordering comparisons.

This retrospective study predicts frozen binary responses. It does not evaluate changes of prompt, decoding, benchmark version or scoring. Models in a collection may be related; identity deduplication does not remove shared training or ancestry. HELM identity alignment remains unresolved, and name-proxy exclusion is a bounded stress condition. Conditional item-draw uncertainty does not establish transfer to future model families. Matrix means need not match official benchmark aggregates. No new LLM evaluations or API costs are measured, so reduced observation count alone cannot establish a measured end-to-end inference saving.

Efficient evaluation could encourage wider, cheaper checks, but inaccurate reconstructed scores can mislead model selection or overstate small differences. Full evaluation remains appropriate when decisions require precision unsupported by subset estimates. This work does not establish contamination resistance, fairness across applications or deployment safety. Agent-based tools participate in literature inspection, method development, implementation, experimental planning, writing and review in this research workflow; their scientific contributions require the same evidence checks as the rest of the study. No human-subject experiment or new model training is performed.
