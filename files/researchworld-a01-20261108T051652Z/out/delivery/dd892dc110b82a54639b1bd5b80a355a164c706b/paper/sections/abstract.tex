\begin{abstract}
A developer who wants a model to top a multi-task leaderboard can fold data related to some sub-datasets into its training, raising its scores there.
We ask how large this \emph{targeted investment} must be for a given target model to rank first, and how this depends on the rule that aggregates sub-dataset scores.
We define investment as the minimum cost of raising the target's scores, within a per-task cap, so that it is strictly first while all other models stay fixed, and we measure it as the number of tasks touched ($K$), the total score points ($L_1$), and the largest single-task raise.
We solve this exactly (closed forms and MILPs, tested against brute force) for nine aggregation rules on two public leaderboards: the Open LLM Leaderboard (6 tasks, about 4500 models) and HELM MMLU (57 subjects, 79 models).
The answer is conditional.
Under a permissive cap (scores may rise to 100) the arithmetic mean is the most expensive rule in points; for the twenty closest challengers on the Open LLM Leaderboard the median costs 3.6 points [95\% CI 1.3, 9.0] against 29.2 [26.4, 31.4] for the mean, and on HELM the Condorcet-winner requirement costs 0.30 [0.28, 0.31] of the mean's points, yet Borda and the Condorcet requirement touch many more of the 57 subjects.
Under a cap limiting the raise the picture changes: with a cap anchored in observed fine-tuning gains, first place becomes infeasible beyond a rule-dependent rank on the Open LLM Leaderboard (for the mean, beyond rank 110; far earlier for min, median and Condorcet) and the mean is the most feasible rule there; on HELM with a fixed $+10$-point cap plurality and median are the most feasible rules, whereas a cap transferred from the Open LLM Leaderboard leaves 85--96\% of HELM targets infeasible for every rule.
Under the permissive cap, $L_1$ grows with current rank with log-log slopes of 0.24--0.64 on the Open LLM Leaderboard.
The rule ordering is not robust to score normalisation or to dropping one task; we report where it holds and where it fails.
\end{abstract}
