## Closest permitted study and fair positioning

Javanmardi et al. already predict sets within the probability simplex from examples labeled with distribution vectors. In their notation a first-order predictor g(x) lies in ΔK and a score d(λ,g(x)) is split-conformalized. Their Table 1 includes:

- TV: (1/2)∑k|λk−g(x)k|.
- KL: ∑k λk log(λk/g(x)k), with the usual zero conventions where meaningful.
- First Wasserstein, 1 minus inner product, and a second-order Dirichlet relative-likelihood score.

Their §5 measures efficiency by the fraction of a discretized simplex accepted by each predicted region, evaluating coverage as well. Their work already supplies controlled synthetic and real distribution-label examples. Therefore claims that conformalizing vector outputs, choosing distribution distances, or simplex-size evaluation are themselves new would be inaccurate.

The present study can hold a training-only fitted predictor fixed to compare geometry, coordinate simultaneity, group calibration and stresses under fully controlled mechanisms with independent replications. It should include directly relevant cheap first-order scores such as TV and preferably KL, or justify their omission. Reproducing all neural/second-order models is a distinct project, not necessary to isolate score/calibration effects. The exact final comparison is the plan owner's decision.

Observed synthetic compositions, observed annotation histograms, underlying annotation probabilities and softmax estimates are different targets. In §4.1 Javanmardi et al. explicitly warn that noisy-label conformalization guarantees the noisy target; their transfer to clean probabilities uses a bounded-score-noise assumption and enlarged thresholds. Do not silently interpret an observed-vector region as a confidence set for a latent probability function.

## Mathematical conventions and direct implications

These are definitions/standard reasoning for the experiment owner to derive/check, not measured research findings:

- Δ^(K−1)={y≥0:∑y=1}; clr(y)=log y−mean(log y)·1 requires y>0. The clr vector lies in the sum-zero subspace. An orthonormal basis gives ilr coordinates with the same Euclidean norm. Counting clr-L2 and orthonormal-ilr-L2 as different region methods would duplicate the same construction.
- For two compositions y,p with K=3, v=y−p sums to zero. Apart from zero entries, one sign has only one entry; its magnitude equals the sum of magnitudes on the opposite side. Hence TV(y,p)=0.5||v||₁=||v||∞. The same pointwise score, calibration quantile and region result from TV and raw maximum residual. This identity does not hold for general K≥4; K=10 remains a distinct comparison. This is direct algebra, not an empirical finding.
- A single calibrated scalar score on the entire vector gives simultaneous-region coverage by rank exchangeability; it need not give useful conditional coverage. Any train-fitted positive scale may enter the score without invalidating the split theorem if frozen before calibration. Good adaptation is a separate empirical property.
- Coordinatewise α calibration provides coordinate marginal guarantees, not joint 1−α coverage. Calibrating each coordinate at α/K and intersecting uses a union bound, with no coordinate independence needed. Validity holds for the true simultaneous region; an arbitrary projection/renormalization of interval endpoints need not preserve it.
- Fixed observed groups permit separate calibration; sparse groups must retain q=∞ when the required rank exceeds their calibration count. Global replacement of ∞ with a finite maximum changes the theorem.
- If only group frequencies change and all within-group joint laws remain fixed, group-conditional calibration laws stay applicable and their weighted average gives test marginal coverage. This is narrower than arbitrary covariate or conditional shift.
- For iid continuous scores conditional on a trained model, with rank k≤n, the population coverage F(S_(k)) has Beta(k,n+1−k) law; the finite-test covered count is beta-binomial. Quantile discreteness yields mean k/(n+1). Ties and nonexchangeability change this reference. Holding a calibration set fixed does not guarantee its realized coverage is at least the nominal level.
- Uniform-simplex candidates drawn from Dirichlet(1,…,1) estimate a common raw-simplex volume fraction. Distinct methods should use the same candidates for a paired comparison. The conditional binomial MC error within one fixed region and the replication-to-replication variation answer different questions; report both when material. Near-zero high-dimensional hits are resolution limits, not measured exact zero volume.
- Log-ratio and raw radii use different coordinates and units. A radius comparison is valid only within the same score normalization/geometry. Smaller volume at materially lower coverage is not evidence of a better reliability-efficiency tradeoff.
- Zero replacement Tε(y)=(y+ε)/(1+Kε) is deterministic but changes the target. One may explicitly evaluate transformed-target coverage, or map a transformed set back by its inverse image; neither licenses treating undefined raw log ratios as valid scores.
