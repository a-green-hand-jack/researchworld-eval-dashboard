# Findings note — what each claim C1–C6 and D2–D5 shows (experiment-repo, for the report)

Written against `PLAN.md` (story-repo §2, §5). Every number below is read from a file under `results/` (CSV) or `results/tables/` of this repository
(delivered as `analysis/…`); path + column are given with each statement. Intervals are 95 % percentile bootstrap CIs over targets (1000 resamples, seed 0)
unless said otherwise. "Head-20" = the 20 challengers ranked 2…21 by the equal-weight mean (`tables/head20_rule_comparison.csv`); medians are over those 20 targets, and a median of
`inf` means more than half of them are infeasible. **Nothing here is a statement about the real leaderboard dynamics**: targets act alone, rivals do not react, the caps are scenario assumptions.

## 0. Reading rules (so that no CI or ratio is read differently from how it was made)

* **Paired ratio** (`tables/paired_vs_mean.csv`, `ratio_L1_*`): per target, L1(rule)/L1(mean) on the *same* target, median over targets and its CI, **only over targets feasible under both rules**;
  `n_both_feasible`, `n_only_rule_feasible`, `n_only_mean_feasible`, `n_neither` give the other cases. Under tight caps the conditioning matters (e.g. OLLB emp95: 4160 of 4495 targets are infeasible under the mean).
  `share_rule_cheaper_L1` = fraction of the paired targets with ratio < 1.  `dK_*` = K(rule) − K(mean), same pairing.
* **Different target sets**: score rules (mean, geometric, harmonic, median, min) run on **all** 4496 OLLB models (rank ≥ 2: 4495) ; rank rules (Borda, Condorcet, plurality) and the trimmed mean run on a **57-target sample** (ranks 2–5: 4, 6–20: 15, 21–100: 19, >100: 19).
  Infeasible *shares* of rank rules (57-sample) and score rules (all models) are therefore **not comparable overall**; compare them inside a rank group (`tables/feasibility_by_rank.csv`, columns `infeasible_share_<group>`) or use head-20 / paired tables.
  HELM (79 models) runs every rule on every target, so HELM comparisons have no such issue (but only 4/15/59 targets in rank groups 2–5/6–20/21–100).
* "Ceil" = every score may rise to 100; "emp95/emp99" = own score + the 95th/99th percentile of observed fine-tune − base gains per task (OLLB); HELM "hemp95/hemp99" = a **transfer** of the OLLB MMLU-PRO gain quantile (≈ 2.9 / 6.4 raw points) as a uniform per-subject cap, since HELM has no base/fine-tune pairs. Every emp95 statement on HELM is conditional on that transfer.
* Rank-rule results are exact MILP optima (no solve hit the time limit: `unproven` is 0 in every file that has the column; Copeland: `proven_L1/proven_K` all true).
  Files produced before the `unproven` flag existed (the robustness draws `*_noise_*`, `*_taskboot_*`, `*_tasksub_*`, `weighted_*`, `ollb_closed`, `l2_cost`, `ollb_trim`) contain no NaN; I verified independently that
  every infeasible/feasible label of `ollb_trim.csv` (456 rows) equals "does raising everything to the cap beat the best rival", so no trimmed-mean time-out is hidden there. For the draw files I did not repeat this check; their infeasible shares could in principle contain time-outs (a single solve takes seconds, the limit is 120 s).

## 1. Theory anchor (C1, C2) — `theory_checks.csv`, `theory_pred.csv`

* Closed forms were re-derived independently of the solvers (`src/theory_checks.py`, `src/theory_table.py`) and compared with the solver output.
  `theory_checks.csv` (`n_checked`, `n_pass`): **no proposition failed** (every row n_pass = n_checked; HELM 78/79 targets, OLLB 4495, caps ceil/front0/gain10; P1–P5 defined in the script header).
  `theory_pred.csv`: `match_L1/K/Linf` is true on every row where a prediction exists: mean and min, all caps, all targets (OLLB 35 968 rows each, HELM 474); median: K on all 474 HELM rows and on the 4503 OLLB rows where a prediction exists (all ceil targets), L1/Linf on all HELM rows but only 8 OLLB rows, because with n = 6 (even) only a ceil-K prediction is derived (empty `pred_*` = no prediction, not a mismatch).
* **C1 (mean): supported as a formula, narrower than stated as a pattern.** L1* = n(τ+ε) − Σx exactly: max abs deviation from solver output 1.1·10⁻¹³ (OLLB), 2.5·10⁻¹² (HELM) over all feasible rows (`ollb_closed.csv`, `helm_main.csv`: `L1` vs `gap`·n + n·ε; I recomputed this from the `gap` column).
  "K = 1 under ceil" holds for the *head* of the leaderboard only: OLLB head-20 K median = 1 [1,1] under ceil, 2 [2,2] under emp95, 3 [3,4] under gain10 (`head20_rule_comparison.csv`); over **all** OLLB targets only 9.2 % need K = 1 under ceil (median K = 2; `ollb_closed.csv`, mean rule, `K`). On HELM (n = 57 subjects) K is far from 1: head-20 K median 7 [4.5, 9] under ceil, 28.5 [17.5, 36] under gain10.
  K grows as caps tighten (OLLB head-20 mean: 1 → 2 → 3 for ceil → emp95 → gain10; HELM 7 → 28.5 for ceil → gain10) — as claimed. Rank-2 OLLB target: L1 = 4.77, K = 1 for every cap (`ollb_closed.csv`, `rank_mean == 2`, mean).
* **C2 (order statistics): the K step function is supported only after a correction, and the L1 part is contradicted.**
  Verified formulas: median needs the target to have `(n+1)/2` (odd n) values ≥ τ+ε, so K = max(0, ⌊n/2⌋+1 − #tasks already ≥ τ+ε) (odd n; even-n and ceil case in `theory_table.py`); min needs `#{x_t < τ+ε}` tasks (infeasible if any cap < τ+ε).
  The stated "needs ≥ ⌊n/2⌋+1 tasks" is a *worst case* (target already below the rivals on every task). Measured head-20 K: median 1 (OLLB, ceil) and 10 [6.5, 10.5] (HELM, ceil); min 2 [2,2] (OLLB) and 3.5 [2,5] (HELM, ceil) — far below n/2+1 = 4 / 29 and n = 6 / 57, because the challengers already beat the rival's decisive scores on most tasks.
  **L1 of median and min is not "of the same order or larger" than the mean's; it is much smaller**: paired ratio L1(median)/L1(mean) = 0.60 [0.60, 0.60] (OLLB, ceil, all 4495), 0.156 [0.14, 0.19] (HELM, ceil, 78); min: 0.251 [0.248, 0.253] and 0.147 [0.12, 0.179] (`paired_vs_mean.csv`, group `all`).
  (Reason: the mean must buy the whole rival-gap × n in points, median/min only fix the few decisive tasks.) Trimmed mean (OLLB, 57-sample) lies between the mean and the median in L1: ratio to mean 0.815 [0.779, 0.851] (ceil), K not computed (`ollb_trim.csv` has no `K`).

## 2. C3 — rule ordering of minimum investment

Measured, conditional on the cap; the direction for ordinal rules was not assumed.

* **L1, head-20 (same targets, `head20_rule_comparison.csv`, `L1_med` with `L1_lo`–`L1_hi`), ceil.**
  OLLB: median 3.6 [1.3, 9.0] < Borda 6.3 [4.8, 8.8] < min 10.4 [6.3, 15.2] < Condorcet 11.4 [10.4, 14.4] < plurality 16.4 [14.7, 19.1] < harmonic 18.0 [13.3, 20.8] < geometric 21.9 [18.8, 23.8] < trimmed 24.4 [21.3, 26.0] < mean 29.2 [26.4, 31.4].
  HELM: plurality 16.0 [6.5, 21.1] ≈ min 17.0 [6.9, 25.3] ≈ median 17.8 [11.4, 34.0] < Condorcet 64.8 [24.5, 94.1] < Borda 147 [55, 206] < harmonic 242 < geometric 258 < mean 278 [177, 360] (CIs of adjacent items overlap; the top three are not separated, nor are harmonic/geometric/mean).
  **So the mean (additive, compensatory) is the most expensive rule in L1 under ceil on both datasets; the ordinal rules are not more expensive than the mean.** Tighter caps: with emp95/gain10 many cells are infeasible (§3); where finite the OLLB order is unchanged (gain10: median 3.6 < Borda 6.3 < min 10.4 < Condorcet 11.4 < harmonic 18 < trimmed 24.4 < geometric 24.3 < mean 29.2; plurality infeasible for 65 % of head-20).
* **Paired against the mean over all targets of a group** (`paired_vs_mean.csv`; HELM ceil, group `all`, n = 78; ratio L1(rule)/L1(mean)): geometric 0.961 [0.953, 0.967], harmonic 0.925 [0.910, 0.936], Borda 0.776 [0.725, 0.823], Condorcet 0.295 [0.282, 0.307], median 0.156, min 0.147, plurality 0.068 [0.063, 0.073].
  OLLB ceil: geometric 0.852 [0.850, 0.853], harmonic 0.697, median 0.599, min 0.251 (all 4495 targets); Borda 0.372 [0.333, 0.436], Condorcet 0.640 [0.560, 0.655], plurality 0.500 [0.447, 0.572], trimmed 0.815 (57-sample).
  **Exceptions / conditions**: (i) for the closest challengers (rank 2–5, only 4 targets) the intervals are wide and often straddle 1 (e.g. OLLB ceil Condorcet 0.914 [0.407, 1.41], plurality 0.839 [0.592, 1.09], median 1.38 [0.03, 2.0]) — no ordering statement for rank 2–5 beyond "not distinguishable from the mean for several rules";
  (ii) under emp95 the geometric mean is *not* cheaper than the mean where both are feasible: 1.05 [0.996, 1.07] (OLLB emp95, n_both_feasible = 111 of 4495; selection: the cheap-for-geometric targets are infeasible for the mean etc.);
  (iii) HELM under gain10, `geomean` 0.99 [0.97, 1.04] and `harmonic` 0.999 [0.93, 1.03]: no difference from the mean.
* **K (breadth) behaves differently from L1 and depends on the dataset** (`paired_vs_mean.csv`, `dK_*`, ceil): on HELM Borda needs **more** tasks than the mean (+28.5 [27, 29]) and Condorcet +12 [9, 14], median +4 [3, 4]; min −4 [−4, −3], plurality −7 [−9.5, −4]. On OLLB (n = 6) differences are 0–2 tasks (Borda +1 [1, 2], Condorcet +2 [2, 2], min +1, median 0).
  Hence "cheaper in points" and "touches fewer tasks" are different statements: Borda/Condorcet are cheaper in L1 but spread the raise over many more of the 57 HELM subjects.
* Statement that survives all checks: the *mean-like* rules (arithmetic, geometric, harmonic) cost the most points; median/min/plurality the fewest where feasible; Borda and Condorcet in between and strongly dataset-dependent. The order is **not robust to the normalisation** (C5) and the feasibility ordering is reversed (C4).
* Condorcet on OLLB uses the Condorcet-winner requirement (exact MILP) on the 57-sample; the Copeland-score ranking (`rank_rule`) is only available for HELM (NaN on OLLB). D2 below.

## 4. C5 — robustness

* **Raw vs normalised OLLB scores** (D7; `tables/variants_rule_order.csv`, variant `raw`; `kendall_tau_rule_order_vs_main` of the L1 ordering of rules, head-20): **the ordering is not robust**: tau = 0.214 (ceil), 0.429 (gain10). Head-20 L1 medians on raw scores, ceil: Borda 6.0 [4.1, 7.5], harmonic 8.2 [6.8, 10.0], Condorcet 11.6 [8.4, 13.6], min 12.5 [10.9, 14.0], geometric 13.1 [11.0, 14.2], **median 16.5 [14.6, 17.9] (vs 3.6 normalised)**, mean 19.0 [15.4, 19.7] (vs 29.2), plurality 22.5 [19.6, 23.3] (vs 16.4).
  What survives: Borda is among the cheapest and Condorcet/min mid-field in both; what does not: median (cheapest normalised, among the dearest raw) and the arithmetic mean being the most expensive. Reason: the normalised columns subtract task floors with very different scales (GPQA/MUSR/MMLU-PRO).
* **No-merge and top-500 slices** (`variants_rule_order.csv`): no-merge tau = 1.0 (ceil), 0.786 (gain10); top-500 tau = 0.857 (ceil), 0.643 (gain10). Head-20 L1 medians in no-merge differ by ≤ 2.8 points from the main run (ceil: median 4.4 [1.3, 11.2] vs 3.6, Borda 5.9 vs 6.3, plurality 13.6 vs 16.4, mean 29.2 identical); in top-500 the largest change is plurality 9.0 [7.2, 13.5] vs 16.4 (ceil), Borda 5.0 vs 6.3. Conclusion C3 holds on these slices; where tau < 1 the swaps are between rules whose CIs overlap (gain10 plurality is infeasible for 65 % of main head-20 vs 30 %/10 % in no-merge/top-500).
  Note that "no-merge" and "top-500" change the set of rivals, so the rank-2…21 challengers are different models.
* **Task granularity**: the claim "rule order depends on the number of tasks" is addressed by (a) OLLB (n = 6) vs HELM (n = 57), where mean/median/min *breadth* K differs by an order of magnitude (§1–§2) and the mean-vs-ordinal L1 ordering is the same in direction but different in magnitude; (b) task subsets (`tables/robust_tasksub.csv`, `robust_tasksub_slopes.csv`, HELM, gain10, R = 50 random subsets of size n ∈ {3, 6, 12, 24, 48}; **the targets are the rank-2 and rank-5 models *under the rule in each subset*, so the gap changes with n; only two ranks were run**):
  for the rank-5 challenger, L1 grows about linearly in n for the mean (log-log slope of median L1 on n = 1.06) and geometric mean (1.02), K slope 0.94 / 0.92; for median (−0.07) and min (−0.08) L1 is flat in n, and K stays 1–2.5; Borda/Condorcet/plurality L1 slopes 0.89 / 0.62 / −0.17 with K slopes 0.74 / 0.68 / 0.46. For the rank-2 challenger slopes are noisy and can be negative (Borda −1.45, Condorcet −1.65, plurality −1.83) because at large n the rank-2 challenger under an ordinal rule is often almost tied (median L1 ≈ 0.01).
  No claim of a smooth scaling law should be made from these two ranks. The log-log slope uses the five-point median curve without CI.
* **Bootstrap over tasks and evaluation noise** (`tables/robust_noise_taskboot.csv`; HELM and OLLB, R = 100 draws, gain10; **10 targets = ranks 2–11 under the mean**; columns `cv_*` = coefficient of variation of L1 over draws per target, median over targets with a CI over targets; `rel_dev_median_*` = median draw L1 / observed L1 − 1; `infeasible_share`):

  Task bootstrap (HELM): mean cv 0.218 [0.148, 0.505], median 0.579, min 0.92 [0.71, 1.53] (30 % infeasible draws). **Reading**: the mean-like rules are the most stable in relative terms; median and min — the rules with the smallest L1 — are the least stable, so their advantage in C3 is smaller than its sampling variation for single targets.
* **Leave-one-task-out (OLLB, `tables/loto_rule_order.csv`)**: head-20 challengers re-defined under the 5 remaining tasks; `kendall_tau_rule_order_vs_full6` per dropped task, ceil: BBH 0.929, IFEval 0.786, MATH Lvl 5 0.714, GPQA 0.571, MMLU-PRO 0.429, **MUSR −0.071**; over caps median tau 0.64 (ceil) / 0.60 (emp95) / 0.70 (gain10), min −0.07 / 0.05 / 0.0. ## 5. C6 — targeted vs general (uniform) improvement

* **Algebraic fact that drives the result**: the min-max (Linf) plan is the uniform raise, so `Linf == u_star` in every row (max |Linf − u*| = 9.4·10⁻⁷, `tables/targeted_vs_uniform.csv` column `max_abs_Linf_minus_ustar`); and L1(targeted) ≤ L1(uniform) always (max violation −3.8·10⁻¹¹, i.e. none). The comparison is therefore the *volume* ratio L1/u_L1 and the *breadth* ratio K/u_K (u_K = n unless caps bind).
* **Mean rule: targeted and uniform need exactly the same number of points** (ratio 1 [1, 1] in every group, dataset and cap: `targeted_vs_uniform.csv`, rule `mean`, `ratio_L1_*`); targeted investment saves nothing in volume under the mean — it only concentrates it (K = 1 vs u_K = 6 on OLLB head ranks; HELM ceil rank 6–20 K = 8 [7.0, 9.0] vs 57).
* **Every other rule: targeted is cheaper in points.** OLLB ceil, rank 6–20: min 0.269 [0.207, 0.303], median 0.333 [0.333, 0.333], plurality 0.253 [0.218, 0.302], Borda 0.364 [0.338, 0.392], Condorcet 0.365 [0.325, 0.413], harmonic 0.439 [0.427, 0.447], geometric 0.599 [0.580, 0.639], trimmed 0.667. HELM ceil rank 6–20: min 0.044, median 0.111, plurality 0.111, Condorcet 0.324, Borda 0.596, harmonic 0.758, geometric 0.871 [0.859, 0.876].
  Targeted ≈ uniform (`share_ratio_ge_0p9` > 0.5) only for the geometric mean under tight caps: OLLB emp95 rank 6–20 geometric ratio 0.951 [0.887, 0.974] (69 % of targets ≥ 0.9), HELM gain10 6–20 0.932 [0.912, 0.964] (80 %), harmonic HELM gain10 0.865 (40 %).
  The answer to C6 is thus: targeted investment on few tasks wins in L1 for the rank rules, median, min, plurality, harmonic and (loosely) geometric rules, and for the mean never in volume (always in breadth). Rank 2–5 rows have n = 3–4 targets (CIs wide).

## 6. D2–D5

* **D2 — Copeland vs Condorcet-winner requirement (HELM, `tables/copeland_vs_condorcet.csv`, exact MILP, 7 caps × 78 targets)**: the Condorcet-winner bound is **tight within ε**: `ratio_L1_med` = 1 (CI [1, 1]) in every cap/group with both feasible except front0 (0.984 [0.983, 0.985] for ranks 21–100, 0.987 [0.984, 0.989] for 6–20, up to 1.32 points cheaper); `dK_med` = 0; `share_equal_L1` = 0 only because of ε-level differences (`max_violation_cope_gt_cond` ≤ 0 everywhere = the bound is never violated). `n_cope_feasible_cond_not` = 0 everywhere: Copeland never reaches first place where Condorcet cannot. `n_not_proven` = 0. So the claim "the Condorcet-requirement result is an upper bound that is nearly attained" is supported on HELM; **it is untested on OLLB** (Copeland MILP with 4496 models not run).
* **D3 — convex (L2) cost** (`tables/l2_vs_l1.csv`, mean and geometric only, HELM+OLLB): with a quadratic cost the optimal plan touches **all** tasks (`K_L2opt` = n = 6 / 57 in every group), whereas the L1 optimum touches K = 1…8 (OLLB/HELM head, ceil: 1 / 2–8, gain10 up to 30). So **"concentration on one task" (C1) is an artefact of the linear cost**, as the plan anticipated. Volume cost of the convex plan relative to the L1 optimum: mean 1.00 (same points; the total is fixed), geometric 1.12–1.54 (OLLB ceil 6–20: 1.33 [1.33, 1.33]; HELM ceil 6–20: 1.12 [1.12, 1.14]). Only mean and geometric were run with L2 (the L2 problems for other rules were not formulated).
* **D4 — weighted mean** (`tables/weighted_mean.csv`; 200 seeded Dirichlet weight vectors, **same 136 / 78 challengers**; `ratio_L1_*` = per-target median over draws of L1(weighted)/L1(equal-weight), median over targets): ceil: OLLB 0.475 [0.473, 0.479], HELM 0.400 [0.375, 0.423]; emp95/hemp95: 0.509 [0.474, 0.529] / 0.427 [0.336, 0.634]; pooled 5–95 % range of the ratio is wide (OLLB ceil 0.21–0.85; OLLB emp95 0.13–1.26).
  Random weights make the median target cheaper (plausibly because the raise can go on a heavily weighted task; not tested separately), but the weights also change who the rivals are, so this is "weighting changes the cost by a factor of about 2 in either direction", not a clean effect. Infeasible shares: OLLB emp95 0.557 weighted vs 0.375 equal (more infeasible under random weights), HELM gain10 0.654 vs 0.628. `spearman_rank_w_vs_rank_mean` 0.997 (OLLB) / 1.0 (HELM): the challengers' rank order is almost unchanged by weights.
* **D5 — ε = 0.1 vs 1e-3** (`tables/eps01_vs_main.csv`): L1 changes by 0 … +3.6 % (medians; e.g. OLLB ceil mean +0.33 % [0.33, 0.33], HELM gain10 min +3.2 % [2.7, 3.9]); infeasible shares move by ≤ 2.6 percentage points (largest: HELM gain10 geometric 0.692 → 0.718). **L1 and feasibility conclusions are not tie-driven. K is less stable**: the share of targets whose K changes is 0–17 % at ceil but up to 86 % for HELM gain10 geometric, 78 % harmonic, 50 % mean (K on 57 subjects shifts by one or two tasks when the margin grows; the K *medians* in the other tables are unaffected at the level of the CIs shown but should not be quoted to the unit on HELM).

## 7. Things that do not hold or are not done (state in limitations)

1. C2's L1 prediction (L1 of median/min ≥ mean) is **contradicted**; K(median) = ⌊n/2⌋+1 holds only as the maximum (fewer when the target already beats the rival on some tasks).
2. C1's "K = 1 under ceil" holds for the head of OLLB (n = 6) only.
3. The rule ordering in C3 is not robust to raw vs normalised scores (tau 0.21 / 0.43) or to dropping MUSR (tau −0.07); it is robust to no-merge/top-500 (tau 0.64–1.0).
4. Rank rules and trimmed mean on OLLB: 57-target samples only; Copeland on OLLB not run; no K for the trimmed mean.
5. emp caps on HELM are a transfer from OLLB (MMLU-PRO quantile), labelled `hemp95/99`.
6. Evaluation-noise, task-bootstrap and task-subset results use 10 (ranks 2–11) or 2 (ranks 2, 5) challengers only and the gain10 cap only; no CI on the log-log task-subset slopes.
7. L2 only for mean/geometric; weighted-mean only for the mean.
8. Rivals do not react and the cap model is an assumption: all statements are about minimum investment under the stated scenario, not about what a developer would find.
9. No MILP time-limit hit anywhere in the files that record it (`unproven` = 0, Copeland `proven_*` true); the robustness-draw files have no such flag (see §0).
