# Captions and placement

All measurements below are from the completed synthetic experiment, not from LLM responses. Recommended main-paper allocation is selection and budget figures plus the method table; decision, full grids and MSE may go in the appendix if space is tight. Each figure is natively 5.5 inches wide for the official single-column ICLR 2027 template. Insert at full linewidth; further reduction will make labels too small. All points are plotted; selected intermediate tick labels are omitted to prevent collisions. The table source fits the 5.5-inch text area at 8 pt. Every numerical panel has 20000 independent simulation repetitions per setting. Percentage points (pp) mean 100 times an accuracy difference; pp² means 10000 times mean squared accuracy error.

## figures/synthetic-selection.pdf

**Selection optimism and interval validity depend on search size and shared errors.** All candidates have true accuracy 0.4, so search cannot improve true quality. Columns vary the shared-error probability rho, which equals pairwise outcome correlation in this equal-quality setting. Top: mean selected search accuracy minus the selected candidate's known true accuracy, for n = 32, 128 and 512 iid questions. Bottom: selected-candidate coverage of nominal 95% Clopper–Pearson (CP) intervals at n = 64. Reused CP applies an ordinary interval to the winner's search score; simultaneous CP uses marginal error 0.05/K; extra audit evaluates the frozen full-search winner on n independent additional questions. The gray line marks 95% coverage. Shading gives marginal 95% Monte Carlo intervals: mean ±1.96 MCSE for optimism and exact binomial intervals for coverage. Some bands are narrower than markers. The extra audit costs n additional model calls beyond the nK search calls. The interval correction changes the interval, not the reused point score.

## figures/synthetic-budgets.pdf

**Reliable reporting trades uncertainty against search resources.** A frozen bank of K = 32 candidates has a default at 0.4 and 31 other true accuracies equally spaced from 0.32 to 0.48 (spread parameter 0.08). Columns vary shared-question mixture probability rho; for unequal accuracies it is not exactly pairwise outcome correlation. Top: average nominal 95% CP interval width. Bottom: average true accuracy of each protocol's selected candidate; the dashed line is the best available accuracy, 48%. Full search uses n questions and nK calls. Reused CP and simultaneous CP share this search; extra audit adds n fresh questions/calls and shares its winner. Question split uses floor(n/2) search questions and the remainder for an independent audit, preserving n total distinct questions while using fewer calls. Call split uses s = floor(nK/(K+1)) search questions and h = nK − sK audit questions, preserving nK calls while allowing more distinct questions. All three full-search methods have the same selected quality; they appear as one gray curve below. Shading is mean ±1.96 MCSE and can be narrower than markers. Width alone does not establish validity: reused CP can undercover (selection figure). Each method's interval targets its own selected candidate; split protocols can select a different candidate.

## tables/synthetic-methods.tex

**Numerical comparison of reporting protocols under two declared settings.** Entries are means with Monte Carlo standard errors in parentheses, based on 20000 independent repetitions per cell. Bias is reported score minus the protocol's selected candidate's known accuracy; coverage concerns that candidate's accuracy; width is the mean nominal 95% CP interval width; quality is its mean true accuracy. Bias and width use percentage points, coverage and quality use percent. The first setting has equal candidate accuracies 0.4; the second has a 0.4 default and remaining candidates equally spaced from 0.32 to 0.48. Both have shared-question probability rho = 0.5. Simultaneous CP preserves the reused point estimate and bias. Extra audit adds n calls; question split preserves n total questions; call split preserves nK total calls. Costs include search and audit. Parenthetical errors rounded to 0.000 are below 0.0005 in displayed units, or exactly zero when selected truth is constant. The CSV retains full precision and search/audit counts. All methods assume a frozen bank and iid questions; neither finite-pool population inference nor prompt generation from benchmark labels is represented.

## figures/synthetic-decisions.pdf

**A conservative frozen-pair decision can become unreliable after winner selection.** At n = 512 with all candidate accuracies equal to 0.4, a false-superiority event occurs when a nondefault selected winner's lower two-sided 95% CP endpoint exceeds the frozen default's upper endpoint. Frequency is unconditional over all repetitions, not conditional on making a claim and not a false-discovery rate. Ordinary intervals have at most 5% family error for a fixed pair before selection (dashed line); this bound does not transfer to the selected pair. Simultaneous CP uses per-candidate error 0.05/K and protects comparisons within the frozen bank. Columns vary the equal-quality correlation rho. Shading gives marginal exact 95% binomial Monte Carlo intervals and can be narrower than markers. At rho = 0, K = 128, the ordinary-rule false-superiority frequency is 9.89% (MCSE 0.211 pp). Zero observed events among 20000 repetitions still permit a one-sided 95% upper risk of about 0.015%; they do not establish zero risk. This figure concerns this particular interval-nonoverlap rule, not a paired test or all superiority procedures.

## figures/synthetic-optimism-grid.pdf

**All 288 settings of the synthetic selection experiment.** Color is mean reused-score optimism (selected search accuracy minus selected true accuracy), in percentage points. Rows of panels vary the quality-spread parameter (0 or 0.08); columns vary shared-question mixture probability rho. Each panel crosses six sample sizes n with eight frozen-bank sizes K. The default has true accuracy 0.4; at spread zero all candidates equal 0.4, while spread 0.08 places other candidates evenly over [0.32, 0.48] for K > 2; K = 2 has one challenger at 0.48, and K = 1 has only the default. Thus varying K changes the candidate quality grid when spread is nonzero; use equal-quality panels to isolate search-budget effects. Color range includes small negative Monte Carlo estimates (minimum −0.176 pp); expectation optimism is nonnegative under the assumed frozen-bank iid model. This is a complete-grid display, not a simultaneous inferential statement about extrema; per-cell MCSEs and seeds are in the plotting input CSV.

## figures/synthetic-coverage-grid.pdf

**Naive 95% interval coverage across the complete synthetic grid.** Panel settings and candidate qualities match the optimism grid. Color shows the fraction of 20000 repetitions in which the winner's ordinary two-sided 95% CP interval contains its known true accuracy. A white dot marks a cell whose marginal exact 95% Monte Carlo upper confidence endpoint is below 95% coverage; these are per-cell diagnostics without correction across cells. A cell without a dot is not evidence of guaranteed coverage. Shared-question mixture probability equals pairwise outcome correlation only when accuracies are equal. The full 288-cell CSV contains exact plotting means, MCSEs and random seeds.

## figures/synthetic-mse.pdf

**Score accuracy depends on protocol, resource budget and candidate dependence.** For K = 32, mean squared error (MSE) is the average squared difference between the reported score and the selected candidate's true accuracy, expressed in squared percentage points (logarithmic y axis). Panel rows vary quality spread; columns vary shared-question mixture probability rho. Candidate qualities and budget definitions match the other synthetic figures. Simultaneous CP is omitted because it retains the reused point score and therefore has identical MSE. Shading is mean ±1.96 MCSE on the untransformed MSE scale, displayed on the logarithmic axis. Extra audit incurs n additional calls; question split and call split change selection resources and may change the selected candidate. These synthetic outcomes do not imply one protocol dominates for actual LLM search or all budgets.
